## State Requirements

The Delaware Master Plumber license is regulated by the **Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners**.

Applicants seeking licensure by examination must satisfy one of Delaware's applicable experience pathways before receiving approval to sit for the Master Plumber exam.

Applicants who hold a **journeyman certificate from a federally approved plumbing apprenticeship program** must provide a copy of the apprenticeship program Certificate of Completion and proof of at least **two years of work experience under the supervision of a master licensee after receiving the certificate**.

Applicants who do not have a recognized journeyman certificate must provide proof of at least **seven years of work experience under the supervision of a master licensee**. The Delaware Board reviews that experience before determining whether the candidate may proceed to the Apprenticeship By-Pass Exam.

After completing the required preliminary steps, the applicant must receive Board approval to sit for the Master Plumber examination. ProV then provides the current Candidate Information Bulletin and scheduling instructions.

Delaware requires a minimum score of **70%** to pass. Applicants who do not receive a passing score after three examination attempts must reapply as a new applicant and obtain Board approval before testing again after the applicable waiting period.

Passing the examination alone does not automatically issue the Master Plumber license. The Board must also verify the applicant's work experience, apprenticeship documentation when applicable, application materials, official examination score, and all remaining licensure requirements.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Delaware Master Plumber study plan should begin with **Drain, Waste and Vent Systems**. Candidates should understand how waste moves through a plumbing system and how proper venting protects trap seals and supports effective drainage.

Review drainage pipe sizing, slope, cleanouts, traps, fixture drains, branches, stacks, building drains, building sewers, indirect waste, and vent-system relationships.

**Venting** should be studied as a system rather than a collection of separate rules. Candidates should understand why vents are required, how vents connect to drainage systems, and how vent sizing and arrangement affect plumbing-system performance.

**Water Supply and Distribution** is another major study area. Review potable water piping, distribution systems, pressure, valves, fixture supply, pipe sizing, materials, and protection of the water supply.

Candidates should also understand **backflow-related concepts** and the importance of protecting potable water from contamination or cross-connection hazards.

**Fixtures and Fittings** should include proper installation, connection, accessibility, supports, clearances, and the way fixtures interact with drainage and water-supply systems.

**Water Heaters** require attention to installation, temperature and pressure protection, connections, drainage, combustion-related requirements when fuel-fired, and other applicable code provisions.

**Fuel Gas Systems** should be studied using the International Fuel Gas Code. Candidates should review gas piping materials, sizing, developed length, pressure, appliance demand, shutoff valves, appliance connectors, pressure testing, combustion air, and venting.

Gas sizing questions should be approached methodically. Identify the fuel type, system pressure, appliance demand, developed length, and applicable sizing method before choosing a table or pipe size.

**Plumbing Math** should be practiced regularly. Master-level plumbers need to work comfortably with measurements, fractions, decimals, slopes, areas, volumes, and other calculations used in layout, sizing, and installation.

For calculation questions, write down the known values and determine exactly what the problem asks for before selecting a formula or method. This can reduce errors caused by rushing into arithmetic before understanding the problem.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

**Isometrics and Plan Reading** should be part of regular practice. Candidates should be able to interpret piping diagrams, identify branches and stacks, follow changes in direction, recognize fittings, and visualize the complete plumbing system from a drawing.

A good plan-reading exercise is to trace one system at a time. Follow the sanitary drainage system, then the vent system, then the water distribution, and then any fuel gas piping shown.

**Storm and Roof Drainage** should include roof drains, leaders, storm piping, sizing concepts, discharge, and the relationship between roof area and drainage-system design.

**Safety** should not be treated as a minor subject. Plumbing work can involve tools, ladders, excavation, hot work, pressure testing, heavy materials, gas systems, confined areas, and other hazards.

The **International Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used actively during practice. Rather than reading it continuously, use a question to identify the relevant subject and then locate the applicable section, table, or exception.

The same approach works with the **International Fuel Gas Code, 2018**. Practice locating gas piping, appliance, venting, combustion air, and testing requirements until the structure of the book becomes familiar.

**Plumbing Basics for Contractors** can help reinforce trade knowledge when the problem involves a practical plumbing concept rather than a specific code section. Use it to strengthen terminology, field relationships, calculations, and installation understanding.

When a practice question is missed, determine why. The problem may involve weak code navigation, misunderstanding a plumbing principle, a math error, plan-reading difficulty, or confusion between plumbing and fuel gas requirements.

Organize missed questions by category. If repeated errors involve venting, concentrate additional review there. If math or isometrics are weaker, build dedicated sessions around those subjects.

As preparation progresses, move into mixed-topic question sets. A real exam can switch quickly from drainage to water supply, then to gas piping, plumbing math, water heaters, or storm drainage.
