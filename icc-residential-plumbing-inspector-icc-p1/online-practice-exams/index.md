## Test Information and Study Materials

The online practice-question product includes **3 months of access**. Candidates can use the access period to create a structured study routine covering all six official P1 examination categories while repeatedly practicing with the 2021 International Residential Code.

Begin with **Sanitary Drainage** because it represents 23% of the examination. Review approved drainage materials, fittings, joints, connections, building drains, building sewers, branches, stacks, cleanouts, backwater valves, sumps, ejectors, traps, and indirect waste systems.

Drainage-system questions often require careful identification of the type of piping involved. Candidates should distinguish among fixture drains, branch drains, stacks, building drains, and building sewers before selecting the applicable requirement.

Drainage sizing should receive focused practice. Candidates may need to use drainage fixture units, pipe sizing information, slope requirements, and tables to determine whether a proposed or installed drainage system is acceptable.

Fittings are also important. Drainage piping must direct waste through the system using approved fittings and configurations. Candidates should practice recognizing whether a particular change in direction or connection uses an acceptable fitting.

Cleanouts should be included in regular study. Inspectors need to evaluate required locations, accessibility, and installation of cleanouts throughout the drainage system.

Traps are part of the Sanitary Drainage content area. Candidates should understand trap type, size, design, location, installation, and how traps work with the venting system to protect the building from sewer gases.

Indirect waste systems should also be reviewed. These systems use air gaps or air breaks in appropriate applications to protect fixtures and equipment from contamination or drainage-system backup.

Next, focus on **Vents**, which represent 22% of the exam. Review vent materials, joints, connections, grades, sizing, venting methods, and installation requirements.

A plumbing vent system protects trap seals by allowing air movement within the drainage system. Candidates should understand this purpose because it helps explain why vent sizing, connections, location, and termination requirements matter.

Vent sizing questions often require use of code tables. Practice identifying the drainage load, pipe size, developed length, and other conditions required before selecting the applicable vent size.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

Different venting methods can appear on the examination. Candidates should become familiar with the methods permitted by the IRC and practice recognizing which method is described in a question.

Vent installation also involves termination requirements and protection from improper connections. Candidates should identify whether the question is testing vent size, vent method, connection point, grade, or termination before searching the reference.

**Water Supply and Distribution** represent 21% of the examination. Review approved piping materials, joints, connections, cold- and hot-water service, distribution piping, sizing, valves, protection against contamination, pressure, volume, potable water, and treatment systems.

Water-distribution questions should begin with identification of the system. Candidates should distinguish between the water service and interior distribution piping before applying sizing or installation provisions.

Potable-water protection deserves substantial attention. Inspectors must verify that the water supply remains protected from contamination. Candidates should review backflow prevention, cross-connection control, air gaps, and other applicable protective measures.

Pressure and volume are also part of the official content. Residential plumbing systems must provide adequate water while remaining within code requirements. Candidates should understand how pressure conditions affect equipment and piping installations.

Valves should be included in preparation because they are an important part of system control and isolation. Candidates should understand where shutoff valves are required and how they are used in residential water systems.

**General Requirements** represent 14%. Study permit-related responsibilities, pipe protection, installation, supports, sleeves, penetrations, protection against freezing, corrosion, stress, strain, and physical damage.

Piping systems often pass through framing, walls, floors, crawl spaces, attics, and exterior areas. Inspectors need to verify that the pipe is properly supported and protected from building movement, freezing, physical damage, and structural interference.

Building-component damage is another official inspection task. Plumbing installation cannot compromise structural members beyond what the code permits. Candidates should review requirements affecting cutting, boring, notching, and protection of framing members.

Testing is also included in General Requirements. Plumbing systems must undergo required tests, and the inspector must verify that these tests are performed as required before systems are concealed or placed into service.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

**Water Heaters** represent 12% of the exam. Review appliance approval, installation, service and distribution piping, clearances, locations, protection, pans, temperature and pressure relief valves, discharge piping, thermal expansion, and pipe sizing.

Temperature and pressure relief provisions should receive focused study because they are important safety components of water-heater installations. Candidates should understand how the relief valve and discharge piping must be arranged.

Thermal expansion should also be reviewed. Closed water systems can require provisions to address pressure increases caused by water heating.

Fuel-fired water heaters introduce additional requirements. Candidates should be prepared to evaluate fuel-gas piping, combustion air, and required venting in addition to the plumbing side of the installation.

**Fixtures** represent 8%. Study minimum required fixtures, approved fixture materials, installation, clearances, waste receptors, appliances, flow rates, temperatures, faucets, fittings, and accessories.

Fixture questions should begin by identifying what is being inspected. The issue may be the fixture itself, its clearance, its connection, its flow rate, temperature control, or the installation of a fitting or accessory.

Practice should also include fixture installation and sealing. Inspectors need to verify that fixtures are properly positioned, secured, connected, and installed so the completed plumbing system operates as intended.

Timed practice is important throughout the 3-month access period. The P1 examination provides 120 minutes for 60 questions, averaging approximately two minutes per question. Familiar questions should be answered efficiently so additional time remains for pipe-sizing tables and detailed plumbing scenarios.

When a practice question is missed, identify the reason. The problem may involve searching the wrong plumbing section, selecting the wrong table, confusing a vent with a drain, overlooking a backflow requirement, misreading fixture units, or failing to recognize a fuel-gas component.

If drainage questions are difficult, devote additional study time to sizing, fittings, traps, and cleanouts. If venting takes too long, practice recognizing venting methods and using vent tables. If water-supply questions are challenging, focus on sizing, pressure, valves, and protection of potable water.
