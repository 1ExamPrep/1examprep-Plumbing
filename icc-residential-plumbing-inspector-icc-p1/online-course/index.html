The ICC P1 Residential Plumbing Inspector exam evaluates knowledge related to residential plumbing inspection and code-compliant plumbing system installation. Candidates should be prepared for questions involving general requirements, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, vents, materials, joints, connections, grades, sizing, methods, installation requirements, and inspection decision-making.

Open Book Test

The ICC Residential Plumbing Inspector P1 exam is an open book test. Candidates may use the approved reference during the exam, but they must know how to use that reference efficiently. Open book testing rewards candidates who are familiar with the book, organized in their study habits, and comfortable locating code requirements under timed conditions.

For this exam, candidates should become comfortable using the International Residential Code, 2021. You should know how to use the table of contents, index, chapter organization, definitions, tables, figures, notes, footnotes, and exceptions. Many residential plumbing inspection questions require more than finding a section number. Candidates must read the full requirement and apply it correctly to the condition described in the question.

A strong open book strategy begins with identifying the subject of the question. If the question involves fixtures, locate the fixture provisions. If it involves water heaters, move to the water heater requirements. If it involves water supply or distribution, focus on supply piping, valves, backflow, sizing, and installation rules. If it involves sanitary drainage or venting, identify whether the question is asking about materials, slope, fixture units, cleanouts, traps, vent size, vent location, or installation method.

Open book does not mean the answer will be obvious. Residential plumbing inspection questions may turn on a specific fixture type, pipe size, fitting, joint, material, slope, developed length, venting method, connection point, water heater safety requirement, cleanout location, or code exception. Candidates should read the full question, identify the key condition, locate the correct section, and review the complete requirement before choosing an answer.

Because the P1 exam includes 60 questions in 2 hours, pacing is important. Some questions may be answered quickly, while others may require more time with the code book. Candidates should avoid spending too much time on one difficult question during the first pass. Mark difficult questions and return to them after answering the questions that can be solved more quickly.

Test Information and Study Materials

The P1 exam requires a practical understanding of residential plumbing inspection and the ability to apply requirements from the International Residential Code, 2021. Candidates should study with attention to how residential plumbing requirements are organized and how inspection conditions affect the rule that applies.

For general requirements, candidates should review plumbing system scope, approved materials, installation responsibilities, protection of piping, testing concepts, identification of code terms, and general inspection expectations. These topics help build the foundation for understanding how residential plumbing installations are evaluated for compliance.

For plumbing fixtures, candidates should study fixture installation, fixture connections, fixture clearances, fixture supply requirements, drainage connections, and related residential plumbing provisions. Inspection questions may require candidates to identify whether a fixture is properly installed, connected, trapped, vented, and accessible for its intended use.

For water heaters, candidates should review water heater installation, access, location, safety provisions, temperature and pressure relief requirements, discharge piping, pan and drainage requirements when applicable, and related system connections. Water heater questions often involve safety details and installation conditions that must be read carefully.

For water supply and distribution, candidates should study pipe materials, pipe sizing concepts, valves, pressure considerations, protection from contamination, backflow protection, fixture supply, hot and cold water distribution, and installation requirements. Water supply questions may require table use or careful review of specific installation conditions.

For sanitary drainage, candidates should review drainage materials, drainage fixture units, pipe sizing, slope and grade, cleanouts, fixture connections, branch drains, stacks, building drains, and drainage system layout. Drainage questions often depend on exact pipe size, slope, connection, fixture type, and cleanout location.

For vents, candidates should study vent materials, vent sizing, vent connections, vent terminals, wet venting concepts, distance limitations, and installation methods. Venting is a major residential plumbing inspection topic because small layout differences can change the requirement that applies.
