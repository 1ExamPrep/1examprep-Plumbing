Open Book Test

The ICC Plumbing Plans Examiner P3 exam is an open book test. Candidates may use approved references during the exam, but they must know how to use those references efficiently. Open book testing rewards candidates who are familiar with the books, organized in their study habits, and comfortable locating code requirements under timed conditions.

For this exam, candidates should become comfortable using ICC A117.1-2017, the International Plumbing Code, 2021, and the International Fuel Gas Code, 2021. You should know how to use the table of contents, indexes, chapter organization, definitions, tables, figures, notes, footnotes, and exceptions. Many plumbing plan review questions require more than finding a section number. Candidates must read the full requirement and apply it correctly to the plan condition described in the question.

A strong open book strategy begins with identifying the subject of the question. If the question involves fixtures, water heaters, water distribution, sanitary drainage, storm drainage, interceptors, separators, indirect waste, cleanouts, traps, vents, or commercial plumbing system requirements, the International Plumbing Code is usually the primary reference. If the question involves fuel gas piping, gas-fired appliances, regulators, shutoff valves, combustion air, chimneys, vents, vent connectors, or fuel gas system installation, the International Fuel Gas Code is usually the primary reference. If the question involves accessible plumbing fixtures, clear floor spaces, grab bars, lavatory clearances, water closet clearances, urinals, drinking fountains, bathing fixtures, reach ranges, or accessibility dimensions, ICC A117.1-2017 may be the primary reference.

Open book does not mean the answer will be obvious. Plumbing plans examiner questions may turn on a specific fixture type, occupancy condition, submitted drawing detail, accessibility dimension, pipe size, fitting, joint, material, slope, developed length, venting method, storm drainage condition, connection point, water heater safety requirement, cleanout location, gas appliance condition, or code exception. Candidates should read the full question, identify the key condition, locate the correct reference, and review the complete requirement before choosing an answer.

Test Information and Study Materials

The P3 exam requires a practical understanding of plumbing plan review and the ability to apply requirements from ICC A117.1-2017, the International Plumbing Code, 2021, and the International Fuel Gas Code, 2021. Candidates should study with attention to how plumbing, fuel gas, and accessibility requirements are organized and how proposed plan conditions affect the rule that applies.

For general administration, candidates should review code scope, permits, construction documents, approvals, duties and powers, alternative materials and methods, violations, stop work concepts, materials, installation responsibilities, and compliance language. These topics help build the foundation for understanding how plumbing plans are reviewed and approved.

For construction documents, candidates should focus on what information plumbing plans should communicate. This may include fixture layouts, fixture schedules, water distribution risers, sanitary drainage risers, venting diagrams, storm drainage layouts, roof drain information, cleanout locations, water heater information, fuel gas piping diagrams, equipment input ratings, interceptor details, accessibility layouts, and coordination with architectural plans. Plans examiner questions may ask whether the submitted information is sufficient to determine compliance.

For plumbing fixtures, candidates should study fixture installation, fixture counts, fixture connections, fixture clearances, fixture supply requirements, drainage connections, indirect waste conditions, and related commercial plumbing provisions. Plan review questions may require candidates to determine whether the proposed fixture layout, fixture type, or fixture count supports code compliance.

For accessibility, candidates should review accessible toilet compartments, lavatories, sinks, drinking fountains, urinals, water closets, bathing fixtures, grab bars, clear floor spaces, knee and toe clearance, reach ranges, controls, and fixture approach requirements. Accessibility questions often depend on exact dimensions, fixture location, mounting height, clearance, and relationship between the fixture and surrounding elements.

For water heaters, candidates should review water heater installation, access, location, safety provisions, temperature and pressure relief requirements, discharge piping, pan and drainage requirements when applicable, and related system connections. Plan review questions may ask whether the drawings show enough information to determine if a water heater installation can meet code requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

For water supply and distribution, candidates should study pipe materials, pipe sizing concepts, valves, pressure considerations, protection from contamination, backflow protection, fixture supply, hot and cold water distribution, and installation requirements. Water supply questions may require table use or careful review of specific plan conditions.

For sanitary drainage, candidates should review drainage materials, drainage fixture units, pipe sizing, slope and grade, cleanouts, fixture connections, branch drains, stacks, building drains, building sewers, and drainage system layout. Drainage questions often depend on exact pipe size, slope, connection, fixture type, cleanout location, and how the proposed system is shown on the drawings.

For vents, candidates should study vent materials, vent sizing, vent connections, vent terminals, wet venting concepts, common venting, circuit venting, distance limitations, and installation methods. Venting is a major plumbing plan review topic because system layout and fixture grouping can change the requirement that applies.

For storm drainage, candidates should review roof drains, secondary or emergency overflow drainage, storm drainage systems, conductors, leaders, scuppers, sizing concepts, rainfall rates, roof area, and drainage system layout. Commercial storm drainage questions may require table use or careful reading of the drainage condition described in the question.

For interceptors and separators, candidates should study grease interceptors, oil separators, sand interceptors, special waste conditions, indirect waste, maintenance access, installation requirements, and system connections. These topics often involve both code definitions and installation rules, and plan review questions may focus on whether the proposed system identifies the required device and access conditions.

For fuel gas systems, candidates should study the International Fuel Gas Code carefully. Important areas include gas piping materials, pipe sizing, developed length, appliance input, fittings, valves, regulators, sediment traps, pressure testing, leak detection, protection of piping, appliance connections, combustion air, chimneys, vents, and vent connectors. Fuel gas plan review questions may require careful use of tables and close attention to the proposed system layout.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

For materials, joints, connections, and grades, candidates should review approved piping materials, fitting requirements, joint methods, transition rules, supports, protection, slope, and connection details. Plan review questions may ask whether a proposed material, joint, grade, or connection is acceptable based on the code and the condition described.

Plumbing plans examiners should also practice thinking like a reviewer. Exam questions may describe a submitted plan and ask what the code requires, what information is missing, what condition is acceptable, what correction is needed, or which reference applies. Candidates should build the habit of identifying the system, locating the code requirement, reading the full section, and applying it to the proposed design condition.

Good preparation includes repeated practice with the actual references. Use the table of contents to understand each book’s structure. Use the index to find terms quickly. Review definitions often. Practice using tables carefully and reading all notes, exceptions, and footnotes. Build the habit of confirming the full requirement before selecting an answer.

Timed practice is also important. Since the exam includes 60 questions in 2.5 hours, candidates should practice moving steadily through questions. Some questions may be answered quickly, while others may require code lookup, table interpretation, accessibility review, or careful comparison between related provisions. Practicing under timed conditions helps candidates learn when to keep moving and when to return to a difficult question later.
