## Open Book Test

The ICC P3 Plumbing Plans Examiner examination is an **open book test**. Candidates have two hours and thirty minutes to complete 60 multiple-choice questions and must follow current ICC examination rules regarding allowable references and testing materials.

The testing period provides an average of approximately two and a half minutes per question. Because the examination is open book, reference-navigation skill is extremely important. Candidates should not expect to search broadly through all three references for every answer.

When a question involves water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, fixtures, storm drainage, or general plumbing requirements, the 2021 International Plumbing Code will usually be the principal reference. Questions involving gas-fired equipment, gas piping, combustion air, or fuel-gas venting may require the 2021 International Fuel Gas Code.

Accessibility questions involving plumbing fixtures and accessible facilities may require ICC A117.1-2017. Candidates should be comfortable identifying when a question involves an accessibility requirement rather than only a general plumbing-code requirement.

Effective open-book preparation should focus on learning the organization of the references. Candidates should know where major plumbing chapters are located, how to use indexes and tables, and how to recognize which reference applies from the wording of the question.

Practice questions are especially useful when treated as navigation exercises. After answering, locate the supporting section and review the surrounding provisions, exceptions, tables, and related definitions.

## Test Information and Study Materials

The online practice-question product includes **3 months of access**. Candidates can use the access period to build a structured study schedule around the official P3 content areas and repeatedly practice applying the 2021 IPC, 2021 IFGC, and ICC A117.1-2017.

Begin with **Water Supply and Distribution** because it represents the largest percentage of the exam. Review water-service and distribution piping, approved materials, connections, sizing, valves, pressure requirements, backflow protection, and hot-water distribution.

Plans examiners should learn to review a water-distribution layout systematically. Identify the service entrance, distribution piping, branches, fixture demand, equipment connections, valves, and backflow-protection requirements before evaluating individual details.

Water-pressure questions should be approached carefully because both insufficient and excessive pressure can affect plumbing system design. Candidates should understand how pressure conditions influence the plan-review process and which provisions apply.

Next, focus on **Fixtures**. Fixture review involves more than identifying a toilet or lavatory. Plans may need to be evaluated for required fixtures, approved fixture types, installation, clearances, accessibility, waste receptors, and other conditions.

Accessibility should be integrated into fixture preparation. ICC A117.1-2017 provides requirements affecting accessible plumbing fixtures and facilities. Candidates should recognize when the scenario requires use of the accessibility standard in addition to the plumbing code.

For **Vents**, study the function and arrangement of plumbing vent systems. Review vent sizing, vent connections, vent termination, wet venting, common venting, circuit venting, and other methods addressed by the code.

Plan-review questions may describe a drainage and vent arrangement rather than simply asking for a definition. Candidates should identify the venting method shown or described and then determine whether the design meets the applicable requirements.

**Sanitary Drainage** should receive substantial study time. Review drainage materials, fittings, slope, sizing, fixture-unit loads, building drains, building sewers, cleanouts, connections, and installation requirements.

Drainage sizing often requires candidates to identify the correct table and understand the information provided in the problem. Practice translating fixture information into the values needed to evaluate pipe size and system capacity.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

For **Water Heaters**, study safety devices, temperature and pressure relief valves, discharge piping, pans, thermal expansion, locations, protection, piping connections, and fuel-gas-related requirements for gas-fired equipment.

A gas-fired water-heater scenario may require both the IPC and IFGC. Candidates should learn to separate the plumbing aspects from the fuel-gas aspects so they can move to the appropriate reference efficiently.

Combustion air and venting should also be reviewed when preparing for fuel-fired water-heater questions. These systems must support safe appliance operation and proper removal of combustion products.

For **Traps, Interceptors, Separators, Special Piping, and Storage Systems**, review the purpose and installation of traps and specialized devices. Candidates should understand when interceptors or separators are required and how special waste or storage systems are addressed.

Trap questions should include trap seals, prohibited arrangements, installation, and protection of the drainage system from sewer gases. Plans examiners should be able to identify whether proposed fixture drainage includes compliant trapping.

For **Storm Drainage**, study roof drainage, leaders, conductors, sizing, secondary or overflow drainage where applicable, and the overall movement of rainwater from the roof to the approved point of disposal.

Storm-drainage plan review often requires candidates to work with roof areas and drainage-system sizing. Practice identifying the information needed before consulting the applicable table.

**Health Care Plumbing** represents a smaller portion of the exam but should still be included in preparation. Healthcare facilities can include specialized plumbing systems and requirements that differ from ordinary commercial buildings.

**General Requirements** provide the foundation for many other subjects. Review piping protection, installation, supports, penetrations, protection against physical damage, and other requirements that can apply throughout the plumbing system.

Plan-review preparation should emphasize the ability to analyze an entire system rather than only isolated components. A plumbing plan may appear acceptable in one area but contain an issue involving fixture accessibility, pipe sizing, venting, backflow protection, drainage, or fuel-gas equipment elsewhere.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

A useful study method is to review each question in three stages: identify the system, identify the controlling condition, and identify the reference. This approach helps prevent unnecessary searching and encourages code-based reasoning.

Timed practice should be included throughout the 3-month access period. The examination provides 150 minutes for 60 questions, averaging approximately two and a half minutes per question. Straightforward questions should be answered efficiently so more time remains for detailed sizing, accessibility, or code-table questions.

When a practice question is missed, determine why. The issue may involve choosing the wrong reference, overlooking an accessibility requirement, using the wrong sizing table, confusing drainage and venting provisions, or failing to recognize a fuel-gas component.

If water-distribution questions are difficult, spend additional time with sizing, pressure, backflow, and piping requirements. If fixture questions create uncertainty, combine IPC fixture review with ICC A117.1 accessibility study. If venting questions take too long, learn the organization of the venting chapter and practice identifying different venting methods.

A strong P3 preparation routine combines plumbing-code knowledge, accessibility awareness, fuel-gas familiarity, plan-review reasoning, and repeated reference navigation. The goal is to understand why a design does or does not comply and to locate the supporting requirement efficiently.
