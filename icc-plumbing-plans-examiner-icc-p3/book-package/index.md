## Test Information and Study Materials

Effective preparation for the ICC Plumbing Plans Examiner - P3 exam should begin with code navigation. Candidates should learn the structure of each reference, including the table of contents, index, definitions, chapter organization, tables, figures, and exceptions. The exam requires candidates to find information quickly and apply it correctly to plan review scenarios.

Plumbing plan review procedures are a major part of preparation. Candidates should understand what construction documents must show and how a plans examiner checks proposed work for compliance. This may include reviewing fixture schedules, pipe sizes, riser diagrams, slope, venting, cleanouts, storm drainage, equipment information, and required protection. Candidates should practice identifying missing or incomplete information on plans because plan review is often about whether the submitted documents demonstrate compliance.

Fixture calculations should receive careful attention. Candidates should review minimum plumbing fixture requirements, occupancy-based fixture counts, fixture types, public and employee facilities, drinking fountains, service sinks, accessible fixtures, and related exceptions. Accessibility standards may affect fixture location and usability, so candidates should be ready to use both the International Plumbing Code and ICC A117.1 when reviewing fixture layouts.

Water supply and distribution study should include potable water protection, pipe sizing, pressure, volume, valves, service piping, distribution piping, hot water delivery, backflow prevention, water hammer, thermal expansion, and protection from contamination. Plans examiner questions may require checking whether a design provides adequate water supply and whether the proposed system includes required protection.

Sanitary drainage is another central study area. Candidates should review drainage fixture units, building drains, building sewers, horizontal branches, stacks, offsets, pipe slope, cleanouts, traps, interceptors, sumps, ejectors, indirect waste, and special waste. Drainage questions often involve tables, diagrams, and detailed code conditions. Candidates should practice locating the correct sizing table and reading all notes before selecting an answer.
