Exam Details

The Florida Commercial Plumbing Inspector ICC 2P examination contains 60 multiple-choice questions. Candidates receive 2 hours and 30 minutes to complete the examination.

The official examination content outline includes:

  • General Requirements: 8%
  • Fixtures: 15%
  • Water Heaters: 12%
  • Water Supply and Distribution: 18%
  • Sanitary Drainage: 13%
  • Vents: 15%
  • Traps, Interceptors, Separators, Special Piping, and Storage Systems: 9%
  • Storm Drainage: 5%
  • Health Care Plumbing: 5%

Water Supply and Distribution is the largest examination category at 18%. Candidates should be comfortable with potable water systems, distribution piping, sizing, materials, valves, pressure, backflow protection, cross-connection control, and related inspection requirements.

Fixtures and Vents each represent 15% of the examination. Fixture preparation should include installation, clearances, accessibility, supports, connections, required fixtures, and fixture-specific requirements. Vent preparation should include vent sizing, connections, terminations, venting methods, and protection of trap seals.

Sanitary Drainage accounts for 13%, while Water Heaters represent 12%. Candidates should understand drainage-system design and installation as well as water-heater safety, connections, relief protection, and installation requirements.

The remaining content includes general requirements, traps and interceptors, special piping systems, storm drainage, and health care plumbing. These smaller categories still contribute important questions and should be part of a complete study plan.

Open Book Test

The Florida Commercial Plumbing Inspector ICC 2P examination is an open-book test.

The approved references for the 2021 examination cycle include the International Plumbing Code, 2021, International Fuel Gas Code, 2021, and ICC A117.1-2017 Standard for Accessible and Usable Buildings and Facilities.

Open-book testing requires more than having the correct books available. With 60 questions and 2 hours and 30 minutes, candidates have an average of about two and a half minutes per question. Efficient code navigation can make a significant difference in time management.

A sanitary drainage question should immediately point toward the drainage chapters of the IPC. A question involving gas-fired water-heating equipment may require the IFGC. A question concerning accessible plumbing fixtures, clear floor space, or mounting dimensions may require ICC A117.1-2017.

Candidates should practice using indexes, definitions, tables, exceptions, figures, and cross-references in all three references. Many inspection questions involve exact dimensions, allowable materials, sizing, clearances, or specific installation conditions.

During preparation, identify the subject before opening a book. This approach develops both technical understanding and faster reference selection.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the Florida Commercial Plumbing Inspector certification category. Florida regulates building code inspectors according to specific disciplines and certification categories.
  2. Meet the applicable qualification requirements. Applicants must qualify through an accepted pathway involving experience, education, existing certification, or Board-approved training.
  3. Submit the appropriate Florida application. Building code inspector applications are processed through the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation.
  4. Receive authorization for required examinations. Florida applicants must satisfy the state's application requirements before completing the applicable certification examinations.
  5. Prepare for the ICC Commercial Plumbing Inspector technical examination. Study the 2021 IPC, 2021 IFGC, ICC A117.1-2017, and the official ICC 2P examination content areas.
  6. Take the ICC 2P technical examination. The exam contains 60 multiple-choice questions and allows 2 hours and 30 minutes.
  7. Complete the Florida Principles and Practice examination. Florida applicants seeking certification by examination must also satisfy this separate state examination requirement.
  8. Complete remaining certification requirements. Florida reviews examination results together with the applicant's required qualifications and documentation before issuing certification.

State Requirements

Commercial plumbing inspectors in Florida are regulated through the Building Code Administrators and Inspectors Board under the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation.

Florida requires inspectors to hold certification appropriate to the category in which they perform inspections. Commercial Plumbing Inspector is a technical certification category focused on commercial plumbing systems and related code requirements.

Applicants seeking certification by examination must qualify through an accepted Florida pathway involving relevant construction or inspection experience, post-secondary education combined with experience, existing certifications, or Board-approved training.

Florida applicants seeking licensure by examination must pass an applicable International Code Council technical examination as well as the separate Florida Principles and Practice examination.

The ICC 2P examination addressed by this product is the technical examination component for commercial plumbing inspection. It does not replace preparation for the Florida Principles and Practice examination.

ICC National Certification examinations for Florida candidates are offered through the PRONTO remote-proctored testing platform. The Florida Principles and Practice examination is administered separately through Florida's approved examination process.

Passing the ICC 2P examination alone does not automatically issue a Florida Commercial Plumbing Inspector certification. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable state application, qualification, examination, and licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Florida Commercial Plumbing Inspector study plan should begin with Water Supply and Distribution, the largest category at 18% of the examination.

Review potable water systems, water-service piping, distribution piping, pipe sizing, approved materials, pressure, valves, supports, protection, connections, and testing requirements.

Backflow prevention and cross-connection control are important commercial inspection subjects. Candidates should understand how potable water systems are protected from contamination and how different plumbing arrangements affect the type of protection required.

Commercial water systems may serve a wide variety of fixtures, equipment, and occupancies. Candidates should be comfortable reading sizing tables and evaluating installation conditions rather than relying only on memorized pipe sizes.

Fixtures represent 15% of the examination. Review minimum fixture requirements, fixture installation, supports, connections, clearances, faucets, flushing devices, drinking fountains, sinks, lavatories, toilets, urinals, and other commercial fixtures.

Accessibility should be included in fixture preparation. The ICC A117.1-2017 reference addresses accessible and usable facilities and can be important for questions involving plumbing fixture clearances, heights, locations, approach spaces, and accessible toilet and bathing rooms.

Vents also represent 15%. Candidates should understand individual vents, common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, vent stacks, stack vents, vent terminals, sizing, connections, and permitted venting arrangements.

Vent questions often require candidates to visualize how the entire drainage and vent system operates. Practice identifying fixture drains, traps, vent connections, horizontal branches, stacks, and termination points.

Sanitary Drainage represents 13%. Review drainage fixture units, pipe sizing, slopes, cleanouts, fittings, building drains, building sewers, stacks, branches, materials, and testing.

Drainage questions frequently involve tables. Candidates should practice selecting the correct table and carefully reading the conditions, footnotes, and limitations that apply.

Water Heaters represent 12%. Study installation locations, temperature and pressure relief protection, discharge piping, supports, pans, connections, access, safety requirements, and fuel-gas considerations where applicable.

Gas-fired water heaters can involve both plumbing and fuel-gas requirements. Candidates should recognize when a question requires the IPC and when the IFGC provides the controlling requirement.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

Traps, Interceptors, Separators, Special Piping, and Storage Systems represent 9%. Review trap requirements, trap seals, grease interceptors, oil separators, special waste systems, indirect waste, and other specialized piping arrangements.

Commercial buildings often include systems not found in ordinary residential construction. Restaurants, health care facilities, laboratories, industrial spaces, and other occupancies may require special plumbing components and additional inspection knowledge.

General Requirements represent 8%. Candidates should review piping protection, support, sleeving, penetrations, approved materials, installation, inspections, testing, and code-administration requirements.

Piping must be protected from physical damage, corrosion, freezing, stress, and strain. Inspectors must also evaluate how piping passes through structural members, rated assemblies, walls, floors, and exterior openings.

Storm Drainage represents 5%. Review roof drainage, conductors, leaders, storm drains, sizing, secondary drainage where applicable, and approved disposal methods.

Storm-drainage questions may require candidates to apply rainfall rates, roof areas, pipe-sizing tables, or system configuration requirements. Practice reading the applicable code tables carefully.

Health Care Plumbing represents 5%. Candidates should become familiar with plumbing systems and special requirements associated with health care environments.

Health care plumbing can involve specialized fixtures, equipment, water systems, drainage, and medical-related piping. Candidates should recognize when ordinary commercial plumbing requirements are supplemented by special provisions.

The 2021 International Plumbing Code should be used actively during preparation. Rather than reading the code continuously, work through practice questions and locate the sections that control each answer.

The 2021 International Fuel Gas Code should receive focused navigation practice for questions involving gas piping, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, and fuel-fired equipment.

The ICC A117.1-2017 standard should be studied specifically for accessibility. Plumbing inspectors should be able to recognize requirements affecting accessible water closets, lavatories, urinals, bathing facilities, drinking fountains, and related spaces.

Dimensions matter in accessibility questions. Candidates should practice locating exact requirements for clear floor space, mounting height, reach, approach, grab-bar location, and fixture placement.

One useful study strategy is to classify each practice question by reference before looking up the answer. Determine whether the question belongs primarily in the IPC, IFGC, or accessibility standard.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

When a practice question is missed, determine why. The problem may be weak code navigation, misunderstanding the plumbing system, using the wrong table, missing an exception, selecting the wrong reference, or overlooking a condition in the question.

Organize missed questions by content category. If water distribution creates repeated difficulty, focus additional review there. If venting, accessibility, sanitary drainage, or water heaters produce more errors, build dedicated study sessions around those areas.

As exam day approaches, move into mixed-topic practice. A commercial plumbing inspection exam may shift rapidly between fixtures, water supply, drainage, vents, accessibility, water heaters, storm drainage, and special piping systems.

With 60 questions in 2 hours and 30 minutes, candidates have an average of approximately two and a half minutes per question. Efficient reference navigation helps preserve extra time for calculations, tables, and more complicated inspection scenarios.

The included 3 months access gives candidates time to move through multiple preparation stages, including initial code familiarization, topic-focused study, weak-area review, mixed-question practice, and timed preparation.
