Exam Details

The ICC Residential Plumbing Inspector exam is identified as P1 (Florida ICC 1P). According to ICC’s published P1 exam outline, the exam is structured as 60 multiple-choice questions with an open-book format and a 2-hour time limit. The outline content areas emphasize the practical work of a residential plumbing inspector—verifying compliance for fixtures, water heaters, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, and venting.

The most important skill to build for this exam is not memorizing page after page. It’s developing the ability to locate the correct requirement quickly and confirm the details accurately. Many questions are written so that the correct answer depends on a note, an exception, a table value, or a defined term. When your navigation system is strong, you spend less time searching and more time answering with confidence.

Open Book Test

The ICC P1 Residential Plumbing Inspector exam is an open book exam. In an open-book setting, your advantage comes from organization and repetition—knowing where topics live, recognizing common code “paths,” and practicing lookups until they feel automatic.

A smart open-book study strategy focuses on:

  • Code navigation speed: Move quickly to the correct chapter, section, and table without scanning aimlessly.
  • Accuracy checkpoints: Confirm table notes, footnotes, exceptions, and special conditions before choosing an answer.
  • Scenario discipline: Identify what the question is truly testing so you don’t waste time in the wrong area.
  • Timed practice: Build pacing so you can finish confidently within the time limit and still review flagged items.

This package is built to support those habits—so your study time turns into measurable exam-ready skill.

Test Information and Study Materials

The most productive way to prepare for a residential plumbing inspector exam is to study like an inspector. That means practicing how you’ll verify compliance in real life: locate the requirement, confirm the details, and apply it to the scenario consistently. With the exam’s open-book format and pacing, your study strategy should be built around a repeatable workflow—not passive reading.

Many candidates improve faster when they organize study into system-based buckets and drill them with timed lookups:

  • Sanitary drainage: Practice locating requirements tied to system components and installation concepts that show up frequently in inspection scenarios.
  • Venting: Build a repeatable routine for finding vent requirements, confirming sizing/method rules, and checking exceptions.
  • Water supply and distribution: Strengthen navigation for materials, joints and connections, and protection of potable water.
  • Fixtures and installation expectations: Practice questions that require you to confirm placement, installation, and compliance conditions.
  • Water heaters and related safety requirements: Train quick lookups for the installation conditions that can change the correct answer.
  • Definitions and trigger terms: Learn when to check defined terms early so you avoid wrong assumptions and wasted time.

Timed sets matter. Even short, consistent timed sessions build pacing and reduce second-guessing. The goal is to arrive on exam day with a process you trust: identify, locate, confirm, answer.
