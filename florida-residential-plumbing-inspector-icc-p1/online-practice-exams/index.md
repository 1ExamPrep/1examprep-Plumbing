## Open Book Test

The ICC Residential Plumbing Inspector examination is an **open-book test**.

Open-book testing still requires efficient preparation. With 60 questions and a 2-hour limit, candidates have an average of about **2 minutes per question**. Searching randomly through the IRC for every answer can consume valuable time.

A better strategy is to identify the plumbing subject before opening the code. A drainage question should immediately point toward sanitary drainage provisions. A venting question should direct attention to the vent chapters, while a fixture or water-heater question should suggest a different area of the IRC.

Practice should also include using the code's index, tables, definitions, and cross-references. Many inspection questions require candidates to find a specific dimensional requirement, sizing rule, material limitation, or exception.

During preparation, attempt each practice question before looking up the answer. Then use the IRC to confirm the applicable requirement. This strengthens both plumbing knowledge and code-navigation ability.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong ICC Residential Plumbing Inspector study plan should begin with the three largest content areas: **Sanitary Drainage, Water Supply and Distribution, and Vents**.

**Sanitary Drainage** represents 23% of the examination. Candidates should understand how wastewater moves through residential piping systems and how the IRC regulates sizing, slope, materials, cleanouts, connections, and installation.

Review fixture drains, branch drains, drainage stacks, building drains, building sewers, cleanout locations, drainage fittings, and approved piping materials.

Drainage inspection questions often require candidates to determine whether a piping arrangement, size, slope, or fitting is acceptable. Practice identifying the specific condition in the question before selecting a code section.

**Water Supply and Distribution** represents 22%. Candidates should review potable water piping, distribution systems, pressure, materials, valves, connections, fixture supply, protection against contamination, and sizing concepts.

Residential plumbing inspectors should understand how water enters a dwelling and is distributed to fixtures and equipment. Questions can involve pipe sizing, pressure, protection, materials, accessibility, and installation conditions.

**Backflow protection and cross-connection control** are important water-supply concepts. Candidates should understand how potable water systems are protected from contamination and which installations require protective devices or methods.

**Vents** also represent 22%. Vent systems protect trap seals and support proper drainage-system performance. Candidates should understand vent sizing, vent connections, vent terminations, wet venting, individual vents, common vents, and other permitted arrangements.

Venting questions can be challenging because the answer may depend on both the fixture arrangement and pipe size. Practice visualizing the complete system instead of focusing only on a single pipe.

**General Requirements** account for 14%. This category includes broader plumbing inspection responsibilities such as permits, testing, approved materials, piping protection, structural protection, and installation requirements.

Piping must be protected from physical damage, corrosion, freezing, stress, and strain. Candidates should understand how plumbing installations interact with building framing and how the code protects both the piping and the structure.

**Water Heaters** represent 11%. Study water-heater installation, location, temperature and pressure relief protection, discharge piping, connections, clearances, pans, supports, and other applicable residential requirements.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

Water-heater questions may require close attention to the installation condition. A requirement can differ depending on location, fuel type, surrounding construction, or equipment configuration.

**Fixtures** account for 8%. Review fixture materials, required fixtures, clearances, installation, supports, connections, faucets, traps, and fixture-specific requirements.

Inspection-focused preparation should include recognizing whether fixtures are properly installed, accessible, supported, connected, and supplied with appropriate drainage and water connections.

The **2021 International Residential Code** should be used actively throughout preparation. Practice locating plumbing requirements by topic rather than trying to memorize the entire code.

One effective method is to read a practice question and identify the main keyword. Terms such as cleanout, trap, vent, fixture, water heater, supply piping, or drainage stack can help point toward the correct chapter or section.

Tables deserve special attention. Plumbing codes frequently use tables for pipe sizing, fixture-unit relationships, developed lengths, spacing, and other requirements. Practice reading both table headings and footnotes carefully.

Definitions can also affect the correct answer. Plumbing terminology is precise, and two similar words may refer to different parts of a system. When a question seems ambiguous, checking the code definition can help clarify the intended meaning.

When a practice question is missed, determine why. The issue may be weak code navigation, misunderstanding of system operation, overlooking a table note, confusing drainage with vent requirements, or missing an exception.

Organize missed questions by category. If sanitary drainage remains difficult, focus additional study there. If venting or water distribution produces repeated errors, dedicate separate review sessions to those topics.

As preparation progresses, combine categories into mixed-question sets. A realistic exam may switch from a water-heater question to drainage, then fixtures, vents, water supply, or general requirements.

Because the exam contains 60 questions in 2 hours, time management matters. Candidates should answer familiar questions efficiently and preserve extra time for complicated code lookups or table interpretation.
