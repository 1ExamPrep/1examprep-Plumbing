Exam Details

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors issues residential, commercial, and dual contractor licenses. The C-78 Solar Plumbing, Liquid Systems Only classification is the commercial specialty license for qualifying solar water-heating work using liquid as the heat-transfer medium.

The qualifying party is the individual whose experience, trade knowledge, and examination qualifications support the contractor license. This person must satisfy the applicable Arizona requirements and remain associated with the licensed business in the qualifying capacity recognized by the state.

C-78 preparation may include solar water heating, solar pool heating, thermosyphon systems, direct systems, indirect systems, collectors, storage tanks, expansion tanks, heat exchangers, pumps, valves, sensors, controls, piping, insulation, pressure relief, freeze protection, roof mounting, testing, maintenance, mathematics, and OSHA safety.

Direct open-loop systems circulate potable water through the solar collector. Candidates should understand the conditions that affect the suitability of these systems, including climate, water quality, freeze potential, stagnation, piping layout, controls, and protection of system components.

Indirect closed-loop systems use a separate heat-transfer fluid that circulates between the collectors and a heat exchanger. Preparation may include heat-transfer fluids, heat exchangers, expansion control, pumps, air elimination, pressure, fill procedures, flushing, freeze protection, and separation from the potable-water system.

Thermosyphon systems rely on natural circulation created by temperature and density differences. Candidates should understand the relationship between the collector and storage tank, pipe routing, flow resistance, system mounting, supports, and proper component placement.

Collector preparation may include flat-plate collectors, evacuated-tube collectors, absorber surfaces, glazing, headers, risers, insulation, enclosures, mounting hardware, orientation, tilt, spacing, shading, roof attachment, weather protection, and access for service.

Storage preparation may include solar storage tanks, auxiliary tanks, tank placement, insulation, connections, temperature control, pressure relief, thermal expansion, heat exchangers, stratification, and coordination with backup water-heating equipment.

Pump and control preparation may involve circulators, differential-temperature controllers, sensors, relays, switches, check valves, isolation valves, balancing valves, flow meters, and control sequences. Candidates should understand how controls start, stop, and protect a solar-thermal system.

Exam Details (continued)

Freeze-protection study may include drainback systems, antifreeze systems, recirculation methods, pipe drainage, insulation, control strategies, heat tracing, fluid concentration, and climate-related installation decisions.

Overheat and stagnation preparation may include pressure relief, expansion tanks, heat rejection, collector covers, control logic, fluid selection, system sizing, storage capacity, and procedures used when solar energy exceeds the available load.

Piping preparation may include approved materials, sizing, supports, insulation, expansion, valves, unions, drains, vents, penetrations, routing, slope, compatibility, corrosion protection, pressure testing, flushing, and identification.

Solar pool-heating preparation may involve collector arrays, pumps, filters, valves, bypass arrangements, check valves, sensors, controllers, roof mounting, piping layout, drainage, flow balancing, system startup, and seasonal shutdown.

Roof-mounted system preparation may include ladders, fall protection, attachment points, structural coordination, collector supports, roof penetrations, flashing, wind exposure, lifting, material handling, and safe access for installation and maintenance.

Trade mathematics may involve fractions, decimals, percentages, area, volume, flow, pressure, temperature difference, capacity, pipe length, offsets, heat transfer, collector area, storage volume, and unit conversion.

Passing the required examination does not automatically issue the C-78 contractor license. The Arizona Registrar of Contractors must review and approve the complete application before the business may advertise, bid, contract for, or perform work requiring the classification.

State Requirements

The C-78 classification is an Arizona commercial specialty contractor license for qualifying solar-plumbing systems that use liquid as the heat-transfer medium. It does not provide residential contracting authority.

The authorized scope includes qualifying installation, alteration, and repair of solar water-heating systems operating within the temperature limitation established for the classification. Covered systems may include thermosyphon, direct open-loop, and indirect closed-loop arrangements.

The classification may include collectors, storage tanks, expansion tanks, heat exchangers, piping, valves, pumps, sensors, and low-voltage controls associated with the solar-plumbing system. Air-based solar heating is excluded because the classification is limited to liquid systems.

The proposed qualifying party must demonstrate the experience, knowledge, and skills needed to supervise or perform the covered work. Experience documentation may need to describe collectors, storage, heat exchangers, pumps, piping, valves, controls, freeze protection, commercial installations, service work, and field supervision.

The legal business entity applying for the license must be properly established and identified. The company name, ownership information, qualifying-party records, EIN documentation, contractor bond, solar warranty, and application should remain consistent.

Arizona contractor applicants must complete applicable background requirements and provide the identity, experience, examination, business, and disclosure information required for state review.

A commercial contractor license bond is required. The applicable bond amount depends on the anticipated gross volume of commercial work and the license category.

Arizona license applications involving a solar classification require solar warranty documentation. The warranty should address the solar installation in the manner required by Arizona law and the licensing application process.

Because C-78 is a commercial-only classification, the residential financial-protection requirements associated with Arizona residential and dual licenses do not apply.

After licensing, the contractor must remain within the authorized C-78 scope, maintain the required bond, renew the license, follow applicable solar-warranty responsibilities, and keep business and qualifying-party information current.
