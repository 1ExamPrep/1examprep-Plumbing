## Exam Details

The Springfield, Missouri Journeyman Plumber exam is administered through the International Code Council (ICC) contractor/trades testing program, with testing delivered at Pearson VUE test centers. The W25 Missouri (Springfield) Journeyman Plumber exam is a **70-question**, multiple-choice exam with a **3-hour time limit**. The listed reference for this exam is the **2018 International Plumbing Code**.

To stay on pace, treat the exam like a job-site call: read carefully, identify what the question is really asking, and go straight to the code section that controls the answer. Many candidates lose time by searching broadly. Strong candidates use the IPC structure—definitions, scoping language, tables, and exceptions—to confirm the correct choice quickly.

**Major content areas** for the W25 Journeyman Plumber exam are weighted toward practical code use. Expect your study time to pay off most when you focus on:

- **Administration / General Regulations (40%)** – installation requirements, definitions/terms, testing, and materials
- **Plumbing Fixtures (17%)** – including water heaters, water closets and urinals, and specialty fixtures
- **Water Supply and Distribution (14%)** – distribution/piping, pressure, and potable water protection
- **Sanitary Drainage (13%)** – building drainage, sumps/ejectors, indirect and special waste systems
- **Plumbing Vent Requirements (7%)** – vent connections/grades and vent terminals
- **Traps, Interceptors, and Separators (7%)** – installation and application requirements
- **Storm Drainage (2%)**

## Test Information and Study Materials

The exam is multiple-choice and designed to measure how well you apply code concepts—not just whether you can memorize a rule. The most effective study strategy is to combine **code reading** with **targeted practice**:

- **Start with the big-weight sections**: Administration/General Regulations and Plumbing Fixtures. Build comfort with common terms, installation requirements, and where the IPC places key requirements.
- **Create a navigation routine**: When you miss a question in practice, don’t just note the right answer—write down the exact IPC section and what clue in the question should have sent you there.
- **Practice “two-step confirmation”**: (1) locate the likely section quickly, then (2) confirm by reading the controlling sentence, exception, or table note.
- **Train your timing**: The exam is 70 questions in 3 hours. Practice answering with a steady pace, leaving time to review flagged questions at the end.

When you study the IPC, focus on how code writers communicate requirements. Words like “shall,” “shall not,” “exception,” “where,” “listed,” and “approved” matter. Getting comfortable with that language helps you avoid common multiple-choice traps that feel right in the field but don’t match the written code requirement.
