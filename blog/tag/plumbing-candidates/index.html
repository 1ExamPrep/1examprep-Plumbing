Where should you start if your plumbing exam is coming up?

Three things trip up most plumbing license candidates, and none of them is a lack of trade knowledge. The first is the code. Your exam is written on one code, the International Plumbing Code or the Uniform Plumbing Code, in one edition, and studying the wrong one costs points on every sizing question. Check the edition in your candidate bulletin before you buy a single book.

The second is the clock. On open-book exams the people who run out of time are the ones who look up answers they should already know roughly where to find, so learn the chapter layout of the code until you can place any topic in seconds. The third is plumbing math: fixture units, drainage and vent sizing, and water supply sizing come up on almost every exam. The posts here link to the matching state pages, where every package ships from our fulfillment center, 1 Exam Prep.

What does a six-week study plan for a plumbing license exam look like?

Six weeks at about an hour a day is enough for most candidates who already work in the trade. The plan below fits a journeyman or master plumber exam built on the 2024 IPC or UPC. Adjust the weeks for your own license; the order of the work stays the same.

  1. Week 1: learn the code, not the answers. Read the table of contents of your code book until you can name the chapter for any topic without looking. Time yourself finding ten random sections a night and write down how long each one took.
  2. Week 2: mark the book. Tab the chapters and tables you keep coming back to, within the rules of your bulletin. The complete books, tabs and course package arrives with the tabs already placed, which saves most of this week.
  3. Week 3: plumbing math. Work one type a night: drainage fixture units, building drain and stack sizing, vent sizing, water supply fixture units and pipe sizing, and water heater relief. Practise with a timer from the first night.
  4. Week 4: first full practice exam. Sit a complete timed exam in one go. Sort every miss into three piles: did not know the rule, could not find it in time, or misread the question. The pile sizes tell you where weeks 5 and 6 go.
  5. Week 5: fix the biggest pile. If you ran out of time, drill lookups. If you did not know the rule, reread those sections. If you misread, slow down on the words "except", "not" and "minimum".
  6. Week 6: two more timed exams and rest. Take a full practice exam early in the week and another three days before the test, then stop. The last two days are for sleep, not new material.

How do you adjust the plan for other plumbing exams?

The same six weeks work for most plumbing exams with two changes. Where a business and law exam is part of the license, as for contractors in Arizona, California and Nevada, give it its own week before the trade exam. Where the exam is closed book, weeks 1 and 2 become memorising the sections the exam is built on rather than learning to find them.

Keep one error log for all six weeks, on paper or in a phone note, whichever you will actually open. Write the question, the section that answers it and how long it took you, and reread the log every Sunday. Most lost points come from fewer than ten sections.

Questions we get asked most

How long should I study for a plumbing license exam?

Most candidates plan six to eight weeks at about an hour a day: learning the code layout, practicing sizing and fixture unit questions, and taking timed practice exams.

Is the plumbing exam open book?

Some are. Florida contractor and ICC inspector exams allow the listed references; some journeyman exams are closed book. Your candidate bulletin decides.

IPC or UPC?

Your exam uses the code your state or city adopted. Every package here names its code.
