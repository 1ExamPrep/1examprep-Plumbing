- **Passing Standard:** 73%

The current LLP examination outline includes the following subject areas:

**Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems** is the largest individual subject area, accounting for 10 of the 40 exam questions. **Isometrics** is the second-largest category with seven questions. Candidates should practice reading and interpreting plumbing drawings, identifying pipe directions, following vertical and horizontal piping, understanding fixture relationships, and translating a plumbing arrangement into an isometric representation.

**Code Requirements**, **Water Supply and Distribution**, and **Fuel Gas Systems** each account for four questions. These areas reward candidates who understand practical plumbing work and can locate applicable requirements quickly.

**General Knowledge** covers three questions, while Plumbing Math, Water Heaters, Fixtures and Fittings, and OSHA Safety contain two questions each. Smaller categories still matter on a 40-question examination, so a complete study plan should address the entire outline.

## Licensing Steps

The Tennessee Limited Licensed Plumber credential is issued to individuals through the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors. The process is different from obtaining Tennessee's full contractor license classifications.

2. **Check local requirements.** Before performing plumbing work, determine whether the municipality or county accepts the state LLP credential or requires separate local licensing, testing, permits, or inspections.

## State Requirements

Tennessee defines the Limited Licensed Plumber credential around plumbing work costing less than $25,000 per project. The state LLP program applies where a plumber is required to hold the credential and where the applicable jurisdiction uses the state licensing structure.

The Tennessee Board advises applicants to check with local government before beginning work. Some jurisdictions have local plumbing licensing requirements, while the LLP credential is particularly relevant in municipalities utilizing the Tennessee Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections.

Plumbing work within the LLP framework includes qualifying work involving piping, fixtures, and appliances inside residential or commercial buildings for the supply of gas, water, or other liquids and for disposal of wastewater or sewage.

The current state information distinguishes the LLP credential from plumbing work performed outside residential or commercial buildings, including utility connections and irrigation systems.

Tennessee also identifies general maintenance work costing less than $500 as exempt from the LLP license requirement. Candidates should understand that licensing requirements and project limits are separate from the technical examination itself.

The LLP credential is issued to an individual rather than being the same type of entity-based contractor license used for Tennessee's larger contractor classifications. The state LLP process centers on the trade examination, individual application, identification requirements, and applicable licensing fee.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Tennessee LLP study plan should reflect the examination weighting. Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems and Isometrics together account for a major portion of the 40-question test, making these areas especially important.

**Prioritize DWV systems.** Review drainage piping, venting, traps, fixture connections, sizing, cleanouts, installation requirements, and code navigation. Practice locating answers in the International Plumbing Code rather than relying only on memory.

**Practice plumbing isometrics.** Learn to recognize vertical and horizontal pipe runs, fixture relationships, branch connections, drainage paths, and vent arrangements. Isometric questions can become more manageable with repeated drawing and interpretation practice.

**Strengthen water supply and distribution knowledge.** Review water piping, sizing concepts, pressure, distribution methods, valves, and applicable IPC requirements.

**Review fuel gas systems.** Study gas piping concepts, appliance relationships, venting, combustion, and installation requirements. Use the International Fuel Gas Code as a study resource to strengthen this area.

**Practice plumbing math.** Use Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters to work calculations repeatedly. Focus on understanding how to set up a problem and identify the correct units before calculating an answer.

**Study fixtures and water heaters.** Although these categories contain fewer questions, candidates should understand fixture installation principles, fittings, water heater requirements, and related code provisions.

**Prepare OSHA safety for recall.** Review common construction hazards, protective equipment, ladders, excavation considerations, tools, and other jobsite safety topics.

**Use timed practice.** With 40 questions and two hours available, candidates should practice answering questions efficiently while knowing when to use a reference lookup.

**Track weak subjects.** Missed questions should guide the next study session. If errors are concentrated in DWV, isometrics, math, fuel gas, or code navigation, spend additional time in that category before repeating a full practice set.
