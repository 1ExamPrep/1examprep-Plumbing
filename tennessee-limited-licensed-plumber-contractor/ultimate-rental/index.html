Exam Details

The Tennessee Limited Licensed Plumber examination is administered by Prov. The current examination contains 40 questions, and candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the test.

The current LLP examination outline contains the following subject areas:

  • General Knowledge: 3 questions
  • Plumbing Math: 2 questions
  • Code Requirements: 4 questions
  • Water Supply and Distribution: 4 questions
  • Water Heaters: 2 questions
  • Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems: 10 questions
  • Fixtures & Fittings: 2 questions
  • Isometrics: 7 questions
  • Fuel Gas Systems: 4 questions
  • OSHA Safety: 2 questions

Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems is the largest individual portion of the examination, accounting for 10 questions. Isometrics follows with 7 questions. Code Requirements, Water Supply and Distribution, and Fuel Gas Systems each contain 4 questions. These areas should receive significant attention during exam preparation.

The remaining questions cover General Knowledge, Plumbing Math, Water Heaters, Fixtures & Fittings, and OSHA Safety. A complete study plan should address every category while giving additional practice time to the larger drainage, venting, and isometric portions of the exam.

State Requirements

The Tennessee Limited Licensed Plumber license applies to plumbing projects with a total cost of less than $25,000. Tennessee's current rules also require plumbers to comply with applicable state and local municipality requirements, including permit and inspection requirements.

The LLP credential is different from a Tennessee contractor license. Current Tennessee renewal materials specifically state that an LLP is not authorized to use the designation "contractor" unless the individual also holds a valid contractor license. For plumbing projects of $25,000 or more, Tennessee requires the appropriate contractor license with a qualifying mechanical or plumbing classification.

The LLP scope includes qualifying plumbing work involving piping, fixtures, and appliances within residential or commercial buildings for supplying gas, water, or other liquids and disposing of wastewater or sewage. The LLP definition does not extend to plumbing work performed outside residential or commercial buildings, including utility connections or irrigation systems.

Candidates should also consider local requirements before planning work. The state LLP credential operates alongside local licensing, permitting, and inspection requirements, and the rules can differ depending on the municipality where the plumbing project is located.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Tennessee LLP study plan should begin with Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems because this subject represents 10 of the 40 questions. Candidates should spend time reviewing sanitary drainage, drainage fittings, vent systems, traps, cleanouts, sizing concepts, piping materials, and related plumbing-code requirements.

Isometrics accounts for 7 questions and deserves dedicated practice. Candidates should become comfortable interpreting plumbing diagrams, following piping paths, recognizing drainage and vent relationships, and applying plumbing principles to illustrated system layouts. This type of preparation is most effective when candidates actively work through diagrams rather than relying only on reading.

Code Requirements and Water Supply and Distribution each contain 4 questions. The International Plumbing Code, 2021 should be used frequently while studying these subjects. Candidates should learn how the code is organized and practice working with definitions, tables, sections, exceptions, and index entries.

Fuel Gas Systems also contains 4 questions. The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021 provides additional study material involving gas piping, materials, sizing, appliances, combustion air, venting, and chimneys. Candidates can use this reference to strengthen their understanding of fuel gas concepts before testing.

Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition supports the Plumbing Math category. Candidates should practice solving problems involving dimensions, measurements, formulas, and trade calculations instead of only reading worked examples. Repetition helps develop greater familiarity with the calculations used in plumbing work.

OSHA Safety contributes 2 questions. The Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 provides broader construction-safety material for reviewing hazards and safe work practices associated with plumbing jobsites.

General Knowledge, Water Heaters, and Fixtures & Fittings complete the examination outline. These smaller categories should remain part of the preparation plan so candidates do not leave gaps in otherwise strong plumbing knowledge.
