  • Plumbing Math: 2 questions

  • Water Supply and Distribution: 4 questions

Candidates should prepare across the entire outline rather than concentrating only on the largest categories. State Requirements

The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors regulates the Limited Licensed Plumber license. The LLP license is specifically designed for plumbing work with a total cost of less than $25,000 per project when the work is performed in municipalities using the Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections.

Tennessee defines plumbing work for this license to include the construction, alteration, repair, improvement, movement, demolition, installation, or furnishing of labor for piping, fixtures, and appliances within residential or commercial buildings for the supply of gas, water, liquids, or the disposal of wastewater or sewage. The LLP provisions do not extend the license to plumbing work outside residential or commercial buildings such as utility connections or irrigation systems.

General maintenance work costing less than $500 is exempt from the LLP licensing requirement under Tennessee's current LLP guidance. Local licensing rules remain important because municipalities may have requirements that differ from the state LLP program.

Unlike the Tennessee CMC-A Plumbing Contractor classification, the LLP license is a limited plumbing license for projects below the statutory LLP project threshold. Candidates who intend to perform larger plumbing projects should understand the difference between the LLP license and Tennessee's contractor-level plumbing classifications before selecting their licensing path.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong LLP study plan should begin with Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems because this category accounts for 10 questions, or one-quarter of the current examination. Candidates should study sanitary drainage, venting concepts, traps, drainage fittings, piping requirements, and the organization of the plumbing code sections that address these systems.

Isometrics accounts for 7 questions, making drawing interpretation and plumbing-system visualization another major area. Candidates should practice following water, drain, waste, and vent piping through plumbing diagrams and become comfortable identifying connections, sizing relationships, and system layouts.

Code Requirements and Water Supply and Distribution each contain 4 questions. The International Plumbing Code, 2021 should be used regularly when preparing for these subjects. Candidates should practice moving through code chapters and using tables rather than relying only on memorization.

Fuel Gas Systems also contains 4 questions. The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021 can support additional study of fuel gas piping, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, sizing, and related concepts. Even when a study reference is not on the current primary LLP exam-room list, it can still be valuable for building subject knowledge before testing.

Plumbing Math contains 2 questions, but calculation skills can also support other exam topics. Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition provides a structured resource for reviewing measurements, formulas, dimensions, and practical plumbing calculations. Candidates should work problems themselves rather than simply reading solutions.

OSHA Safety accounts for 2 questions. The Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 provides additional safety study material covering construction hazards and work practices. Candidates should understand the safety principles that apply to plumbing work and construction environments.

Water Heaters and Fixtures & Fittings each contribute 2 questions, while General Knowledge contributes 3. These sections should remain part of the study plan even though they contain fewer questions. A balanced preparation routine can prevent avoidable gaps in knowledge.
