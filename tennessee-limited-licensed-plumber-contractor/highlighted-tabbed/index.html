Licensing Steps

Determine whether the LLP license applies to your work. Tennessee's Limited Licensed Plumber license applies to qualifying plumbing work with a total cost of less than $25,000 per project in municipalities that use the Tennessee Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections.

Prepare for the LLP trade examination. The Tennessee LLP license is issued to individuals who satisfy the applicable requirements and pass the Limited Licensed Plumber trade examination. Preparation should include both plumbing knowledge and familiarity with the reference materials used during testing.

Register for the Prov examination. Candidates schedule the Tennessee LLP trade examination through Prov and should make sure they select the correct license examination during registration.

Complete the examination requirement. Candidates take the open-book LLP examination and complete the required testing process. Passing the examination satisfies the trade exam portion of the LLP licensing path.

Complete the Tennessee LLP application. Applicants must submit the Initial Limited Licensed Plumber application and provide the information required by the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors.

Check local requirements. Tennessee advises plumbers to check with the local government where they plan to work. Some jurisdictions accept the state LLP license, while others may have separate licensing, permit, or inspection requirements.

State Requirements

The Tennessee Limited Licensed Plumber license is regulated by the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors. It is an individual license for qualifying plumbing work performed under the state's LLP framework.

The LLP license applies to plumbing work costing less than $25,000 per project in municipalities using the Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections. Tennessee's LLP description includes covered plumbing work involving piping, fixtures, and appliances inside residential or commercial buildings for the supply of gas, water, or other liquids and for the disposal of wastewater or sewage.

The LLP framework does not extend that definition to plumbing work outside residential or commercial buildings such as utility connections or irrigation systems. Tennessee also states that general maintenance work costing less than $500 is exempt from the LLP licensing requirement.

Local requirements remain important. A municipality may have its own plumbing licensing requirements, and contractors should determine which license, permits, inspections, and local approvals apply before performing work.

Candidates planning to perform plumbing work at or above the contractor licensing threshold should also understand the difference between the Limited Licensed Plumber credential and Tennessee's contractor-level plumbing classifications. The LLP is specifically structured for qualifying plumbing projects below the LLP monetary limit.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Tennessee LLP study plan should reflect the weighting of the current examination. Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems accounts for 10 questions, making it the most heavily represented subject. Candidates should become comfortable with drainage piping, fittings, traps, cleanouts, vents, materials, sizing concepts, and related code provisions.

Isometrics accounts for 7 questions and should receive dedicated practice. Plumbing isometric questions can require candidates to interpret piping arrangements, visualize system relationships, follow drainage and vent lines, and apply plumbing knowledge to illustrated layouts.

The International Plumbing Code, 2021 should be used regularly during preparation. Candidates should practice working with definitions, tables, exceptions, chapter organization, and index terminology. Learning where a subject is located can be just as useful as memorizing individual requirements during an open-book exam.

Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition supports preparation for Plumbing Math. Candidates should work through calculations rather than simply reading examples. Repeated practice helps strengthen familiarity with formulas, measurements, dimensions, and numerical problem solving.

Fuel Gas Systems accounts for 4 questions. The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021 gives candidates additional material for studying piping, sizing, appliances, combustion air, vents, chimneys, materials, and other gas-system concepts.

OSHA Safety contains 2 questions. Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 can help candidates review construction workplace safety and common jobsite hazards. Safety preparation should remain part of the overall study plan even though it represents a smaller percentage of the examination.

Water Supply and Distribution and Code Requirements each contain 4 questions. Candidates should use the International Plumbing Code regularly for these areas and become comfortable locating technical requirements through chapters, tables, definitions, and index entries.

Water Heaters and Fixtures & Fittings each contain 2 questions, while General Knowledge contains 3. These categories should be incorporated into study sessions so that preparation covers the complete LLP examination outline.
