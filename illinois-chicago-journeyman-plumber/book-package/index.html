Exam Details

The Chicago plumbing written examination is administered through Continental Testing Services as part of the City of Chicago plumbing licensing process.

The current written examination contains 100 questions and is timed for 3 hours. Candidates must earn a score of 70 or higher on the written test to pass and become eligible to take the Chicago Plumbing Practical Examination.

The current written examination content outline includes:

  • Plumbing Theory, Practices & Job Safety – 20 questions
  • Code Requirements for Plumbers & Contractors – 2 questions
  • Plumbing Systems – 49 questions
  • Drawings, Charts & Plans – 8 questions
  • Cutting & Assembling Plumbing System Components – 11 questions
  • Troubleshooting, Maintenance & Repairs – 10 questions

Plumbing Systems is the largest examination category with 49 questions. This section covers the plumbing provisions contained in Chapter 18-29 of the Chicago Building Code, including administration, definitions, general regulations, fixtures, water heaters, water supply, sanitary drainage, indirect and special waste, vents, traps, separators, interceptors, storm drainage, and swimming pools.

Plumbing Theory, Practices & Job Safety accounts for 20 questions. Candidates should be prepared for plumbing mathematics, public-health principles, planning, tools, equipment, basic electricity, workplace safety, and OSHA-related subjects.

The remaining questions address code requirements for plumbers and contractors, blueprint and plan reading, piping materials and assembly, and troubleshooting and repair.

Closed Book Test

The Chicago Journeyman Plumber written examination is a closed book test. Candidates may not bring their own calculators, books, notes, or other printed reference materials into the written examination.

The testing provider supplies any reference information that is required to answer particular examination questions. Candidates should therefore use the books in this package for advance preparation rather than expect to search them during the test.

Closed-book preparation should focus on understanding plumbing principles, recognizing Chicago code requirements, completing calculations, reading plans, identifying proper piping methods, and applying technical knowledge to realistic plumbing situations.

Practice should gradually move from reference-assisted study to answering questions without the books. When a question is missed, return to the appropriate reference, review the underlying concept, and then attempt similar questions from memory.

Licensing Steps

The Chicago Journeyman Plumber licensing process includes both written and practical examinations as well as documented apprentice experience.

  1. Complete qualifying apprenticeship experience. Current examination requirements call for four years of work as a Plumber's Apprentice.
  2. Maintain the required apprentice credential. Applicants must provide a copy of their applicable apprentice license as part of the examination documentation.
  3. Obtain employer verification. Candidates must provide an original signed letter from a current or former employer that is a licensed and bonded plumbing contractor.
  4. Document employment dates and experience. The employer letter must establish that the applicant completed the required four years of apprentice plumbing work and include the contractor's license information.
  5. Apply for the Chicago Journeyman Plumber examination. Complete the required City of Chicago and Continental Testing Services application process.
  6. Prepare for the written examination. Study Chicago plumbing-code provisions, theory, calculations, safety, plans, installation practices, system components, troubleshooting, and repair.
  7. Pass the written examination. Candidates must score 70 or higher to become eligible for the practical examination.
  8. Complete the Chicago Plumbing Practical Examination. The practical examination evaluates drawing and piping-assembly skills.
  9. Pass the required practical components. Candidates must achieve the required score on each practical examination component.
  10. Complete the licensing process. After meeting the written, practical, experience, and application requirements, complete the remaining City of Chicago requirements for Journeyman Plumber licensure.

State Requirements

The Chicago Journeyman Plumber examination is associated with professional plumbing licensure in the City of Chicago. Candidates must meet the licensing and examination requirements applicable to plumbing work performed within the city.

Current Continental Testing Services requirements state that applicants must document four years of work as a Plumber's Apprentice. The experience must be verified through an original signed employer letter from a licensed and bonded plumbing contractor.

The verification letter must establish the dates of employment and confirm completion of the required apprentice experience. It must also include the employer's plumbing contractor license number, and the applicant must provide a copy of the apprentice license.

Candidates currently licensed as an Apprentice Plumber by either the City of Chicago or the State of Illinois may apply through the applicable current examination process.

The licensing examination has two stages. Candidates first pass the written examination and then proceed to the Chicago Plumbing Practical Examination. Both are required parts of the professional licensing process.

The practical examination contains two major components. One requires candidates to complete plumbing specifications for a three-story residential building by adding required sewer, drain, waste, and vent piping, including an overhead sewer with an ejector system, in accordance with the current Chicago Plumbing Code.

The second practical component involves a copper pipe assembly project. Candidates must interpret a drawing, calculate required tubing dimensions, measure and cut tubing, assemble the specified sections, and solder the completed project.

A score of at least 75 is required on each part of the practical examination. Candidates who fail only one practical component are required to repeat the portion on which they scored below the passing standard.

Test Information and Study Materials

Plumbing Theory, Practices & Job Safety contains 20 questions. Candidates should review public-health principles, plumbing mathematics, planning, OSHA requirements, tools, equipment, and basic electrical concepts encountered in plumbing work.

Mathematics is especially important because plumbing problems may involve volume, pressure, grade, pipe dimensions, offsets, measurements, and unit conversions. The official sample examination includes calculation problems involving water-main capacity, drain-line pitch, hydrostatic pressure, and pipe offsets.

Code Requirements for Plumbers & Contractors contains 2 questions. Although it is a small portion of the examination, candidates should understand applicable Chicago requirements governing plumbers, contractors, and tunneling permits.

Plumbing Systems contains 49 questions and should receive the greatest amount of study time. Review Chicago requirements involving fixtures, faucets, water heaters, potable water distribution, sanitary drainage, indirect waste, vents, traps, separators, interceptors, storm drainage, swimming pools, and general plumbing regulations.

The official sample questions demonstrate the importance of knowing detailed Chicago requirements. Topics include service-pipe inspections, underground soldered piping, definitions of minor repairs, sump requirements, vent distances, grease-interceptor capacity, roof drains, shower drainage, and related code provisions.

Drawings, Charts & Plans contains 8 questions. Candidates should be comfortable reading blueprints, locating fixtures, interpreting rough-in information, understanding symbols, and completing building plans according to plumbing requirements.

The Blueprint Reading and Drafting for Plumbers reference can be particularly useful for this section. Practice identifying fixture locations, dimensions, piping routes, waste and vent arrangements, and information supplied by fixture manufacturers.

Cutting & Assembling Plumbing System Components accounts for 11 questions. Review water-flow and connection requirements, piping materials, sizing, drainage requirements, and joining methods for copper, steel, cast iron, plastic, and acid-resistant piping materials.

Hands-on understanding matters in this section. Candidates should know how common plumbing materials are measured, cut, joined, supported, and installed, as well as when specific materials and connection methods are appropriate.

Troubleshooting, Maintenance & Repairs contains 10 questions. Review fixture and appliance repair, water distribution problems, wastewater systems, drainage and vent issues, pumps, water heaters, and common service conditions.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

The official sample examination includes troubleshooting questions involving electric water heaters and submersible pumps. Candidates should understand how plumbing and mechanical components operate so they can identify likely causes of failures.

A strong preparation routine should alternate between technical reading, calculations, code questions, blueprint interpretation, and closed-book practice. Candidates should not spend all of their study time on one area simply because it feels more familiar.

As the examination date approaches, complete practice sessions without books or calculators to better match the actual testing environment. Review missed questions afterward using the references and identify whether each error resulted from code knowledge, mathematics, terminology, plan reading, or practical plumbing concepts.
