About this Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor package

Prepare for the North Carolina Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor examination with a focused reference-book package covering exterior plumbing systems, residential code requirements, code administration, licensing laws, business operations, and project management.

The Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor classification is designed for a defined scope of exterior plumbing work. It includes exterior building sewer piping, exterior water-service piping two inches or smaller, exterior backflow preventers installed on piping two inches or smaller, and water-filtration systems. Candidates should understand both the technical requirements for this work and the legal limits of the classification.

This package includes the five references identified for the North Carolina Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor examination: the North Carolina Administrative Code and Policies, 2018 Edition; North Carolina Plumbing Code, 2018 Edition; North Carolina Residential Code, 2018 Edition; NC Board’s Laws & Rules, 2026 Edition; and NASCLA Business, Law, and Project Management, 7th Edition.

Each book supports a different part of the licensing examination. The North Carolina Plumbing Code is the primary technical resource for exterior building sewer piping, exterior water services, approved materials, sizing, protection, testing, backflow prevention, connections, cleanouts, and installation requirements.

The North Carolina Residential Code supports questions involving plumbing systems serving qualifying residential buildings. Candidates may use it to review water-service installation, building sewer connections, piping materials, burial requirements, backflow protection, and related residential construction provisions.

The North Carolina Administrative Code and Policies addresses permits, inspections, approvals, construction documents, code enforcement, alternative materials, and the authority and responsibilities of code officials. These administrative requirements are important because exterior plumbing work must be properly permitted and inspected when required.

The NC Board’s Laws & Rules reference explains the statutes and administrative rules governing plumbing contractors in North Carolina. It supports preparation involving license classifications, examination requirements, professional conduct, supervision, permit responsibilities, advertising, enforcement, and the limits placed on restricted licenses.

NASCLA Business, Law, and Project Management supports the Business and Law portion of the examination. It covers business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, project administration, employment responsibilities, safety programs, insurance, recordkeeping, and risk management.

Exam Details

The North Carolina Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor licensing process includes a technical examination and a Business and Law examination. The Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor technical examination allows two hours. The Business and Law examination allows one and one-half hours.

The Business and Law examination may not be required when an applicant already holds a currently active contractor license issued by the same Board and passed the Board’s Business and Law examination after August 2003. The applicant must meet the Board’s conditions for the exemption.

The technical examination focuses on the work authorized by the restricted classification. Candidates should prepare for questions involving exterior building sewer piping, exterior water-service piping two inches or smaller, exterior backflow preventers on piping two inches or smaller, water-filtration systems, permits, inspections, safety, and applicable plumbing-code requirements.

Exterior building sewer preparation may include approved piping materials, minimum size, slope, cleanouts, changes in direction, connections, trench conditions, bedding, support, protection from damage, testing, and the transition between the building drain and the exterior sewer system.

Exterior water-service preparation may involve approved materials, sizing, pressure, burial depth, separation from sewer piping, protection against freezing and physical damage, valves, joints, connections, backflow protection, and prevention of contamination.

Backflow-prevention study should include the purpose of protecting potable water, common backflow conditions, approved devices, installation location, accessibility, drainage, testing considerations, protection from freezing, and restrictions associated with the two-inch piping limitation.

Water-filtration preparation may include equipment location, piping connections, bypass arrangements, shutoff valves, drainage, pressure considerations, service access, protection against contamination, and installation according to applicable code and manufacturer requirements.

Candidates should also understand trenching and excavation safety. Exterior plumbing work may expose workers to cave-ins, underground utilities, water accumulation, traffic hazards, falling materials, and restricted access. Safe excavation procedures remain an important part of professional plumbing work.

Administrative questions may address when permits are required, inspection responsibilities, correction of noncompliant work, approved materials, construction documents, code-official authority, and the process for obtaining approval before concealing or placing a system into service.

Open Book Test

The North Carolina Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring the references assigned to the examination category. Only books approved for that examination may be taken into the testing room.

The current reference list identifies the North Carolina Administrative Code and Policies, North Carolina Plumbing Code, North Carolina Residential Code, Board’s Laws & Rules, and the approved Business and Project Management reference for Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor candidates.

Hard-bound, spiral-bound, and ring-bound editions may be used when they remain in their original published condition. Approved loose-leaf codebooks must remain in the original binders provided by the publisher.

Commercially available, non-removable index tabs may be permanently attached to the pages. Highlighting is permitted. Written notes, formulas, calculations, underlining, homemade tabs, unauthorized divider pages, loose paper, taped inserts, copied material, and printed PDF editions are not allowed.

Open-book testing requires preparation because the examination period is limited. Candidates should not expect to read unfamiliar chapters from beginning to end during the test. A better method is to identify the subject, select the correct reference, locate the governing section, and confirm the answer efficiently.

A question involving exterior building sewer slope or cleanouts should direct the candidate to the Plumbing Code. A question involving a residential water-service connection may require the Residential Code. A permit or inspection question may require the Administrative Code and Policies.

Questions involving the permitted scope of a Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor, professional conduct, supervision, or Board authority should direct the candidate to the NC Board’s Laws & Rules. Business questions involving contracts, estimating, financial management, or project administration may require the NASCLA reference.

Candidates should practice using the table of contents, index, definitions, chapter headings, tables, figures, and cross-references in every book. Reading the complete provision is important because exceptions, limitations, and connected sections may affect the correct answer.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the restricted classification. Review the authorized work and make sure it matches the applicant’s experience and intended services.
  2. Obtain the required references. Applicants should have the current approved books and prepare with the same references used during the examination.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Collect records showing experience in the installation, maintenance, service, or repair of plumbing systems.
  4. Complete the contractor examination application. Select the Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor classification and provide the requested personal and employment information.
  5. Obtain the required background report. Follow the Board’s instructions for securing and submitting the nationwide criminal background record report.
  6. Submit experience verification. Provide properly completed employer statements and the supporting employment or earnings records requested by the Board.
  7. Submit the completed application. Include all required forms, signatures, notarizations, supporting documents, and applicable examination fees.
  8. Receive examination approval. The Board reviews the application, experience, background information, and supporting records before authorizing the applicant to test.
  9. Schedule the examinations. Approved applicants receive instructions for scheduling the technical and Business and Law examinations.
  10. Prepare the approved books. Make sure every reference complies with the current rules for binding, tabs, highlighting, inserts, and markings.
  11. Complete the examinations. Take the two-hour technical examination and the one-and-one-half-hour Business and Law examination unless a Board-approved exemption applies.
  12. Complete license issuance requirements. Passing the examinations satisfies the testing component, but all remaining Board requirements must be completed before the license is issued.

State Requirements

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors administers the Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor license. The Board reviews applications, verifies experience, authorizes examinations, issues licenses, and enforces the laws and rules governing licensed plumbing work.

Applicants must document 1,500 hours, or approximately nine months of full-time experience, in the installation, maintenance, service, or repair of plumbing systems. The experience may involve work for which a plumbing license was or was not required.

Approved academic or technical training directly related to plumbing may account for up to one-half of the experience requirement. This means qualifying education may provide credit for up to 750 hours.

A current active licensee of the North Carolina Irrigation Contractor Licensing Board may qualify through an alternative path when the applicant also holds an approved municipal Backflow Inspector certification or demonstrates the required experience maintaining, servicing, or repairing plumbing-system components.

An applicant with current active certification as an On-Site Wastewater Contractor may also qualify through an alternative path when the applicant completes the applicable plumbing education or demonstrates the required experience with plumbing-system components.

The Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor license authorizes exterior building sewer piping, exterior water-service piping two inches or smaller, exterior backflow preventers installed on piping two inches or smaller, and water-filtration systems.

The license does not provide the same scope as a Plumbing Class I or Plumbing Class II Contractor license. License holders must remain within the work expressly authorized by the restricted classification.

Restricted license holders may not offer or perform plumbing contracting outside the approved scope. Performing work beyond the classification may result in enforcement or disciplinary action.

Passing the examination does not independently authorize contracting. The applicant must receive the active Restricted Limited Plumbing Contractor license before advertising, bidding, contracting, or performing work that requires this credential.

Licensed contractors are responsible for obtaining required permits, arranging inspections, supervising work, maintaining current license information, and complying with applicable plumbing codes, safety requirements, Board rules, and professional standards.

Test Information and Study Materials

Build a study plan around exterior water services, exterior building sewers, backflow prevention, water filtration, permits, inspections, licensing laws, and contractor business practices.

Practice using the Plumbing Code to locate approved materials, sizing requirements, burial provisions, cleanout rules, separation requirements, connections, testing procedures, and protection against contamination or damage.

Review the Residential Code for provisions affecting exterior plumbing connected to single-family dwellings and other qualifying residential buildings. Learn when the residential reference applies and when the Plumbing Code provides the controlling requirement.

Use timed navigation exercises. Select a topic, locate it through the index, identify the applicable section, read the complete provision, and review related exceptions or cross-references.

Study the Administrative Code and Policies together with the technical codes. Proper plumbing work involves both technical installation and compliance with permits, inspections, approvals, and code-enforcement procedures.

Review the Board’s Laws & Rules regularly. Candidates should understand the precise limits of the restricted license and avoid confusing it with the broader Plumbing Class I or Class II classifications.

For the Business and Law examination, study contracts, bidding, estimating, project scheduling, financial controls, employment practices, insurance, recordkeeping, safety programs, and risk management.
