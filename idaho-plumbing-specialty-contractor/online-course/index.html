Exam Details

DOPL publishes an Idaho plumbing exam bulletin that outlines the format, scoring, and approved reference materials for each plumbing exam. For the Idaho Plumbing Specialty Contractor exam, the bulletin includes these key details:

  • Time Allowed: 4 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 75% (Plumbing Contractor / Specialty Contractor)
  • Exam Fee: $75 (non-refundable)
  • Question Format: Four-option multiple-choice; the bulletin also states there are True/False questions
  • Results: You receive exam results upon completion (reported as pass/fail)
  • Approved Reference for Specialty Contractor Exam: DOPL Contractor’s Business and Law Reference Manual

Testing setup and scheduling (high level): The bulletin explains you must submit an application and application fee before scheduling, and once approved to test, you schedule and pay for the exam through DOPL’s published scheduling process. Seats can be limited, and late arrival can result in being turned away and forfeiting the exam fee.

Open Book Test

DOPL states that all plumbing license exams are open book, including the Plumbing Specialty Contractor exam. Open book does not mean “look up everything.” It means you must be prepared to use the allowed reference efficiently while maintaining a steady pace during a timed exam.

DOPL’s bulletin provides specific open-book rules you should build into your study habits:

  • You may highlight your book.
  • You may tab different sections.
  • You may leave notes in your reference material.
  • Loose paper in your reference material will not be allowed.

How to prepare for open-book performance:

  • Learn what the question is asking. Is it testing a definition, a requirement, an exception, a responsibility, or a procedural step?
  • Know where to look. The Specialty Contractor exam reference is the Contractor’s Business and Law Reference Manual. The faster you can move to the right section, the more time you keep for thinking and answering.
  • Confirm quickly, then move on. Use the book to verify details and avoid over-searching.
  • Practice in exam-compliant conditions. If you study with loose notes and extra sheets, you’ll feel unprepared when those tools are not allowed in the testing room.

Licensing Steps

The exact licensing path depends on your personal qualifications and the specialty category you are pursuing, but DOPL’s plumbing exam bulletin describes an exam-and-scheduling flow that generally follows this order:

  1. Confirm you are applying for the correct credential. This product is designed for the Plumbing Specialty Contractor exam.
  2. Submit your application and application fee to DOPL. The bulletin explains you must apply before you can schedule a test.
  3. Receive your approval letter to test. Once approved, you follow DOPL’s instructions to schedule and pay for the exam.
  4. Prepare your reference material. Organize the Contractor’s Business and Law Reference Manual in a way that supports fast lookups (highlighting/tabs/notes as permitted, no loose paper).
  5. Take the exam and receive results upon completion. DOPL reports pass/fail upon exam completion.
  6. If needed, reapply for reexamination. The bulletin states an applicant receiving a failing grade may apply for reexamination upon payment of the exam fees.

Test Information and Study Materials

Because this is an open-book exam, studying effectively is about building a repeatable system. The goal is to reduce searching and increase confidence. Here are practical methods that align with DOPL’s open-book rules and the Specialty Contractor exam format:

  • Build “question recognition” skills. Most exam questions fall into patterns—definitions, responsibilities, required steps, exceptions, and scenario-based judgment. If you can recognize the pattern quickly, you’ll know which part of the manual to consult.
  • Create a clean tab strategy. Tabs are helpful when they guide you to major areas. Too many tabs can slow you down. A simple system that you can repeat consistently is often faster than an overbuilt one.
  • Highlight for speed, not decoration. Highlight keywords, thresholds, exceptions, and decision points—items that help your eyes land on what matters in a long paragraph.
  • Practice the “find-and-confirm” routine. Read the question, identify the topic, locate the relevant section, confirm the detail, answer, and move on. This is the exact behavior you want to automate before test day.
  • Use timed practice sets. The exam has a 4-hour time limit. Timed practice helps you learn how long is “too long” to search and when to choose the best answer and keep moving.
  • Review missed questions by learning the location. Don’t just memorize the right answer—learn where it is in the manual. Open-book speed improves when your brain remembers “where to find it.”
  • Stay exam-compliant. Since loose paper isn’t allowed, build your organization directly into the manual with permitted tabs, highlighting, and notes.

A practical study rhythm many contractor candidates use:

  • Session A: Review one major manual section and map its structure (headings, terms, common rules)
  • Session B: Short timed set focused on that section to practice navigation
  • Session C: Review missed questions and confirm the exact supporting language in the manual
  • Session D: Mixed timed set to build pacing and confidence across sections

This approach keeps your preparation practical and aligned to the open-book environment—so the manual becomes a tool you can use quickly instead of a book you struggle to navigate.
