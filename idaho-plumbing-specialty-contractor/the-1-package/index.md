## Open Book Test

DOPL’s bulletin explains what is allowed in reference materials during the exam:

## Test Information and Study Materials

The best way to prepare for a timed, open-book contractor exam is to train the exact behaviors you’ll use on exam day. This package is designed to support that through structured course access and a reference-based approach.

- **Study in “find-and-confirm” mode.** Read a question, identify what it’s asking, navigate to the likely section, confirm the key detail, and answer. This builds speed and reduces panic on exam day.
- **Build strong question recognition.** Specialty contractor business-and-law exams frequently test responsibilities, requirements, definitions, and scenario judgment. If you can label the question type quickly, you’ll know where to look.
- **Use tabs and highlighting strategically.** Since DOPL permits tabs and highlighting (but prohibits loose paper), your organization must be built into the manual itself. Keep it clean and consistent so it remains useful under pressure.
- **Practice with time constraints.** A 4-hour exam requires pacing discipline. Timed practice sets help you learn when to confirm in the manual and when to make a best-choice decision and move forward.
- **Review missed questions by locating the rule.** Don’t just learn the correct answer—learn where it lives in the manual. Open-book speed improves when your brain remembers “where to find it.”
- **Keep your prep exam-compliant.** Avoid study habits that rely on loose worksheets or insert pages since those cannot be used in the testing room.

**Suggested use of the 1 year course timeline:**

- **Weeks 1–4:** Build familiarity with major manual sections and create a clean navigation system
- **Weeks 5–8:** Increase practice volume and begin timed sets
- **Weeks 9–12:** Focus on weaker areas and run mixed timed practice to build confidence
- **Final stretch:** Short, frequent practice sets and review of missed question locations to sharpen speed

This approach keeps preparation steady and realistic—so you’re not cramming and not relying on exam-day searching to carry you.
