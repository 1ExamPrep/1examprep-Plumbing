Open Book Test

DOPL states Idaho plumbing license exams are open book, including the Plumbing Specialty Contractor exam. Open book is helpful—but only when you prepare correctly. The fastest way to struggle on an open-book exam is to treat it like you can look up everything. Under a time limit, you need a process that keeps you moving.

DOPL’s bulletin also outlines what is allowed in your reference materials:

  • You may highlight your reference book.
  • You may tab different sections.
  • You may leave notes in the reference material.
  • Loose paper in reference material will not be allowed.

That means your study system should be built into the book and your habits—not dependent on extra printouts or removable note pages. A strong open-book strategy typically looks like this:

  • Recognize the question type quickly: definition, requirement, responsibility, exception, or scenario-based judgment.
  • Navigate to the right section: use your familiarity with the manual’s layout and your permitted tabs/highlighting.
  • Confirm the exact detail: verify what the manual says so you can choose the best answer confidently.
  • Protect your pace: if you’re searching too long, you risk losing time for easier questions.

This Ultimate package is designed to help you develop those habits through a structured course plan, rather than leaving you to guess what to study next.

Test Information and Study Materials

Open-book contractor exams reward a specific kind of preparation: not just reading, but practicing the exact behavior you’ll use on exam day. Your goal is to reduce searching and increase confidence. Use the course and your rental manual to build a repeatable system like the one below.

  • Study in “lookup mode,” not “reading mode.” Instead of trying to read the manual cover-to-cover, practice locating answers. Read a question, identify the topic, navigate to the right section, confirm the detail, answer, and move on.
  • Build question recognition. Many questions fall into predictable categories: definitions, responsibilities, required steps, exceptions, and scenario judgment. If you can label the category quickly, your navigation becomes faster.
  • Use tabs strategically. Tabs are most helpful when they point to major sections and high-use areas. Too many tabs can slow you down and create clutter.
  • Highlight for speed. Highlight keywords, thresholds, and exceptions. Avoid highlighting entire paragraphs; it reduces scan speed because everything looks important.
  • Practice with a timer. The exam is 4 hours, and time disappears quickly when you’re searching. Timed practice sets help you learn when to confirm in the book and when to make a best-choice decision and keep moving.
  • Review missed questions by learning the location. After each set, don’t just learn the right answer—learn where it lives in the manual. Open-book speed improves when your brain remembers “where to find it.”
  • Stay exam-compliant during prep. Since loose paper isn’t allowed, avoid building habits that rely on loose worksheets or insert pages. Train with the same setup you’ll bring to the testing room.

A practical weekly rhythm you can follow with 1 year of course access:

  • Weekdays: Short, consistent study blocks (topic review + navigation drill)
  • 1–2 days per week: Timed practice sets (mixed question types) to build pacing
  • Weekly review: Locate and mark the correct supporting language in the manual for every missed question

Consistency is the advantage. When you combine a structured course plan with repeated open-book practice, your confidence improves because your process becomes automatic.
