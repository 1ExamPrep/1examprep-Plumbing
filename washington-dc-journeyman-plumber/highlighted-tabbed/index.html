Exam Details

The official examination is identified as the 302 Washington DC Journeyman Plumber exam. It is an open book examination based on the approved plumbing code reference.

  • Exam: 302 Washington DC Journeyman Plumber
  • Number of Questions: 70 multiple-choice questions
  • Time Allowed: 3 hours
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Approved Reference: 2012 International Plumbing Code

The published examination content outline includes the following subject areas:

  • Plumbing Fixtures and Terminology: 6%
  • Plumbing Fundamentals: 14%
  • Traps, Cleanouts, and Interceptors: 6%
  • Special Requirements: 4%
  • Drainage Requirements: 16%
  • Vent Requirements: 16%
  • Water Distribution: 11%
  • Plan Reading: 27%

Plan Reading is the largest portion of the examination at 27%. Drainage Requirements and Vent Requirements each represent 16%, while Plumbing Fundamentals represents 14% and Water Distribution represents 11%.

Because the examination includes multiple substantial categories, candidates should avoid concentrating on only one chapter of the code. A balanced study schedule can help develop familiarity with the complete range of plumbing subjects that may appear on test day.

Open Book Test

The Washington DC Journeyman Plumber examination is an open book test. The 2012 International Plumbing Code is identified as the approved reference for the examination.

Open book testing does not mean candidates can rely on researching every answer from the beginning. With 70 questions and a three-hour time limit, efficient code navigation matters. Some questions may be answered quickly from trade knowledge, while others may require locating a specific section, table, definition, or exception.

The highlighted and tabbed book is designed to support this type of navigation. Tabs provide recognizable locations throughout the reference, while highlighting can make important requirements easier to find during study and review.

A useful preparation method is to identify the subject of a question before opening the book. Determine whether the problem involves fixtures, sanitary drainage, venting, water distribution, traps, cleanouts, interceptors, or another plumbing topic. Then move directly to the applicable portion of the code.

Candidates should also practice using plumbing tables. Pipe sizing, fixture units, drainage requirements, venting, and water-distribution questions may require careful interpretation of tables along with surrounding code language.

Reading complete code provisions is also important. A plumbing rule may include exceptions, limitations, definitions, or related sections that change how it applies. Candidates should confirm the entire requirement before selecting an answer.

Licensing Steps

Washington DC plumbing licenses are regulated through the District of Columbia Board of Industrial Trades. Candidates pursuing a Journeyman Plumber license by examination must satisfy the applicable experience, documentation, and examination requirements.

  1. Accumulate qualifying plumbing experience. Applicants using the examination pathway must document the amount of practical work experience required for Journeyman licensing.
  2. Prepare employment verification documentation. Qualifying experience must be supported by employment verification documentation.
  3. Prepare supporting tax records. Journeyman applicants applying through the examination pathway are required to provide tax records along with employment verification.
  4. Submit the license application. Complete the applicable application through the District of Columbia occupational and professional licensing system.
  5. Establish examination eligibility. Applicants must demonstrate the required amount of qualifying experience before sitting for the examination.
  6. Schedule the examination. Washington DC industrial trades examinations may be scheduled through PSI after the applicable licensing and examination requirements are met.
  7. Prepare with the approved code reference. Study the 2012 International Plumbing Code and practice locating relevant provisions efficiently.
  8. Take the Journeyman Plumber examination. Complete the 70-question open book exam within the 3-hour testing period.
  9. Complete the remaining licensing process. Follow District instructions associated with licensing after satisfying the applicable examination and application requirements.

The District also recognizes an examination-waiver pathway for individuals who provide acceptable proof of completion of a nationally certified apprenticeship program. Applicants using that pathway must provide the required completion documentation.

State Requirements

For a Washington DC Journeyman Plumber applicant using the examination pathway, the District identifies an experience requirement of 4 years or 8,000 work hours.

Applicants using this pathway must provide tax records and an employment verification letter documenting the qualifying plumbing experience. The employment verification documentation should support the length and nature of the applicant's work experience.

The District requires applicants to show the appropriate amount of experience before sitting for the licensing examination. This makes experience documentation an important part of the application process rather than something completed only after passing the exam.

Applicants who have completed a nationally certified apprenticeship program may qualify for a Journeyman examination waiver when they provide acceptable proof of completion, such as the applicable certificate, formal designation documentation, or official transcripts.

Washington DC plumbing and gasfitting licenses are administered through the Board of Industrial Trades. The District offers separate classifications for plumbing, gasfitting, and combined plumbing and gasfitting work, so candidates should make sure they are preparing for the correct examination.

Test Information and Study Materials

Plumbing Fixtures and Terminology: Review plumbing fixture requirements, fixture installation, common plumbing terminology, and code definitions. Understanding the terminology used by the code can make it easier to interpret more complex questions.

Plumbing Fundamentals: Plumbing Fundamentals represents 14% of the examination. Study general code provisions, materials, installation principles, pipe sizing concepts, protection requirements, and the basic rules that apply across multiple plumbing systems.

Traps, Cleanouts, and Interceptors: Review trap requirements, trap seals, cleanout locations, accessibility, grease interceptors, separators, and related installation provisions. Candidates should understand both what these components do and where the code requires them.

Special Requirements: Review plumbing installations and conditions that require provisions beyond ordinary fixture, drainage, vent, and water-distribution rules. Knowing how to recognize specialized conditions can help candidates locate the appropriate code section more quickly.

Drainage Requirements: Drainage accounts for 16% of the examination. Review sanitary drainage systems, pipe sizing, drainage fixture units, horizontal and vertical drainage piping, slopes, fittings, cleanouts, connections, and applicable tables.

Practice tracing drainage systems from fixtures through branches, stacks, and building drains. This can help candidates understand how individual code requirements work together within an entire plumbing system.

Vent Requirements: Vent Requirements also represents 16% of the examination. Study vent sizing, vent connections, vent terminals, individual vents, common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, and other permitted venting arrangements.

Understanding the purpose of the vent system can make code provisions easier to apply. Candidates should be able to recognize the type of venting arrangement described by a question and then locate the applicable requirements.

Water Distribution: Water Distribution represents 11% of the examination. Review water-supply and distribution piping, sizing, pressure, valves, materials, connections, backflow-related provisions, and other requirements affecting potable water systems.

Plan Reading: At 27%, Plan Reading is the largest individual content category. Candidates should prepare to interpret plumbing layouts and apply code requirements to information presented in plans or system descriptions.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

Plan-reading questions can require candidates to combine several skills at once. A question may involve identifying a system, calculating fixture units, selecting a pipe size, applying a drainage or vent table, or interpreting the relationship between fixtures and piping.

A productive study method is to alternate between code review and practice questions. Review a chapter, then answer questions requiring you to locate and apply the corresponding provisions. This helps reinforce both technical understanding and navigation speed.

Timed practice can be particularly useful. Track how long it takes to identify a subject, reach the correct code chapter, locate the applicable section or table, and determine the answer. Subjects that consistently take too long may need additional review.

The highlighted and tabbed format can support this process by making major portions of the code easier to recognize. However, candidates should learn what each tab represents and how the surrounding code is organized rather than relying only on visual cues.
