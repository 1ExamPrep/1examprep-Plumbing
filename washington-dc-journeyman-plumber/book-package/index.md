## State Requirements

Washington DC is a federal district rather than a state, but plumbing licenses are regulated locally through the **District of Columbia Board of Industrial Trades**.

For the standard **Journeyman Plumber examination pathway**, current District application instructions require **4 years, or 8,000 hours, of qualifying work experience**.

Candidates using the examination pathway must provide **tax records and an employment verification letter**. The employment verification letter should document the length of employment and the type of plumbing work performed and should be signed by the supervising master.

The District also recognizes a waiver pathway for applicants who have completed a nationally certified apprenticeship program. Documentation may include a certificate of completion, formal designation certificate, or official transcripts, depending on the program.

Washington DC offers separate plumbing, gasfitting, and combined plumbing and gasfitting licenses. The **Journeyman Plumber** exam is different from the **Journeyman Plumber with Gas** examination. The standard Journeyman Plumber test uses only the 2012 International Plumbing Code, while the combined examination also uses the 2012 International Fuel Gas Code and includes a separate gas-content category.

Current Washington DC plumbing and gasfitting licenses expire on **March 31 of even-numbered years**. Renewal is handled separately from the initial examination and licensing process.

The individual Journeyman Plumber credential should also be distinguished from a **Plumbing Contractor license**. A plumbing contractor business must meet separate District business, designated-master, registration, and bonding requirements.

Candidates who plan to move from journeyman-level work toward business ownership or master-level licensing should understand that each credential has its own qualification and application requirements.

## Test Information and Study Materials

**Plumbing Fixtures and Terminology** account for 6% of the examination. Candidates should understand common plumbing definitions, fixture classifications, installation requirements, fixture connections, clearances, and the terminology used throughout the International Plumbing Code.

Definitions can affect the interpretation of many other code provisions. Candidates should become familiar with the terminology used by the IPC so they can understand exactly what a question is asking.

**Plumbing Fundamentals** represent 14%. Review plumbing-system principles, materials, installation practices, code organization, general requirements, system operation, and practical plumbing calculations.

Journeyman candidates should understand how fixtures, drainage, vents, water distribution, traps, cleanouts, and other components work together as part of a complete plumbing system.

**Traps, Cleanouts, and Interceptors** account for 6%. Study trap requirements, trap seals, prohibited arrangements, cleanout locations, accessibility, interceptors, separators, and the situations in which specialized devices are required.

Questions may require candidates to determine whether a trap arrangement is acceptable, where a cleanout must be installed, or when an interceptor or separator is required.

**Special Requirements** represent 4%. Although this is a smaller portion of the examination, candidates should include these provisions in their preparation because specialized plumbing situations can test knowledge outside the major drainage and venting chapters.

**Drainage Requirements** account for **16%**. Candidates should review drainage fixture units, pipe sizing, horizontal and vertical drainage piping, building drains, building sewers, slopes, changes in direction, connections, materials, and sanitary drainage tables.

Drainage calculations deserve regular practice. Candidates should be comfortable determining the fixture-unit load on a section of piping and then using the appropriate IPC table to select the required drainage pipe size.

**Vent Requirements** also represent **16%**. Review individual vents, common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, vent sizing, vent connections, vent terminals, and the relationship between vent systems and trapped fixtures.

Venting questions can be especially sensitive to fixture arrangement and piping configuration. Candidates should practice reading diagrams and plans so they can determine which type of venting method is being used.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

**Water Distribution** accounts for 11%. Study water-service piping, water-distribution systems, fixture supply requirements, pipe sizing, valves, pressure considerations, backflow protection, water heaters, and potable-water system requirements.

Water-distribution questions may require candidates to identify pipe sizes or evaluate whether an installation meets code requirements. Practice moving between tables and related code sections instead of treating each provision separately.

**Plan Reading** represents **27%**, making it the largest section of the current Journeyman Plumber examination. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting plumbing drawings, tracing piping systems, identifying fixtures and fittings, and using plans to answer code-based questions.

Plan-reading questions can combine several plumbing subjects at once. A single drawing may require the candidate to identify a drainage route, determine fixture-unit totals, evaluate venting, locate cleanouts, or determine water-distribution requirements.

A useful preparation technique is to study plans one system at a time. Begin with the sanitary drainage layout, then examine the vent system, water distribution, fixtures, and special devices. Breaking a plan into separate systems can make complex drawings easier to analyze.

The examination allows **three hours for 70 questions**, so time management matters. Candidates should practice working through question sets under timed conditions and learn when to move on from a difficult problem and return later.

Repeated use of the **2012 International Plumbing Code** can improve both speed and confidence. Learn where important chapters begin, which tables appear frequently, and how the IPC directs the reader from one provision to another.

When a practice question is answered incorrectly, determine why. The problem may involve selecting the wrong table, misreading a plan, using the wrong fixture-unit value, confusing drainage and vent requirements, overlooking a special provision, or misunderstanding how a plumbing system is arranged.

Over time, straightforward questions should require less reference searching. Candidates can then reserve more examination time for plan-reading questions, calculations, and detailed code applications.
