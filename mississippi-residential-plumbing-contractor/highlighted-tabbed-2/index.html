Exam Details

The Mississippi Residential Plumbing contractor examination evaluates knowledge associated with residential plumbing systems, water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, fixtures, traps, piping materials, fuel-gas systems, residential construction provisions, and construction safety.

Candidates should combine practical plumbing experience with repeated use of the listed references. Examination questions may require direct code lookup, interpretation of a table, selection of an approved material, sizing of piping, evaluation of a drainage or venting arrangement, or application of a safety requirement.

The International Plumbing Code, 2021, is the primary plumbing reference included in this package. It supports preparation involving general plumbing requirements, fixtures, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, indirect and special waste, vents, traps, interceptors, separators, storm drainage, and referenced standards.

Water-distribution questions may involve pipe sizing, pressure, flow, fixture units, backflow protection, cross-connection control, valves, water heaters, hot-water distribution, insulation, materials, and installation requirements.

Candidates should understand how available pressure, developed length, elevation, fixture demand, pipe material, and code tables can affect water-distribution sizing. Effective preparation requires practice identifying the correct table and reading the applicable notes.

Sanitary-drainage questions may involve drainage fixture units, pipe slope, horizontal branches, building drains, stacks, cleanouts, changes in direction, connections, materials, and system layout.

Drainage systems depend on proper sizing, slope, support, venting, and cleanout access. Candidates should review how individual fixture drains connect to branches, stacks, building drains, and building sewers.

Venting questions may involve individual vents, common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, stack venting, vent stacks, air admittance devices, vent terminals, sizing, developed length, and allowable connections.

Venting provisions often require careful reading because the approved arrangement depends on fixture type, pipe size, developed length, connection point, and system configuration. Candidates should study diagrams and related sections rather than relying only on a single sentence.

Fixture questions may involve minimum fixture requirements, installation, clearances, accessibility, water closets, lavatories, bathtubs, showers, sinks, laundry fixtures, floor drains, drinking fountains, and fixture connections.

Trap questions may involve trap seals, prohibited traps, trap arms, vent connections, cleanouts, interceptors, separators, and protection against siphonage or backpressure.

Exam Details (continued)

Plumbing-material questions may involve approved pipe types, fittings, joining methods, protection from damage, support spacing, underground installations, corrosion protection, thermal expansion, and compatibility with the system being installed.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021, supports preparation involving fuel-gas piping, appliance installation, combustion air, venting, chimneys, regulators, shutoff valves, pressure, sizing, testing, and safety requirements.

Fuel-gas questions may require candidates to determine pipe size, identify permitted materials, review appliance connections, locate shutoff-valve requirements, evaluate combustion-air provisions, or select an appropriate venting method.

Gas-piping calculations require careful use of the applicable tables. Candidates may need to identify the gas type, pressure, allowable pressure drop, connected load, developed length, and table conditions before selecting a pipe size.

Combustion-air questions may involve indoor air, outdoor air, openings, ducts, confined spaces, appliance locations, and the volume available for combustion. Candidates should learn how the required method changes with the installation conditions.

Venting questions may involve vent categories, chimney connections, vent connectors, terminations, clearances, sizing, common venting, draft, and appliance requirements. Candidates should read the general rules together with any equipment-specific provisions.

The International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2021, supports review of residential construction provisions that can affect plumbing work. Plumbing contractors often coordinate piping with framing, foundations, floors, walls, fireblocking, insulation, equipment spaces, crawl spaces, attics, and structural openings.

Residential plumbing installations must be coordinated with the building structure. Candidates should understand how notching, boring, sleeves, penetrations, support, access, clearances, and protection from physical damage can affect piping installation.

The residential code also helps candidates understand how plumbing systems interact with dwelling layout, sanitation, building planning, structural systems, mechanical equipment, and other residential components.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1926, supports preparation involving federal construction-safety requirements. Residential plumbers may need to review excavation, trenching, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, tools, material handling, housekeeping, and general jobsite responsibilities.

Exam Details (continued)

Plumbing work may involve trenches, crawl spaces, ladders, heavy fixtures, power tools, sharp materials, pressurized systems, hot work, and coordination with other trades. Candidates should connect written safety requirements with practical field conditions.

The four references function as a coordinated technical library. A single residential plumbing question may involve a plumbing-code provision, a fuel-gas requirement, a residential construction condition, and a jobsite safety responsibility.

Mississippi contractor applicants may also need separate preparation for applicable Business and Law requirements. A Mississippi Business and Law reference is not included in this trade-only package.

Open Book Test

The Mississippi Residential Plumbing contractor examination is open book. Candidates may use permitted references during the examination, subject to current testing rules for approved titles, editions, highlighting, tabs, notes, markings, and supplemental materials.

An open-book examination still requires detailed preparation. Having the correct books available does not help when a candidate cannot identify the proper chapter, locate an effective index term, interpret a table, recognize an exception, or complete a sizing problem within the available testing time.

The included books are professionally highlighted and tabbed to support more efficient navigation. Highlighting identifies important plumbing provisions, fuel-gas requirements, residential construction topics, safety regulations, definitions, tables, and frequently reviewed subjects.

Candidates should review every prepared tab before examination day. Learn what each tab identifies, study the surrounding material, and practice moving between chapters, indexes, definitions, tables, figures, exceptions, and related sections.

Questions involving water supply, sanitary drainage, venting, fixtures, traps, storm drainage, cleanouts, interceptors, or piping materials may lead to the International Plumbing Code.

Questions involving gas piping, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, shutoff valves, regulators, pressure testing, or pipe sizing may lead to the International Fuel Gas Code.

Questions involving structural coordination, residential framing, penetrations, access, building layout, crawl spaces, attics, or dwelling provisions may require the International Residential Code.

Questions involving excavation, trenching, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, tools, material handling, or jobsite safety may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

Search terminology is important. A drainage question may involve fixture units, horizontal branches, building drains, stacks, cleanouts, slope, or changes in direction.

A venting question may involve trap arms, individual vents, wet venting, circuit venting, stack vents, vent terminals, developed length, or air admittance devices.

A fuel-gas question may involve developed length, connected load, pressure, allowable pressure drop, appliance demand, regulators, shutoff valves, or pipe-sizing tables.

Candidates should read the surrounding material instead of stopping at the first matching phrase. Definitions, exceptions, table notes, installation conditions, and referenced sections can change how a requirement applies.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the appropriate classification. Determine that the Mississippi Residential Plumbing classification matches the plumbing and fuel-gas services the business intends to provide.
  2. Select the qualifying party. Identify the person who will qualify the business and complete the required examinations.
  3. Document plumbing experience. Organize project information involving water distribution, drainage, venting, fixtures, gas piping, supervision, service, or project management.
  4. Choose the applicant structure. Determine whether the application will be submitted by an individual, corporation, limited liability company, partnership, or another accepted business entity.
  5. Prepare the contractor application. Gather the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, experience, financial, insurance, reference, and supporting information.
  6. Submit the application. Send the completed filing to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for review.
  7. Respond to application requests. Provide missing documents, corrections, explanations, or additional information requested during the review process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Follow the examination registration instructions after authorization to test is issued.
  9. Schedule the required examinations. Select an available examination date and approved testing location.
  10. Order the prepared references. Allow up to 15 business days for the highlighted and tabbed book package order.
  11. Review every prepared tab. Learn what each tab identifies and study the highlighted sections before relying on the references during timed practice.
  12. Prepare for the trade examination. Review plumbing code, fuel gas, residential building provisions, calculations, and construction safety.
  13. Prepare for Business and Law. Obtain separate preparation for applicable Mississippi contractor business and law requirements.
  14. Complete timed practice. Work through code questions, sizing tables, calculations, and reference-navigation exercises under structured conditions.
  15. Take the required examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the references and materials permitted under current testing rules.
  16. Complete outstanding requirements. Submit any remaining records, corrections, or supporting documents requested during final review.
  17. Receive licensing approval. Passing the examinations does not automatically issue a contractor license. The complete application and supporting documentation must also be approved.
  18. Maintain the license. After issuance, the contractor is responsible for renewals, insurance, permits, business records, taxes, safety obligations, and ongoing compliance.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the applicable contractor licensing process. Applicants should select the classification that accurately reflects the residential plumbing or fuel-gas work their business intends to perform.

The qualifying party must have a relationship with the applicant that satisfies the applicable requirements. Depending on the business structure, the qualifying party may be an owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee.

The contractor application may require business-registration information, ownership records, qualifying-party documentation, detailed plumbing experience, references, financial information, insurance records, and other supporting materials.

Application information should be complete and consistent across company filings, ownership records, experience documents, and supporting materials. Missing or conflicting information can affect the review process.

Passing the required examinations is one part of contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy the application, documentation, experience, financial, insurance, and final approval requirements.

Residential plumbing contractors may also be responsible for local permits, inspections, adopted plumbing and fuel-gas codes, utility requirements, employee safety, insurance, tax registrations, and project-specific approvals.

Local requirements can vary by project location. Contractors should confirm applicable permitting, inspection, plumbing-code, fuel-gas, utility, and local licensing requirements before beginning regulated work.
