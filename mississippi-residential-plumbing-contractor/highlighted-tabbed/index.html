Exam Details

The Mississippi Residential Plumbing examination is administered through PSI as part of the contractor licensing process overseen by the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. The Residential Plumbing classification requires a trade examination.

The current examination contains 80 questions. Candidates receive 4 hours to complete the test and must earn a minimum score of 70 percent to pass.

The current examination content outline includes:

  • General Plumbing Knowledge and Regulations: 15 questions
  • Piping, Valves, and Controls: 6 questions
  • Fixtures and Equipment: 4 questions
  • Water Supply: 4 questions
  • Drain, Waste and Vent and Sewers: 17 questions
  • Isometric Analysis: 9 questions
  • Safety: 4 questions
  • Specialty Applications: 6 questions
  • Fuel Gas: 5 questions
  • LP Gas: 3 questions
  • Fuel Oil: 2 questions
  • Piping: 5 questions

Drain, waste, vent, and sewer systems represent the largest individual portion of the examination, followed closely by general plumbing knowledge and regulations. Candidates should also devote substantial preparation time to isometric analysis, piping systems, fuel gas, specialty applications, fixtures, water supply, valves, controls, and construction safety.

The examination may include questions based on trade knowledge and general industry practices in addition to information drawn directly from reference materials. Candidates should therefore understand how plumbing systems function and be prepared to apply practical knowledge instead of depending exclusively on book searches.

Open Book Test

The Mississippi Residential Plumbing Contractor examination is an open-book test. PSI allows candidates to bring approved reference materials into the examination center under its testing rules.

Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Temporary removable tabs, including Post-it-style tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before testing begins.

Candidates may not write in their reference materials during the examination. Loose or attached additional papers are also prohibited. PSI permits a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator for the Residential Plumbing examination.

For code questions, candidates must use the code editions identified on the current PSI reference list. The current PSI bulletin lists the 2021 International Plumbing Code, 2018 Fuel Gas Code, and 2021 International Residential Code among the permitted references for the Residential Plumbing examination.

Book familiarity is important even when the test is open book. Candidates should practice using indexes, chapter headings, tables, definitions, diagrams, and code sections before examination day. The ability to recognize where an answer is likely located can reduce time spent searching through unrelated material.

Licensing Steps

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates the Residential Plumbing contractor classification. Candidates must complete the licensing process required by the Board in addition to passing the applicable examination.

  1. Complete the residential contractor application. Begin with the appropriate Mississippi State Board of Contractors application for residential licensure.
  2. Select the Residential Plumbing classification. Applicants should identify the classification corresponding with the plumbing work they intend to perform.
  3. Identify the qualifying party. The qualifying party is the individual connected with the application who is responsible for satisfying the applicable examination requirements.
  4. Provide required experience information. Applicants must document relevant work and experience associated with the requested classification.
  5. Submit supporting application materials. Complete the identifying, business, qualifying-party, insurance, employment, reference, and other information required by MSBOC.
  6. Complete examination eligibility requirements. The Board determines when the qualifying party is eligible for the required examinations.
  7. Receive testing authorization. Once eligibility is established, PSI provides the information necessary for examination registration and scheduling.
  8. Prepare for the Residential Plumbing examination. Study plumbing systems, code requirements, fuel gas, safety, and isometric analysis while practicing reference navigation.
  9. Complete required examinations. Candidates must satisfy the examination requirements associated with their Mississippi contractor application.
  10. Complete final Board requirements. Passing the examination is one component of the process. The complete application must meet MSBOC requirements before a license is issued.

State Requirements

The Mississippi Residential Plumbing classification is a residential license classification involving the installation, maintenance, and repair of residential plumbing systems. The holder of this classification is limited to residential plumbing work.

Applicants pursuing this classification must apply through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors and complete the Board's qualifying-party and examination process. Residential Plumbing is identified by MSBOC as a classification requiring a trade examination.

The application process requires candidates to provide information concerning the requested classification, qualifying party, experience, business structure, and supporting documentation applicable to the residential license.

MSBOC determines examination eligibility before PSI can proceed with testing. Candidates should therefore address the application and exam-preparation processes together rather than waiting until all studying is complete before working on the licensing application.

Mississippi residential licenses are renewed annually. Contractors are responsible for maintaining the license and meeting applicable renewal requirements established by the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.

Test Information and Study Materials

Start your preparation with drain, waste, vent, and sewer systems because this is the largest subject area on the examination. Review drainage-system design, fixture units, pipe sizing, venting, cleanouts, slopes, materials, connections, and related requirements. Practice finding the applicable provisions in the International Plumbing Code rather than relying only on memorization.

General plumbing knowledge and regulations are also heavily represented. Candidates should understand terminology, system requirements, materials, installations, code principles, and practical plumbing practices. Definitions are especially important because plumbing-code questions often depend on the precise meaning of a technical term.

Isometric analysis accounts for nine questions on the current exam. Candidates should practice interpreting plumbing drawings and recognizing how fixtures, branches, stacks, vents, drains, and piping systems relate to one another. Isometric questions may require both visualization skills and knowledge of plumbing-code requirements.

Piping, valves, and controls should receive dedicated review. Become familiar with common piping materials, permitted uses, fittings, joints, valves, supports, connections, and installation practices. Understanding why a particular material or valve is used can help with both direct trade questions and code-based questions.

Water supply preparation should include water distribution, pipe sizing concepts, pressure, fixtures, connections, and protection of the potable water system. Backflow protection and cross-connection concepts are important areas for plumbing contractors to understand.

Fixtures and equipment should be studied together with applicable code requirements. Review installation, clearances, connections, fixture requirements, water heaters, and equipment-related provisions that may apply to residential plumbing systems.

Fuel gas represents a dedicated portion of the examination. Study gas piping, materials, sizing concepts, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, shutoff requirements, and related installation principles. LP gas and fuel oil also appear separately on the examination outline and should not be overlooked.

OSHA safety is another tested subject. Plumbing contractors frequently work around excavations, trenches, ladders, power tools, electrical equipment, elevated work areas, and active construction sites. Become familiar with the organization of 29 CFR Part 1926 so safety information can be located efficiently.

Some trade-knowledge questions may be answered quickly, while isometric or code-research questions may require additional time.
