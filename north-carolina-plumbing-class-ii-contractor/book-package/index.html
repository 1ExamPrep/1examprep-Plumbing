Exam Details

The North Carolina Plumbing Class II Contractor licensing process includes a Plumbing Contractor technical examination and a Business and Law examination. The technical Plumbing Contractor examination allows four hours. The Business and Law examination allows one and one-half hours.

The Business and Law examination may not be required when an applicant already holds a currently active contractor license issued by the same Board and passed the Board’s Business and Law examination after August 2003. Applicants seeking this exemption must meet the Board’s applicable conditions.

The technical portion focuses on the plumbing knowledge required for single-family detached residential dwellings. Candidates should prepare for questions involving water service, water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, materials, installation methods, testing, inspections, permits, and residential code administration.

Water-distribution preparation should include approved piping materials, sizing methods, pressure, fixture supply, shutoff valves, water-service installation, backflow protection, cross-connection control, thermal expansion, protection against freezing, and prevention of contamination.

Sanitary drainage study may involve drainage fixture units, horizontal and vertical pipe sizing, slope, changes in direction, cleanouts, building drains, building sewers, stacks, branch piping, sumps, ejectors, and connections to approved disposal systems.

Venting preparation should address trap-seal protection, individual vents, common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, stack venting, vent stacks, air admittance valves, vent sizing, developed length, termination, and protection of vent openings.

Fixture-related questions may cover water closets, lavatories, kitchen sinks, bathtubs, showers, laundry fixtures, floor drains, hose connections, fixture supports, clearances, installation, drainage connections, water supplies, and minimum residential plumbing provisions.

Water-heater preparation may include approved locations, temperature and pressure relief protection, discharge piping, drain pans, thermal expansion, shutoff valves, connections, combustion considerations, clearances, and installation requirements referenced by the Residential Code.

Administrative subjects may include permit applications, construction documents, inspections, approvals, corrections, code-official authority, contractor responsibilities, and procedures used to determine whether residential plumbing work complies with the applicable code.

Exam Details (continued)

The Business and Law examination addresses the responsibilities of operating a contracting business. Candidates should review contracts, bidding, estimating, project scheduling, financial controls, employment requirements, insurance, safety programs, recordkeeping, licensing regulations, and risk management.

Open Book Test

The North Carolina Plumbing Class II Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates are required to bring the approved reference books assigned to the Class II examination. Only the books listed for the examination category may be taken into the testing room.

The current reference list identifies the North Carolina Administrative Code and Policies, North Carolina Residential Code, Board’s Laws & Rules, and Business and Project Management reference for Plumbing Class II Contractor candidates.

Original hard-bound, spiral-bound, or ring-bound reference books may be used when they remain in their approved published condition. Commercially available, non-removable index tabs may be permanently attached to the pages. Approved loose-leaf codebooks must remain in the original binders provided for them.

Highlighting is permitted. Written notes, added formulas, calculations, underlining, homemade tabs, unauthorized divider pages, loose paper, taped inserts, and other additions are not permitted. Printed PDF versions of the reference books are not accepted for examination use.

Candidates should prepare the books carefully before test day. Every tab and marking should comply with the current examination requirements. Books containing prohibited alterations or unauthorized materials may be rejected at the testing site.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. The examination time is limited, and candidates should not expect to read unfamiliar chapters from beginning to end while testing. Preparation should focus on recognizing the topic, selecting the correct book, finding the governing section, and confirming the answer efficiently.

A question about residential drainage sizing should direct the candidate to the Residential Code. A question involving permits or inspection procedures may require the Administrative Code and Policies. Licensing, supervision, or professional-conduct questions should direct the candidate to the Board’s Laws & Rules.

Questions about contracts, financial management, project scheduling, employment practices, insurance, or risk management may require the business reference. Knowing which book controls the subject can save valuable time during the examination.

Practice should include using each book’s table of contents, index, definitions, chapter headings, tables, figures, appendices, and cross-references. Candidates should read the complete code section rather than relying on an isolated sentence because exceptions or related conditions may affect the answer.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the Class II scope. Plumbing Class II applies to plumbing work in single-family detached residential dwellings.
  2. Obtain the required references. Applicants should have the current books identified for the Class II examination before submitting the examination application.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Collect records showing the required hands-on plumbing experience in installation, maintenance, service, or repair.
  4. Complete the examination application. Select Plumbing Class II and provide the requested personal, employment, experience, and licensing information.
  5. Obtain the required background report. Follow the Board’s instructions for ordering and submitting the nationwide criminal background record report.
  6. Submit experience verification. Provide properly completed experience forms and supporting earnings or employment documentation.
  7. Submit the complete application package. Include the required signatures, notarizations, supporting records, and examination fee.
  8. Wait for application review. The Board must receive, review, and approve the completed application, background report, and experience documentation before the applicant is eligible to test.
  9. Receive scheduling instructions. Approved candidates receive information for scheduling the examination with the Board’s third-party testing provider.
  10. Prepare the approved books. Confirm that each reference complies with the current rules for binding, highlighting, tabs, inserts, and markings.
  11. Complete the required examinations. Take the Plumbing Contractor technical examination and the Business and Law examination unless an applicable exemption or waiver has been approved.
  12. Complete license issuance requirements. Passing the examinations satisfies the testing component, but all remaining Board requirements must be completed before the license is issued.

State Requirements

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors administers Plumbing Class II licensing. The Board reviews applications, verifies experience, authorizes examinations, issues licenses, and enforces the laws and rules governing licensed contractors.

Plumbing Class II is limited to plumbing work in single-family detached residential dwellings. Plumbing Class I is the broader classification and applies to commercial, residential, industrial, and institutional buildings. Applicants should choose the classification that matches their qualifying experience and intended scope of work.

Applicants for Plumbing Class I or Class II contractor licensing must document 4,000 hours of qualifying hands-on experience in the installation, maintenance, service, or repair of plumbing systems.

Approved education may account for up to 50 percent of the experience requirement. The education must be directly related to the license sought and completed through a college, university, community college, or technical institute as part of a certificate, diploma, or degree program.

The application process requires verified experience documentation, a nationwide criminal background report, required signatures, supporting records, and the applicable examination fee. Incomplete applications may be returned or delayed.

Applicants must possess the necessary current North Carolina codebooks, the Board-issued Laws & Rules reference, and the approved business and project management book when they apply. Submitting the application indicates that the applicant has the required references and is prepared to test.

North Carolina does not offer general license reciprocity for Plumbing Class II. A technical-examination waiver may be available to an applicant who holds an eligible South Carolina Residential Plumbing contractor license, has taken and passed the applicable South Carolina technical examination, meets North Carolina’s experience requirements, and completes the entire North Carolina application process.

A technical-examination waiver applies only to the technical portion. The applicant must still pass the North Carolina business examination unless a separate exemption applies.

Passing an examination does not independently authorize plumbing contracting. The individual or business must receive the applicable active license and comply with the Board’s requirements before performing work that requires licensure.

State Requirements (continued)

Licensed contractors are responsible for operating within the Class II scope, obtaining required permits, arranging inspections, providing proper supervision, maintaining accurate licensing information, and complying with applicable codes, laws, and professional standards.

Test Information and Study Materials

Create separate study plans for the technical Plumbing Contractor examination and the Business and Law examination. For the technical portion, rotate among water distribution, drainage, venting, fixtures, water heaters, materials, testing, inspections, and administrative procedures.

Practice using the Residential Code to locate pipe-sizing tables, fixture requirements, drainage provisions, venting methods, water-heater rules, and material standards. Learn which chapter controls each major residential plumbing subject.

Review definitions carefully. Terms used in plumbing codes and licensing rules may have specific meanings that affect how a requirement is applied. Understanding the defined terms can make code questions easier to interpret.

Use timed reference exercises. Select a topic, locate it through the index, identify the controlling section, review the complete requirement, and check for related exceptions or cross-references.

Study the Administrative Code and Policies alongside the Residential Code. Technical plumbing knowledge should be supported by an understanding of permits, inspections, approvals, construction documents, and code-enforcement responsibilities.

For the business examination, review contracts, bids, estimating, job costing, financial statements, project scheduling, employment responsibilities, insurance, safety programs, recordkeeping, and risk management.
