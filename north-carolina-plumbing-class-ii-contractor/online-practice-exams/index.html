  2. Meet the qualifying experience requirement. Plumbing Class II contractor applicants generally must document qualifying experience related to the plumbing systems covered by the license.
  3. Use qualifying education when applicable. Approved education directly related to the trade may count toward part of the experience requirement when allowed.
  4. Obtain the required study and reference materials. Prepare with the current code, Board, and Business and Law references used for the examination.

  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow the Board's current application procedures for any required background review.
  7. Receive examination approval. Eligible candidates may proceed with testing after the Board reviews and approves the application.
  8. Schedule the examination. Arrange the applicable Plumbing Class II Contractor examination after receiving authorization.
  9. Prepare for technical plumbing questions. Review potable water, drainage, waste, venting, fixtures, piping, materials, and residential code provisions.

  12. Complete the required examinations. Meet the technical and Business and Law requirements unless an applicable exemption or waiver applies.
  13. Complete final licensing requirements. Follow the Board's instructions for license activation after all applicable requirements have been satisfied.

Exam preparation should run alongside application preparation. Candidates should make sure their experience documentation and licensing information are organized while also working through the technical and Business and Law study material.

State Requirements

The North Carolina Plumbing Class II Contractor classification is limited to plumbing systems in single-family detached dwellings. The scope includes potable water and drain, waste, and vent piping systems within that residential limitation.

North Carolina distinguishes Plumbing Class II from Plumbing Class I. Class I authority extends to plumbing systems in broader building types, while Class II is limited to the residential scope established for single-family detached dwellings.

The standard experience requirement for a Plumbing Class II Contractor applicant generally includes 4,000 hours of qualifying experience in the installation, maintenance, service, or repair of plumbing systems related to the license sought.

Approved academic or technical education directly related to plumbing may count toward a portion of the experience requirement when allowed under Board rules. Candidates using education toward qualification should make sure the program and documentation satisfy the applicable requirements.

Applicants should understand that passing the examination does not automatically issue the contractor license. Experience documentation, application approval, examination completion, and final licensing requirements all form part of the overall process.

North Carolina contractor licensing also places responsibility on the license holder to understand the authorized scope of work. Candidates preparing for Class II should know that the license is specifically limited and should not be treated as unrestricted plumbing authority.

Business and Law preparation is also important because a contractor is responsible for more than performing technical plumbing work. Operating a contracting business involves legal, administrative, financial, contractual, and project-management responsibilities that are separate from field installation knowledge.
