Exam Details

The Arizona C-37 and CR-37 Plumbing trade exam contains 80 questions. Candidates must earn a minimum passing score of 70%, and the time allowed for the exam is 210 minutes.

The exam content outline includes major plumbing trade areas connected to residential and commercial plumbing work. Candidates should prepare for topics such as:

  • General Plumbing Knowledge
  • Water Supply Systems
  • Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems
  • Fuel Gas Systems
  • Hydronic Heating and Boilers
  • Fire Protection and Sprinkler Systems
  • Plumbing Fixtures, Appliances, and Equipment
  • Piping, Fittings, Valves, and Supports
  • Plans, Estimating, and Calculations
  • On-Site Wastewater and Sewage Treatment Provisions
  • Excavation, Underground Work, and Utility Awareness
  • Safety

General plumbing knowledge may include trade terminology, tools, equipment, materials, installation practices, testing procedures, inspections, troubleshooting, and field conditions. Candidates should understand how plumbing systems are installed and how code requirements apply to real residential and commercial projects.

Water supply questions may involve potable water piping, pressure, flow, pipe sizing, fixtures, valves, backflow prevention, water heaters, tanks, pumps, and related system components. Drain, waste, and vent questions may involve traps, cleanouts, fixture units, venting methods, sanitary drainage, slope, indirect waste, storm drainage, and system testing.

Fuel gas topics may include gas piping materials, gas sizing, pressure, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, shutoff valves, regulators, clearances, pressure testing, and safety procedures. Mechanical and hydronic topics may involve boilers, pumps, heat transfer, expansion tanks, controls, piping arrangements, equipment rooms, and light commercial hydronic systems.

Fire protection topics may involve NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R sprinkler system standards. Candidates should understand how these standards apply to different building types, including commercial sprinkler systems, one- and two-family dwelling sprinkler systems, and sprinkler systems for residential occupancies up to and including four stories in height.

Open Book Test

The Arizona Plumbing Residential / Commercial Contractor (CR-37) Exam is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. The references are not provided at the testing center.

The approved exam-room references include:

  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
  • International Fuel Gas Code, 2018
  • International Mechanical Code, 2018
  • International Plumbing Code, 2018
  • NFPA 13: Installation of Sprinkler Systems, 2019

  • NFPA 13R - Sprinkler Systems for Residential Occupancies up to and Including Four Stories in Height, 2019

Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator during the exam. Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index references during the exam.

Additional papers are not allowed with approved references. This includes loose papers and attached papers. References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Permanent tabs are tabs that would tear the page if removed. Temporary tabs, including removable sticky notes or Post-It style tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

Licensing Steps

Arizona contractor licensing is handled by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. A candidate pursuing the CR-37 Plumbing Residential / Commercial Contractor classification should review the current state licensing requirements and application instructions before applying.

The qualifying party for the license must complete the applicable examination requirements for the classification. Arizona contractor applicants may also need to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam, which covers state-specific contractor laws, rules, and regulatory responsibilities.

After the required examination process is completed, the applicant submits the contractor license application and supporting documents to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. The application process may include business entity information, qualifying party information, experience documentation, bonding, financial responsibility, background-related forms, and other state-required documents.

Applicants should apply for the classification that matches the work they intend to perform. The CR-37 classification is a dual residential and commercial plumbing classification. It is intended for contractors who need both the residential and commercial scopes connected to plumbing work.

Passing the trade exam does not automatically issue a contractor license. The exam supports the trade competency portion of the process, but the license is issued only after the Arizona Registrar of Contractors approves the complete application for the applicable classification.

State Requirements

Arizona uses contractor license classifications to define the type of work a contractor may legally contract for and perform. The CR-37 Plumbing classification allows the scopes of work permitted by the commercial C-37 Plumbing and residential R-37 Plumbing classifications.

The plumbing scope includes installation, alteration, and repair of plumbing when performed within property lines and within the allowed classification scope. This can include piping, fixtures, appliances, water supply systems, drainage systems, waste systems, vent systems, fuel gas piping, pressure vessels, tanks, water heaters, and equipment common to the plumbing industry.

The residential and commercial plumbing scope may also involve fire protection systems as they relate to water sprinkler systems, sewage treatment systems, and solar applications within the scope of the plumbing classification. Candidates should understand that plumbing work can overlap with mechanical, fire protection, excavation, utility, boiler, and general contracting work. Contractors should operate only within the scope of the license issued to them and use properly licensed subcontractors when work falls outside that scope.

Residential plumbing projects may involve homes, dwelling units, water heaters, fixtures, water supply piping, drainage and vent systems, fuel gas piping, sprinkler systems covered by residential standards, and on-site wastewater provisions. Commercial plumbing projects may involve larger systems, commercial fixtures, equipment rooms, sprinkler coordination, hydronic heating, boilers, backflow prevention, inspections, utility coordination, and more complex code requirements.

The exam book package supports study for the trade exam, while the state licensing process is handled separately through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants should follow the state process for application timing, examination requirements, bonding, entity documentation, and final license approval.

Test Information and Study Materials

Preparation for the Arizona CR-37 exam should begin with the exam content outline and the approved reference list. The exam covers a broad plumbing scope, so candidates should divide study time by system type: plumbing code, fuel gas, mechanical systems, sprinkler standards, on-site wastewater, safety, math, pipe trades, boilers, hydronic heating, and underground utility awareness.

The International Plumbing Code should receive significant study time. Candidates should practice locating sections on fixtures, water supply, drainage, venting, traps, cleanouts, pipe materials, slope, storm drainage, indirect waste, water heaters, testing, and inspections. Code navigation is important because many questions require knowing where the rule is located rather than simply remembering the answer.

The International Fuel Gas Code should be studied for gas piping materials, sizing, appliance connections, shutoff valves, regulators, venting, combustion air, pressure testing, and installation safety. Fuel gas questions often require careful reading because small details can affect the correct answer.

The International Mechanical Code, Boiler Operator’s Guide, and Modern Hydronic Heating reference support preparation for hydronic heating, boilers, pumps, controls, equipment rooms, combustion, mechanical coordination, and residential or light commercial heating systems. The NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R references should be studied for sprinkler system concepts, system types, installation rules, components, and water supply requirements. Candidates should understand the difference between the standards and the types of occupancies each standard addresses.

The Arizona on-site wastewater reference should be reviewed for sewage treatment and wastewater-related provisions. This is especially important for candidates preparing for the residential and commercial scope because plumbing work may involve on-site wastewater system requirements depending on project location and system type.

Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters should be used for trade math practice.
