## Reference Books

- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**
 The federal construction safety reference covers workplace safety requirements for construction activities. It supports review of subjects such as personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolding, fall protection, excavations, electrical safety, tools, material handling, and other jobsite safety responsibilities.
- **International Fuel Gas Code, 2018**
 This code addresses fuel gas systems, gas piping, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, installation requirements, inspections, and related safety provisions. It is an important reference for plumbing work involving gas systems and fuel-fired equipment.
- **International Mechanical Code, 2018**
 This code provides requirements for mechanical systems, equipment, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, duct systems, appliances, and related installations. It helps candidates review mechanical topics that may overlap with plumbing and fuel gas work.
- **International Plumbing Code, 2018**
 This primary plumbing reference covers fixtures, water supply, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, storm drainage, piping materials, installation requirements, testing, inspections, and other plumbing system provisions.
- **NFPA 13: Installation of Sprinkler Systems, 2019**
 This standard addresses the design and installation of automatic fire sprinkler systems. It includes technical requirements for system components, spacing, water supplies, piping, installation, and related fire protection provisions.
- **NFPA 13D - Installation of Sprinkler Systems in One- and Two-Family Dwellings and Manufactured Homes, 2019**
 This standard focuses on sprinkler systems installed in one- and two-family dwellings and manufactured homes. It addresses residential system requirements, water supplies, sprinkler placement, piping, and installation considerations for these occupancies.
- **NFPA 13R - Sprinkler Systems for Residential Occupancies up to and Including Four Stories in Height, 2019**
 This standard covers sprinkler systems for qualifying residential occupancies up to and including four stories in height. It addresses system design, installation, coverage, components, and requirements specific to the occupancies within its scope.
- **On-site Wastewater Treatment Facility Provisions in Unified Water Quality Permit Rule, Arizona Administrative Code Title 18, Chapter 9, 2006**
 This Arizona-specific reference addresses provisions for on-site wastewater treatment facilities. It supports review of state requirements involving wastewater systems, permitting, installation, operation, and related water quality considerations.

## Test Information and Study Materials

The best way to prepare for a multi-reference open-book exam is to create a study system around the books. Begin by assigning each major subject to the appropriate reference. Plumbing system questions generally point toward the International Plumbing Code. Fuel gas questions belong in the International Fuel Gas Code. Mechanical topics may require the International Mechanical Code. Sprinkler questions require careful attention to the building or occupancy type so the correct NFPA standard can be selected.

Safety questions should be approached through the OSHA construction standards. Learn the organization of 29 CFR Part 1926 and become familiar with the subparts that address common construction hazards. For wastewater questions, review the Arizona Administrative Code provisions included in the package.

Practice using indexes, chapter titles, section numbers, tables, and definitions. Many code questions can be answered more quickly when the candidate recognizes the correct search term. For example, a question may use a trade phrase that differs from the formal index term. Learning related terminology can improve search speed.

Tables deserve special attention. Plumbing, gas, mechanical, and sprinkler references frequently use tables to establish capacities, clearances, dimensions, spacing, sizing, and material requirements. Candidates should practice reading table headings, notes, exceptions, and referenced sections.

Cross-references are also important. A code section may direct the reader to another chapter, table, standard, or exception. Do not stop at the first matching phrase. Read enough surrounding material to understand how the rule applies.

Timed practice can help candidates develop a realistic exam strategy. Work through practice-oriented questions while using the books. Track which references take the longest to navigate and spend additional time learning those indexes and chapter layouts.

A practical method is to divide questions into three groups: questions you can answer immediately, questions that require a quick reference search, and questions that require deeper research. Completing the faster questions first may leave more time for difficult code searches later.
