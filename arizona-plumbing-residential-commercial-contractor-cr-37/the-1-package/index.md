## Exam Details

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors licenses residential, commercial, and dual contractors throughout the state. The CR-37 Plumbing classification provides dual authority for qualifying plumbing work performed in residential and commercial settings.

The qualifying party is the individual whose experience, knowledge, and examination qualifications support the contractor license. This person must satisfy the applicable Arizona requirements and remain associated with the licensed business in the qualifying capacity recognized by the state.

CR-37 exam preparation may include water supply, drainage, vents, fixtures, fuel-gas systems, mechanical equipment, boilers, hydronic heating, fire-sprinkler provisions, wastewater systems, safety, utility location, estimating, and plumbing calculations.

Water-distribution preparation may involve water services, approved piping materials, fixture demand, pressure, elevation, friction loss, valves, backflow protection, water heaters, thermal expansion, insulation, supports, and testing.

Sanitary drainage and venting preparation may include building drains, building sewers, fixture drains, traps, cleanouts, vent sizing, vent connections, slope, developed length, fittings, materials, and testing procedures.

Fuel-gas preparation may involve approved materials, pipe sizing, appliance demand, shutoff valves, sediment traps, regulators, pressure testing, combustion air, equipment connections, and venting.

Mechanical and hydronic preparation may include boilers, circulating pumps, expansion tanks, air separators, heat exchangers, valves, controls, heat emitters, piping arrangements, system filling, purging, and pressure or temperature protection.

Fire-sprinkler preparation may address system types, water supplies, piping arrangements, sprinklers, valves, inspection, testing, and installation provisions. NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R apply to different building and residential occupancy conditions.

On-site wastewater preparation may involve treatment-facility provisions, disposal systems, site conditions, installation concepts, setbacks, and Arizona requirements contained in the provided administrative-code material.

Trade mathematics may include measurements, fractions, decimals, percentages, elevations, offsets, pipe length, area, volume, pressure, flow, slope, capacity, and unit conversions. Candidates should be comfortable selecting the appropriate formula or code table before completing a calculation.

OSHA preparation may include excavations, trenching, ladders, tools, electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, material handling, fall protection, housekeeping, and safe working conditions around active construction sites.

## State Requirements

The CR-37 classification is an Arizona dual specialty contractor license covering qualifying plumbing work in residential and commercial settings. Contractors must remain within the scope authorized by the classification.

The proposed qualifying party must demonstrate the experience, knowledge, and skills needed to supervise or perform the covered work. Experience documentation may need to describe water piping, sanitary drainage, vents, fixtures, gas piping, equipment, hydronic systems, service, repair, residential projects, commercial projects, and field supervision.

The qualifying party must remain associated with the applicant business in the capacity recognized by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Experience records, examination information, and the person’s business role should remain consistent throughout the application process.

The legal business entity applying for the license must be properly established and identified. The business name, ownership information, qualifying-party records, EIN documentation, contractor bond, and license application should remain consistent.

Arizona contractor applicants must complete applicable background requirements and provide the identity, experience, examination, business, and disclosure information required for state review.

A contractor license bond is required. Because CR-37 is a dual classification, the applicable bond requirement combines the residential and commercial components based on the anticipated gross volume of work.

The residential portion of the CR-37 license is also subject to Arizona’s residential financial-protection requirements. The applicant must satisfy the applicable Residential Contractors’ Recovery Fund obligation or authorized alternative.

After licensing, the contractor must remain within the CR-37 scope, maintain the required bond, renew the license, and keep qualifying-party and business information current. Changes involving ownership, entity structure, address, bond, or qualifying party may require additional filings.
