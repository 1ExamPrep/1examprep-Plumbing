## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Florida Plumbing Plans Examiner study plan should begin with **Water Supply and Distribution**, which represents 19% of the examination.

Review potable water-service piping, water distribution piping, sizing, pressure, materials, valves, fixture supplies, protection against contamination, and testing requirements.

**Backflow protection and cross-connection control** deserve focused study. Plans examiners should understand how proposed potable water systems are protected from contamination and when different backflow-prevention methods are required.

Commercial plumbing plans may include water risers, fixture-unit calculations, pipe sizes, pressure information, equipment connections, and backflow devices. Candidates should practice evaluating whether the plans contain enough information to demonstrate code compliance.

**Fixtures** represent 15% of the examination. Review required fixture counts, fixture types, installation, supports, clearances, drinking fountains, water closets, urinals, lavatories, showers, sinks, and other commercial fixtures.

Fixture review should include **accessibility**. The ICC A117.1-2017 standard provides requirements affecting accessible plumbing fixtures and spaces. Candidates should be comfortable locating exact dimensional and clearance requirements.

Plans examiners may need to evaluate clear floor spaces, fixture heights, grab-bar locations, approach spaces, turning areas, and other accessibility features shown on architectural and plumbing drawings.

**Vents** also represent 15%. Study vent stacks, stack vents, individual vents, common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, vent terminals, vent sizing, and permitted configurations.

Venting questions can require candidates to understand how the complete drainage system operates. Practice reading riser diagrams and identifying fixture drains, traps, horizontal branches, stacks, and vent connections.

**Sanitary Drainage** represents 13%. Review drainage fixture units, horizontal drainage piping, stacks, building drains, building sewers, slopes, sizing, cleanouts, fittings, materials, and testing.

Drainage-system sizing frequently requires use of tables. Candidates should practice selecting the correct table and reading all applicable headings, notes, and limitations.

**Traps, Interceptors, Separators, Special Piping, and Storage Systems** represent 11%. Review trap seals, prohibited traps, grease interceptors, oil separators, indirect waste, special waste systems, and other specialized commercial plumbing installations.

Commercial buildings may include kitchens, laboratories, health care facilities, industrial processes, and other uses that require plumbing systems beyond ordinary domestic fixtures.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

**Water Heaters** represent 10%. Review water-heating equipment, temperature and pressure relief protection, discharge piping, installation location, supports, pans, access, connections, and applicable safety provisions.

Gas-fired water heaters may require coordination between the IPC and IFGC. Candidates should be able to identify when fuel-gas piping, combustion air, or venting provisions must also be reviewed.

**Storm Drainage** represents 7%. Review roof drains, secondary drainage, leaders, conductors, storm piping, roof areas, sizing, and approved discharge arrangements.

Plans examiner preparation should include storm-drainage calculations and table navigation. A proposed system may require verification of roof area, rainfall conditions, pipe size, number of drains, and secondary drainage provisions.

**Health Care Plumbing** represents 5%. Candidates should become familiar with specialized plumbing requirements associated with health care facilities and related systems.

Health care facilities can contain plumbing and piping systems that require special materials, arrangements, equipment, or protection. Candidates should recognize when specialized code sections apply.

**General Requirements** represent 5%. This category can include plan completeness, permits, approved materials, piping protection, structural protection, testing, installation requirements, and general code administration.

A plans examiner should verify that submitted documents contain enough information for a complete review. Missing pipe sizes, unclear fixture counts, incomplete riser diagrams, absent accessibility dimensions, or missing system calculations can prevent a proper determination of compliance.

**Plumbing riser diagrams** are an important study area. Candidates should practice tracing water supply, sanitary drainage, and vent systems through a building and understanding the relationships among fixtures, branches, stacks, and mains.

For water distribution systems, review how fixture-unit demand, pipe length, available pressure, elevation, and other design conditions affect sizing.

For sanitary drainage, understand how drainage fixture units relate to pipe capacity and how horizontal and vertical piping may be sized differently.

For vent systems, understand how fixture arrangements and drainage connections affect permitted vent methods and sizing.

The **2021 International Plumbing Code** should be used actively throughout preparation. Rather than reading the code straight through, work practice questions and locate the specific provisions that control the answer.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

The **2021 International Fuel Gas Code** should be used for fuel-gas piping, gas-fired appliances, combustion air, and venting questions. Learn to recognize when a plumbing plan-review question crosses into fuel-gas requirements.

The **ICC A117.1-2017** standard should be reviewed regularly for accessible fixture and facility requirements. Accessibility questions often depend on exact dimensions, so familiarity with the standard's organization is useful.

Tables deserve substantial practice. The plumbing and fuel-gas codes use tables for pipe sizing, fixture units, drainage capacity, vent sizing, fuel-gas systems, and other technical requirements.

When using a table, always confirm the title, units, conditions, and footnotes. A candidate can locate the correct table and still reach the wrong answer by applying the wrong row or column.

When a practice question is missed, determine why. The error may involve weak code navigation, selecting the wrong reference, misunderstanding system operation, overlooking an exception, or using a table incorrectly.

Organize missed questions by category. If water supply remains difficult, dedicate additional review there. If accessibility, drainage, venting, water heaters, or special piping produce repeated errors, build focused study sessions around those subjects.

As exam day approaches, use mixed-topic practice. A real plans examiner exam can move quickly from fixtures to water distribution, then vents, drainage, accessibility, storm systems, or fuel-gas requirements.

With **60 questions in 2 hours and 30 minutes**, candidates have an average of approximately two and a half minutes per question. Efficient reference navigation can preserve additional time for calculations, tables, and multi-code questions.
