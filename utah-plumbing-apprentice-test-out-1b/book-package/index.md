## State Requirements

The Utah Plumbing Apprentice Test-Out Exam Program is intended only for individuals who are **enrolled in or planning to enroll in plumbing apprenticeship education in Utah**. Each test-out examination corresponds to a specific semester of apprenticeship learning objectives.

Test Out 1B focuses on knowledge normally learned during the **second semester** of apprenticeship training. Passing the exam allows an eligible student to bypass that semester of coursework, but it does not eliminate the remaining apprenticeship, work-experience, or journeyman licensing requirements.

Utah regulates apprentice plumbers through the **Division of Professional Licensing**. An apprentice plumber applicant must identify the proposed supervisor of the apprenticeship.

During the initial three years of training, a licensed apprentice plumber generally works under the immediate supervision of a licensed master plumber, journeyman plumber, residential master plumber, or residential journeyman plumber. Beginning in the fourth year of training, Utah law permits qualifying apprentice plumbers to work without supervision for limited periods under the conditions established by state law and rule.

For future **Journeyman Plumber** licensure, Utah provides a pathway involving at least four years of full-time training and instruction as a licensed apprentice plumber under an approved program. Utah also provides other qualifying experience pathways established by statute and administrative rule.

Passing Test Out 1B should therefore be viewed as one step within plumbing apprenticeship education. It demonstrates competency in a defined portion of the curriculum but does not replace Utah's requirements for licensing, work experience, supervision, or journeyman examinations.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Test Out 1B study plan should begin with the **2018 International Plumbing Code**, especially Chapters 4, 5, and 10. These chapters are directly identified in the published learning objectives for the examination.

**Chapter 4** addresses plumbing fixtures, faucets, and fixture fittings. Candidates should understand how the code organizes fixture requirements, installation provisions, required clearances, materials, and related requirements.

**Chapter 5** addresses water heaters. Study installation requirements, safety provisions, connections, protection, and other code requirements associated with water-heating equipment.

**Chapter 10** addresses traps, interceptors, and separators. Candidates should understand the role of traps in plumbing systems, applicable installation rules, trap-seal concepts, and the purpose of interceptors and separators.

Second-semester apprenticeship study also includes practical plumbing topics associated with fixtures, piping materials, valves, water supply concepts, and water-heating systems. Candidates should connect the code requirements with real-world plumbing applications whenever possible.

Plumbing mathematics is another important part of apprenticeship training. Practice working with measurements, dimensions, pipe-related calculations, offsets, and other mathematical concepts encountered in the plumbing trade.

The **International Fuel Gas Code** and **International Mechanical Code** should not be ignored simply because the IPC chapters are specifically listed in the learning objectives. All six references in this package are identified as permitted testing materials, and candidates should know the general organization of each one.

When studying a model-code provision, check the corresponding Utah amendment. This helps establish the habit of distinguishing between the nationally published code language and requirements modified for Utah.

Use permanent tabs strategically rather than placing a tab on every page. Useful tab locations may include major IPC chapters, definitions, frequently used tables, fuel-gas sections, mechanical sections, and the beginning of each Utah amendment document.

Practice with a timer. Two hours for 60 questions means candidates should avoid spending excessive time on one lookup. If a practice question takes too long, identify why. The problem may be unfamiliar terminology, using the wrong book, not knowing the chapter structure, or overlooking the Utah amendments.
