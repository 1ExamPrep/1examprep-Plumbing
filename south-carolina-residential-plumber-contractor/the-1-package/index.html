Exam Details

The South Carolina Residential Plumbing technical exam is part of the licensing process for applicants seeking a Residential Plumber license through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. The exam is administered through PSI after the applicant has received examination eligibility from the Commission.

The Residential Plumbing technical exam contains 65 multiple-choice questions. Applicants must obtain a passing score of 67.7% on the technical examination. The exam is designed to measure the minimum knowledge needed to perform residential plumbing work for the public in a competent and responsible manner.

The technical exam covers plumbing trade knowledge and code-based subject areas. Students should prepare for questions involving plumbing fixtures, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, cleanouts, piping materials, piping methods, equipment, system testing, definitions, safety, and related residential plumbing installation topics.

South Carolina residential specialty contractor applicants are also generally required to pass the Business Management and Law for Residential Builders examination unless their individual licensing path provides otherwise. The business and law exam is separate from the Residential Plumbing technical exam and focuses on business management, licensing laws, contracts, project management, estimating, financial management, taxes, liens, insurance, bonding, labor responsibilities, environmental rules, and related contractor responsibilities.

This 1 Package supports preparation for both exam areas by including residential plumbing trade references, the South Carolina residential business and law reference, self study course access for business and trade, live classes, and Application Service.

Open Book Test

The South Carolina Residential Plumbing technical exam is an open book test. Candidates may use permitted reference books during the exam. Books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed. Permanent tabs are allowed. Books must not contain loose papers, removable sticky notes, extra attached pages, or unapproved notes.

This package includes highlighted and tabbed rental books to support open-book exam preparation. Highlighting helps students identify important sections, while tabs help students move more quickly to major chapters, topics, and reference areas. Students should still practice using each book before exam day so the layout becomes familiar.

Open book does not mean the exam is simple. Candidates may need to locate a specific code section, interpret a table, understand a definition, apply a rule to a residential plumbing scenario, or choose between similar answer options. A strong preparation plan includes both subject review and repeated practice using the books under exam-style conditions.

Students should become comfortable using the International Plumbing Code, the International Residential Code, OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, and the South Carolina NASCLA residential business law reference. Plumbing system questions may point to the plumbing code. Residential dwelling questions may point to the residential code. Safety questions may point to OSHA. Business and law questions may point to the South Carolina NASCLA guide.

Licensing Steps

South Carolina Residential Plumber applicants must apply through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. Applicants seeking licensure by examination must submit the required application materials, document qualifying experience, and receive approval before scheduling the required examinations through PSI.

Once the Commission approves an applicant for testing, the applicant receives examination eligibility and may schedule the required exam with PSI. Applicants should follow the Commission’s instructions for application forms, supporting documentation, experience affidavits, fees, and exam eligibility requirements.

The standard examination path includes the Residential Plumbing technical exam and the Business Management and Law for Residential Builders exam. The technical exam measures residential plumbing trade knowledge. The business and law exam measures knowledge of contractor business practices, licensing responsibilities, contracts, project management, financial responsibilities, insurance, bonding, liens, taxes, labor requirements, and legal obligations.

Some applicants may qualify for an examination waiver based on specific licenses or credentials recognized by South Carolina. Applicants who believe they may qualify for a waiver should follow the current Commission instructions and submit the required documentation for review.

After passing the required examinations and meeting state licensing requirements, applicants must submit any required final documents and fees to the Commission. Depending on the scope of work and applicable requirements, bonding may be part of the residential licensing process. State Requirements

The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission handles Residential Plumber licensing for residential plumbing work within the Commission’s jurisdiction. Individuals seeking licensure must meet the Commission’s application, experience, and examination requirements before a license can be issued.

Residential plumbing work may involve water supply systems, drainage systems, venting systems, plumbing fixtures, piping, equipment, materials, and related installations in residential structures. Candidates preparing for the license should be ready to demonstrate practical plumbing knowledge and the ability to use approved references during the open-book technical exam.

Applicants pursuing licensure by examination must receive Commission approval before scheduling exams through PSI. The Commission determines eligibility, and PSI administers the examinations. Students should prepare for both the technical trade exam and the business and law exam when both are required for their licensing path.

The licensing process may involve application review, experience verification, examination approval, exam results, final documentation, fees, and bonding requirements where applicable.
