Exam Details

The South Carolina Residential Plumbing technical exam is part of the licensing process for applicants seeking a Residential Plumber license through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. The exam is administered through PSI after the applicant has been approved for examination eligibility by the Commission.

The Residential Plumbing technical exam contains 65 multiple-choice questions. Applicants must obtain a passing score of 67.7% on the technical examination. The exam is designed to measure the minimum knowledge needed to perform residential plumbing work for the public in a competent and responsible manner.

The Residential Plumbing exam covers plumbing trade knowledge and code-based subject areas. Students should prepare for questions related to plumbing fixtures, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, cleanouts, materials, piping methods, equipment, testing, code definitions, safety, and related residential plumbing installation topics.

South Carolina residential specialty contractor applicants are also generally required to pass the Business Management and Law for Residential Builders examination unless their individual licensing path provides otherwise. The business and law exam is separate from the Residential Plumbing technical exam and focuses on contractor business responsibilities, licensing laws, contracts, project management, estimating, financial management, taxes, liens, insurance, bonding, labor requirements, environmental rules, and related business topics.

This online exam prep course focuses on the Residential Plumbing technical exam listed in the product title. Students preparing for the full South Carolina residential licensing process should also prepare for the business and law requirement when it applies to their licensing path.

Open Book Test

The South Carolina Residential Plumbing technical exam is an open book test. Candidates may use permitted reference books during the exam. Books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed. Permanent tabs are allowed. Books must not contain loose papers, removable sticky notes, extra attached pages, or unapproved notes.

Open-book exams require preparation and organization. Simply bringing the books into the exam room is not enough. Students need to understand where information is located, which reference applies to each topic, and how to move efficiently through chapters, indexes, tables, definitions, and code sections.

This course helps students study with an open-book strategy. Plumbing code questions may require the International Plumbing Code. Residential dwelling questions may require the International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings. Safety questions may require OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Learning when to use each reference is a major part of exam preparation.

Students should practice locating common plumbing topics before exam day. Important areas include fixtures, drainage, venting, water supply, materials, system testing, cleanouts, traps, pipe sizing concepts, residential code provisions, and OSHA construction safety requirements. The more familiar students are with the books, the more prepared they can feel when working through exam questions.

Licensing Steps

South Carolina Residential Plumber applicants must apply through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. Applicants seeking licensure by examination must submit the required application materials, document qualifying experience, and receive approval before scheduling the required examination through PSI.

Once the Commission approves an applicant for testing, the applicant receives examination eligibility and may schedule the required exam with PSI. Applicants should follow the Commission’s instructions for application forms, supporting documentation, experience affidavits, fees, and exam eligibility requirements.

The standard examination path includes the Residential Plumbing technical exam and the Business Management and Law for Residential Builders exam. The technical exam measures residential plumbing trade knowledge. The business and law exam measures knowledge of contractor business practices, licensing responsibilities, contracts, project management, financial responsibilities, insurance, bonding, and legal obligations.

Some applicants may qualify for an examination waiver based on specific licenses or credentials recognized by South Carolina. Applicants who believe they may qualify for a waiver should follow the current Commission instructions and submit the required documentation for review.

After passing the required examinations and meeting state licensing requirements, applicants must submit any required final documents and fees to the Commission. Depending on the scope of work and state requirements, a surety bond may be required for residential plumbing work that exceeds the applicable threshold.

The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission handles Residential Plumber licensing for residential plumbing work within the Commission’s jurisdiction. Individuals seeking licensure must meet the Commission’s application, experience, and examination requirements before a license can be issued.

Residential plumbing work may involve water supply systems, drainage systems, venting systems, plumbing fixtures, piping, equipment, materials, and related installations in residential structures. Candidates preparing for the license should be ready to demonstrate practical plumbing knowledge and the ability to use approved references during the open-book technical exam.

Applicants pursuing licensure by examination must receive Commission approval before scheduling exams through PSI. The Commission determines eligibility, and PSI administers the examinations. Students should prepare for both the technical trade exam and the business and law exam when both are required for their licensing path.

Licensing is more than passing a test. The process may involve application review, experience verification, examination approval, exam results, final documentation, fees, and bonding requirements where applicable. A clear study plan helps students prepare for the exam portion while staying aware of the broader licensing path.
