**2) Learn how to confirm instead of reread**
 Many candidates lose time by rereading large sections. A better approach is to confirm only what decides the answer. Train your eyes to look for:

- **Conditions** that define when a rule applies
- **Exceptions** that modify or remove a general rule
- **Definitions** that change what the scenario actually means
- **Requirement language** that signals what must be done

Over time, this becomes a faster, calmer routine: you locate what you need, confirm the deciding detail, and move on.

**3) Build “memory of location” with repeated lookups**
 Open-book exams reward familiarity. You build that familiarity by repeatedly locating common topics. Each lookup strengthens your memory of where similar requirements live. Instead of relying on luck or a slow search, you begin to recognize patterns: where certain types of requirements are likely found, and how the code organizes related rules.

**4) Use a pacing strategy that protects your time**
 One of the most common open-book traps is turning a single question into a long research project. A better method is confirm-and-move: confirm what decides the answer, make your selection, and keep momentum. If you’re doing timed practice, treat pacing as part of the skill you’re training—not something you’ll “figure out on exam day.”
