Exam Details

Idaho’s Plumbing Journeyman exam is administered as a computer-based test at designated testing locations and is completed under a time limit. Key exam details include:

  • Exam: Plumbing Journeyman Exam
  • Time Allowed: 4 hours
  • Passing Score: 75% minimum passing score
  • Exam Fee: $75 (non-refundable)
  • Open Book: Yes (approved references only)
  • Testing Locations: Computer-based testing at Idaho regional offices (Boise, Coeur d’Alene, and Blackfoot)

Because the exam is open book, candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references. Code books are not provided at the test site, and only approved materials are allowed during the exam.

Open Book Test

The Idaho Plumbing Journeyman exam is an open book exam. That’s good news—if you prepare correctly. Open book does not mean you have unlimited time to look everything up. It means you must be able to use your code book efficiently while staying on pace.

Idaho’s exam guidance allows candidates to:

  • Highlight the approved book
  • Tab sections for faster navigation
  • Leave notes in the reference material

However, open-book rules also restrict what you can bring and how your materials can be prepared. For example, loose paper is not allowed in your reference materials, and no other materials are permitted (including alternate versions of the code book such as commentaries, illustrated versions, handbooks, and similar add-ons). The safest approach is to build your system directly into the code book using clean tabs, purposeful highlighting, and consistent organization.

What open-book exams really test:

  • Topic recognition: identifying whether a question relates to definitions, materials, drainage, venting, fixtures, sizing, testing, or installation requirements
  • Code navigation: finding the correct chapter/section quickly without guessing
  • Accuracy under time pressure: confirming the exact requirement (including exceptions) before selecting an answer
  • Pacing: protecting your time by avoiding long searches that break your rhythm

Test Information and Study Materials

Your best results come from treating the UPC as a tool you practice with—just like you will on exam day. Below are practical, exam-aligned ways to study with the 2015 UPC while staying within open-book rules.

  • Learn the structure before you chase details. Start by understanding how the code is organized: how chapters are laid out, how sections are numbered, and where definitions and general requirements live. When you know the “map,” you waste less time searching.
  • Study in “lookup mode,” not “reading mode.” Read a practice question, identify the topic, find the section, confirm the rule, and answer. This builds the exact muscle memory an open-book exam rewards.
  • Tab by major topics. Tabs work best when they point to major categories you return to often (for example, definitions, drainage, venting, fixtures, water distribution, traps, and testing). Avoid over-tabbing; too many tabs can slow you down and make it harder to find what you need.
  • Highlight for speed. Highlight keywords, thresholds, and exceptions—things your eyes should land on quickly. Avoid highlighting whole paragraphs; heavy highlighting makes scanning harder because everything looks important.
  • Practice exceptions intentionally. Many code questions hinge on a small exception or condition. During practice, train yourself to check for “unless,” “except,” “shall not,” and similar language so you don’t miss a key detail.
  • Use timed practice sets. A four-hour exam requires pacing. Do short timed sets multiple times per week so you learn how long a “reasonable lookup” should take before you move on.
  • Review missed questions by learning the location. Don’t just memorize the right answer—learn where the supporting rule is located. Open-book speed improves when your brain remembers “where to find it” rather than “how to memorize it.”
  • Keep your study setup exam-compliant. Since loose paper isn’t allowed, avoid building a study habit that depends on removable notes or insert pages. Practice with the same book setup you’ll bring into the exam.

A simple weekly study rhythm you can follow:

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

  • 2 sessions per week: Topic review + navigation drills (practice finding sections quickly)
  • 2 sessions per week: Timed question sets (mix topics and force yourself to keep pace)
  • 1 session per week: Review misses and confirm the exact supporting language in the UPC

When you practice consistently like this, the code becomes familiar, your lookups become faster, and your confidence increases because the exam environment starts to feel like “just another practice set.”
