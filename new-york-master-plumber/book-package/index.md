## State Requirements

The Master Plumber license discussed here is a **New York City Department of Buildings license**. It applies to regulated plumbing work performed in New York City under the requirements of the New York City Construction Codes and related laws and rules.

Applicants must satisfy one of several experience pathways. One primary pathway requires at least **seven years of total qualifying experience within the ten years before application** involving installation of plumbing systems and the planning or design of plumbing systems under the direct and continuing supervision of a Licensed Master Plumber in the United States. Under that pathway, at least two years of the qualifying experience must be completed as a registered journeyman plumber as required by applicable New York City provisions.

Other pathways are available for candidates with qualifying mechanical engineering or engineering technology education, New York State-licensed Registered Architects or Professional Engineers, certain city-agency plumbing experience, and qualifying plumbing inspection experience.

The Department requires candidates to pass both the written and practical examinations before submitting the background application for the license. The current written examination passing requirement is **70%**.

After successfully completing the examination process, applicants must submit the required licensing documentation and complete the Department's background investigation. The licensing application is submitted through **DOB NOW: Licensing**.

Passing the examinations does not automatically result in license issuance. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable experience, documentation, fitness, character, background, and license application requirements.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong New York Master Plumber study plan should begin with the **2022 NYC Plumbing Code**. Candidates should understand the organization of plumbing systems even though the book cannot be used during the examination.

**Sanitary drainage** should receive substantial attention. Study drainage system layout, fixture units, horizontal and vertical piping, slope, cleanouts, building drains, building sewers, branches, stacks, and connections between system components.

**Venting** is another essential subject. Candidates should understand the purpose of vent systems and how venting protects trap seals and supports proper drainage system operation. Review individual vents, common vents, circuit venting, wet venting, stack venting, vent sizing, and termination principles.

**Water supply and distribution** should be studied from both a design and installation perspective. Review water service, distribution piping, pressure, sizing, valves, backflow protection, water heaters, and relationships between demand and pipe capacity.

**Plumbing fixtures** should include fixture requirements, connections, installation, accessibility considerations, and the relationship between fixture counts and building use.

**Traps, interceptors, and separators** should be reviewed carefully. Candidates should understand their purpose, location, installation, maintenance, and relationship to the drainage system.

**Storm drainage** should also be included in preparation. Review roof drainage concepts, leaders, conductors, storm piping, sizing considerations, and methods used to direct stormwater away from buildings.

The **2022 NYC Fuel Gas Code** should receive dedicated study rather than being treated as a secondary reference. Candidates should understand gas piping materials, sizing concepts, installation, pressure testing, appliance connections, shutoff valves, combustion air, venting, and safe fuel gas practices.

**Combustion air and venting** are particularly important because improper installation can create serious hazards. Candidates should understand how appliances receive adequate combustion air and how combustion products are safely removed.

The **2022 NYC Building Code** should be studied for requirements that interact with plumbing work. Plumbing systems pass through walls, floors, shafts, ceilings, and other building elements, making coordination with broader building requirements necessary.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

The **Administrative Code** should be reviewed for licensing, enforcement, responsibility, and maintenance provisions. A Master Plumber must understand not only how systems are installed but also the professional and regulatory framework surrounding licensed work.

Because the examination is closed book, candidates should avoid relying entirely on passive reading. Use active recall, written practice, system diagrams, and problem-solving exercises to build stronger retained knowledge.

One effective method is to draw a complete plumbing system from memory. Include the water service, distribution lines, fixtures, traps, sanitary drainage, vents, building drain, and building sewer. Then compare the drawing against applicable code requirements.

Do the same for fuel gas systems. Trace the system from the point of supply through distribution piping to appliances and identify the valves, piping considerations, combustion air, and venting requirements that affect safe operation.

Practice should also include calculations and sizing exercises where applicable. Plumbing examinations often require candidates to combine technical rules with tables, system demand, fixture loads, piping relationships, and practical installation knowledge.

Mixed-topic practice is especially useful during the final stage of preparation. Move between drainage, venting, water supply, fuel gas, fixtures, storm drainage, Building Code requirements, and administrative responsibilities rather than studying one subject exclusively.

When a practice question is missed, determine why. The problem may involve an unfamiliar code requirement, incorrect system reasoning, confusion between similar plumbing methods, or weak understanding of a calculation. Review the underlying concept before returning to practice.

The practical examination should also influence study. Candidates should develop a working understanding of plumbing systems rather than memorizing isolated code sentences. A strong Master Plumber should be able to identify a system, understand how its parts interact, and recognize whether an installation follows applicable New York City requirements.
