## State Requirements

The ICC Commercial Plumbing Inspector P2 credential is a **national ICC certification**. It is not tied to a single state licensing program, and earning the credential does not automatically authorize a person to perform official plumbing inspections in every jurisdiction.

States, cities, counties, municipalities, and other authorities having jurisdiction may establish their own requirements for plumbing inspectors. Those requirements may include ICC certification, state certification, trade experience, education, continuing education, registration, or employment by a governmental or approved inspection agency.

Candidates should therefore distinguish between earning the ICC P2 credential and meeting the requirements of the jurisdiction where they intend to work. The ICC examination evaluates commercial plumbing inspection knowledge, while each jurisdiction determines who may exercise official inspection authority.

Commercial Plumbing Inspectors may be responsible for verifying approved materials, pipe sizing, system testing, fixture installations, water heaters, backflow protection, drainage and vent systems, storm drainage, accessibility, and special plumbing systems. The exact scope of official authority depends on the applicable jurisdiction.

## Test Information and Study Materials

The online practice-question product includes **3 months of access**. The access period allows candidates to divide preparation into manageable subjects and revisit difficult plumbing-code topics throughout the study period.

Begin with the **International Plumbing Code** and become familiar with the organization of the major plumbing-system chapters. Practice locating requirements for piping materials, supports, protection, fixtures, water heaters, water distribution, sanitary drainage, vents, traps, storm drainage, and special piping.

For **fixtures**, review material approvals, installation, clearances, access, fixture fittings, flow rates, waste receptors, and related requirements. Accessibility should be studied alongside fixture requirements because a technically correct plumbing fixture installation can still require specific accessible dimensions and clearances.

For **water heaters**, review equipment approval, location, clearances, pans, temperature and pressure relief valves, discharge piping, expansion control, fuel connections, combustion air, and venting. These topics may require candidates to move between the IPC and IFGC.

For **water supply and distribution**, study water-service and distribution piping, pressure, fixture-unit calculations, sizing, shutoff valves, backflow prevention, nonpotable systems, and hot-water requirements.

For **sanitary drainage**, review pipe sizing, slope, drainage fixture units, cleanouts, horizontal and vertical drainage, building drains, building sewers, sumps, ejectors, and related installation rules.

Venting preparation should include vent sizing, vent connections, vent terminals, wet venting where applicable, common vent arrangements, circuit venting, and other approved vent-system configurations.

Traps, interceptors, and separators require familiarity with trap seals, prohibited configurations, grease interceptors, oil separators, and other installations associated with special waste streams.

Storm drainage should also be included in the study plan. Candidates may encounter questions involving roof drains, storm piping, sizing, secondary drainage, conductors, leaders, and system discharge.

Timed practice helps candidates become more efficient. The current national P2 listing identifies 60 questions in a 2-1/2-hour testing period. That means candidates should develop a steady pace while preserving enough time to locate code provisions for more detailed questions.
