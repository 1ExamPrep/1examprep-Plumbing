The California C-36 Plumbing classification covers the installation and maintenance of systems that provide safe water and properly dispose of fluid waste in structures and fixed works. The classification also includes piping, storage tanks, and venting for gases and liquids; water and gas piping from the property owner's side of the utility meter to structures or fixed works; certain gas appliances and connections; water-heating equipment; and plumbing-related health and safety devices.

That broad scope means C-36 exam preparation requires more than familiarity with basic plumbing installation. Exam Details

The California Contractors State License Board divides the Plumbing (C-36) trade examination into five major content areas. The percentages are approximate and can help candidates organize their study time.

  • Planning and Estimating — 22%
    This section includes scope of work and code compliance, pipe sizing and venting requirements, shop drawings, plans and specifications, job-site inspections, project coordination, water conservation, and estimating costs for materials, equipment, labor, and overhead.
  • Underground and Rough Systems — 22%
    Topics include excavation and underground installations, piping layout and installation, fuel-gas piping, insulation, and system testing.
  • Finish Plumbing Installations — 20%
    This area includes plumbing fixtures and appliances, water heaters, shut-off valves, specialty installations, and industrial and commercial piping and sanitary systems.
  • Service, Repair, and Remodel Plumbing Systems — 19%
    Topics include troubleshooting plumbing problems, isolating leaks and damage, repairing plumbing systems, retrofitting plumbing, and disposing of plumbing materials.
  • Safety — 17%
    The safety section covers personnel safety, job-site safety, and environmental safety.

The C-36 trade examination uses a multiple-choice format with four choices for each question and one best answer. Some questions require mathematical computation, and a calculator is provided during the examination.

There is no penalty for guessing, so candidates should attempt each question. Careful reading is important because a question may include details involving piping arrangements, project conditions, troubleshooting symptoms, measurements, or safety hazards that determine which answer is best.

Closed Book Test

The California Plumbing (C-36) trade examination is a closed-book examination. Reference materials cannot be used during the trade test.

This makes active practice especially useful. Technical references can be used while studying, but candidates should also practice recalling information and applying plumbing principles without immediately consulting a book.

When a practice question is missed, review the underlying concept instead of simply memorizing the correct answer. For example, if a question involves venting, review why the vent is needed, how the system functions, and what conditions affect proper installation. This approach can help when the actual examination tests the same concept using different wording.

Closed-book preparation should include repeated review of calculations, installation sequences, troubleshooting procedures, and safety principles. The objective is to become comfortable enough with the material to recognize the best response without depending on a reference during the test.

Licensing Steps

The California Contractors State License Board administers contractor licensing and the C-36 Plumbing classification. Applicants generally move through qualification, application, examination, and license-issuance requirements.

  1. Meet the basic qualifications. An individual must generally be at least 18 years old and have the experience and skills required to manage construction activities, including field supervision, or be represented by a qualifying individual who meets those requirements.
  2. Document qualifying experience. The qualifying individual generally needs at least four years of qualifying experience in the C-36 classification during the 10 years immediately before filing the application. Qualifying experience is generally at journey level or as a foreman, supervising employee, or contractor.
  3. Submit the appropriate CSLB application. Applicants who are required to take examinations complete the applicable contractor license examination application and provide required qualifying experience information.
  4. Complete fingerprint requirements. Applicants who are subject to fingerprinting must complete the applicable background-check process according to CSLB instructions.
  5. Complete the required examinations. Unless an examination waiver applies, the qualifying individual generally must pass the C-36 Plumbing trade examination and the California Law and Business examination.
  6. Complete license-issuance requirements. After the required examination process, applicants must satisfy applicable bond, workers' compensation, asbestos examination, fee, and documentation requirements before an active contractor license can be issued.

State Requirements

California generally requires the qualifying individual to have at least four years of qualifying experience in the classification within the 10 years immediately before the application is filed. The experience must meet the applicable CSLB standards for journey-level or qualifying supervisory work.

Education, apprenticeship training, or technical instruction may qualify for credit toward part of the experience requirement under CSLB rules. Practical trade experience remains part of the qualification process.

Applicants who are required to test generally complete both the C-36 Plumbing trade examination and the California Law and Business examination unless an applicable examination waiver is available.

Fingerprint and background-check requirements also apply when required by CSLB. Applicants receive applicable instructions during application processing.

An active California contractor license requires a contractor bond. The contractor bond amount is $25,000. A separate Bond of Qualifying Individual may also be required depending on the business structure and the relationship of the qualifying individual to the licensed entity.

Applicants must also satisfy applicable workers' compensation requirements. CSLB license-issuance requirements can include proof of workers' compensation insurance, qualifying self-insurance documentation, or an allowable exemption based on the applicant's circumstances.

Applicants may also be required to complete CSLB's asbestos open-book examination before license issuance. This requirement is separate from the C-36 trade examination. The Plumbing trade test itself is closed book.

Reference Books

The following references support study for subjects associated with the California C-36 trade examination. Because the actual C-36 trade test is closed book, these materials are intended for preparation before exam day.

  • 2025 California Building Code, California Code of Regulations, Title 24
    A California building-code reference supporting review of construction requirements and building conditions that can affect plumbing systems, equipment placement, penetrations, coordination, and installation work.
  • 2025 California Green Building Standards Code, California Code of Regulations, Title 24
    A reference supporting study of green-building requirements and water-conservation concepts that can affect plumbing system planning and installation.
  • 2025 California Mechanical Code
    A technical code reference useful for studying mechanical systems and requirements that interface with plumbing, equipment, fuel-gas systems, and related construction work.
  • 2025 California Plumbing Code, California Code of Regulations, Title 24
    A core reference for studying water supply, drainage, venting, fixtures, piping materials, fuel gas, installation requirements, testing, and related plumbing concepts.
  • California Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices, 2014 Edition
    A reference for reviewing work-zone traffic-control concepts and devices that can be relevant when plumbing work affects public streets, roadways, or pedestrian areas.
  • Underground Service Alert Guidelines
    A resource supporting review of underground utility awareness, excavation preparation, and damage-prevention practices for work near buried facilities.
  • Cal/OSHA General Industry & Electrical Safety Orders
    A California safety reference covering workplace hazards, electrical safety, equipment, employee protection, and related occupational safety requirements.
  • Cal/OSHA Construction & Electrical Safety Orders
    A construction safety reference supporting study of excavation, job-site hazards, electrical safety, equipment, access, and worker protection relevant to plumbing operations.

Test Information and Study Materials

For Planning and Estimating, review plans, specifications, pipe sizing, venting, code compliance, site conditions, project coordination, water conservation, quantities, equipment, labor, and overhead. Practice questions can help candidates become more comfortable applying these concepts to project situations rather than studying them only as definitions.

Plumbing plans may require candidates to interpret piping routes, fixture locations, equipment, dimensions, and project requirements. When working through practice questions, pay attention to what information is provided and determine which details affect the answer.

Use the 2025 California Plumbing Code during study to strengthen understanding of water supply, sanitary drainage, venting, piping materials, fixtures, fuel-gas systems, and testing concepts. Since the actual trade examination is closed book, return to practice afterward and test whether you can apply the concepts without the reference.

For Underground and Rough Systems, study excavation, underground installation, rough piping layout, fuel-gas piping, insulation, and testing. Practice should reinforce the sequence of work from preparing a site through installing and testing the system before concealment.

Underground plumbing requires awareness of existing utilities. Review the Underground Service Alert Guidelines as part of preparation for excavation-related topics. Candidates should recognize the importance of identifying buried facilities and coordinating excavation safely before work proceeds.

Some plumbing projects affect roadways or pedestrian areas. Work-zone traffic-control concepts can therefore be relevant when excavation, equipment, or workers are positioned near moving traffic. Use the traffic-control reference to strengthen familiarity with these situations.

For Finish Plumbing Installations, review fixtures, appliances, shut-off valves, water heaters, specialty equipment, and commercial and industrial systems. Questions may involve correct installation procedures, system connections, accessibility, testing, or final operation.

Water heaters are specifically included in the C-36 exam outline. Review water connections, shut-off devices, safety components, installation considerations, and connections to applicable fuel or energy systems.

For Service, Repair, and Remodel Plumbing Systems, concentrate on troubleshooting. Practice questions may describe symptoms and require you to determine the likely cause or the most appropriate diagnostic step rather than simply identify a plumbing component.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

Develop a logical troubleshooting process. Consider what part of the system is affected, whether the problem involves pressure, flow, drainage, venting, leakage, blockage, temperature, or another condition, and then determine which inspection or repair step makes sense.

Remodeling introduces another layer of complexity because new plumbing must often be connected to existing systems and construction. Practice evaluating access, piping routes, fixture changes, system capacity, code considerations, and coordination with other trades.

For Safety, review personnel protection, excavation hazards, electrical hazards, tools, equipment, environmental concerns, and job-site practices. Plumbing work can involve sewage, pressurized systems, hot equipment, fuel gas, chemicals, underground utilities, heavy materials, and confined locations.

Safety questions often require practical judgment. When a question describes a hazardous condition, identify the hazard first and then consider which response best protects workers, occupants, or the public.

The Cal/OSHA references can provide deeper study for California safety requirements. Use them to strengthen understanding of construction hazards while preparing, then return to online practice and test your ability to recognize the appropriate safety response without the books.

Water conservation appears within the current Planning and Estimating examination area. The California Green Building Standards Code can support preparation involving efficient fixtures, system planning, and water-related conservation concepts.

The California Mechanical Code can also support study where plumbing and mechanical systems interact. Larger residential, commercial, and industrial projects frequently require coordination among plumbing, mechanical, structural, and electrical work.

Calculations should be part of regular C-36 preparation. Some examination questions require mathematical computation, so candidates should practice reading problems carefully, identifying the correct units, selecting the required calculation, and confirming that the answer is reasonable.

Use the included three-month access period to work in stages. Begin with broad practice across all five content areas, identify weaker subjects, and then spend additional time studying and practicing those areas. Later in your preparation, mix different topics within the same session so you become comfortable moving between estimating, rough plumbing, finish work, repair, and safety.
