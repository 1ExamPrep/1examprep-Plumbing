About this Plumbing (C-36) Contractor package

Prepare for the California Plumbing (C-36) contractor licensing examination with a focused book reference package covering plumbing systems, underground and rough installations, finish plumbing, service and repair work, building-code requirements, job-site safety, and related technical subjects. This package brings together references that can help candidates organize their studies and strengthen the trade knowledge needed for the California C-36 examination.

The California C-36 Plumbing classification covers the installation and maintenance of systems that provide safe water and properly dispose of fluid waste in structures and fixed works. The classification also includes piping, storage tanks, and venting for gases and liquids; water and gas piping from the property owner's side of the utility meter to a structure or fixed work; certain gas appliances, flues, and gas connections; water-heating equipment; and plumbing-related health and safety devices.

That broad scope means C-36 exam preparation involves much more than installing fixtures or assembling pipe. Candidates should understand planning and estimating, plumbing-code requirements, pipe sizing, venting, underground installations, rough piping, fuel-gas systems, water heaters, fixtures, valves, specialty installations, troubleshooting, repairs, remodeling, environmental considerations, and workplace safety.

This book reference package is designed for candidates who want a structured collection of technical resources to support those subjects. Experienced plumbers may have strong practical knowledge in the work they perform every day while having less exposure to commercial systems, code interpretation, traffic control, underground utility procedures, specialty piping, or formal safety requirements. Organized reference study can help fill those gaps before exam day.

The California Plumbing (C-36) trade examination is a closed-book examination. The books and references in this package are intended for preparation before the test and cannot be used during the C-36 trade examination. Candidates should use them to understand the material thoroughly and then practice recalling and applying important principles without relying on the books.

Exam Details

The California Contractors State License Board study guide for the Plumbing (C-36) examination scheduled on or after March 1, 2025 divides the trade test into five major content areas. The percentages are approximate and provide a useful guide for organizing study time.

  • Planning and Estimating — 22%
    This section includes scope of work and code compliance, pipe sizing and venting requirements, shop drawings, plans and specifications, job-site inspections, project coordination, water conservation, and estimating materials, equipment, labor, and overhead.
  • Underground and Rough Systems — 22%
    Topics include excavation and underground installations, piping layout and installation, fuel-gas piping, insulation, and system testing.
  • Finish Plumbing Installations — 20%
    This portion covers plumbing fixtures and appliances, water heaters, shut-off valves, specialty installations, and industrial and commercial piping and sanitary systems.
  • Service, Repair, and Remodel Plumbing Systems — 19%
    This section includes troubleshooting plumbing problems, isolating leaks and damage, completing repairs, retrofitting plumbing systems, and disposing of plumbing materials.
  • Safety — 17%
    Safety subjects include personnel safety, job-site safety, and environmental safety.

The C-36 trade examination uses a multiple-choice format with four choices for each question and one best answer. Some questions require mathematical computation, and a calculator is provided during the examination. There is no penalty for guessing, so candidates should attempt every question.

Planning and Estimating and Underground and Rough Systems each represent approximately 22% of the examination. Candidates should therefore give substantial attention to plans, code compliance, sizing, calculations, excavation, rough piping, fuel-gas installations, and system testing while maintaining balanced preparation across all five areas.

Closed Book Test

The California Plumbing (C-36) trade examination is a closed-book examination. Reference materials cannot be used during the trade test.

The code books and technical references in this package should therefore be used to build knowledge before exam day. Candidates should become familiar with plumbing concepts, terminology, code relationships, installation procedures, calculations, safety practices, and troubleshooting methods while studying.

A practical closed-book study strategy is to review a topic in the appropriate reference and then explain the important points without looking at the book. For example, after studying a drainage and vent system, review the purpose of the system, layout considerations, sizing concepts, fittings, testing, and common installation issues from memory.

Apply the same method to water supply, fuel gas, water heaters, excavation, and safety. The objective is to understand why a procedure or requirement matters rather than simply memorizing a page number or isolated sentence.

Licensing Steps

The California Contractors State License Board administers contractor licensing and the C-36 Plumbing classification. Applicants generally progress through experience qualification, application processing, examinations, and license-issuance requirements.

  1. Meet the basic licensing qualifications. The qualifying individual must satisfy California's requirements for contractor licensing, including applicable age and experience standards.
  2. Document qualifying experience. The qualifying individual generally needs at least four years of qualifying experience in the classification during the 10 years immediately before filing the application.
  3. Submit the appropriate CSLB application. Applicants who are required to take examinations must complete the applicable contractor license examination application and provide the required information about qualifying experience.
  4. Complete fingerprint requirements. Applicants subject to fingerprinting must follow CSLB instructions for completing the required background-check process.
  5. Complete required examinations. Unless an examination waiver applies, the qualifying individual generally must pass the C-36 Plumbing trade examination and the California Law and Business examination.
  6. Complete license-issuance requirements. After completing the examination process, applicants must satisfy applicable bonding, workers' compensation, asbestos examination, documentation, and other CSLB requirements before an active license can be issued.

The precise licensing process can vary based on business structure, prior California license history, the qualifying individual, and whether an examination waiver or other licensing provision applies.

State Requirements

California generally requires a qualifying individual to document at least four years of qualifying experience in the requested classification during the 10 years immediately before applying. Qualifying experience may include journey-level work or experience in another recognized capacity such as foreman, supervising employee, contractor, or owner-builder.

Qualifying education, apprenticeship training, or technical instruction may receive credit toward part of the experience requirement under CSLB rules. Practical field experience remains an important part of qualification.

Applicants who are required to take examinations generally must pass both the appropriate trade examination and the California Law and Business examination unless an applicable waiver is available.

Fingerprinting and background-check requirements also apply when required by CSLB. Applicants receive instructions for completing these requirements during application processing.

Before an active contractor license can be issued, the applicable contractor bond must be on file. California requires a $25,000 contractor bond. A separate Bond of Qualifying Individual can also be required in certain licensing arrangements.

Applicants must satisfy applicable workers' compensation requirements and any other license-issuance requirements associated with their business structure or qualifying individual.

New contractor license applicants must also complete CSLB's asbestos open-book examination before license issuance if that requirement has not previously been satisfied. The asbestos examination is separate from the C-36 trade examination. The Plumbing trade test itself is closed book.

Test Information and Study Materials

For Planning and Estimating, candidates should review how to establish project scope, interpret plans and specifications, identify applicable code requirements, size piping systems, evaluate venting requirements, inspect job sites, coordinate with other trades, and estimate material, labor, equipment, and overhead.

Code study is particularly important for C-36 preparation. Plumbing contractors work with systems whose performance affects sanitation, potable water, gas distribution, drainage, and occupant safety. Use the California Plumbing Code as a central resource for understanding how these systems are organized and regulated.

Water conservation is another part of the current C-36 examination outline. The California Green Building Standards Code can support study of conservation concepts that affect plumbing fixtures, systems, and construction practices.

For Underground and Rough Systems, review excavation, underground utility awareness, piping layout, installation procedures, supports, fuel-gas piping, insulation, and testing. Candidates should understand how rough systems are planned and installed before walls, floors, or ceilings are closed.

Underground work introduces additional safety and coordination concerns. Review utility locating, excavation planning, protection of existing facilities, trench conditions, access, equipment, and proper job-site controls. The Underground Service Alert Guidelines can help reinforce awareness of buried utilities and damage prevention.

Some plumbing projects can affect streets, sidewalks, or other public areas. Traffic-control knowledge may therefore be useful when workers, equipment, excavation, or materials are positioned near vehicle or pedestrian movement. Review the traffic-control reference with work-zone safety in mind.

For Finish Plumbing Installations, study fixtures, appliances, water heaters, shut-off valves, specialty installations, and commercial or industrial piping systems. Candidates should understand proper installation sequence, connections, accessibility, testing, and final system operation.

Water-heating equipment is an important part of the C-36 classification. Review installation concepts involving water connections, gas or energy interfaces where applicable, temperature control, relief devices, venting considerations, and other safety-related components.

For Service, Repair, and Remodel Plumbing Systems, concentrate on troubleshooting rather than simply replacing components. Review how to identify the source of a problem, isolate a system safely, determine an appropriate repair, retrofit existing plumbing, and restore the system to service.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

Repair questions may require candidates to interpret symptoms rather than being told exactly what has failed. Build the habit of identifying likely causes, checking relevant system components, and selecting a logical diagnostic sequence.

Remodel work can introduce additional challenges because plumbing systems must be adapted to existing construction. Candidates should consider existing pipe locations, building conditions, fixture changes, code compliance, access, system capacity, and coordination with other trades.

For Safety, review personnel protection, job-site hazards, environmental concerns, electrical safety, excavation, tools, equipment, and safe material handling. Plumbers can encounter sharp materials, energized equipment, confined locations, hot surfaces, pressurized systems, chemicals, sewage, gas, and excavation hazards.

The Cal/OSHA references can help candidates study California workplace safety requirements in greater depth. Instead of memorizing isolated language, concentrate on recognizing hazards and understanding the safe response to common construction conditions.

Because the trade examination is closed book, active recall should be part of every study session. Read a section of a reference, close the book, and summarize what you learned. Then work through a practical example involving sizing, layout, installation, troubleshooting, or safety.

Practice calculations regularly. The official C-36 study guide notes that some exam questions require mathematical computation. Candidates should be comfortable identifying the information provided, selecting the correct calculation, tracking units, and determining whether a final answer is reasonable.

A balanced study plan should rotate between planning, rough systems, finish plumbing, service and repair, and safety. This prevents candidates from spending all their preparation time on the plumbing work they already know best while overlooking less familiar areas that still appear on the examination.
