## Licensing Steps

The California Contractors State License Board administers contractor licensing and the C-36 Plumbing classification. Applicants generally progress through experience qualification, application processing, examinations, and license-issuance requirements.

1. **Meet the basic licensing qualifications.** The qualifying individual must satisfy California's requirements for contractor licensing, including applicable age and experience standards.
2. **Document qualifying experience.** The qualifying individual generally needs at least four years of qualifying experience in the classification during the 10 years immediately before filing the application.
3. **Submit the appropriate CSLB application.** Applicants who are required to take examinations must complete the applicable contractor license examination application and provide the required information about qualifying experience.
4. **Complete fingerprint requirements.** Applicants subject to fingerprinting must follow CSLB instructions for completing the required background-check process.
5. **Complete required examinations.** Unless an examination waiver applies, the qualifying individual generally must pass the C-36 Plumbing trade examination and the California Law and Business examination.
6. **Complete license-issuance requirements.** After completing the examination process, applicants must satisfy applicable bonding, workers' compensation, asbestos examination, documentation, and other CSLB requirements before an active license can be issued.

The precise licensing process can vary based on business structure, prior California license history, the qualifying individual, and whether an examination waiver or other licensing provision applies.

## State Requirements

California generally requires a qualifying individual to document at least four years of qualifying experience in the requested classification during the 10 years immediately before applying. Qualifying experience may include journey-level work or experience in another recognized capacity such as foreman, supervising employee, contractor, or owner-builder.

Qualifying education, apprenticeship training, or technical instruction may receive credit toward part of the experience requirement under CSLB rules. Practical field experience remains an important part of qualification.

Applicants who are required to take examinations generally must pass both the appropriate trade examination and the California Law and Business examination unless an applicable waiver is available.

Fingerprinting and background-check requirements also apply when required by CSLB. Applicants receive instructions for completing these requirements during application processing.

Before an active contractor license can be issued, the applicable contractor bond must be on file. California requires a $25,000 contractor bond. A separate Bond of Qualifying Individual can also be required in certain licensing arrangements.

Applicants must satisfy applicable workers' compensation requirements and any other license-issuance requirements associated with their business structure or qualifying individual.

New contractor license applicants must also complete CSLB's asbestos open-book examination before license issuance if that requirement has not previously been satisfied. The asbestos examination is separate from the C-36 trade examination. The Plumbing trade test itself is closed book.

## Test Information and Study Materials

For **Planning and Estimating**, candidates should review how to establish project scope, interpret plans and specifications, identify applicable code requirements, size piping systems, evaluate venting requirements, inspect job sites, coordinate with other trades, and estimate material, labor, equipment, and overhead.

Code study is particularly important for C-36 preparation. Plumbing contractors work with systems whose performance affects sanitation, potable water, gas distribution, drainage, and occupant safety. Use the California Plumbing Code as a central resource for understanding how these systems are organized and regulated.

Water conservation is another part of the current C-36 examination outline. The California Green Building Standards Code can support study of conservation concepts that affect plumbing fixtures, systems, and construction practices.

For **Underground and Rough Systems**, review excavation, underground utility awareness, piping layout, installation procedures, supports, fuel-gas piping, insulation, and testing. Candidates should understand how rough systems are planned and installed before walls, floors, or ceilings are closed.

Underground work introduces additional safety and coordination concerns. Review utility locating, excavation planning, protection of existing facilities, trench conditions, access, equipment, and proper job-site controls. The Underground Service Alert Guidelines can help reinforce awareness of buried utilities and damage prevention.

Some plumbing projects can affect streets, sidewalks, or other public areas. Traffic-control knowledge may therefore be useful when workers, equipment, excavation, or materials are positioned near vehicle or pedestrian movement. Review the traffic-control reference with work-zone safety in mind.

For **Finish Plumbing Installations**, study fixtures, appliances, water heaters, shut-off valves, specialty installations, and commercial or industrial piping systems. Candidates should understand proper installation sequence, connections, accessibility, testing, and final system operation.

Water-heating equipment is an important part of the C-36 classification. Review installation concepts involving water connections, gas or energy interfaces where applicable, temperature control, relief devices, venting considerations, and other safety-related components.

For **Service, Repair, and Remodel Plumbing Systems**, concentrate on troubleshooting rather than simply replacing components. Review how to identify the source of a problem, isolate a system safely, determine an appropriate repair, retrofit existing plumbing, and restore the system to service.
