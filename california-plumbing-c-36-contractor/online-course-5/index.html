Exam Details

The California Plumbing (C-36) Contractor Examination evaluates knowledge of plumbing project planning, estimating, underground systems, rough plumbing, finish installations, repairs, remodeling, and safety.

The CSLB C-36 examination study guide for examinations scheduled on or after March 1, 2025, identifies five major categories:

  • Planning and Estimating — 22%: Project scope, code compliance, pipe sizing, venting, drawings, water conservation, and estimating materials, labor, equipment, and overhead.
  • Underground and Rough Systems — 22%: Excavation, underground installations, piping layout, fuel gas systems, insulation, and testing.
  • Finish Plumbing Installations — 20%: Fixtures, appliances, water heaters, shut-off valves, specialty installations, and commercial piping.
  • Service, Repair, and Remodel Plumbing Systems — 19%: Diagnosing problems, locating leaks, repairing piping, retrofitting systems, and material disposal.
  • Safety — 17%: Personnel protection, job site safety, and environmental safety.

These percentages represent approximate examination weighting. Planning and Estimating and Underground and Rough Systems are the two largest categories, each representing 22%.

The examination uses multiple-choice questions with four answer choices and one best answer. Some questions require calculations, and a calculator is provided during testing. There is no penalty for guessing.

Our C-36 Trade Course follows these examination categories to help students concentrate on relevant plumbing knowledge and practical problem-solving.

Closed Book Test

Both the California C-36 Plumbing Trade Examination and California Law and Business Examination are closed book. Plumbing codebooks, manuals, notes, and other reference materials cannot be used during testing.

The Law and Business examination requires independent knowledge of licensing, contracts, financial management, employment responsibilities, insurance, and workplace safety.

State Requirements

The California Contractors State License Board regulates plumbing contractors under the C-36 specialty contractor classification.

California Code of Regulations, Title 16, Section 832.36 describes plumbing work involving a safe supply of water, proper disposal of fluid waste, and associated piping and equipment.

The classification includes qualifying water supply systems, drainage and waste systems, gas and liquid piping, water heaters, certain gas appliances, backflow prevention devices, regulating valves, and related equipment.

Applicants must satisfy applicable experience, application, examination, and license issuance requirements. Licensed contractors must also comply with relevant plumbing, building, mechanical, environmental, and safety regulations.

Plumbing projects may involve excavation, underground utilities, pressurized piping, sanitary systems, and other hazards requiring appropriate precautions and technical knowledge.

California Law and Business Exam Preparation Included

The California Law and Business Exam Prep Online Course is included with this Ultimate Package.

California plumbing contractors must understand business responsibilities alongside installation and service expertise. Operating a contracting business involves licensing, financial management, employment requirements, insurance, construction contracts, and safety.

The CSLB Law and Business examination study guide identifies seven major categories:

  • Business Organization and Licensing — 13%: Company organization, licensing, advertising, and subcontracting.
  • Business Finances — 15%: Cash management, budgeting, taxes, and financial reporting.
  • Employment Requirements — 20%: Hiring, supervision, training, employee records, and payroll.
  • Insurance and Liens — 12%: Workers' compensation, business insurance, liens, and related remedies.
  • Contract Requirements and Execution — 21%: Bidding, cost control, project organization, contracts, and payments.
  • Public Works — 5%: Prevailing wage, bonding, and insurance requirements.
  • Safety — 14%: Workplace training, reporting, general safety, and hazardous materials.

Contract Requirements and Execution represents the largest approximate category at 21%, followed by Employment Requirements at 20%.

The Law and Business examination is multiple choice and closed book. The included course uses the same four-layer study approach as C-36 plumbing preparation, helping students maintain a consistent study routine.

Contractors must understand water distribution, drainage, venting, pipe sizing, and how plumbing components connect to building systems.

Underground installations introduce excavation, utility location, piping layout, and testing requirements. Fuel gas systems require knowledge of appropriate materials, connections, and safety procedures.

Finish Plumbing, Service, and Safety

Finish plumbing involves installing fixtures, water heaters, appliances, valves, and other equipment used in residential and commercial buildings.

Plumbing service and repair require understanding how to diagnose leaks, evaluate system performance, identify damaged components, and perform appropriate repairs.

Remodeling can involve adapting existing piping layouts to accommodate new fixtures or building configurations.

Safety remains important throughout plumbing work. Excavations, underground utilities, pressurized systems, electrical equipment, and hazardous conditions require appropriate precautions.
