Exam Details

The New Mexico MM-1 Plumbing Contractor examination is part of the state licensing process administered through the New Mexico Construction Industries Division and its testing provider, PSI.

The MM-1 Plumbing examination contains 100 questions. Candidates are allowed approximately 250 minutes to complete the examination. A minimum score of 75 percent is required to pass.

The examination covers plumbing knowledge in several major areas, including:

  • General plumbing knowledge and principles
  • Drainage, waste, and vent systems
  • Water supply systems
  • Fixtures and equipment
  • Backflow prevention
  • Storm drainage
  • Piping materials and installation
  • Liquid waste and sewage disposal topics
  • Code compliance and New Mexico requirements

These subjects require candidates to work between general plumbing codes, New Mexico-specific amendments and regulations, pipe-trade information, mechanical provisions, and other specialized references. Building familiarity with each book is therefore an important part of exam preparation.

Candidates pursuing contractor licensure in New Mexico must also satisfy the state's Business and Law requirement. The Business and Law exam is separate from the MM-1 trade exam and focuses on contractor licensing requirements, business organization, contracts, project management, financial responsibilities, labor and tax subjects, risk management, and related business-law topics.

Open Book Test

The New Mexico MM-1 Plumbing Contractor examination is an open book test. Candidates may use approved references in accordance with the testing provider's examination rules.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before entering the testing room. Permanent tabs may be used to organize the books. Temporary tabs such as removable Post-It-style tabs are not permitted.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity especially important. With 100 questions and several technical books available, candidates need to recognize which reference applies to a question before beginning a detailed search.

For example, a question about plumbing code requirements may direct you toward the Uniform Plumbing Code or the New Mexico Plumbing Code, while a pipe-size or trade-reference question may require IPT’s Pipe Trades Handbook. Questions involving liquid waste may require the New Mexico Liquid Waste Disposal Regulations, while mechanical provisions may involve the Uniform Mechanical Code or New Mexico Mechanical Code.

The highlighted and tabbed preparation of the rental books gives candidates visual landmarks throughout the reference library. Repeated practice using these materials can help make open-book navigation more efficient and predictable.

Licensing Steps

The New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department's Construction Industries Division regulates contractor licensing for plumbing work. The MM-1 classification is the primary plumbing contractor classification within the state's mechanical contractor framework.

  1. Confirm the MM-1 classification. Candidates should ensure the MM-1 Plumbing classification matches the scope of work they intend to perform.
  2. Document qualifying plumbing experience. The qualifying party must meet the experience requirements applicable to the MM-1 classification.
  3. Apply for Qualifying Party approval. Experience documentation and the required application materials must be submitted for review.
  4. Receive approval to test. Candidates must be approved before scheduling the applicable contractor examinations.
  5. Prepare for the MM-1 trade examination. Study the technical subjects and practice navigating the approved references.
  6. Complete the Business and Law requirement. New Mexico contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable Business and Law examination requirement.
  7. Pass the required examinations. Candidates must meet the minimum passing score for the examinations associated with their license classification.
  8. Complete the contractor license application. The business entity must submit the remaining licensing documents required by the Construction Industries Division.
  9. Provide required bond and registration documentation. New Mexico requires contractors to satisfy applicable bond and business-registration requirements.

State Requirements

New Mexico contractor licenses are issued through the Construction Industries Division. The state's system uses a Qualifying Party structure, meaning the contractor business must have an approved individual whose experience and examination qualifications support the requested license classification.

The MM-1 Plumbing classification is associated with professional plumbing work performed within the scope authorized by New Mexico contractor licensing rules. Candidates seeking Qualifying Party status must document the required experience and complete the examination requirements for the classification.

Current New Mexico licensing information identifies a $10,000 contractor license bond as part of the contractor licensing process. The bond must comply with state requirements and remain in effect as required for licensure.

The qualifying business entity must also complete the registrations and licensing documentation required by the state. Workers' compensation compliance applies when required under New Mexico law.

Passing the MM-1 examination does not by itself create a contractor license. Candidates must complete the full state licensing process, including qualifying-party requirements, business documentation, bond requirements, and other applicable approvals.
