Open Book Test

The Washington DC Journeyman Plumber with Gas examination is an open book test. The approved references are the 2012 International Plumbing Code and the 2012 International Fuel Gas Code.

Open book testing rewards familiarity with the references. With 70 questions and four hours available, candidates should avoid beginning every question with a broad search through both books. A better approach is to identify the subject first, select the correct reference, move to the relevant chapter, and then locate the applicable section, table, or definition.

The highlighted and tabbed format helps support that process. Tabs provide recognizable visual locations, while highlighting can make important information easier to identify once the correct section has been reached.

Practice using tables in both codes. Plumbing questions may require fixture-unit calculations, drainage sizing, vent sizing, or water-distribution information. Fuel-gas questions may require candidates to identify the appropriate piping-sizing table and apply information such as system length, demand, or piping conditions.

Candidates should also practice following cross-references. A question may begin in one code section but require consultation of another section, table, exception, or definition before the correct answer becomes clear.

Licensing Steps

Washington DC plumbing and gasfitting licenses are regulated through the District of Columbia Board of Industrial Trades. Candidates pursuing a Journeyman Plumber with Gas credential through the examination pathway must satisfy the applicable experience and documentation requirements.

  1. Accumulate qualifying plumbing and gasfitting experience. Applicants using the examination pathway must document the qualifying work experience required for Journeyman licensure.
  2. Prepare employment verification documentation. Qualifying experience must be supported with employment verification signed by the appropriate master tradesperson.
  3. Prepare supporting tax records. Journeyman applicants using the examination pathway must provide tax records along with experience verification.
  4. Apply through the District licensing system. Submit the required application and supporting documentation through the District of Columbia occupational and professional licensing process.
  5. Establish examination eligibility. Applicants must demonstrate the appropriate amount of qualifying experience before sitting for the examination.
  6. Schedule the examination. Washington DC industrial trades examinations may be scheduled through PSI.
  7. Study both approved references. Use the 2012 International Plumbing Code and 2012 International Fuel Gas Code to prepare across the complete examination outline.
  8. Take the open book examination. Complete the 70-question Journeyman Plumber with Gas exam within the 4-hour time limit.
  9. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Follow the District's licensing instructions after satisfying the applicable examination and application requirements.

The District also recognizes an examination-waiver pathway for individuals who provide acceptable proof of completion of a nationally certified apprenticeship program. Applicants using that pathway must provide the required completion documentation.

State Requirements

Washington DC offers licenses for plumbing, gasfitting, and combined plumbing and gasfitting work through the Board of Industrial Trades.

For a Journeyman applicant using the examination pathway, the District identifies a requirement of 4 years or 8,000 work hours of qualifying experience. Applicants must provide tax records and an employment verification letter documenting that experience.

Before sitting for the examination, applicants must show the appropriate amount of experience. Employment verification letters should document the length of employment and the nature of the work performed and be signed by the appropriate master tradesperson.

Applicants who have completed a nationally certified apprenticeship program may qualify for a waiver of the Journeyman examination when acceptable proof of completion is submitted. Accepted documentation may include a certification of completion, formal designation certificate, or official transcripts as applicable.

The District's current licensing information identifies a certificate of completion and an employment verification letter among the documents used for plumbing and gasfitting applicants.

Test Information and Study Materials

Plumbing Fixtures and Terminology: Review fixture requirements, fixture installation, plumbing terminology, definitions, and common code concepts. Understanding definitions can make it easier to interpret questions that depend on precise plumbing terminology.

Plumbing Fundamentals: This category represents 13% of the examination. Study general plumbing requirements, materials, piping principles, installation methods, protection requirements, and the fundamental rules that apply across multiple plumbing systems.

Traps, Cleanouts, and Interceptors: Review trap seals, prohibited configurations, cleanout placement, accessibility, interceptors, separators, and related requirements. Understand the purpose of these components as well as the code provisions governing installation.

Special Requirements: Study plumbing installations that require specialized provisions beyond ordinary drainage, water distribution, fixture, and venting rules. Recognizing specialized conditions can help candidates locate the proper code section quickly.

Drainage Requirements: Drainage represents 14% of the exam. Review drainage fixture units, horizontal and vertical drainage piping, pipe sizing, slope, fittings, building drains, branches, cleanouts, and applicable tables.

Venting Requirements: Venting also represents 14%. Review vent sizing, vent connections, terminals, individual vents, common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, and other permitted arrangements. Understanding why the vent system is required can help candidates apply code rules to unfamiliar layouts.

Water Distribution: Water Distribution represents 12% of the examination. Study potable water piping, sizing, materials, pressure considerations, valves, connections, backflow-related provisions, and other water-system requirements.

Plan Reading: Plan Reading accounts for 14%. Candidates should practice interpreting plumbing plans, layouts, fixture arrangements, piping routes, and system information while applying applicable code requirements. These questions may combine several plumbing concepts in a single problem.

Gas: Gas represents 20% of the examination, making it the largest individual category. Study the 2012 International Fuel Gas Code carefully, including fuel-gas piping systems, sizing methods, appliance connections, combustion air, venting systems, chimneys, and installation requirements.

For gas-piping sizing questions, candidates should practice choosing the correct table based on the system information provided. Carefully read table headings, notes, and conditions rather than assuming every sizing table is used in the same way.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

Candidates should also distinguish between plumbing venting and fuel-gas venting. These systems serve different purposes and are governed by different provisions. Recognizing the type of venting involved is an important first step in choosing the correct reference.

A useful study routine is to separate preparation into plumbing sessions and gas sessions, then combine the subjects during timed practice sets. This builds familiarity with each book while also developing the ability to switch references quickly.

Timed practice can reveal whether missed questions come from weak trade knowledge, choosing the wrong reference, difficulty finding a code section, or mistakes interpreting a table. Use those results to guide additional study.
