## Exam Details

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors regulates contractor licensing in the state. PSI administers Arizona’s trade-specific contractor examinations. A person applying as the qualifying party for an R-37R license may be required to satisfy the applicable trade examination and experience requirements as part of the licensing process.

The Arizona Plumbing, Residential Contractor trade examination contains 100 questions. Candidates receive 240 minutes to complete the examination, and a score of at least 70 percent is required to pass.

The examination content includes drainage, waste and vent systems; indirect waste; water supply systems; backflow prevention; roof drains and storm drainage; plumbing fixtures; water heaters; accessibility; general regulations; specialty plumbing; interceptors and traps; isometric analysis; fuel gas; septic and sewer systems; fire sprinklers; and excavation.

Exam questions may require a candidate to interpret code provisions, identify the correct material or installation method, use a sizing table, apply a formula, evaluate a piping arrangement, or determine which standard governs a specific situation. Practical trade knowledge remains important because some questions may be based on accepted industry practices in addition to the published references.

Candidates should be prepared to recognize the subject of a question before opening a book. Residential sprinkler questions may require NFPA 13D or NFPA 13R, depending on the occupancy and system described. Excavation safety questions may involve OSHA, while hydronic heating and boiler questions require the specialized technical references.

## Licensing Steps

The first step is to review the Arizona Registrar of Contractors’ license classifications and determine whether the R-37R residential plumbing classification matches the work the business plans to perform. The residential classification should not automatically be treated as authorization for commercial plumbing or work outside its defined scope.

The license applicant must identify a qualifying party. The qualifying party is the person who meets the applicable knowledge, experience, and examination requirements for the requested classification and maintains the required relationship with the licensed business.

The qualifying party must complete the required trade examination unless the Arizona Registrar of Contractors approves an applicable waiver. New license applicants may also be required to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules course and examination. This requirement is separate from the R-37R plumbing trade examination.

The applicant must complete the required background checks and organize the documentation requested for the selected business structure. Arizona licenses may be issued to entities such as sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, and corporations. The information provided in the application should match the business’s legal records.

A contractor bond must be obtained in the amount and form required for the applicant’s license type and circumstances. The applicant must then submit the completed license application, qualifying-party information, examination results, background-check documentation, bond, entity records, and other required supporting materials to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

The agency reviews the application before issuing a license. Missing, inconsistent, or incomplete information may delay the review. Applicants should use the current application forms and instructions available from the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Begin studying by dividing the references into subject groups. Place the International Plumbing Code at the center of drainage, venting, water supply, fixtures, traps, storm drainage, and water-heater review. Use the International Fuel Gas Code for gas piping, appliances, combustion air, and venting. Use the International Mechanical Code for mechanical and hydronic system requirements.

Review the differences among NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. Each standard applies to different sprinkler-system circumstances. Candidates should practice identifying the occupancy and system described in a question before selecting the applicable NFPA book.

Practice drainage and vent calculations, water-pipe sizing, fuel gas sizing, slopes, offsets, areas, volumes, pressure relationships, and isometric interpretation. Mathematics questions are easier to manage when the candidate understands the formula and has practiced finding supporting tables.

Use timed practice sessions with the highlighted and tabbed books. Record how long it takes to locate answers, which index terms work best, and which references remain difficult to navigate. Revisit those books until their organization becomes familiar.
