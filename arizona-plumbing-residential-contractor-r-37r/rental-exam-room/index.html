Exam Details

The Arizona Plumbing, Residential Contractor (R-37R) trade examination is associated with the residential plumbing contractor classification. It evaluates a candidate’s knowledge of plumbing codes, safety standards, technical calculations, installation requirements, and trade practices related to residential work.

The exam library includes several different types of references. The International Plumbing Code supports questions involving water distribution, sanitary drainage, vents, fixtures, traps, indirect waste, storm drainage, and water heaters. The International Fuel Gas Code addresses fuel gas piping, appliances, combustion air, venting, testing, and related installation requirements.

The International Mechanical Code covers mechanical systems, equipment, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, and related installations. NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R address different sprinkler-system applications and residential occupancy conditions. Candidates should understand which standard applies to the scenario described in a question.

The OSHA construction regulations support safety preparation involving excavation, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, material handling, electrical hazards, and jobsite practices. The Arizona onsite wastewater reference addresses state provisions affecting wastewater treatment facilities and related disposal requirements.

Questions may ask candidates to identify the correct code section, choose an approved material, apply a sizing table, interpret a definition, evaluate a piping arrangement, select an installation method, or determine which standard applies to a specific residential condition.

Strong preparation should combine technical review with timed practice. Candidates should learn how the references are organized and practice locating answers without reading entire chapters during the exam.

Open Book Test

The Arizona R-37R trade examination is an open-book test. Approved references may be used during the exam under the testing provider’s current rules and security procedures.

Open-book testing requires candidates to work efficiently. The correct answer may be available in a reference, but limited testing time means candidates cannot search every publication for every question. A productive strategy begins by identifying the subject, selecting the most likely book, choosing an effective index term, and reviewing the surrounding section carefully.

For plumbing questions, candidates should become familiar with the organization of the International Plumbing Code. Common subject areas include drainage sizing, fixture units, venting, cleanouts, traps, water supply, backflow prevention, water heaters, storm drainage, and materials.

Fuel gas preparation should include system sizing, demand, shutoff valves, regulators, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, testing, and installation requirements. Mechanical review may include equipment, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, and related residential systems.

Fire sprinkler questions may require the candidate to distinguish among NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. Understanding the purpose and scope of each standard is essential because they apply to different building and system conditions.

Candidates should also review the current testing bulletin before exam day to confirm the rules for bringing books into the testing room. Book condition, binding, markings, tabs, notes, and loose materials may be regulated.

Licensing Steps

Begin by confirming that the R-37R residential plumbing classification matches the work the applicant or business intends to perform. Arizona maintains residential, commercial, and dual classifications, so the selected license should correspond with the planned scope of contracting work.

The applicant must identify a qualifying party. The qualifying party is responsible for satisfying the applicable experience, knowledge, and examination requirements connected with the requested classification.

The qualifying party must complete the required trade examination unless an approved waiver applies. Arizona applicants may also be required to complete a separate statutes and rules requirement as part of the licensing process.

Applicants must complete the required background-check process and gather the documentation associated with the selected business structure. Arizona contractor licenses may be issued to eligible entities such as sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, and corporations.

The applicant must obtain the required contractor bond and prepare supporting documents such as identification, entity records, qualifying-party information, experience documentation, examination results, bond records, and signed application forms.

The completed application is submitted to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors for review. Passing the trade examination is an important step, but it does not automatically result in license issuance. The applicant must satisfy the full licensing process and receive approval from the state.

State Requirements

Arizona contractor licensing is regulated by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants must meet the requirements associated with the requested classification, qualifying party, experience, examinations or approved waivers, background checks, contractor bond, business documentation, and application review.

The qualifying party should have experience that is relevant to residential plumbing work. Applicants should maintain records describing employment, project responsibilities, supervisory duties, and hands-on trade experience connected with the classification.

The legal business name and ownership information should remain consistent across business records, identification documents, background-check reports, bond paperwork, and the contractor license application. Inconsistent information may delay review or require corrections.

The R-37R classification is intended for residential plumbing work within its defined scope. It should not automatically be treated as authorization for commercial plumbing projects or work outside the classification.

Applicants should use current state forms and instructions when submitting their application. Examination procedures, licensing forms, and administrative requirements may be updated over time.

Test Information and Study Materials

Use the 6 months of course access to build a structured study schedule. Begin with the International Plumbing Code because it supports many of the primary plumbing subjects, including water supply, sanitary drainage, vents, fixtures, traps, water heaters, storm drainage, and indirect waste.

Study the International Fuel Gas Code separately and practice locating information about system demand, pipe sizing, appliance connections, shutoff valves, regulators, combustion air, venting, and testing.

Review the International Mechanical Code for equipment, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, and related mechanical installations. Learn the major chapter structure so mechanical questions can be distinguished from fuel gas or plumbing questions.

Compare the purposes of NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. Practice identifying which sprinkler standard applies based on the type of residential occupancy and system described in a question.

OSHA preparation should include excavation safety, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, fall protection, and safe jobsite procedures. The Arizona wastewater reference should be reviewed for state-specific onsite treatment provisions.

Timed reference-navigation practice is important. Work through practice questions with the actual rental books, track how long it takes to locate answers, and identify which index terms and chapter headings produce the best results.

Do not rely only on locating isolated lines. Read the surrounding text, definitions, exceptions, notes, and table headings. A nearby qualification may change how a provision applies to the question.
