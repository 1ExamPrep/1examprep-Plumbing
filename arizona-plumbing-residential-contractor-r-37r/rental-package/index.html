Exam Details

The Arizona Plumbing, Residential Contractor R-37R examination is intended for candidates pursuing a residential plumbing contractor classification in Arizona. The examination evaluates a candidate’s ability to understand residential plumbing systems, related codes, technical calculations, safety requirements, and accepted trade practices.

The exam may cover drainage, waste and vent systems, indirect waste, water supply, backflow prevention, roof drains, storm drainage, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, accessibility, specialty plumbing, interceptors, traps, isometric analysis, fuel gas, septic and sewer systems, residential fire sprinklers, excavation, hydronic heating, boilers, and pipe-trade mathematics.

Questions may require candidates to locate a code section, interpret a definition, use a sizing table, identify an approved material, select an installation method, calculate a measurement, evaluate a piping layout, or determine which reference applies to a specific situation.

The reference library includes several different types of publications. The international codes address plumbing, fuel gas, and mechanical systems. The NFPA standards focus on sprinkler installations. OSHA regulations address construction safety, while the Arizona-specific materials cover onsite wastewater treatment and underground utility-location practices. The mathematics, boiler, hydronic heating, and pipe-trade books provide additional technical support.

Candidates should study the role of each publication before beginning timed practice. Knowing which book is likely to contain an answer can be just as important as knowing how to search within that book.

Open Book Test

The Arizona R-37R trade examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use approved reference materials during the examination, subject to the current testing rules. Open-book testing requires preparation because the exam library is too large to search effectively without a clear navigation method.

Highlighted and tabbed references can help candidates recognize major sections, tables, formulas, definitions, and commonly reviewed subjects. Tabs provide visual landmarks, while highlighting helps important information stand out during study and practice.

Candidates should become comfortable with the table of contents, index, chapter organization, definitions, appendices, tables, diagrams, and calculation resources in each book. They should also practice identifying the correct reference before searching for the answer.

For example, drainage and vent questions are generally connected with the International Plumbing Code. Fuel gas sizing and combustion-air questions may require the International Fuel Gas Code. Mechanical or hydronic questions may involve the International Mechanical Code or the hydronic heating reference. Residential sprinkler questions may require NFPA 13D or NFPA 13R, depending on the building and system described.

The highlighted and tabbed books should be used regularly throughout the 6-month course access period. Repeated timed practice can help candidates improve familiarity with the books, reduce unnecessary searching, and build a more consistent exam strategy.

Licensing Steps

Begin by reviewing the Arizona residential contractor classifications and confirming that the R-37R plumbing classification matches the work the business intends to perform. The selected classification should reflect the type of residential plumbing work the applicant plans to offer.

The applicant must identify the qualifying party for the license. The qualifying party is responsible for meeting the experience, knowledge, and examination requirements associated with the requested classification.

The qualifying party must complete the required trade examination unless an authorized waiver applies. Arizona applicants may also need to complete the applicable statutes and rules requirement as part of the licensing process.

Applicants must complete the required background-check process and gather the records associated with the selected business structure. Supporting documents may include identification, entity information, qualifying-party records, examination results, experience documentation, and signed application forms.

The applicant must obtain the required contractor bond and submit the completed license application with the required supporting materials. The Arizona Registrar of Contractors reviews the application and determines whether the applicant has satisfied the requirements for license issuance.

Passing the R-37R examination does not automatically issue a contractor license. Examination completion is one part of a broader licensing process that includes experience, background checks, bonding, business documentation, and application review.

State Requirements

Arizona contractor licenses are issued to qualifying business entities. Applicants may operate under an eligible structure such as a sole proprietorship, partnership, limited liability company, or corporation. The legal business information used on the application should match the entity’s official records.

Each license must have an eligible qualifying party. The qualifying party must have relevant experience and satisfy the applicable examination requirements for the requested classification. Experience should relate directly to the residential plumbing work covered by the license.

Applicants should maintain accurate records of employment, project responsibilities, supervisory duties, and hands-on trade experience. The state reviews the submitted information and determines whether the experience meets the classification requirements.

Applicants must also complete the applicable background-check process, obtain the required bond, submit the correct forms, and provide the requested supporting documentation. Incomplete or inconsistent information may delay the review of the application.

The R-37R classification is a residential plumbing classification. It should not be treated as automatic authorization for commercial plumbing work or work outside the defined residential scope.
