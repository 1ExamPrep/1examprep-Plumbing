## Exam Details

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors administers contractor licensing in Arizona, while PSI provides the trade-specific contractor examination program. The individual serving as the qualifying party is generally responsible for satisfying the examination and experience requirements connected with the requested license classification.

The Arizona R-37R Plumbing Residential trade examination contains 100 questions. Candidates are allowed 240 minutes to complete the test and must achieve a minimum score of 70 percent. The examination may include questions based on the listed references as well as general trade knowledge and accepted industry practices.

Major examination areas include drainage, waste and vent systems; indirect waste; water supply systems; backflow prevention; roof drains and storm drainage; plumbing fixtures; water heaters; accessibility; general regulations; specialty plumbing; interceptors and traps; isometric analysis; fuel gas; septic and sewer systems; fire sprinklers; and excavation.

Questions may require candidates to identify code requirements, select materials, interpret tables, calculate system sizes, recognize safe installation procedures, analyze piping arrangements, or determine which reference applies to a particular situation. Candidates should be prepared to move between plumbing, fuel gas, mechanical, fire sprinkler, wastewater, OSHA, boiler, hydronic heating, and pipe-trade resources.

## Licensing Steps

Begin by reviewing the Arizona Registrar of Contractors classification descriptions and confirming that the R-37R residential plumbing classification matches the work the business intends to perform. Arizona issues licenses to business entities, including sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, and corporations. The license belongs to the licensed business rather than personally belonging to the qualifying party.

Select the qualifying party who will support the license application. The qualifying party must meet the applicable experience and examination requirements and must remain associated with the licensed entity in the capacity recognized by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

Complete the required Arizona Statutes and Rules training course and examination when applicable. This requirement is separate from the R-37R trade examination and covers Arizona laws and rules affecting licensed contractors. An approved waiver may apply in limited circumstances, but it must be reviewed through the official state process.

Prepare for and pass the R-37R trade examination unless an authorized examination waiver applies. PSI provides scheduling for Arizona trade examinations after eligibility is established. Candidates may test through an approved testing format offered by PSI and should follow the identification, security, scheduling, and reference-material instructions in the current candidate bulletin.

Complete background checks for the individuals identified by the application requirements. Background-check reports have limited validity periods, so applicants should coordinate this step with the rest of the application process.

Obtain the required contractor bond and assemble the appropriate application documents for the selected business structure. Applicants should also prepare government-issued identification and any entity records, disclosures, experience documentation, financial-protection materials, and supporting forms required for their situation.

Submit the completed contractor license application through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors’ online customer portal or by another method accepted by the agency. Examination results must remain valid when the application is submitted. The Arizona Registrar of Contractors reviews the application and may request corrections or additional documentation before issuing the license.

## Reference Books

- **Code of Federal Regulations – 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**
Covers federal construction-safety requirements involving topics such as personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, electrical hazards, excavation, fall protection, material handling, and jobsite safety.
- **International Fuel Gas Code, 2018**
Addresses fuel gas piping systems, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, installation requirements, sizing methods, inspections, and related residential fuel gas provisions.
- **International Mechanical Code, 2018**
Provides requirements for mechanical systems, equipment installation, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, hydronic systems, appliances, and related mechanical work.
- **International Plumbing Code, 2018**
Covers plumbing fixtures, water distribution, sanitary drainage, vents, traps, storm drainage, indirect waste, water heaters, materials, testing, and other plumbing-system requirements.
- **NFPA 13: Installation of Sprinkler Systems, 2019**
Provides technical requirements for the design and installation of automatic sprinkler systems, including components, spacing, water supplies, piping, testing, and system acceptance.
- **NFPA 13D: Installation of Sprinkler Systems in One- and Two-Family Dwellings and Manufactured Homes, 2019**
Addresses residential sprinkler systems specifically intended for one- and two-family homes and manufactured housing.
- **NFPA 13R: Sprinkler Systems for Residential Occupancies up to and Including Four Stories in Height, 2019**
Covers sprinkler-system requirements for qualifying low-rise residential occupancies, including system components, design considerations, installation, and water-supply provisions.
- **On-site Wastewater Treatment Facility Provisions in Unified Water Quality Permit Rule, Arizona Administrative Code Title 18, Chapter 9, 2006**
Addresses Arizona requirements affecting onsite wastewater treatment facilities, including regulatory provisions connected with wastewater systems and disposal practices.
- **Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition**
Supports pipe-trade calculations involving measurements, areas, volumes, offsets, angles, slopes, conversions, pressure, capacity, and practical plumbing mathematics.
- **IPT’s Pipe Trades Handbook, Robert A. Lee, 2016**
Provides pipe-trade reference information, formulas, tables, fitting data, layout guidance, measurements, materials, and practical information used by plumbers and pipefitters.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong R-37R study plan should combine code navigation, technical review, calculations, and timed practice. Begin by separating the reference materials into major subject groups: plumbing, fuel gas, mechanical, fire sprinklers, wastewater, safety, hydronic heating, boilers, mathematics, pipe trades, and utility-location procedures.

Study drainage, waste, and vent systems by practicing pipe sizing, fixture-unit calculations, vent arrangements, cleanout requirements, slope, materials, testing, and prohibited connections. Review water-distribution subjects such as sizing, pressure, backflow prevention, valves, service piping, water heaters, thermal expansion, and fixture requirements.

Fuel gas preparation should include pipe-sizing methods, appliance demand, pressure, regulators, shutoff valves, sediment traps, combustion air, venting, connectors, clearances, and installation requirements. Mechanical review should address equipment, ventilation, exhaust systems, hydronic components, boilers, and related residential mechanical concepts.

Fire sprinkler study should account for the differences among NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. Candidates should understand the types of occupancies and systems addressed by each standard. Practice locating information involving sprinkler spacing, protection areas, piping, water supplies, system components, installation, inspection, and testing.

For wastewater and excavation topics, review onsite treatment requirements, sewer connections, trenching, underground utilities, soil and excavation hazards, protective systems, access, spoil placement, and safe work practices. OSHA questions may require candidates to recognize employer responsibilities and proper construction-site procedures.

Mathematics and isometric analysis should be practiced repeatedly. Work through measurement conversions, offsets, areas, volumes, slopes, pressure, capacity, pipe lengths, fitting allowances, and diagram interpretation. The goal is to understand the method well enough to complete calculations efficiently without becoming stuck on a single question.

Timed practice is essential. Complete sample questions while using the same references you expect to use during the examination. Track which books are difficult to navigate, which topics produce repeated errors, and which calculations require more practice. Use those results to adjust the study schedule.
