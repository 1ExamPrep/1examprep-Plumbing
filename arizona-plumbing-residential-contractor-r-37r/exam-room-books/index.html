Exam Details

The Arizona Plumbing, Residential Contractor (R-37R) trade examination evaluates knowledge associated with residential plumbing work and related systems. The exam may require candidates to apply code provisions, interpret technical requirements, use tables, recognize installation methods, and identify safe construction practices.

Plumbing subjects can include drainage, waste and vent systems, water distribution, backflow prevention, plumbing fixtures, traps, interceptors, water heaters, storm drainage, indirect waste, materials, testing, and general installation requirements. Candidates may also need to interpret piping layouts and determine how code provisions apply to residential conditions.

Fuel gas questions may involve pipe sizing, appliance connections, shutoff valves, regulators, combustion air, venting, testing, and installation requirements. Mechanical questions may involve ventilation, exhaust systems, equipment, combustion air, appliances, and related mechanical installations.

Fire sprinkler preparation requires candidates to understand the different applications of NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. Each standard addresses a different type of sprinkler-system application or occupancy condition. Identifying the correct standard is an important first step when answering a sprinkler question.

Construction-safety questions may come from 29 CFR Part 1926. Candidates should be prepared to review excavation, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, fall protection, electrical hazards, material handling, and other jobsite requirements.

The Arizona onsite wastewater reference supports questions involving state provisions for onsite wastewater treatment facilities. Candidates should become familiar with the terminology, organization, and regulatory structure used in this publication.

Questions may ask a candidate to identify the correct code section, choose an approved material, apply a sizing table, interpret a definition, recognize an unsafe condition, or determine which standard governs the situation described. Preparation should therefore combine trade knowledge with reference-book navigation.

Open Book Test

The Arizona R-37R trade examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use approved reference materials during the examination, subject to the current testing provider’s rules for permitted books and book preparation.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. The exam library includes multiple detailed codes and technical standards, and searching every book for every answer can consume valuable time. Candidates should learn the purpose of each reference before attempting timed practice.

A practical navigation process begins by identifying the subject of the question. A sanitary drainage question will usually lead to the International Plumbing Code. A fuel gas sizing question will likely require the International Fuel Gas Code. A mechanical ventilation question may belong in the International Mechanical Code, while a residential sprinkler question may require NFPA 13D or NFPA 13R.

After selecting the book, candidates should use the table of contents or index to locate the most relevant chapter or term. Reading the surrounding section is important because exceptions, notes, definitions, and table headings may change how a requirement applies.

Regular practice with the actual exam books can help candidates recognize chapter layouts and useful index terms. Candidates should also practice using tables, diagrams, cross-references, and appendices rather than relying only on highlighted passages or memorized page numbers.

Before testing, review the current examination bulletin for rules concerning binding, tabs, highlighting, handwritten notes, loose papers, inserts, and other book modifications. Only references and preparation methods permitted under the current exam rules should be used in the testing room.

Licensing Steps

Begin by confirming that the R-37R residential plumbing classification matches the work the applicant or business plans to perform. Arizona offers residential, commercial, and dual contractor classifications, and the selected classification should correspond with the intended scope of work.

Identify the qualifying party for the contractor license. The qualifying party is the individual responsible for meeting the applicable experience, knowledge, and examination requirements connected with the requested classification.

Complete the required R-37R trade examination unless an authorized waiver applies. A separate Arizona statutes and rules requirement may also apply as part of the contractor licensing process.

Complete the required background-check process and gather the documents associated with the selected business structure. Arizona contractor licenses may be issued to eligible business entities such as sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, and corporations.

Obtain the required contractor bond and prepare the supporting application records. These may include identification, business formation documents, qualifying-party information, examination results, experience documentation, background-check records, bond information, and signed application forms.

Submit the completed application to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. The agency reviews the materials and determines whether the applicant has met the requirements for the requested license classification.

Passing the trade exam does not automatically issue a contractor license. The applicant must complete the full licensing process and receive approval before performing work that requires the license.

State Requirements

Arizona contractor licensing is administered by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants must satisfy the requirements associated with the requested classification, qualifying party, examinations or authorized waivers, experience, background checks, contractor bonding, business records, and application documentation.

The R-37R classification applies to residential plumbing contracting within the scope assigned to that classification. Candidates should not assume that a residential classification automatically authorizes commercial plumbing work or activities outside the defined scope.

The qualifying party should have experience related to the work covered by the requested license. Applicants should maintain records describing employment, project responsibilities, supervisory duties, and hands-on plumbing experience.

Business names, ownership information, and qualifying-party details should remain consistent across formation documents, identification, background-check records, bond paperwork, and the contractor license application. Inconsistent information may lead to requests for corrections or additional documentation.

Applicants should use the current forms and instructions issued for their application cycle. Examination procedures, administrative forms, and licensing requirements may be updated.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation by assigning each major exam subject to the appropriate book. This prevents unnecessary searching and helps build a consistent navigation method.

Use the International Plumbing Code for water distribution, drainage, waste and vent systems, plumbing fixtures, traps, water heaters, storm drainage, indirect waste, materials, inspections, and testing. Practice finding sizing tables, definitions, and installation requirements.

Use the International Fuel Gas Code for gas piping, system demand, sizing, appliance connections, shutoff valves, regulators, combustion air, venting, and testing. Become familiar with the arrangement of the chapters and technical tables.

Use the International Mechanical Code for ventilation, exhaust, mechanical equipment, appliances, combustion air, and related systems. Practice recognizing when a question belongs in the mechanical code rather than the plumbing or fuel gas code.

Review the scope and purpose of NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. Candidates should identify the occupancy or system described in the question before selecting a sprinkler standard.

Study OSHA requirements involving excavation, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, fall protection, electrical safety, and jobsite hazards. Review the Arizona wastewater publication separately so its terminology and organization become familiar.

Complete timed navigation sessions using all eight books. Select questions from different subjects and record how long it takes to identify the correct reference and locate the supporting section. Revisit the subjects that consistently take the longest.
