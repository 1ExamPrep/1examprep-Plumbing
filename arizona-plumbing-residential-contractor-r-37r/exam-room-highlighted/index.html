Exam Details

The Arizona Plumbing, Residential Contractor R-37R trade examination evaluates knowledge associated with residential plumbing work and related systems. Candidates should be prepared to work with code requirements, technical standards, construction safety regulations, and Arizona-specific wastewater provisions.

Primary subject areas may include drainage, waste and vent systems, water distribution, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, fuel gas piping, mechanical installations, residential fire sprinkler systems, onsite wastewater treatment, excavation, and construction safety.

Questions may require a candidate to locate a code section, interpret a definition, use a table, select an installation method, identify an approved material, evaluate a system arrangement, or determine which reference governs a particular condition.

The reference library includes publications with different purposes. The International Plumbing Code addresses core plumbing systems. The International Fuel Gas Code focuses on fuel gas piping and related equipment. The International Mechanical Code covers mechanical installations and associated systems. The NFPA standards apply to sprinkler-system installation, while OSHA regulations support construction safety and excavation preparation.

The Arizona onsite wastewater reference contains state-specific provisions that may apply to wastewater treatment facilities and disposal systems. Candidates should understand the role of each reference before beginning timed practice.

A strong preparation strategy combines technical knowledge with reference navigation. Candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question first, selecting the most likely book, locating the appropriate chapter or index entry, and reading the surrounding material carefully.

Licensing Steps

Begin by confirming that the Arizona R-37R residential plumbing contractor classification matches the work the applicant or business intends to perform. Arizona offers different classifications for residential, commercial, and broader contracting scopes.

The applicant must identify the qualifying party for the license. The qualifying party is the individual responsible for satisfying the experience, knowledge, and examination requirements associated with the requested classification.

The qualifying party must complete the applicable trade examination unless an authorized waiver applies. Additional licensing education or examination requirements may apply separately from the plumbing trade exam.

Applicants must complete the required background-check process and gather the documents associated with the selected business structure. Arizona contractor licenses may be issued to eligible entities such as sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, and corporations.

The applicant must obtain the required contractor bond and prepare the supporting materials requested for the application. These materials may include identification, entity documents, qualifying-party information, examination records, experience documentation, background-check information, bond records, and signed forms.

The completed application is submitted to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors for review. Passing the trade examination does not automatically issue the license. The applicant must satisfy the full state licensing process and receive approval before performing work that requires the contractor license.

State Requirements

Arizona contractor licensing is administered by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants must meet the requirements associated with the requested classification, qualifying party, experience, examination, background checks, bonding, business records, and application documentation.

The R-37R classification applies to residential plumbing work within the scope assigned to that classification. Candidates should not assume that a residential license automatically authorizes commercial plumbing work or contracting activities outside the defined scope.

The qualifying party should have experience relevant to residential plumbing systems and the work covered by the license. Applicants should maintain organized records describing employment, projects, responsibilities, supervision, and hands-on trade experience.

Business names and ownership information should be consistent across formation documents, identification records, background-check information, bond paperwork, and the contractor license application. Differences between documents may delay the application review.

Applicants should use the current state forms and licensing instructions when preparing an application. Required documents, procedures, and administrative requirements may be updated.

Begin preparation by learning the function of each included reference. The International Plumbing Code should be central to the review of water supply, drainage, vents, plumbing fixtures, traps, storm drainage, indirect waste, materials, and water heaters.

Use the International Fuel Gas Code to review gas piping, sizing, demand, appliance connections, shutoff valves, regulators, combustion air, venting, and testing. Practice distinguishing fuel gas questions from plumbing and mechanical questions.

The International Mechanical Code should be used for subjects involving mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, and associated installations. Learn the major chapter organization so relevant provisions can be located efficiently.

Review the differences between NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. Each standard applies to different sprinkler-system conditions and occupancy types. Candidates should first identify the type of building or system described before selecting a sprinkler reference.

Timed practice should be completed with the highlighted and tabbed books. Select practice questions from different subjects and record how long it takes to identify the correct book and locate the supporting information.
