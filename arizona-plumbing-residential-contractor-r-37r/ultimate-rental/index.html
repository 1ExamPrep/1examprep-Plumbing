Exam Details

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors regulates contractor licensing in Arizona, and PSI administers the trade-specific contractor examinations. The person designated as the qualifying party must satisfy the examination, experience, and other applicable requirements for the requested classification unless an authorized waiver applies.

The Arizona Plumbing, Residential Contractor R-37R trade examination evaluates a candidate’s ability to understand and apply residential plumbing requirements and related trade knowledge. Preparation may involve drainage, waste and vent systems, water distribution, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, fuel gas systems, mechanical systems, onsite wastewater, fire sprinklers, excavation safety, hydronic heating, boilers, pipe-trade mathematics, and technical reference interpretation.

Questions may require candidates to locate code provisions, interpret definitions, use sizing tables, identify permitted materials, review installation requirements, apply mathematical formulas, evaluate piping arrangements, or select the correct standard for a particular residential system. Candidates should be able to distinguish between subjects covered by the plumbing, fuel gas, mechanical, sprinkler, wastewater, OSHA, boiler, and hydronic heating references.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books support this process by making major sections easier to recognize during study. Candidates should still read surrounding provisions, understand exceptions, and practice applying the material. Highlighting and tabs are navigation tools, not substitutes for trade knowledge or consistent preparation.

Open Book Test

The Arizona R-37R trade examination is an open-book test. Open-book testing allows approved reference materials to be used during the examination, but it also requires candidates to manage a large amount of technical information within a limited testing session.

Successful open-book preparation involves learning which book applies to each subject. The International Plumbing Code is a central resource for water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, fixtures, traps, storm drainage, indirect waste, and water heaters. The International Fuel Gas Code supports questions involving gas piping, appliances, combustion air, venting, regulators, and system sizing. The International Mechanical Code addresses mechanical installations and related equipment.

The three NFPA sprinkler standards address different sprinkler-system applications. OSHA regulations support construction safety and excavation review. The Arizona wastewater provisions address onsite treatment requirements, while the remaining books provide mathematics, boiler, pipe-trade, hydronic heating, and safe-digging information.

All rental books in this package are highlighted and tabbed to assist with organized study and faster navigation. Candidates should practice using the prepared references before exam day so that the tabs, chapter layouts, tables, indexes, and highlighted sections become familiar.

Reference materials must comply with PSI’s current examination rules. Candidates should review the current candidate bulletin before testing for requirements concerning acceptable books, binding, tabs, highlighting, markings, notes, and materials permitted in the examination environment.

Licensing Steps

Begin by reviewing the Arizona Registrar of Contractors’ license classifications and confirming that the R-37R residential plumbing classification corresponds with the work the business intends to perform. Arizona maintains separate residential, commercial, and dual classifications, so applicants should select the classification that matches their planned contracting activities.

Identify the qualifying party for the license. The qualifying party must be regularly employed by the applicant and possess the experience, knowledge, and skills required for the classification. The qualifying party may be an owner or another eligible person associated with the applicant business.

Complete the examinations required for the classification. The qualifying party generally must complete the applicable trade examination and Arizona Statutes and Rules course unless an approved waiver applies. Trade examination scheduling is handled through PSI, while the Statutes and Rules requirement follows the process established by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

Complete the required background checks. Individuals listed on the license application must follow the current background-check procedure and provide the requested information. Background checks have a limited validity period, so this step should be coordinated with the rest of the application process.

Obtain the required contractor bond and gather the documents associated with the applicant’s business structure. Supporting materials may include identification, business formation records, qualifying-party information, examination results, background-check information, experience records, bond documentation, and signed application forms.

Submit the completed application and required materials to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Examination requirements must be completed before the licensing application can be accepted. Passing examination results generally must be submitted within the period established by the state and PSI.

Application Service is included with this Ultimate package to assist with organizing the contractor license application. State review and license approval remain under the authority of the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

State Requirements

Arizona contractor licenses are issued to qualifying business entities rather than functioning as personal trade certificates. Applicants may apply under an eligible structure such as a sole proprietorship, partnership, limited liability company, or corporation. The business information shown on the application should remain consistent with the entity’s legal records and supporting documents.

Each license must have a qualifying party who meets the applicable experience, knowledge, skills, and examination requirements. Relevant experience should reflect the type of work permitted under the requested residential plumbing classification. Applicants should maintain clear records of employment, supervisory duties, projects, responsibilities, and hands-on trade experience.

Applicants must also satisfy the applicable examination or waiver requirements, complete required background checks, provide government-issued identification, obtain the required contractor bond, pay the applicable state fees, and submit a complete license application. Residential license applications may also involve requirements connected with Arizona’s residential recovery fund.

The R-37R classification is a residential specialty classification. It should not automatically be treated as authority for commercial plumbing work or work outside the classification’s defined scope. Contractors seeking broader authority should review the commercial and dual license classifications offered by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

Test Information and Study Materials

Use the one-year course access and prepared rental books together. Begin by studying one major subject group at a time, then reinforce the material through reference-navigation practice. Learn the role of each book before attempting timed questions.

Practice locating plumbing-code provisions for drainage, vents, water supply, fixtures, traps, water heaters, storm drainage, and indirect waste. Review fuel gas sizing, appliance connections, combustion air, regulators, shutoff valves, and venting. Compare the applications of NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R so that the correct standard can be selected efficiently.

Mathematics practice should include measurements, conversions, slopes, areas, volumes, offsets, pressure, capacity, and pipe sizing. Candidates should also review excavation safety, underground utility procedures, wastewater provisions, boiler systems, and hydronic heating principles.

Complete timed practice sessions with the highlighted and tabbed books. Track which subjects take the longest to research, which index terms lead to useful sections, and which calculations require additional review. Consistent practice can improve both technical understanding and open-book navigation.
