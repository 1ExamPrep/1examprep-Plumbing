Exam Details

The North Carolina Plumbing Technician examination allows four hours. It is a technical examination focused on plumbing work, code compliance, safety, and the responsibilities associated with the technician qualification.

The examination may address water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, indirect waste, special waste, storm drainage, traps, interceptors, separators, fuel gas systems, residential plumbing, permits, inspections, and licensing laws.

Water-supply preparation should include approved materials, water-service installation, pipe sizing, pressure requirements, valves, fixture supplies, backflow protection, cross-connection control, thermal expansion, protection from freezing, and prevention of contamination.

Drainage study may involve drainage fixture units, horizontal and vertical pipe sizing, minimum slopes, changes in direction, cleanouts, building drains, building sewers, stacks, branch piping, sumps, ejectors, and connections to approved disposal systems.

Venting preparation should address trap-seal protection, individual vents, common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, stack venting, vent stacks, relief vents, air admittance valves, developed length, pipe sizing, and termination requirements.

Fixture questions may involve water closets, lavatories, sinks, bathtubs, showers, laundry fixtures, floor drains, drinking fountains, service sinks, fixture supports, clearances, accessibility, drainage connections, and minimum fixture requirements.

Water-heater subjects may include approved locations, temperature and pressure relief protection, discharge piping, drain pans, shutoff valves, thermal expansion, combustion considerations, venting, clearances, and installation requirements.

Fuel gas preparation may include approved pipe and tubing materials, pipe sizing, pressure limitations, regulators, shutoff valves, appliance connectors, testing, combustion air, chimneys, venting systems, equipment installation, and protection of piping from damage.

Residential plumbing questions may require candidates to identify requirements that apply specifically to one- and two-family dwellings and townhouses. Candidates should understand when the Residential Code controls and how its plumbing provisions relate to the general Plumbing Code and Fuel Gas Code.

Administrative and licensing questions may address permits, inspections, construction documents, approvals, code-official authority, technician supervision, license responsibilities, prohibited conduct, and enforcement procedures.

Open Book Test

The North Carolina Plumbing Technician examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring the approved references assigned to the examination and ensure that each book meets the current testing requirements.

The North Carolina Administrative Code and Policies, Plumbing Code, Fuel Gas Code, Residential Code, and Board’s Laws & Rules are identified for Plumbing Technician examinations. Candidates should prepare with these same editions so the chapter organization, section numbers, tables, definitions, and indexes are familiar before test day.

Original hard-bound, spiral-bound, or ring-bound editions are permitted when they remain in their published condition. Approved loose-leaf codebooks must remain in the original binders supplied by the publisher.

Commercially available, permanently attached index tabs may be used. Highlighting is permitted. Written notes, formulas, calculations, underlining, homemade tabs, unauthorized divider pages, loose sheets, taped inserts, and printed PDF copies are not allowed.

Candidates should inspect every book before the examination. Unauthorized markings or added material may prevent a reference from being accepted at the testing center.

Open-book testing still requires thorough preparation. The four-hour testing period does not provide enough time to read unfamiliar chapters from the beginning. Candidates should practice identifying the subject, selecting the correct reference, locating the governing section, and confirming the answer efficiently.

A sanitary drainage question should direct the candidate to the Plumbing Code. A gas-pipe sizing question belongs in the Fuel Gas Code. A residential water-heater question may require the Residential Code, while a permit or inspection question may require the Administrative Code and Policies.

Questions involving licensing authority, supervision, professional conduct, or technician responsibilities should direct the candidate to the Board’s Laws & Rules. Knowing which book controls each topic can reduce unnecessary searching during the examination.

Practice should include the table of contents, index, definitions, chapter headings, tables, figures, appendices, and cross-references in every book. Candidates should read the complete provision because exceptions, limitations, or related sections may affect the correct answer.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the plumbing technician qualification. Review the available Plumbing Technician Class I and Class II qualifications and choose the category that matches the applicant’s experience and intended work.
  2. Obtain the required reference books. Applicants should have the approved books before submitting the examination application and should be ready to test using those references.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Collect records showing hands-on experience in plumbing installation, maintenance, service, or repair.
  4. Complete the technician application. Provide the requested personal information, employment history, qualification category, and licensing information.
  5. Obtain the required criminal background report. Follow the Board’s current instructions and include the required report with the application.
  6. Submit employer verification forms. Each applicable employer must verify the qualifying plumbing experience using the required forms.
  7. Provide employment records. Include the supporting wage, tax, or employment documentation requested by the Board.
  8. Submit the completed application and examination fee. Ensure that all forms are complete, signed, notarized when required, and supported by the necessary records.
  9. Receive examination approval. The Board must review and approve the application, background report, and experience documentation before the applicant may test.
  10. Schedule the examination. Approved applicants receive instructions for scheduling the computer-based examination with the Board’s testing provider.
  11. Prepare the exam books. Confirm that all references comply with the current rules for binding, commercial tabs, highlighting, and markings.
  12. Complete the Plumbing Technician examination. Follow all testing-center rules involving identification, security, reference materials, and examination conduct.
  13. Complete license issuance requirements. Passing the examination satisfies the testing component, but the applicant must complete all remaining Board requirements before the technician license is issued.

State Requirements

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors regulates Plumbing Technician licensing. The Board evaluates applicants, verifies experience, authorizes examinations, issues licenses, and enforces the laws and rules governing licensed plumbing work.

An applicant for a Plumbing Technician examination must document 3,000 hours of qualifying experience in the installation, maintenance, service, or repair of plumbing systems. The experience must be relevant to the technician qualification requested.

Approved trade education may provide up to 50 percent credit toward the experience requirement. Eligible education must be directly related to the trade and completed through a college, university, community college, or technical institute as part of an applicable certificate or diploma program.

The application must include experience-verification forms completed by the appropriate employers or licensees. Supporting employment records, such as the documentation requested in the application packet, must also be submitted.

Applicants must obtain the nationwide criminal background report required by the Board. The completed application, background report, experience records, and required fees must be received and approved before the applicant is authorized to sit for the examination.

A Plumbing Technician qualification is different from a Plumbing Contractor license. A technician credential does not independently authorize the holder to advertise, bid, contract, or operate a plumbing contracting business.

Technicians perform work within the employment and supervision structure permitted by North Carolina law and Board rules. The licensed contractor or qualifying license holder remains responsible for contracts, permits, supervision, and work performed under the contractor license.

The Board’s technician application includes Plumbing Technician Class I and Plumbing Technician Class II qualifications. Applicants should select the category that matches the type of plumbing experience they can document and the scope under which they will work.

A person who already holds the equivalent contractor license issued by the Board and seeks the corresponding technician license may qualify for an examination exemption under the Board’s current procedures.

State Requirements (continued)

License holders must keep their personal and employment information current and comply with renewal requirements. Changes in the technician’s employment or association with a license holder should be handled according to the Board’s applicable rules and forms.

Test Information and Study Materials

Build a study schedule around water distribution, drainage, venting, fixtures, water heaters, fuel gas, residential plumbing, code administration, and licensing laws. Rotate among these subjects so every reference receives regular review.

Practice code-table questions involving pipe sizing, drainage fixture units, vent sizing, developed length, water demand, and gas demand. Accurate table use is an important part of technical plumbing preparation.

Review definitions in every codebook. Technical terms may have specific code meanings that determine how a requirement applies. Understanding the defined term can make it easier to eliminate incorrect answers.

Use timed navigation exercises. Select a topic, locate it through the index, identify the controlling section, read the full provision, and check for related exceptions or cross-references.

Study the Administrative Code and Policies alongside the technical codes. Plumbing work must satisfy both installation requirements and administrative procedures involving permits, inspections, approvals, and documentation.

Review the Board’s Laws & Rules regularly. Technician candidates should understand the difference between technician and contractor authority, the role of supervision, and the responsibilities of the licensed contractor under whom work is performed.
