Candidates should also practice working with tables, definitions, exceptions, sizing requirements, and cross-references. Plumbing code questions frequently depend on conditions contained in more than one section, so reading enough surrounding material to understand the complete rule is important.

The goal is not to memorize every line of the code. A more effective strategy is to understand the major plumbing principles, know how the International Plumbing Code is organized, and become efficient at locating the specific requirements needed to answer a question.

  1. Review the Master Plumber licensing requirements. Become familiar with the District requirements associated with the Master Plumber classification.

  3. Submit the license application. Complete the applicable application through the District of Columbia occupational and professional licensing system.
  4. Complete the examination process. Washington DC industrial trades examinations are scheduled through PSI.

State Requirements

Plumbers in Washington DC are regulated by the District of Columbia Board of Industrial Trades. The Board oversees plumbing and gasfitting licenses and establishes licensing requirements designed to ensure that individuals working in the trade have appropriate skills and training.

The District's occupational and professional licensing system includes separate plumbing classifications for apprentices, journeymen, masters, and contractors. Candidates seeking the Master Plumber credential must follow the application and examination requirements associated with that classification.

The Board identifies supporting documents for plumbing and gasfitting applicants that can include a certificate of completion and an employment verification letter. Applicants should prepare the required documentation as part of the licensing process.

Washington DC also maintains a reciprocity pathway for qualifying plumbing professionals from Maryland under its current licensing framework. Reciprocity is separate from the standard examination route and depends on the applicant meeting the District's applicable requirements.

For candidates using the examination route, preparation should focus on the exact Master Plumber examination outline. The test places significant emphasis on plan analysis, drainage, venting, water distribution, and fundamental plumbing knowledge, so candidates should connect practical field experience with code-based study.

Test Information and Study Materials

Plumbing Fixtures and Terminology: Review plumbing fixture requirements, common plumbing terminology, fixture connections, installation requirements, and the definitions used throughout the code. Understanding terminology can make it easier to interpret more complex examination questions.

Plumbing Fundamentals: This area represents 13% of the examination. Candidates should review general plumbing principles, code requirements, materials, installation practices, sizing concepts, and the fundamentals that apply across multiple plumbing systems.

Traps, Cleanouts, and Interceptors: Study trap requirements, trap seals, cleanout locations, access requirements, interceptors, separators, and related installation rules. Practice locating the code sections that govern these components and understanding why they are required.

Special Requirements: Review plumbing installations that are subject to additional or specialized code provisions. Although this category represents a smaller portion of the examination, candidates should know how to recognize when a question falls outside standard fixture, drainage, vent, or water-distribution rules.

Drainage Requirements: Drainage represents 19% of the examination and is one of the largest content areas. Review sanitary drainage sizing, horizontal and vertical drainage piping, slope, fittings, connections, cleanouts, fixture-unit concepts, and other drainage requirements contained in the code.

Practice using drainage tables and tracing a drainage-system problem from the fixture through the piping system. Questions may require candidates to interpret several pieces of information before selecting the correct pipe size or installation method.

Vent Requirements: Venting represents 17% of the exam. Review vent sizing, vent connections, vent terminals, individual and common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, and other methods permitted by the code. Candidates should understand both the purpose of venting and the code rules governing acceptable vent configurations.

Water Distribution: This category represents 11% of the examination. Study water supply and distribution piping, sizing, pressure, valves, backflow-related provisions, materials, connections, and other requirements affecting potable water systems.

Plan and Analysis: At 23%, Plan and Analysis is the largest portion of the Master Plumber examination. Candidates should be prepared to interpret plumbing information, work with system layouts, apply code requirements to plans, and perform calculations or sizing tasks based on the information provided.

Plan-analysis questions reward candidates who understand how individual code rules work together within an entire plumbing system. Practice moving from drawings or system descriptions to the appropriate code provisions rather than treating every code section as an isolated rule.

Select a topic from the examination outline, review the relevant code chapters, and then work through questions that require you to locate the controlling provisions. Track how quickly you can find the answer and identify areas where additional practice is needed.
