## State Requirements

Washington DC is a federal district rather than a state, but plumbing licenses are regulated locally through the **District of Columbia Board of Industrial Trades**.

For the **Master Plumber examination pathway**, current District application instructions identify an experience requirement of **8 years, or 16,000 hours, of qualifying work experience**.

Candidates pursuing the license through examination must provide supporting documentation that includes **tax records and an employment verification letter**. The employment verification letter should be signed by the master and detail the length and nature of the qualifying plumbing experience.

The District's current application information also identifies a certificate of completion and employment verification among the documentation used for plumbing and gasfitting applications, depending on the licensing pathway.

Washington DC licenses plumbing and gasfitting separately as well as through combined license categories. Candidates should select the examination that corresponds to the credential they intend to obtain. The **Master Plumber** exam is different from the **Master Plumber with Gas** exam. The latter includes gas questions and uses both the International Plumbing Code and International Fuel Gas Code.

Plumbing and gasfitting licenses in the District currently expire on **March 31 of even-numbered years**. Renewal is handled separately from the initial examination and licensing process.

A Master Plumber license should also be distinguished from a **Plumbing Contractor license**. The individual master credential and the contracting business credential serve different purposes. Washington DC trade contractors must meet separate business and contractor requirements.

Current District contractor requirements include proof of business registration or a certificate of good standing, a designated DC-licensed master tradesperson, and the applicable plumbing and gasfitting contractor bond.

Candidates who intend to use their Master Plumber credential as part of a contracting business should therefore plan for both the individual trade-license requirements and the separate business licensing requirements that apply to the company.

## Test Information and Study Materials

**Plumbing Fixtures and Terminology** account for 7% of the exam. Candidates should understand plumbing terminology, fixture classifications, fixture requirements, installation clearances, fixture connections, and the code provisions governing common plumbing fixtures.

Definitions are important because a code question may depend on the precise meaning of a term. Candidates should become familiar with terminology used throughout the International Plumbing Code instead of relying only on everyday trade usage.

**Plumbing Fundamentals** represent 13%. This section can involve general plumbing principles, system operation, code fundamentals, materials, calculations, and the relationship between different components of a plumbing system.

Candidates should understand how water-supply, drainage, venting, fixture, and trap requirements work together. Master-level questions can involve more than one code concept, making it useful to practice problems that require several steps.

**Traps, Cleanouts, and Interceptors** account for 7%. Review trap requirements, trap seals, prohibited arrangements, cleanout locations, accessibility, interceptors, separators, and the applications where specialized equipment is required.

Questions may require the candidate to identify when a cleanout is required, how it must be located, whether an interceptor is necessary, or what type of trap arrangement complies with the code.

**Special Requirements** represent 3% of the examination. Although this is the smallest category, candidates should still review specialized plumbing provisions because several questions can affect the overall exam result.

**Drainage Requirements** account for **19%**, making this one of the largest sections. Study drainage fixture units, pipe sizing, horizontal and vertical drainage piping, building drains, building sewers, changes in direction, slopes, connections, sanitary drainage, and applicable sizing tables.

Drainage calculations should receive substantial preparation time. Candidates should practice identifying fixture-unit loads and using the appropriate IPC tables to size drainage piping.

**Vent Requirements** account for **17%**. Review individual vents, common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, vent sizing, vent connections, vent terminals, and the relationship between traps, drainage piping, and vent systems.

Vent questions can require careful reading because the correct answer may depend on pipe size, distance, fixture arrangement, drainage configuration, or the type of venting method being used.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

**Water Distribution** represents 11%. Candidates should study water-service piping, distribution systems, fixture supply requirements, pipe sizing, pressure, backflow protection, valves, water heaters, and other components associated with potable-water delivery.

Master Plumber candidates should practice both code lookups and water-distribution calculations. Being able to move efficiently between tables and related code text can save significant examination time.

**Plan and Analysis** represents **23%**, making it the largest section of the examination. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting plumbing plans, identifying system components, understanding piping layouts, evaluating fixture connections, and analyzing plumbing-system design.

Plan-analysis questions can combine several code areas. A plan may require the candidate to calculate fixture units, size drainage piping, determine venting requirements, evaluate water distribution, or identify a code-compliance problem.

A useful study strategy is to practice reading a plumbing layout and then tracing each system independently. First evaluate the sanitary drainage system, then venting, then water distribution, then fixtures and special equipment. This makes complex plans easier to analyze systematically.

The examination provides **four hours for 100 questions**. Candidates should practice pacing themselves so that detailed plan-analysis questions do not consume too much time early in the test.

Repeated use of the **2012 International Plumbing Code** is one of the most important parts of preparation. Candidates should learn where major chapters begin, which tables are frequently used, and how related provisions are cross-referenced throughout the code.

When a practice question is answered incorrectly, identify the reason. Common problems include choosing the wrong table, overlooking an exception, using the wrong fixture-unit value, confusing drainage and vent sizing, misreading a plan, or missing a special requirement.

As preparation progresses, candidates should work toward solving straightforward questions from knowledge while reserving the code book for confirmation, tables, calculations, and less familiar requirements. This approach can make open-book testing more efficient.
