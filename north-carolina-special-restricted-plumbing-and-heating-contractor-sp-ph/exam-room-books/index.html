Exam Details

The North Carolina Special Restricted Plumbing and Heating Contractor (SP-PH) examination is part of the electrical contractor licensing process in North Carolina. Candidates must apply for examination approval through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors before scheduling the exam. The Board instructs applicants to read the Exam Information Handbook, choose the license classification they want to obtain, complete the Application for Examination packet, and submit the required examination application materials.

After the examination application is approved, the candidate receives a Notice of Approval from the Board. This approval includes information needed to schedule and sit for the exam. Candidates then schedule the examination through PSI, the testing provider used for North Carolina electrical contractor examinations. The authorization period and scheduling instructions should be followed carefully so the candidate can test within the approved timeframe.

The SP-PH exam is a special restricted examination. Candidates should prepare by studying the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2020, reviewing North Carolina electrical contracting licensing law and Board rules, and practicing code navigation. The exam may involve questions related to electrical installation requirements, motors, controls, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, equipment identification, and the legal limits of the SP-PH classification.

Because this is a restricted license classification, candidates should not study as though the license authorizes unlimited electrical contracting. The SP-PH classification is tied to electrical wiring or devices related to plumbing, heating, or air conditioning work. Exam preparation should include careful review of the classification scope so candidates understand the type of work covered by the license and the limits that apply.

A strong exam preparation plan should include both code study and state requirement review. Licensing Steps

The licensing path for the North Carolina Special Restricted Plumbing and Heating Contractor (SP-PH) classification begins with the examination approval process. Candidates should first review the examination information published by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Exam Information Handbook includes instructions, application forms, licensing law materials, Board rules, sample questions, and examination procedures.

The candidate must select the correct examination classification and complete the Application for Examination packet. The completed packet must be submitted to the Board with the required examination fee and any supporting documents required by the application instructions. The Board reviews the application and then sends either a Notice of Approval or a returned application identifying items that must be corrected.

Once approved, the candidate receives authorization to schedule the examination. The candidate then follows the PSI scheduling process using the eligibility information provided for the exam. Candidates should schedule within the authorization period provided by the Board and arrive at the examination with the identification and approved materials required by the exam instructions.

After passing the examination, the candidate must complete the license application process. Passing the exam does not automatically issue or activate the license. The applicant must complete the New License Application for the license classification desired, submit the license application with the applicable license fee, and provide the qualified individual information required by the Board.

Once the license is issued and activated, the licensee must follow the Board’s rules for renewal, classification scope, business name requirements, address updates, and other responsibilities that apply to North Carolina electrical contractors. Candidates should understand that the SP-PH license classification is limited and must be used only within the scope allowed by the Board.

State Requirements

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors administers electrical contractor licensing in North Carolina. The Board regulates electrical contracting classifications, examinations, licensing, renewals, and discipline in accordance with North Carolina electrical contracting law.

The Special Restricted Plumbing, Heating, and Air Conditioning electrical contracting license, identified as SP-PH, authorizes the licensee to install, maintain, or repair electric wiring or devices only as an incident to the licensee’s plumbing, heating, or air conditioning business. This classification is a special restricted license, meaning it allows only a limited phase of electrical contracting work. It is not the same as a Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited electrical contracting license.

The SP-PH classification authorizes wiring or devices directly related to plumbing, heating, or air conditioning equipment. The work is restricted to circuits or parts of circuits operating at voltages not exceeding 600 volts phase-to-phase. The work must also remain within the specific limitations established for this license classification. Candidates should study the scope language carefully because it affects both exam preparation and real-world compliance.

The classification scope also addresses isolation from the building wiring system by an overcurrent protective isolating device, equipment identification, enclosure requirements, panelboard-related limitations, and wiring connected to specific plumbing, heating, or air conditioning equipment. These details are important because the SP-PH license does not authorize general building electrical work. It authorizes only the restricted electrical work tied to the licensee’s plumbing, heating, or air conditioning business and within the limits set by the Board.

North Carolina electrical contracting licenses issued by the Board expire one year after issuance. Licensees are responsible for maintaining their license, renewing on time, and following Board requirements. Anyone pursuing the SP-PH classification should review the Board’s current rules, licensing procedures, renewal responsibilities, and classification limits before operating as a licensed contractor.

Important study areas include electrical definitions, conductor use, branch-circuit requirements, feeder concepts, wiring methods, raceways, boxes, equipment installation, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, motors, controllers, and equipment markings. Plumbing, heating, and air conditioning work often includes electrical components that must be installed and serviced according to code, so candidates should spend extra time reviewing the sections that connect directly to equipment wiring and motor loads.

Motor and control requirements deserve careful attention. Heating and air conditioning equipment may involve motors, compressors, fans, pumps, controls, and disconnecting means. Plumbing equipment may involve pumps, controls, and electrical devices connected to water-related systems. Candidates should use the NEC to understand how circuits, conductors, overload protection, disconnects, and equipment grounding apply to this type of work.

Grounding and bonding should also be part of regular study. Equipment connected to plumbing, heating, and air conditioning systems must be installed with proper attention to electrical safety, equipment grounding, bonding continuity, and shock protection. Candidates should know where grounding and bonding requirements are located in the NEC and how to interpret the code sections that apply to equipment and wiring.

Overcurrent protection and disconnecting means are also important for SP-PH preparation. The state classification language refers to overcurrent protective isolating devices and equipment served by those devices. Candidates should understand how the NEC approaches protection, equipment disconnecting means, conductor protection, motor circuits, and equipment identification. These concepts help connect the legal scope of the license with the code requirements that support safe installation and service work.
