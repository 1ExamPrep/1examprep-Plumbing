## State Requirements

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors administers electrical contractor licensing in North Carolina. The Board regulates electrical contractor classifications, examinations, licensing, renewals, and discipline under North Carolina electrical contracting law and Board rules.

The Special Restricted Plumbing, Heating, and Air Conditioning electrical contracting license, identified as SP-PH, authorizes the licensee to install, maintain, or repair electric wiring or devices only as an incident to the licensee’s plumbing, heating, or air conditioning business. This classification is limited in scope and is not the same as a Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited electrical contractor license.

The SP-PH classification authorizes wiring or devices directly related to plumbing, heating, or air conditioning equipment. The work is restricted to circuits or parts of circuits operating at voltages not exceeding 600 volts phase-to-phase. The work must remain within the specific limitations established for this license classification. Candidates should study the scope language carefully because it affects both exam preparation and real-world compliance.

The classification scope also addresses isolation from the building wiring system by an overcurrent protective isolating device, equipment identification, enclosure requirements, panelboard-related limitations, and wiring connected to specific plumbing, heating, or air conditioning equipment. These details are important because the SP-PH license does not authorize general building electrical work. It authorizes only the restricted electrical work tied to the licensee’s plumbing, heating, or air conditioning business and within the limits set by the Board.

North Carolina electrical contracting licenses issued by the Board expire one year after issuance. Licensees are responsible for renewing on time and keeping license information current. Anyone pursuing the SP-PH classification should review Board licensing procedures, renewal responsibilities, classification limits, and contractor requirements before operating as a licensed electrical contractor.
