## Exam Details

The North Carolina Special Restricted Plumbing and Heating Contractor examination contains 40 multiple-choice questions. Candidates have a maximum of three hours to complete the examination.

The examination is administered through PSI after the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors approves the applicant. Candidates must receive examination authorization before scheduling a testing appointment.

The SP-PH examination evaluates technical, legal, and business knowledge associated with electrical work for plumbing, heating, and air conditioning equipment. Candidates should prepare for questions involving motors, compressors, pumps, controllers, branch circuits, feeders, conductors, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, grounding, bonding, disconnecting means, raceways, boxes, licensing law, Board rules, and contractor business practices.

Motor questions may involve full-load current, conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, feeder sizing, controller ratings, disconnect ratings, and the use of NEC motor tables. Candidates should understand that conductor protection, motor overload protection, and branch-circuit protection are separate subjects that may require different calculations.

Air conditioning and refrigeration questions may involve hermetic refrigerant motor-compressors, multimotor equipment, nameplate ratings, minimum circuit ampacity, maximum overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, equipment grounding, and branch-circuit conductor requirements.

Equipment preparation may include furnaces, boilers, heat pumps, condensing units, air handlers, package units, pumps, fans, blowers, refrigeration equipment, circulation systems, water heaters, humidifiers, and related controls.

Feeder and branch-circuit preparation may include connected load calculations, continuous loads, conductor ampacity, correction factors, adjustment factors, terminal temperature limitations, voltage drop, and overcurrent-device selection.

Control-circuit questions may address control transformers, thermostats, relays, contactors, pressure switches, float switches, low-voltage controls, equipment interlocks, overcurrent protection, and wiring that extends beyond the immediate equipment location.

Grounding and bonding preparation should include equipment grounding conductors, bonding of metal enclosures and raceways, effective ground-fault current paths, grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductors, and connections to plumbing, heating, and air conditioning equipment.

Raceway and cable questions may address rigid metal conduit, intermediate metal conduit, electrical metallic tubing, PVC conduit, flexible metal conduit, liquidtight flexible conduit, cable assemblies, support, bends, fittings, physical protection, raceway fill, wet locations, and outdoor installations.

## Open Book Test

The North Carolina Special Restricted Plumbing and Heating Contractor examination is an open-book test. The 2020 National Electrical Code is the only reference from this package allowed in the examination room for the SP-PH classification.

The Electrical Contractors Licensing Law, Board rules, and NASCLA business reference are included for closed-book preparation. Candidates should review these books thoroughly before the examination because licensing and business questions must be answered without consulting them during the test.

The NEC may be brought in an approved softbound, spiral-bound, or hardbound format. Loose-leaf references and incomplete copies are not permitted.

The reference may be highlighted, underlined, and annotated before the examination. Pencil notes must be highlighted, while notes written in pen do not need to be highlighted. Candidates may not write in the reference during the examination session.

Permanent tabs may be used when they comply with current testing requirements. Temporary tabs, removable notes, divider tabs, loose sheets, attached papers, and unauthorized inserts are not allowed.

Open-book testing requires familiarity with the NEC. Candidates should practice using the index, table of contents, definitions, article structure, tables, exceptions, and cross-references so they can locate information efficiently.

A question involving an air conditioning unit may require the equipment nameplate provisions and the NEC article covering air conditioning and refrigeration equipment. A question involving a pump motor may require the general motor provisions and applicable motor tables.

A conductor-sizing question may require the equipment nameplate, an ampacity table, terminal temperature limitations, and correction or adjustment factors. A grounding question may require both the general grounding article and requirements specific to the equipment or wiring method.

Timed code-navigation practice can help candidates use the three-hour examination period effectively. A practical approach is to identify the subject, select the applicable NEC article, locate the correct section or table, and determine whether an exception or special condition applies.

## State Requirements

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors regulates the SP-PH examination and license. The Board establishes applicant qualifications, reviews experience, authorizes examinations, issues licenses, and enforces the Electrical Contractors Licensing Law and related rules.

An applicant for the Special Restricted Plumbing and Heating classification must document at least 3,000 hours of qualifying experience. At least 2,000 hours must be primary experience. The remaining hours may consist of primary experience, secondary experience, or a combination of both.

Applicants may receive credit for qualifying service gained in the plumbing, heating, or air conditioning field when the experience falls within categories recognized by the Board. Experience must be documented according to the examination application requirements.

The applicant must verify that the applicant is primarily engaged in or regularly employed by a lawful plumbing, heating, or air conditioning business in North Carolina and will serve as the listed qualified individual for that business.

The SP-PH license authorizes installation, maintenance, or repair of electrical wiring or devices only as an incident to the license holder’s plumbing, heating, or air conditioning business.

The authorized wiring must be directly related to plumbing, heating, or air conditioning equipment. It is limited to circuits or portions of circuits operating at no more than 600 volts phase to phase.

The equipment wiring must be electrically isolated from the building wiring system by an overcurrent protective device, such as a fused switch or circuit breaker, installed with complete line-side connections by others.

The protective device must be permanently and legibly marked to identify the equipment it serves. It may be located in an enclosure intended solely for the specific equipment or in a building panelboard when the required downstream wiring and disconnecting arrangement have been installed by others.

## State Requirements (continued)

When the protective device is located in a panelboard serving the building wiring system, wiring must be installed by others from the load-side terminals to a junction box, auxiliary gutter, or similar disconnecting means outside the panelboard. That point must be in sight of and within 50 feet of the equipment served.

The electrical work authorized by the SP-PH classification begins at the load-side terminals of the protective isolating device. Line-side building wiring is outside the restricted scope.

Except for qualifying control wiring, the authorized wiring must remain physically adjacent to the plumbing, heating, or air conditioning equipment. Control wiring that receives its power from the equipment may extend beyond that area when the control voltage does not exceed 120 volts to ground and the wiring is protected from overcurrent.

Wiring performed under the SP-PH classification may not be installed in a location classified as hazardous under the National Electrical Code.

The SP-PH license does not authorize general electrical contracting or unrelated electrical installations. All work must remain within the scope established for plumbing, heating, and air conditioning equipment.

Special restricted classifications are not subject to the same project-value limits that apply to regular Limited and Intermediate electrical contractor licenses. The absence of a project-value limit does not expand the type of work authorized.

Passing the examination does not independently authorize electrical contracting. The appropriate license must be issued and active before the individual or business performs or offers to perform work requiring the SP-PH classification.
