## Exam Details

The **ICC G25 National Standard Journeyman Plumber** examination contains **70 multiple-choice questions** and has a **4-hour time limit**.

The examination is divided into seven major content areas:

- **Administration General Regulations – 40%**
- **Plumbing Fixtures – 17%**
- **Water Supply and Distribution – 14%**
- **Sanitary Drainage – 13%**
- **Plumbing Vent Requirements – 7%**
- **Traps, Interceptors, and Separators – 7%**
- **Storm Drainage – 2%Administration General Regulations** is the largest examination category at 40%. This area includes general installation requirements, code definitions and terms, plan reading, testing, and materials. Candidates should devote significant preparation time to these subjects because they account for a substantial portion of the exam and support many other plumbing topics.

**Plumbing Fixtures** represent 17% of the examination. The official content outline includes water heaters, water closets and urinals, and specialty fixtures. Candidates should understand installation requirements, fixture connections, approved materials, clearances, water supply, drainage, and other code provisions affecting plumbing fixtures and equipment.

**Water Supply and Distribution** account for 14%. This category includes service distribution and piping, water supply and pressure, and protection of the potable water supply. Candidates should become comfortable with water-service installation, distribution piping, pressure requirements, sizing, valves, backflow prevention, and protection against contamination.

**Sanitary Drainage** represents 13% of the examination. This category includes building sanitary drainage, sumps and ejectors, and indirect and special waste systems. Preparation should include pipe sizing, drainage fixture units, slope, cleanouts, connections, drainage layouts, and requirements for special waste conditions.

**Plumbing Vent Requirements** represent 7%. Topics include vent connections and grades along with vent terminals. Candidates should understand how vent systems protect trap seals and maintain proper operation of the sanitary drainage system.

**Traps, Interceptors, and Separators** also represent 7%. Preparation should include trap installation as well as interceptors and separators used where particular waste characteristics require additional treatment or separation.

**Storm Drainage** represents 2% of the G25 examination. Although it is a smaller portion of the test, candidates should still review the applicable storm-drainage requirements in the IPC.

The open-book format makes efficient code navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should become familiar enough with the 2018 International Plumbing Code to know where major subjects are located before beginning a detailed search.

Candidates should understand where to locate general regulations, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, indirect and special waste, vents, traps, interceptors, separators, and storm drainage.

A water-pressure or potable-water protection question should direct attention toward water supply and distribution provisions. A question about building drains, drainage fixture units, cleanouts, or ejectors should point toward sanitary drainage requirements. Venting questions should lead toward the venting provisions, while trap or interceptor questions should direct attention to the applicable trap and special waste sections.

Because candidates have four hours for 70 questions, there is more time per question than on many ICC contractor exams. Even so, inefficient searching can consume valuable testing time. Candidates who understand how the IPC is organized can use that time more effectively on questions involving detailed tables, sizing, plan interpretation, or multiple code requirements.

Practice questions can be used as code-navigation exercises. After answering a question, locate the applicable IPC section and review nearby provisions. This helps reinforce both the specific rule and the structure of the plumbing code.

The online practice-question product includes **3 months of access**. Candidates can use the access period to divide preparation into focused plumbing subjects, repeat difficult topics, and develop greater familiarity with the organization of the 2018 IPC.

Begin with **Administration General Regulations** because it represents 40% of the exam. Review general installation requirements, materials, testing, code definitions, terminology, and plan reading. These subjects form the foundation for many plumbing questions and should be studied throughout the preparation period.

**General Installation Requirements** can involve piping support, protection, approved installation methods, system location, workmanship, connections, and other broad requirements that affect plumbing systems. Understanding these general provisions can help candidates solve questions across several code chapters.

**Code Definitions and Terms** should not be overlooked. Plumbing codes use precise definitions for fixtures, piping components, drainage systems, vents, waste types, and other technical concepts. Correctly interpreting the terminology in a question can be essential to identifying the applicable code requirement.

**Plan Reading** is part of the Administration General Regulations category. Candidates should practice identifying fixtures, piping arrangements, pipe sizes, system connections, and other information on plumbing plans. The goal is to connect what appears on the drawing with the appropriate IPC requirement.

**Testing** is another part of this heavily weighted category. Review the plumbing-code provisions associated with testing completed systems and components before systems are placed into service or concealed where applicable.

For **Plumbing Fixtures**, review water heaters, water closets, urinals, specialty fixtures, fixture connections, and installation requirements. Candidates should understand how fixture installation interacts with water supply, drainage, clearances, and other plumbing provisions.

For **Water Supply and Distribution**, study service distribution and piping, available pressure, distribution-system sizing, valves, potable-water protection, backflow prevention, and other requirements affecting safe water delivery.

Water-supply questions may involve several steps. Candidates may need to identify the fixture demand, determine system conditions, locate the applicable table, and interpret the result correctly. Practicing table navigation can improve both accuracy and speed.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

For **Sanitary Drainage**, review building drains, drainage fixture units, pipe sizing, horizontal drainage, stacks, cleanouts, slope, sumps, ejectors, indirect waste, and special waste. Candidates should understand how fixtures discharge into a properly sized and arranged drainage system.

For **Plumbing Vent Requirements**, study vent connections, grades, terminals, sizing principles, and the relationship between venting and trap-seal protection. Candidates should become familiar with acceptable vent arrangements and conditions that affect vent installation.

For **Traps, Interceptors, and Separators**, review required traps, trap installation, trap seals, prohibited arrangements, interceptors, separators, and applications where special devices are required before waste enters the drainage system.

**Storm Drainage** accounts for 2% of the exam. Review the applicable IPC chapter so that questions involving roof drainage and storm-water piping are not overlooked during preparation.

Timed practice can help candidates develop an effective test-day rhythm. Four hours for 70 questions provides approximately three minutes and twenty-six seconds per question on average. Straightforward code questions may take less time, while plan-reading, sizing, or table-based questions may require more.

Use missed questions to guide additional study. If water-supply sizing is difficult, spend more time with the applicable tables. If venting questions cause problems, review vent arrangements and termination requirements. If plan-reading questions take too long, practice identifying system components before opening the code.

The strongest study routine combines technical review with repeated reference navigation. Instead of memorizing only a correct answer, find the supporting code provision and understand why it applies. That process helps build knowledge that can transfer to new questions on the examination.
