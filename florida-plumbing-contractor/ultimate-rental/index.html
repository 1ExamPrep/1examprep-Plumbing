Exam Details

Florida Certified Plumbing Contractor applicants prepare for two principal examination areas: Plumbing Trade Knowledge and Business and Finance.

Florida's Department of Business and Professional Regulation and Construction Industry Licensing Board oversee the contractor certification program.

  • License Classification: Florida Certified Plumbing Contractor
  • Trade Examination: Plumbing Contractor Trade Knowledge
  • Business Examination: Business and Finance
  • Examination Format: Open book
  • Plumbing Trade Delivery: Scheduled paper-and-pencil testing
  • Business and Finance Delivery: Computer-based testing
  • Examination Registration: Professional Testing, Inc.

The Plumbing Trade Knowledge examination involves technical plumbing systems, materials, calculations, construction requirements, and trade applications. Preparation also involves plumbing drawing and piping layout concepts.

The Business and Finance examination addresses the administrative and financial responsibilities of professional contracting, including business operations, accounting, and construction contract knowledge.

This Ultimate package supports preparation for both examination areas through separate online courses, coordinated study references, and additional business flash cards.

Open Book Test

Florida's Plumbing Contractor Trade Knowledge and Business and Finance examinations are open-book tests. Candidates may consult the specific publications authorized for their examination category and scheduled testing period.

Open-book testing requires more than owning the correct references. Candidates must recognize the subject of a question, locate the appropriate publication, identify the relevant technical information, and apply it correctly.

For plumbing questions, candidates may need to research drainage requirements, water distribution, fixture installation, fuel gas piping, or mathematical formulas. Business questions may require reviewing accounting terminology, construction contracts, or contractor responsibilities.

All rental books in this package are highlighted and tabbed to support reference-navigation practice. Candidates can work with the prepared publications while completing their online coursework and attending live classes.

Florida permits highlighting in approved examination publications and permanently attached tabs containing normal chapter or page headings. Removable tabs and unauthorized inserted materials are not permitted.

Only references included on the applicable official approved-reference list may be brought into each examination room. The trade and business examinations have separate reference requirements.

State Requirements

Florida Plumbing Contractors are regulated under Chapter 489 of the Florida Statutes.

The Plumbing Contractor classification includes plumbing installation, maintenance, repair, alteration, extension, and certain design activities when permitted by law.

Authorized activities include sanitary and storm drainage, water supply systems, venting, septic tanks, irrigation, swimming pool piping, and solar water heating.

The classification also covers specified natural gas, liquefied petroleum gas, medical gas, pressure piping, and related installations within the scope established by law.

Contractors must follow applicable construction codes, project specifications, safety requirements, and professional regulations. Certification also involves meeting applicable experience, examination, financial responsibility, insurance, and administrative qualifications.

How does the Plumbing Contractor exam work?

The Plumbing Contractor license comes from the Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB). Pearson VUE gives the exam. The board approves your application first; then you schedule the exam and bring only the references the candidate bulletin allows.

  • The Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board certifies plumbing contractors statewide; Pearson VUE gives the state trade exam.
  • Florida journeyman and master plumber cards are issued by counties, which use Prov or GITS exams.
  • Florida also certifies plumbing inspectors and plans examiners using ICC exams (1P, 2P and 3P).
