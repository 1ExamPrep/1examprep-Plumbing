Exam Details

The Georgia Master Plumber Class I (Restricted) examination evaluates advanced plumbing knowledge, including regulatory responsibilities, system installation, maintenance, repair, technical calculations, and contractor business practices.

Licensing is administered by the Georgia State Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers. PSI delivers the examination through its computer-based testing program.

Georgia Master Plumber Class I Examination Overview:

  • License Classification: Master Plumber Class I (Restricted)
  • Licensing State: Georgia
  • Licensing Authority: State Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers
  • Examination Provider: PSI
  • Examination Format: Computer-based, multiple-choice examination
  • Examination Type: Closed book
  • Scored Questions: 120
  • Unscored Pretest Questions: 24
  • Total Questions Presented: 144
  • Examination Time: Seven hours, divided into two parts
  • Passing Standard: Minimum final scaled score of 70

State Requirements

Georgia Master Plumber Class I licensing is regulated by the State Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers. Applicants must satisfy the experience, examination, documentation, and professional qualification requirements established for this classification.

Experience Requirement: Under Georgia law effective July 1, 2026, applicants generally must complete at least four years of qualifying full-time plumbing experience.

Licensed Journeyman Experience: At least three years of the required experience must be performed while licensed as a Journeyman Plumber. A transitional provision applies to applicants who obtained their Journeyman Plumber license before March 1, 2025, requiring at least one year of licensed journeyman experience within the four-year total.

Supervision: Qualifying experience must be obtained under the supervision of a licensed Master Plumber and must be relevant to the Class I restricted scope.

Documentation: Applicants must submit the required experience verification, professional references, identity documents, and application materials.

Examination: Applicants must pass the approved Master Plumber Class I examination with a minimum score of 70.

Restricted Scope: Georgia Master Plumber Class I licensing covers plumbing in residential single-family dwellings, townhomes, qualifying multifamily dwellings not exceeding three stories, and commercial buildings not exceeding 20,000 square feet in total building area.

Professional Responsibility: Licensed Master Plumbers must operate within their authorized classification and comply with applicable plumbing codes, safety requirements, and professional standards.

Master Plumber Class II is the unrestricted classification. Understanding the difference helps applicants select the appropriate license for their experience and intended work.

Test Information and Study Materials

Master-level plumbing examination preparation involves technical understanding across multiple systems. Candidates benefit from organizing their studying into specific subjects and practicing how to apply information without consulting their books.

Water Supply and Distribution: Review potable water piping, service connections, valves, pressure considerations, system protection, and distribution requirements.

Sanitary Drainage: Study drainage systems, pipe slopes, fittings, traps, cleanouts, sizing, and waste piping installation.

Venting Systems: Review vent arrangements, fixture connections, system protection, and related plumbing provisions.

Fuel Gas Systems: Study gas piping, appliance installations, venting, combustion air, material selection, and fuel gas safety requirements.

Plumbing Mathematics: Practice measurements, fractions, decimals, geometry, slopes, pipe sizing calculations, and unit conversions.

Installation and Repair: Review pipe joining, plumbing fixtures, material selection, system testing, maintenance, and troubleshooting.

Accessibility: Study accessible plumbing fixture requirements, restroom arrangements, clearances, and applicable design standards.

Construction Safety: Review OSHA provisions involving excavation, trenching, scaffolding, ladders, fall protection, and workplace protection.

Business Responsibilities: Study construction contracts, contractor administration, employment responsibilities, and project management principles.

Georgia Amendments: Review how Georgia-specific provisions affect the requirements established by the International Plumbing Code and International Fuel Gas Code.

For closed-book preparation, a useful method is to study each topic, close the reference, and explain the relevant principle in your own words. Follow with calculations or practical scenarios that require applying the material from memory.

Repeated review of difficult subjects can strengthen knowledge retention and identify areas requiring additional study.
