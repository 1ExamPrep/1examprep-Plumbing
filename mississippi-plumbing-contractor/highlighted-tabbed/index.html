Exam Details

The Mississippi Plumbing contractor examination evaluates knowledge of plumbing systems, installation practices, code navigation, construction safety, and trade mathematics. Candidates should combine practical field experience with repeated use of the listed references.

The International Plumbing Code, 2021 Edition, is the primary technical code included in this package. It addresses plumbing fixtures, water-distribution systems, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, interceptors, piping materials, joints, testing, inspections, storm drainage, and related installation requirements.

Water-distribution preparation may involve pipe sizing, water demand, pressure, service piping, distribution piping, valves, backflow protection, materials, supports, joints, and testing. Candidates may need to interpret code tables or determine which provision applies to a specific installation.

Sanitary-drainage preparation may involve fixture units, pipe sizing, slopes, branches, stacks, building drains, building sewers, cleanouts, materials, connections, and testing requirements. Questions may require direct reference lookup or the application of practical plumbing knowledge.

Venting subjects may include vent sizing, developed length, vent connections, wet venting, common venting, circuit venting, stack venting, vent terminals, and prohibited arrangements. Candidates should understand how venting supports proper drainage and protects fixture trap seals.

Fixture-related questions may involve required quantities, installation, clearances, supports, accessibility, water-supply connections, drainage connections, materials, and testing. Candidates may also encounter questions involving traps, interceptors, indirect waste, special plumbing systems, or storm drainage.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1926, supports preparation involving federal construction-safety requirements. Plumbing contractors may need to review excavation, trench protection, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, personal protective equipment, tools, equipment, material handling, electrical hazards, and other workplace conditions.

Plumbing work can involve trenches, elevated work areas, heavy pipe, power tools, temporary electrical systems, lifting equipment, and interaction with other trades. Candidates should connect the written safety provisions with the practical conditions encountered on plumbing projects.

Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition, supports preparation involving measurements, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, geometry, areas, volumes, pressures, pipe calculations, offsets, conversions, and practical trade applications.

Open Book Test

The Mississippi Plumbing contractor examination is open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted references and following current testing rules for approved titles, editions, highlighting, tabs, markings, notes, and supplemental materials.

An open-book examination still requires focused preparation. Access to the correct references does not help when a candidate cannot identify the proper book, locate an effective index term, interpret a code table, recognize an exception, or complete a calculation within the available time.

The references in this package are professionally highlighted and tabbed to support efficient navigation. Highlighting identifies important provisions, formulas, tables, definitions, and safety subjects. Tabs organize major chapters and frequently reviewed topics.

Candidates should study every prepared tab before examination day. Learn what information is located behind each tab, review the surrounding material, and practice moving between the table of contents, index, chapter headings, definitions, tables, and technical illustrations.

Questions involving fixtures, water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, piping materials, testing, or storm drainage may lead to the International Plumbing Code. Candidates should practice locating information by chapter, table, fixture type, piping system, definition, and index term.

Construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926. Candidates should become familiar with the sections addressing excavation, protective systems, access and egress, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, tools, equipment, and material handling.

Calculation questions may require Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters. Candidates should practice fractions, decimals, ratios, conversions, geometry, pipe measurements, offsets, areas, volumes, and pressure-related calculations.

Practice using related search terms because the wording used in a test question may differ from the terminology found in a book. A drainage question may require searches involving fixture units, stacks, branches, building drains, cleanouts, slopes, or building sewers.

A water-supply question may require searches involving water demand, pressure, service piping, distribution piping, backflow protection, valves, or fixture supply. A venting question may require searches involving trap seals, developed length, wet venting, stack vents, or vent terminals.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the appropriate classification. Determine that the Mississippi Plumbing classification matches the plumbing services the business intends to provide.
  2. Select the qualifying party. Identify the person who will qualify the business and complete the required examinations.
  3. Document relevant experience. Organize project information involving water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, fixtures, piping, installation, supervision, or project management.
  4. Choose the applicant structure. Determine whether the application will be submitted by an individual, corporation, limited liability company, partnership, or another accepted business entity.
  5. Prepare the contractor application. Gather the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, experience, financial, insurance, reference, and supporting information.
  6. Submit the application. Send the completed filing to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for review.
  7. Respond to application requests. Provide missing documents, corrections, explanations, or additional information requested during the review process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Follow the examination registration instructions after authorization to test is issued.
  9. Schedule the required examinations. Select an available examination date and approved testing location.
  10. Prepare with the reference books. Review plumbing code, construction safety, trade mathematics, and open-book navigation.
  11. Complete timed practice. Work through plumbing questions, calculations, and book-lookup exercises under structured conditions.
  12. Prepare for Business and Law. Obtain the separate Mississippi Business and Law reference and complete the required preparation.
  13. Take the examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the references and materials permitted under current testing rules.
  14. Complete outstanding requirements. Submit any remaining documents or corrections requested during final application review.
  15. Receive final approval. Passing the examinations does not automatically issue a contractor license. The application and supporting documentation must also be approved.
  16. Maintain the license. After issuance, contractors are responsible for renewals, insurance, business records, taxes, permits, safety obligations, and ongoing compliance.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the applicable contractor licensing process. Plumbing is a tested classification involving installation, maintenance, and repair of plumbing systems within the approved scope.

Applicants should select the classification that accurately reflects the services their business intends to perform. The qualifying party must have a relationship with the applicant that satisfies the applicable licensing requirements.

The contractor application may require business-registration records, ownership information, qualifying-party documentation, detailed project experience, references, financial information, insurance records, and other supporting materials.

Application details should be complete and consistent across business filings, ownership records, experience documentation, and supporting materials. Missing or conflicting information can affect the review process.

Passing the required examinations is one part of licensing. Applicants must also satisfy the application, experience, documentation, financial, insurance, and final approval requirements.

Plumbing contractors may also be responsible for local permits, inspections, adopted plumbing codes, utility requirements, employee safety, tax registrations, insurance, and project-specific approvals.

Local plumbing requirements may vary by project location. Contractors should confirm the applicable permitting, inspection, and code requirements before beginning regulated work.
