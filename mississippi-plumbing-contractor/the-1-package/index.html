Exam Details

The Mississippi Plumbing contractor examination is intended to evaluate the candidate’s understanding of plumbing systems, installation requirements, code navigation, safety, calculations, and practical trade knowledge. Candidates should approach the examination with both field experience and familiarity with the listed references.

The International Plumbing Code, 2021 Edition, is the principal technical reference included in this package. It addresses plumbing fixtures, water-distribution systems, sanitary drainage, indirect waste, venting, traps, interceptors, piping materials, joints, testing, inspections, storm drainage, and other plumbing-system requirements.

Plumbing code questions may require candidates to identify an approved material, determine a required pipe size, locate a fixture requirement, review a drainage provision, calculate water-supply demand, or interpret a venting rule. Some questions may involve direct code lookup, while others may require the candidate to apply practical plumbing knowledge.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1926, supports preparation involving federal construction-safety requirements. Plumbing contractors may need to review excavation, trenches, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, tools, equipment, material handling, electrical hazards, fall protection, and other workplace responsibilities.

Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters supports preparation involving measurements, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, geometry, pipe calculations, offsets, volumes, areas, pressures, conversions, and practical trade mathematics. Plumbing candidates should be prepared to complete calculations accurately and efficiently.

The NASCLA Mississippi Business and Law guide supports preparation for the contractor business portion of the licensing process. Subjects may include licensing, business organization, estimating, bidding, contracts, project management, financial management, taxes, labor responsibilities, insurance, safety, risk management, and contractor operations.

The plumbing trade examination and the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination serve different purposes. The trade examination focuses on plumbing knowledge and code application, while the business examination addresses the legal, financial, and operational responsibilities of running a contracting company.

Candidates should prepare for both technical and administrative responsibilities. A plumbing contractor may need to understand installation requirements while also managing contracts, employees, insurance, project documentation, scheduling, purchasing, and business records.

Open Book Test

Mississippi contractor examinations administered through PSI are open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted references and following the current rules for approved titles, editions, highlighting, tabs, markings, notes, and supplemental materials.

An open-book examination still requires extensive preparation. Access to the correct books does not help when a candidate cannot identify the relevant reference, locate an effective index term, interpret a code provision, or complete a calculation within the available testing time.

The included rental books are professionally highlighted and tabbed to support more efficient navigation. Highlighting identifies important code sections, safety requirements, formulas, definitions, and business-law topics. Tabs divide the books into major chapters and frequently reviewed subjects.

Candidates should review every prepared tab before test day. Learn what each tab identifies, study the surrounding material, and practice moving between the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, tables, and technical illustrations.

Questions involving fixtures, water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, piping materials, testing, or storm drainage may lead to the International Plumbing Code. Candidates should practice locating information by chapter, definition, table, fixture type, piping system, and index term.

Construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926. Candidates should understand where to find information related to excavation, protective systems, personal protective equipment, ladders, tools, material handling, and other jobsite conditions.

Mathematical questions may require the plumbing and pipefitting mathematics reference. Candidates should practice fractions, decimals, conversions, geometry, volumes, pipe measurements, offsets, and other calculations associated with plumbing work.

Business and law questions may require the NASCLA Mississippi guide. Candidates should become familiar with sections involving licensing, contracts, project management, financial management, employment, insurance, taxes, safety, and risk management.

Practice using related search terms because the wording of a question may differ from the terminology used in the book. A drainage question may require searches involving building drains, branches, stacks, fixture units, slopes, cleanouts, or sanitary systems.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the appropriate classification. Determine that the Mississippi Plumbing classification matches the services the business intends to provide.
  2. Select the qualifying party. Identify the owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee who will qualify the business and complete the required examinations.
  3. Choose the business structure. Decide whether the company will operate as an LLC, corporation, or another accepted business type.
  4. Form the business entity. Establish the LLC or corporation and organize its official ownership, registration, and company records.
  5. Obtain the EIN. File for the Employer Identification Number used for business banking, tax administration, employee hiring, and professional operations.
  6. Document plumbing experience. Organize detailed project information involving plumbing installation, water distribution, drainage, venting, fixtures, piping, supervision, or project management.
  7. Prepare the contractor application. Gather the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, experience, financial, insurance, reference, and supporting information.
  8. Submit the application. Send the completed filing to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for review.
  9. Respond to application requests. Provide missing records, corrections, explanations, or additional information requested during the review process.
  10. Receive examination eligibility. After the qualifying party is approved to test, follow the examination registration and scheduling instructions.
  11. Prepare with the course and books. Review plumbing code, safety, trade mathematics, business law, and reference navigation.
  12. Schedule the required examinations. Select an available examination date and authorized testing location.
  13. Take the examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the references and materials permitted under current testing rules.
  14. Complete outstanding requirements. Submit any remaining documents or corrections requested during final application review.
  15. Receive licensing approval. Passing the examinations does not automatically issue a contractor license. The complete application and supporting documentation must also be approved.
  16. Maintain the license and business. Track renewals, insurance, business records, taxes, permits, safety obligations, and ongoing contractor compliance.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the applicable contractor licensing process. Applicants should select the classification that accurately reflects the plumbing work their business plans to perform.

The qualifying party must have a relationship with the applicant that meets the Board’s requirements. Depending on the business structure, the qualifying party may be an owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee.

The contractor application may require business-registration information, ownership records, qualifying-party documentation, detailed project experience, references, financial information, insurance records, and other supporting materials.

Application information should be accurate and consistent across business filings, ownership records, experience documents, and supporting materials. Missing or conflicting information can affect the application review process.

Mississippi contractor applicants must complete the applicable examination requirements. The plumbing trade examination evaluates technical knowledge, while the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination addresses contractor business and legal responsibilities.

Passing the examinations is one part of the licensing process. The applicant must also satisfy the application, documentation, experience, financial, insurance, and final approval requirements.

Business formation does not create a contractor license. Forming an LLC or corporation and obtaining an EIN establishes the company’s legal and administrative structure, but the applicable contractor credential must still be issued before the business performs work requiring that license.

Plumbing contractors may also be responsible for local permits, inspections, adopted plumbing codes, utility requirements, employee safety, tax registrations, insurance, and project-specific approvals.
