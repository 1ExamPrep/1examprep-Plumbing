## Exam Details

The Mississippi Plumbing Contractor examination is administered through PSI as part of the Mississippi State Board of Contractors licensing process. Before a candidate can schedule the required examination, the contractor license application must first be submitted to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. After the application is reviewed and the candidate is determined eligible to test, the Board notifies PSI of the candidate's eligibility.

The current Mississippi Plumbing Contractor examination contains **100 questions**. Candidates are allowed **300 minutes** to complete the examination, and the minimum passing requirement is **73 percent, or 73 correct answers**.

The examination content covers a broad range of plumbing knowledge. Current exam topics include general knowledge and mathematics, general plumbing requirements, water supply and distribution, plumbing fixtures and water heaters, drain-waste-and-vent systems, traps, cleanouts and interceptors, isometric analysis, gas appliances and piping, and OSHA safety.

The examination places substantial emphasis on practical plumbing systems and the ability to interpret technical information. Drain, waste, and vent systems represent an important portion of the examination, while isometric analysis tests a candidate's ability to understand plumbing layouts and system relationships. Candidates should also be prepared to work with mathematical concepts associated with plumbing calculations and field applications.

Safety is another part of the examination. Plumbing contractors work in construction environments where excavation, ladders, tools, personal protective equipment, jobsite hazards, and other OSHA-regulated activities can be relevant. Familiarity with 29 CFR Part 1926 helps candidates prepare for questions involving federal construction safety requirements.

Because the examination covers multiple technical areas, studying only one section of the plumbing code is not an effective preparation strategy. Candidates should build familiarity with the complete range of subjects represented on the test and practice moving between technical references as needed.

## Open Book Test

The Mississippi Plumbing Contractor examination is an **open-book examination**. PSI allows candidates to bring approved reference materials into the examination center under the rules established for the test.

Current PSI rules allow approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. References may also be tabbed or indexed using permanent tabs. Temporary tabs, including removable Post-it-style tabs, are not permitted and must be removed before the examination begins.

Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved reference materials to the examination center. The references are not supplied at the testing location. PSI also prohibits additional loose or attached papers with the approved references, and candidates may not write in their reference books during the examination session.

Open-book testing makes reference-navigation practice particularly important. Candidates should know how to locate subjects through the index, table of contents, chapters, sections, tables, definitions, and permanent tabs. Simply bringing approved books into the exam does not remove the need to understand the material.

The exam is timed, so efficient research matters. When a candidate identifies the subject of a question quickly and knows which reference is most likely to contain the answer, valuable testing time can be preserved. Live instruction and independent reference practice can help develop these navigation habits before exam day.

## Licensing Steps

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates contractor licensing in Mississippi. Candidates seeking a Plumbing Contractor classification must complete the licensing process applicable to their business and qualifying party.

1. **Submit the contractor license application.** The licensing process begins with an application to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. Candidates cannot simply register directly for the contractor examination before completing the Board's eligibility process.
2. **Provide the required qualifying-party information.** The qualifying party is the individual whose qualifications and examination results support the requested contractor classification.
3. **Submit required supporting documentation.** The Board reviews the application and identifies any missing requirements before examination eligibility is issued.
4. **Meet applicable financial requirements.** Commercial contractor applicants must satisfy the financial documentation and net-worth requirements applicable to their requested classification.
5. **Receive authorization to test.** After the Board determines that the applicant is eligible, MSBOC notifies PSI. PSI then provides the candidate with examination registration information.
6. **Schedule the required examinations.** All Mississippi contractor applicants are required to take the Mississippi Law & Business Management examination. A trade examination is also required when applicable to the classification, including the Plumbing Contractor classification.
7. **Prepare for the Plumbing Contractor exam.** Study the relevant codes and trade references, practice plumbing calculations and isometric analysis, and become comfortable navigating permitted books.
8. **Take and pass the required examinations.** Candidates must meet the minimum passing standard established for each required examination.
9. **Complete any remaining Board requirements.** Examination results are one component of the licensing process. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors must approve the complete application before a contractor license is issued.

## State Requirements

Mississippi requires applicants to complete the contractor licensing process through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors before operating under a regulated contractor classification. The Board regulates both commercial and residential contractors who bid on or perform covered construction work in Mississippi.

For commercial contractor applications, the qualifying party portion of the application must be completed before the applicant becomes eligible for required examinations. All applicants must take the Mississippi Law & Business Management examination, and applicants must also complete a trade examination when one is required for the requested classification.

Commercial applications also include financial qualification requirements. Applicants must submit a reviewed or audited financial statement prepared and signed by a certified public accountant and completed within the period required by the Board. The financial statement is used as part of the contractor licensing review.

The Board distinguishes between major classifications and other classifications when determining minimum net-worth requirements. Applicants should make sure the requested Plumbing Contractor classification, business structure, qualifying party information, and financial documentation are properly addressed when completing the application.

Passing the Plumbing Contractor examination by itself does not create a Mississippi contractor license. The examination is one part of a broader licensing process that includes Board application review and satisfaction of all requirements applicable to the applicant.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation strategy for the Mississippi Plumbing Contractor exam should combine code review, practical trade knowledge, mathematics, isometric interpretation, safety review, and repeated practice using approved reference materials.

Start with the International Plumbing Code and learn its organization. Rather than trying to memorize every provision, become comfortable determining where major plumbing subjects are located. Practice finding requirements involving fixtures, water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, materials, joints, connections, and related plumbing provisions.

Next, build speed with the book's index and table of contents. When practicing a question, identify the key plumbing term first. Search for that term or a closely related term in the index, follow the section reference, and read enough surrounding material to understand the requirement in context. This process develops the same type of lookup skill needed during an open-book examination.

Plumbing mathematics deserves dedicated study time. Candidates may encounter questions that require calculations rather than direct code lookup. Review trade-related arithmetic, measurements, conversions, dimensional reasoning, and other mathematical principles used in plumbing applications. Work practice problems without immediately looking at the solution so you develop a repeatable calculation process.

Isometric analysis should also receive focused attention. The current examination outline assigns a meaningful number of questions to isometric analysis. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting plumbing-system drawings and identifying the relationships among piping, fixtures, vents, drainage, and system components.

OSHA safety review should not be overlooked. Use 29 CFR Part 1926 to become familiar with how construction safety standards are arranged. Pay attention to terminology and the organization of major safety subjects so you can locate applicable requirements efficiently.

Timed practice is particularly valuable because the exam contains 100 questions and allows 300 minutes. While that provides several minutes per question on average, some calculation, isometric, and reference-based questions can require additional research. Developing an efficient workflow can help candidates avoid spending too much time on a single difficult item.
