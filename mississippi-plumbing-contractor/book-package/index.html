Exam Details

The Mississippi Plumbing contractor examination evaluates knowledge of plumbing systems, installation requirements, code navigation, construction safety, and trade mathematics. Candidates should combine practical field experience with repeated study of the listed references.

The International Plumbing Code, 2021 Edition, is the primary technical code included in this package. It addresses plumbing fixtures, water-distribution systems, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, interceptors, piping materials, joints, testing, inspections, storm drainage, and other plumbing-system requirements.

Water-distribution preparation may involve water-service piping, distribution piping, pipe sizing, pressure, demand, valves, backflow protection, materials, joints, supports, testing, and connections to fixtures and equipment. Candidates should learn how the code organizes both general provisions and system-specific requirements.

Sanitary-drainage preparation may involve fixture units, horizontal branches, stacks, building drains, building sewers, slopes, cleanouts, pipe sizing, approved materials, joints, supports, and testing. Questions may require candidates to interpret tables, identify minimum requirements, or apply code provisions to practical installations.

Venting topics may include vent sizing, developed length, connections, wet venting, common venting, circuit venting, stack venting, vent terminals, and prohibited arrangements. Candidates should understand how venting supports drainage-system performance and protects fixture trap seals.

Fixture-related preparation may include required fixture quantities, clearances, installation, supports, water-supply connections, drainage connections, accessibility considerations, and approved materials. Candidates may also review traps, interceptors, separators, indirect waste, special plumbing systems, and storm drainage.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1926, supports preparation involving federal construction-safety requirements. Plumbing professionals may need to review excavation, trench protection, access and egress, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, personal protective equipment, tools, electrical hazards, equipment, and material handling.

Plumbing projects can involve trenches, heavy pipe, elevated work areas, power tools, temporary electrical systems, lifting equipment, and coordination with other trades. Candidates should connect written safety requirements with the practical hazards encountered on construction sites.

Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition, supports preparation involving measurements, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, geometry, areas, volumes, pressures, pipe calculations, offsets, and conversions.

Open Book Test

The Mississippi Plumbing contractor examination is open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted references and following current examination rules for approved titles, editions, highlighting, tabs, notes, markings, and supplemental materials.

Open-book testing still requires detailed preparation. Candidates may have the correct books available but lose valuable time by selecting the wrong reference, using an ineffective index term, overlooking an exception, misreading a table, or applying a requirement outside its intended scope.

Candidates should learn the purpose of each reference before test day. Questions involving fixtures, water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, piping materials, testing, inspections, or storm drainage may lead to the International Plumbing Code.

Construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926. Candidates should become familiar with the sections addressing excavation, protective systems, access and egress, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, tools, equipment, electrical hazards, and material handling.

Calculation questions may require Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters. Candidates should practice fractions, decimals, ratios, conversions, geometry, pipe measurements, offsets, areas, volumes, and pressure-related calculations.

Effective search terminology is an important part of open-book preparation. A drainage question may require searches involving fixture units, horizontal branches, stacks, building drains, cleanouts, slopes, or building sewers.

A water-supply question may involve water demand, service piping, distribution piping, pressure, valves, backflow protection, or fixture supply. A venting question may require searches involving trap seals, developed length, wet venting, stack vents, or vent terminals.

Candidates should practice reading the surrounding material rather than stopping at the first matching phrase. Definitions, exceptions, limitations, and related sections can change how a requirement applies to a specific question.

Timed reference exercises can help improve efficiency. Select a subject, choose the most appropriate book, locate the relevant chapter or index term, find the supporting provision, and confirm the answer. Repeat slow or unsuccessful searches using alternative terminology.

Before preparing the books with highlighting or tabs, review the current examination rules. Only preparation methods and materials permitted by the testing provider should be used.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the appropriate classification. Determine that the Mississippi Plumbing classification matches the plumbing services the business intends to provide.
  2. Select the qualifying party. Identify the person who will qualify the business and complete the required examinations.
  3. Document relevant experience. Organize project information involving water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, fixtures, piping, installation, supervision, or project management.
  4. Choose the applicant structure. Determine whether the application will be submitted by an individual, corporation, limited liability company, partnership, or another accepted business entity.
  5. Prepare the contractor application. Gather the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, experience, financial, insurance, reference, and supporting information.
  6. Submit the application. Send the completed filing to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for review.
  7. Respond to application requests. Provide missing documents, corrections, explanations, or additional information requested during the review process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Follow the examination registration instructions after authorization to test is issued.
  9. Schedule the required examinations. Select an available examination date and approved testing location.
  10. Obtain the reference books. Secure the correct titles and editions required for examination preparation.
  11. Prepare the books. Add only highlighting, tabs, notes, or markings permitted under current examination rules.
  12. Study the technical material. Review plumbing code, construction safety, trade mathematics, and reference-navigation methods.
  13. Complete timed practice. Work through code lookups, plumbing calculations, and practice questions under structured conditions.
  14. Prepare for Business and Law. Obtain separate preparation for applicable Mississippi contractor business and law requirements.
  15. Take the examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the references and materials permitted under current testing rules.
  16. Complete outstanding requirements. Submit any remaining records, corrections, or supporting documents requested during final review.
  17. Receive final approval. Passing the examinations does not automatically issue a contractor license. The application and supporting documentation must also be approved.
  18. Maintain the license. After issuance, contractors are responsible for renewals, insurance, business records, taxes, permits, safety obligations, and ongoing compliance.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the applicable contractor licensing process. Applicants should select the classification that accurately reflects the plumbing services their business intends to perform.

The qualifying party must have a relationship with the applicant that satisfies the applicable requirements. Depending on the business structure, the qualifying party may be an owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee.

The contractor application may require business-registration records, ownership information, qualifying-party documentation, detailed project experience, references, financial information, insurance records, and other supporting materials.

Application information should be complete and consistent across business filings, ownership records, experience documentation, and supporting materials. Missing or conflicting information can affect the application review process.

Passing the required examinations is one part of licensing. Applicants must also satisfy the application, experience, documentation, financial, insurance, and final approval requirements.

Plumbing contractors may also be responsible for local permits, inspections, adopted plumbing codes, utility requirements, employee safety, insurance, tax registrations, and project-specific approvals.

Local plumbing requirements can vary by project location. Contractors should confirm the applicable permitting, inspection, and code requirements before beginning regulated work.
