## Exam Details

The **ICC G24 National Standard Master Plumber** examination contains **100 multiple-choice questions** and has a **4-hour time limit**.

The official G24 content outline divides the examination into seven major areas:

- **Administration General Regulations – 46%**
- **Plumbing Fixtures – 14%**
- **Water Supply and Distribution – 11%**
- **Sanitary Drainage – 11%**
- **Plumbing Vent Requirements – 8%**
- **Traps, Interceptors, and Separators – 8%**
- **Storm Drainage – 2%Administration General Regulations** is the largest content area and represents 46% of the examination. This category includes general installation requirements, code definitions and terms, plan reading, testing, and materials. Because nearly half of the exam comes from this area, candidates should spend significant study time on the general provisions that apply across multiple plumbing systems.

**Plumbing Fixtures** represent 14% of the exam. This category includes water heaters, water closets and urinals, specialty fixtures, bathtubs, showers, sinks, lavatories, and related installation requirements. Candidates should understand how fixture location, connection, supply, drainage, and other requirements apply in actual installations.

**Water Supply and Distribution** account for 11% of the examination. Topics include service distribution and piping, water supply and pressure, and protection of the potable water supply. Candidates should be prepared to work with water-service requirements, distribution piping, pressure considerations, valves, sizing, and backflow protection.

**Sanitary Drainage** also represents 11%. This content area covers building sanitary drainage, sumps and ejectors, and indirect and special waste systems. Candidates should understand drainage fixture units, sizing, slope, cleanouts, piping arrangements, and system connections.

**Plumbing Vent Requirements** represent 8% and include vent connections, grades, and vent terminals. Candidates should understand how vent systems protect trap seals and how the IPC regulates vent arrangement and termination.

**Traps, Interceptors, and Separators** represent another 8%. Study should include trap installation and the use of interceptors and separators for special waste conditions.

**Storm Drainage** represents 2% of the examination. Although it is the smallest content area, it should still be included in a complete study plan.

## Open Book Test

The ICC G24 National Standard Master Plumber examination is an **open book test**. Candidates have four hours to complete 100 multiple-choice questions and must follow current ICC examination rules regarding permitted references and materials.

The open-book format makes efficient reference navigation an important preparation skill. Candidates should become familiar enough with the 2018 International Plumbing Code to recognize the likely location of an answer before beginning a detailed search.

The **International Plumbing Code, 2018** should become a working reference throughout preparation. Candidates should know how the code is organized and where to locate provisions involving general regulations, fixtures, water heaters, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, special waste, vents, traps, interceptors, separators, and storm drainage.

A question involving water-supply pressure or pipe sizing should direct attention toward the water supply and distribution provisions. A question involving drainage fixture units or building drains should lead toward sanitary drainage. Venting questions should direct candidates toward the appropriate venting provisions, while grease or oil-related waste questions may require use of interceptor or separator requirements.

Plan-reading questions may require candidates to interpret pipe sizes, fixture locations, system relationships, risers, or other information presented on plumbing drawings. These questions combine technical plumbing knowledge with the ability to understand construction documents.

Because the examination contains 100 questions in four hours, candidates should not expect to look up every answer from scratch. Efficient navigation and familiarity with common code locations can preserve time for more detailed questions involving calculations, tables, or plan analysis.

Practice questions are especially valuable when used as reference exercises. After answering, locate the applicable IPC provision and review the nearby sections, tables, and definitions. This reinforces both the rule itself and where to find it during an open-book exam.

The online practice-question product includes **3 months of access**. The access period gives candidates time to divide the G24 examination into manageable subjects, repeat difficult areas, and develop stronger familiarity with the 2018 International Plumbing Code.

Begin with **Administration General Regulations** because it represents 46% of the examination. Review general installation requirements, materials, testing, code definitions, terminology, and plan reading. This category is broad and influences many questions throughout the exam.

**General Installation Requirements** can involve system protection, supports, piping installation, approved methods, location, workmanship, and other broad requirements that apply to plumbing systems. Candidates should become comfortable with the sections that establish these baseline rules.

**Code Definitions and Terms** deserve dedicated attention. Plumbing codes use defined terms precisely, and a question may depend on the official meaning of a fixture, fitting, waste type, drainage component, or system arrangement.

For **Sanitary Drainage**, review drainage fixture units, horizontal and vertical drainage, building drains, sumps, ejectors, indirect waste, special waste, slope, cleanouts, and piping sizes. Drainage questions can require both code navigation and careful use of tables.

For **Plumbing Vent Requirements**, study vent connections, vent grades, vent terminals, sizing, and approved vent configurations. Candidates should understand how vents work with the drainage system and how installation conditions affect compliance.
