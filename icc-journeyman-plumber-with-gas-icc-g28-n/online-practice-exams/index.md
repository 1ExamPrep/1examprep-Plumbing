## Exam Details

The **ICC G28 National Standard Journeyman Plumber with Gas** examination contains **70 multiple-choice questions** and has a **3-hour time limit**.

The official examination outline divides the G28 exam into eight major content areas:

- **Administration General Regulations – 34%**
- **Plumbing Fixtures – 11%**
- **Water Supply and Distribution – 11%**
- **Sanitary Drainage – 9%**
- **Plumbing Vent Requirements – 7%**
- **Traps, Interceptors, and Separators – 7%**
- **Storm Drainage – 2%**
- **Gas Piping – 19%Administration General Regulations** is the largest content area at 34%. This category includes general installation requirements, code definitions and terms, plan reading, testing, and materials. Candidates should give this area substantial study time because these foundational subjects affect many different plumbing and gas installations.

**Gas Piping** is the second-largest category at 19%. This portion includes gas piping for service and distribution, gas-piping requirements for specific appliances and equipment, sources of combustion air and duct openings, and fuel-burning appliances, vents, and connectors.

**Plumbing Fixtures** represent 11% of the examination. This area includes water heaters, water closets and urinals, specialty fixtures, bathtubs, showers, sinks, and lavatories.

**Water Supply and Distribution** also represent 11%. Candidates should prepare for service distribution and piping, water supply and pressure, and protection of the potable water supply.

**Sanitary Drainage** accounts for 9% of the examination and includes building sanitary drainage, sumps and ejectors, and indirect and special waste systems.

**Plumbing Vent Requirements** represent 7%, and **Traps, Interceptors, and Separators** represent another 7%. **Storm Drainage** makes up the remaining 2%.

Because the G28 combines plumbing and fuel-gas topics, candidates need to recognize which code controls the situation described in each question. Efficient code selection can save significant time during the examination.

## Open Book Test

The ICC G28 National Standard Journeyman Plumber with Gas examination is an **open book test**. Candidates have three hours to complete 70 multiple-choice questions and must follow current ICC examination rules regarding allowable reference materials.

The open-book format makes reference familiarity especially important because candidates must work with both the 2018 IPC and 2018 IFGC. Knowing which code applies before beginning a detailed search can improve efficiency.

Questions involving fixtures, potable water, drainage, plumbing vents, traps, interceptors, separators, or storm drainage should generally direct attention toward the **2018 International Plumbing Code**.

Questions involving gas-service piping, gas-distribution piping, appliance gas connections, combustion air, fuel-burning equipment, vents, and connectors should direct attention toward the **2018 International Fuel Gas Code**.

Candidates should become familiar with commonly used tables in both references. Plumbing questions may require pipe-sizing or drainage tables, while fuel-gas questions may require gas-pipe sizing or venting information.

The examination allows 180 minutes for 70 questions, providing an average of approximately two minutes and thirty-four seconds per question. Some questions may be answered quickly, while code-table, plan-reading, or sizing questions may require additional time.

Practice questions are particularly valuable when used as code-navigation drills. After answering a question, find the applicable section or table and review surrounding requirements. This helps reinforce both the technical rule and where it is located.

## Test Information and Study Materials

The online practice-question product includes **3 months of access**. The access period provides time to organize study around the eight major content areas and return to subjects that need additional practice.

Begin with **Administration General Regulations** because it represents 34% of the examination. Review general installation requirements, code definitions and terminology, plan reading, testing, and approved materials.

**General Installation Requirements** may affect many different systems. Candidates should understand code provisions involving installation, support, protection, workmanship, materials, accessibility, and related conditions that influence plumbing and fuel-gas work.

**Code Definitions and Terms** should also be studied carefully. Both the IPC and IFGC use defined terms that can affect how an exam question is interpreted. Understanding those definitions can make it easier to identify the correct chapter or requirement.

**Plan Reading** is another important part of Administration General Regulations. Candidates should practice interpreting plumbing layouts, piping arrangements, fixture locations, pipe sizes, risers, and other construction information.

**Testing** should be reviewed across the applicable systems. Understand where the codes address required testing and what requirements apply before plumbing or gas systems are placed into service.

Next, focus heavily on **Gas Piping**. At 19% of the examination, this category deserves regular study. Review service and distribution piping, materials, installation, sizing, pressure-related requirements, valves, appliance connections, and other fuel-gas provisions.

Gas-pipe sizing is a useful topic for repeated practice. Candidates should develop a consistent method for determining the applicable system conditions and selecting the correct sizing table instead of searching broadly through the code.

Study **combustion air** as part of the gas section. Candidates should understand how air requirements for fuel-burning appliances are addressed, including the use of openings and ducts where applicable.

Review **fuel-burning appliances, vents, and connectors** as well. These topics require candidates to understand how appliances safely receive fuel and discharge combustion products.

For **Plumbing Fixtures**, review water heaters, water closets, urinals, specialty fixtures, bathtubs, showers, sinks, lavatories, and applicable installation requirements.
