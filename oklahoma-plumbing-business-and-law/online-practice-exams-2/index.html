Exam Details

The current Oklahoma Plumbing Business and Law examination contains 50 scored questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes, or two hours, to complete the examination. A minimum score of 75% is required, with a current raw passing score of 37 correct answers.

Bidding and Estimating is the largest individual category with 10 questions. Subjects include general estimating and bidding. Candidates should understand how contractors organize project costs, evaluate labor and materials, account for overhead, and develop bids from project information.

Project Management and Supervision contains seven questions. Subjects include general project oversight, budget control, quality control, material control, jobsite safety, scheduling, potentially hazardous materials, environmental protection, submittals and reports, ethics, and liens.

Contracts contains five questions. Candidates should review contract terminology, required elements and components, contract types, change orders, standardized documents, interpretation, warranties, documents and inclusions, and other contractual obligations.

Financial contains eight questions. Topics include business organization characteristics, advantages and disadvantages of different business structures, business start-up, accounting methods, cash-flow terminology, accounts receivable, accounts payable, balance sheets, income statements, company income taxes, financing, checking accounts, and financial ratios.

Labor and Personnel contains five questions. The outline includes the Americans with Disabilities Act, labor standards, requirements for non-citizens and nonresidents, workers’ compensation, federal or state OSHA requirements, new hires, personnel record keeping, and other employment responsibilities.

Risk Management contains four questions covering insurance and bonds. Contractors should understand the purpose of common insurance and bonding requirements and how risk-management decisions affect business operations.

Payroll and Payroll Taxes contains five questions. This section includes payroll taxes, forms, and due-date concepts relevant to employers.

Licensing Requirements contains six questions. Subjects include required insurance and bonds and license renewal. Oklahoma-specific statutes and Plumbing Industry Regulations are especially important for this portion of the examination.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma Plumbing Business and Law examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing the approved reference materials they intend to use at the PSI examination center.

The approved examination references include Oklahoma-specific plumbing statutes and regulations, the Construction Industries Board fine schedule, Oklahoma Lien Law, the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, and Oklahoma workers’ compensation material.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted and can help candidates organize high-value subjects such as licensing, insurance, bonds, liens, estimating, contracts, payroll, and financial management.

Temporary removable tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed. References may not contain written notes before the examination, and candidates may not write in the approved references during the testing session.

Additional loose papers are not allowed with approved exam references. Downloaded references must be properly secured. Current PSI rules permit downloaded material when it is spiral bound, hole-punched and placed in a binder, or stapled in the permitted manner.

A silent, non-printing, non-programmable calculator may be used during the examination. This can be helpful for estimating, financial, payroll, and other business-related calculations.

With 50 scored questions in 120 minutes, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and 24 seconds per question. Effective open-book preparation should include knowing which reference is most likely to contain a particular answer instead of searching through every book for each question.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the Oklahoma Plumbing Contractor classification you are pursuing. Oklahoma recognizes plumbing contractor and residential plumbing contractor licensing categories with different authorized scopes.
  2. Gain qualifying plumbing experience. The standard progression begins with registered apprentice experience obtained while working for a licensed Plumbing Contractor.
  3. Meet journeyman-level requirements. Plumbing contractor applicants must satisfy the underlying journeyman qualification requirements applicable to their licensing path.
  4. Complete the additional contractor experience. Oklahoma contractor applicants must meet the journeyman requirements plus one additional year of qualifying plumbing trade experience.
  5. Use qualifying formal education when applicable. Approved plumbing education can substitute for a portion of the experience requirement under Oklahoma rules.
  6. Maintain documentation. Keep apprentice registration information, employment records, licensing history, transcripts, and other documentation used to establish examination eligibility.
  7. Submit the Plumbing Contractor application. Apply through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board for the appropriate contractor category.
  8. Receive examination authorization. The Board reviews the application and determines whether the applicant meets the qualifications to test.
  9. Schedule the required examination through PSI. Approved candidates arrange testing through PSI.
  10. Prepare for the applicable plumbing trade examination. Study the technical plumbing references and subjects associated with the contractor classification being pursued.
  11. Prepare for Plumbing Business and Law. Review estimating, project management, contracts, finances, labor, insurance, bonds, payroll, liens, and Oklahoma plumbing licensing rules.
  12. Complete the required contractor examinations. Satisfy the applicable plumbing trade and Business and Law examination requirements.
  13. Complete contractor activation requirements. Submit the required bond and insurance documentation to the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board.
  14. Maintain the contractor license. Keep licensing, insurance, bond, renewal, supervision, and continuing education requirements current.

State Requirements

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board administers plumbing licensing under the Plumbing License Law of 1955 and Oklahoma Plumbing Industry Regulations.

Applicants for plumbing licensing examinations must be at least 18 years old. The standard journeyman qualification pathway requires three years of qualifying plumbing trade experience while employed by a licensed Plumbing Contractor. Oklahoma defines one year of verifiable plumbing experience as 2,000 hours.

Applicants using plumbing experience obtained in Oklahoma generally must maintain the required apprentice registration for that experience unless an applicable lawful exception applies. Oklahoma also recognizes certain qualifying military experience and qualifying out-of-state plumbing licenses.

Approved plumbing education may substitute for a portion of the experience requirement. A qualifying associate degree or Career Tech diploma from an approved plumbing educational program containing at least 1,000 classroom hours may substitute for part of the required experience under Oklahoma rules.

Plumbing contractor applicants must meet the applicable journeyman requirements and complete one additional year of qualifying experience in the plumbing trade. The Construction Industries Board evaluates the applicant’s experience before examination authorization.

Oklahoma Plumbing Industry Regulations state that an applicant is issued a contractor license after successfully completing the required contractor examination requirements and satisfying applicable bonding and insurance requirements.

An active Oklahoma Plumbing Contractor is generally required to maintain a $5,000 corporate surety bond payable to the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board and applicable liability insurance documentation. These requirements are important Business and Law subjects because they affect whether a contractor license remains active.

Oklahoma also regulates apprentice supervision. Plumbing helpers performing plumbing work must be properly registered and directly supervised within the ratios established by Oklahoma plumbing rules.

Licensed Oklahoma Plumbing Contractors and Journeymen currently must complete six hours of approved continuing education within the 36 months preceding license expiration, subject to applicable exemptions and current regulations.

If a candidate fails an Oklahoma plumbing examination on the first attempt, the current testing rules require a 30-day waiting period before retesting. Subsequent failures generally require a 90-day waiting period.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Oklahoma Plumbing Business and Law study plan should follow the current examination distribution. Bidding and Estimating, Financial, and Project Management and Supervision account for 25 of the 50 scored questions, making those categories especially important.

Prioritize Bidding and Estimating. Review labor, materials, equipment, overhead, direct costs, indirect costs, and other components that affect project pricing. Practice understanding how an estimate develops into a contractor bid.

Study Project Management. Review budgets, scheduling, quality control, material control, jobsite safety, environmental protection, submittals, reports, ethics, and liens. Understand how these responsibilities interact throughout a construction project.

Know Contract Fundamentals. Study contract terminology, contract types, required elements, change orders, warranties, standardized documents, interpretation, and contract inclusions.

Strengthen Financial Knowledge. Become comfortable with cash flow, accounts receivable, accounts payable, balance sheets, income statements, business financing, accounting methods, business organizations, checking accounts, and financial ratios.

Review Labor and Personnel. Study workers’ compensation, labor standards, new-hire responsibilities, personnel record keeping, employment requirements, and workplace safety obligations.

Understand Insurance and Bonds. These subjects appear under Risk Management and Licensing Requirements. Learn the general business purpose of insurance and bonding as well as Oklahoma-specific contractor requirements.

Practice Payroll and Payroll Taxes. Review payroll taxes, forms, and due-date concepts. Contractors with employees must understand the administrative responsibilities associated with payroll.

Learn Oklahoma Licensing Rules. Practice locating contractor requirements in the Plumbing License Law and Plumbing Industry Regulations. Become familiar with provisions involving licenses, bonds, insurance, renewal, supervision, and compliance.

Know where lien provisions are located. Liens are included in the Project Management portion of the exam. Familiarity with Oklahoma Lien Law can reduce the time needed to research these questions.

Practice open-book navigation. When a question involves Oklahoma plumbing licensing, turn first to the state law or Plumbing Industry Regulations. When it involves broader contractor-business principles, the NASCLA Contractors Guide may be the more useful starting point.

Use permanent tabs strategically. Organize major subjects without overcrowding the references. Useful areas can include estimating, contracts, financial management, liens, insurance, bonds, payroll, licensing, and renewal.

Review missed questions by category. Separate mistakes into estimating, project management, contracts, finance, labor, risk management, payroll, and licensing. Direct additional study toward the categories producing the most errors.
