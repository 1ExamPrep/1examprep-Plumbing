## Open Book Test

The Oklahoma Plumbing Business and Law examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates are responsible for bringing the approved reference materials they intend to use at the PSI examination center.

The approved examination references include Oklahoma-specific plumbing statutes and regulations, the Construction Industries Board fine schedule, Oklahoma Lien Law, the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, and Oklahoma workers’ compensation material.

Approved references may be **highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination**. Permanent tabs are permitted and can help candidates organize high-value subjects such as licensing, insurance, bonds, liens, estimating, contracts, payroll, and financial management.

Temporary removable tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed. References may not contain written notes before the examination, and candidates may not write in the approved references during the testing session.

Additional loose papers are not allowed with approved exam references. Downloaded references must be properly secured. Current PSI rules permit downloaded material when it is spiral bound, hole-punched and placed in a binder, or stapled in the permitted manner.

A silent, non-printing, non-programmable calculator may be used during the examination. This can be helpful for estimating, financial, payroll, and other business-related calculations.

With 50 scored questions in 120 minutes, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and 24 seconds per question. Effective open-book preparation should include knowing which reference is most likely to contain a particular answer instead of searching through every book for each question.

## State Requirements

The **Oklahoma Construction Industries Board** administers plumbing licensing under the Plumbing License Law of 1955 and Oklahoma Plumbing Industry Regulations.

Applicants for plumbing licensing examinations must be at least **18 years old**. The standard journeyman qualification pathway requires three years of qualifying plumbing trade experience while employed by a licensed Plumbing Contractor. Oklahoma defines one year of verifiable plumbing experience as **2,000 hours**.

Applicants using plumbing experience obtained in Oklahoma generally must maintain the required apprentice registration for that experience unless an applicable lawful exception applies. Oklahoma also recognizes certain qualifying military experience and qualifying out-of-state plumbing licenses.

Approved plumbing education may substitute for a portion of the experience requirement. A qualifying associate degree or Career Tech diploma from an approved plumbing educational program containing at least 1,000 classroom hours may substitute for part of the required experience under Oklahoma rules.

Plumbing contractor applicants must meet the applicable journeyman requirements and complete **one additional year of qualifying experience in the plumbing trade**. The Construction Industries Board evaluates the applicant’s experience before examination authorization.

Oklahoma Plumbing Industry Regulations state that an applicant is issued a contractor license after successfully completing the required contractor examination requirements and satisfying applicable bonding and insurance requirements.

An active Oklahoma Plumbing Contractor is generally required to maintain a **$5,000 corporate surety bond** payable to the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board and applicable liability insurance documentation. These requirements are important Business and Law subjects because they affect whether a contractor license remains active.

Oklahoma also regulates apprentice supervision. Plumbing helpers performing plumbing work must be properly registered and directly supervised within the ratios established by Oklahoma plumbing rules.

Licensed Oklahoma Plumbing Contractors and Journeymen currently must complete **six hours of approved continuing education within the 36 months preceding license expiration**, subject to applicable exemptions and current regulations.

If a candidate fails an Oklahoma plumbing examination on the first attempt, the current testing rules require a **30-day waiting period** before retesting. Subsequent failures generally require a **90-day waiting period**.
