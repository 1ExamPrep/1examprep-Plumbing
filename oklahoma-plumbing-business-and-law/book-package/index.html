Exam Details

The Oklahoma Plumbing Business and Law examination is required as part of the plumbing contractor licensing process. Contractor applicants must pass both the applicable plumbing trade examination and the Business and Law portion of the contractor examination.

The Oklahoma Plumbing Business and Law exam contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 120 minutes to complete the examination. The required passing score is 75%.

The examination is divided into eight major content areas:

  • Bidding and Estimating: 10 questions
  • Project Management and Supervision: 7 questions
  • Contracts: 5 questions
  • Financial: 8 questions
  • Labor and Personnel: 5 questions
  • Risk Management: 4 questions
  • Payroll and Payroll Taxes: 6 questions
  • Licensing Requirements: 5 questions

Bidding and Estimating is the largest individual examination category. Candidates should understand general estimating concepts and the bidding process. A contractor must be able to consider project requirements, costs, labor, materials, overhead, and other factors when preparing work proposals.

Project Management and Supervision covers subjects such as general project oversight, budgeting, quality control, material control, jobsite safety, scheduling, hazardous materials, environmental protection, submittals, reports, ethics, and construction liens.

Contracts include terminology, required contract components, contract types, change orders, standardized documents, interpretation, warranties, document requirements, and other contractual obligations.

Financial questions can address business organization, business startup, accounting methods, cash flow, accounts receivable, accounts payable, balance sheets, income statements, company income taxes, financing, checking accounts, and financial ratios.

The examination also includes labor and personnel responsibilities, risk management, insurance, bonds, payroll and payroll taxes, and Oklahoma plumbing licensing requirements.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma Plumbing Business and Law examination is an open-book test. Candidates may bring approved reference materials into the examination center and use them during the test.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination session. Permanent tabs may be used for organization. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not permitted.

References may not contain written notes added before or during the examination. Candidates should become familiar with the examination rules and prepare their materials accordingly before test day.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. With 50 questions and 120 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Searching through unfamiliar books for every answer can consume valuable testing time.

A more effective approach is to learn which reference applies to each subject. Questions about Oklahoma plumbing licensing may lead to the Plumbing License Law or Plumbing Industry Regulations. Questions about liens may require Oklahoma Lien Law. Contractor business questions may involve the NASCLA Contractors Guide.

Practice this process while studying. Read the question, identify the subject, select the most appropriate reference, and then locate the applicable section. Repeated practice can help make reference research more efficient.

Licensing Steps

  1. Gain qualifying plumbing trade experience. Oklahoma contractor applicants must meet the experience requirements established for plumbing licensing.
  2. Meet the plumbing journeyman qualifications. Contractor applicants must satisfy the same basic qualification requirements applicable to an Oklahoma plumbing journeyman.
  3. Complete additional contractor-level experience. Applicants must obtain an additional year of qualifying plumbing trade experience beyond the journeyman-level requirement.
  4. Apply for the plumbing contractor license. Plumbing contractor licensing is administered through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board.
  5. Receive approval for examination. Candidates must be approved before proceeding with the required contractor examinations.
  6. Prepare for the plumbing trade examination. Contractor candidates must demonstrate the applicable technical plumbing knowledge required by Oklahoma.
  7. Prepare for the Plumbing Business and Law examination. Study estimating, project management, contracts, finances, labor, insurance, bonds, payroll, licensing, and other contractor business responsibilities.
  8. Pass both portions of the contractor examination. Oklahoma plumbing contractor applicants must satisfy both the trade and Business and Law examination requirements.
  9. Complete active contractor requirements. After examination requirements are met, the contractor must satisfy applicable bond, insurance, and licensing requirements for active status.

State Requirements

Oklahoma plumbing contractor licensing is administered by the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Plumbing contractor licensing represents the contractor level of the state's plumbing licensing structure.

Applicants for a plumbing contractor license must meet the same qualification requirements applicable to a plumbing journeyman and complete an additional one year of experience in the plumbing trade.

The standard journeyman qualification pathway includes applicants who are at least 18 years old and have three years of qualifying plumbing trade experience while employed by a licensed plumbing contractor. Oklahoma also recognizes certain qualifying military experience and current out-of-state plumbing licenses that meet applicable requirements.

Approved plumbing education can substitute for a portion of the required trade experience. Qualifying education must meet the standards established for plumbing licensing in Oklahoma.

Contractor applicants are required to pass both portions of the contractor examination: the applicable plumbing trade examination and the Plumbing Business and Law examination.

After the required examinations are completed, the contractor license remains inactive until the applicable bond and insurance requirements are satisfied.

An active Oklahoma plumbing contractor is required to maintain a $5,000 corporate surety bond or another qualifying form of security permitted under Oklahoma plumbing regulations.

Active plumbing contractors must also maintain at least $50,000 in commercial general liability insurance. Maintaining required coverage is part of keeping a plumbing contractor license active.

Licensed plumbing contractors also have continuing responsibilities involving apprentice supervision, licensing, advertising, contracts, bids, insurance, and compliance with Oklahoma plumbing laws and regulations.

Reference Books

  • Oklahoma Plumbing License Law of 1955, Oklahoma Statutes, Title 59, Chapter 27
    The Oklahoma-specific reference establishes the statutory framework governing plumbing licensing. It addresses licensing authority, plumbers and plumbing contractors, regulatory responsibilities, and other legal provisions affecting licensed plumbing activity in Oklahoma.
  • Oklahoma Plumbing Industry Regulations, Oklahoma Administrative Code, Title 158, Chapter 30
    This reference contains administrative rules governing the Oklahoma plumbing industry. Candidates can use it to study license qualifications, contractor responsibilities, examination procedures, apprentices, bond and insurance requirements, licensing procedures, and other state regulations.
  • Oklahoma Fine Schedule of the Construction Industries Board, Oklahoma Administrative Code, Title 158, Chapter 10
    This Oklahoma regulatory reference addresses fines associated with violations administered by the Construction Industries Board. It supports preparation involving contractor compliance, enforcement, and the consequences of regulated violations.
  • Oklahoma Lien Law, Oklahoma Statutes, Title 42, Chapter 3
    This reference addresses Oklahoma construction lien provisions. Lien law can affect contractors, subcontractors, suppliers, owners, and construction projects, making it an important part of contractor project-management preparation.
  • NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th Edition
    The NASCLA Contractors Guide is a major contractor business reference covering estimating, bidding, project management, contracts, financial management, business organization, labor, risk management, insurance, bonds, payroll, taxes, and other subjects associated with operating a contracting business.

These books should be used throughout the study process rather than saved only for the final days before the examination. Regular reference practice can help candidates become more familiar with terminology, chapters, regulatory provisions, and business-management concepts.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Oklahoma Plumbing Business and Law study plan should organize preparation around the eight examination categories and connect each subject with the reference most likely to contain the applicable information.

Bidding and estimating deserves substantial attention because it accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand general estimating principles, bid preparation, project costs, and the factors contractors consider before committing to perform work.

Project management and supervision includes several responsibilities encountered during construction projects. Review project oversight, budgeting, quality control, materials management, scheduling, jobsite safety, environmental considerations, submittals, reporting, ethics, and liens.

Contracts should be studied carefully because written agreements establish important responsibilities between contracting parties. Review contract terminology, contract types, required elements, change orders, warranties, interpretation, standardized documents, and other obligations.

Financial management is another major exam category. Candidates should understand basic accounting and business terminology, including cash flow, accounts payable, accounts receivable, balance sheets, income statements, financing, taxes, checking accounts, and financial ratios.

Contractors do not need to become professional accountants to understand the importance of financial management. They should, however, recognize common financial documents and understand how basic financial information affects business operations.

Labor and personnel preparation should include employment responsibilities, labor standards, workers' compensation concepts, workplace safety, new-hire responsibilities, personnel records, and other requirements associated with managing employees.

Risk management includes insurance and bonds. These subjects are particularly important for contractor candidates because Oklahoma plumbing contractors have bond and commercial general liability insurance requirements associated with active contractor status.

Payroll and payroll taxes should be reviewed as part of contractor business operations. Candidates should understand tax responsibilities, forms, due dates, and the importance of properly administering payroll when employing workers.

Licensing requirements deserve focused study using the Oklahoma Plumbing License Law and Plumbing Industry Regulations. Review contractor qualifications, insurance, bonds, license status, renewal responsibilities, and other requirements regulating Oklahoma plumbing contractors.

Timed practice is useful because candidates have 120 minutes to answer 50 questions. During practice, try to answer straightforward business questions efficiently and reserve additional time for questions requiring detailed reference research.
