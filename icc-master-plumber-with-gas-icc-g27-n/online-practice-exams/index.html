Exam Details

The ICC G27 National Standard Master Plumber with Gas examination contains 100 multiple-choice questions and has a 4-hour time limit.

The official G27 examination outline divides the test into eight major content areas:

  • Administration General Regulations – 36%
  • Plumbing Fixtures – 13%
  • Water Supply and Distribution – 9%
  • Sanitary Drainage – 8%
  • Plumbing Vent Requirements – 6%
  • Traps, Interceptors, and Separators – 5%
  • Storm Drainage – 2%
  • Gas Piping – 21%

Administration General Regulations is the largest content area at 36%. This portion includes general installation requirements, code definitions and terms, plan reading, testing, and materials. Candidates should devote significant preparation time to this area because these fundamentals affect many different plumbing and fuel-gas installations.

Gas Piping is the second-largest content area at 21%. This portion covers gas piping for service and distribution, gas-piping requirements associated with specific appliances and equipment, sources of combustion air and duct openings, and requirements involving fuel-burning appliances, vents, and connectors.

Plumbing Fixtures represent 13% of the examination and include water heaters, water closets and urinals, specialty fixtures, bathtubs, showers, sinks, and lavatories.

Water Supply and Distribution account for 9% and include service distribution and piping, water supply and pressure, and protection of the potable water supply.

Sanitary Drainage represents 8% of the test and includes building sanitary drainage, sumps and ejectors, and indirect and special waste systems.

Plumbing Vent Requirements account for 6%, while Traps, Interceptors, and Separators account for 5%. Storm Drainage represents the remaining 2% of the official examination outline.

Open Book Test

The ICC G27 National Standard Master Plumber with Gas examination is an open book test. Candidates have four hours to complete 100 multiple-choice questions and must follow current ICC examination rules regarding allowable reference materials.

The open-book format makes familiarity with both the IPC and IFGC especially important. Candidates should be able to determine quickly whether a question belongs in the plumbing code or fuel-gas code before searching for the answer.

A question involving drainage fixture units, sanitary piping, plumbing vents, traps, water supply, or plumbing fixtures should generally direct attention toward the 2018 International Plumbing Code. Questions involving gas-pipe sizing, fuel-gas distribution, combustion air, appliance venting, or gas connectors should direct attention toward the 2018 International Fuel Gas Code.

Candidates should practice identifying the correct chapter and table before beginning a detailed search. This can save significant time during a 100-question exam.

The G27 examination allows approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question on average. Some questions may be answered from experience or code familiarity, while others may require a detailed table lookup, calculation, or plan interpretation.

Use practice questions as reference-navigation exercises. After selecting an answer, locate the supporting provision and review nearby exceptions, definitions, notes, and tables. That habit helps build the familiarity needed for efficient open-book testing.

State Requirements

The ICC G27 National Standard Master Plumber with Gas examination is a national contractor/trades examination. It provides a standardized technical examination but does not itself create a nationwide plumbing or gas license.

States, counties, municipalities, and other licensing jurisdictions determine whether the G27 examination is accepted for their licensing programs. Requirements may differ significantly depending on where a candidate intends to perform plumbing or fuel-gas work.

Passing the G27 exam and obtaining a license are therefore separate matters. The examination measures technical knowledge, while the applicable jurisdiction determines eligibility, experience requirements, applications, and other licensing conditions.

Candidates pursuing licensure should identify the exact classification they need and confirm that the G27 examination satisfies the applicable trade-examination requirement.

Test Information and Study Materials

The online practice-question product includes 3 months of access. Candidates can use the access period to study plumbing and gas subjects in focused sessions and repeatedly return to topics that require additional review.

Begin with Administration General Regulations because it represents 36% of the exam. Review general installation requirements, definitions, terminology, plan reading, testing procedures, and approved materials. These subjects create a foundation for both plumbing and fuel-gas work.

Plan reading deserves particular attention. Candidates may need to interpret pipe sizes, fixture locations, system layouts, connections, or other information shown on construction documents and then apply the appropriate code requirements.

Next, devote significant preparation time to Gas Piping. At 21%, this is one of the most important sections of the G27 exam. Study gas service and distribution piping, allowable materials, system installation, sizing, pressure considerations, valves, connections, and appliance requirements.

Review combustion air carefully. Fuel-burning appliances require adequate combustion, ventilation, and dilution air under applicable conditions. Candidates should understand the methods used to determine required air and how openings or ducts are arranged.

Also study fuel-burning appliances, vents, and connectors. Questions may involve vent materials, connectors, equipment installation, combustion products, clearances, or other requirements affecting safe appliance operation.

For Plumbing Fixtures, review water heaters, water closets, urinals, specialty fixtures, tubs, showers, sinks, and lavatories. Understand how fixture requirements interact with water supply, drainage, and general installation provisions.

For Water Supply and Distribution, review service piping, distribution systems, pressure, pipe sizing, valves, and protection against contamination. Potable-water protection should be studied carefully because plumbing systems must prevent cross-connections and other conditions that could compromise water quality.

For Sanitary Drainage, review drainage fixture units, horizontal and vertical drainage piping, building drains, cleanouts, slope, sumps, ejectors, indirect waste, and special waste systems.

For Plumbing Vent Requirements, study vent connections, grades, terminals, acceptable configurations, and the relationship between venting and trap-seal protection.

For Traps, Interceptors, and Separators, review trap installation, trap seals, prohibited arrangements, grease interceptors, oil separators, and other devices used for particular waste conditions.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

Storm Drainage represents a smaller portion of the examination, but candidates should still review the applicable IPC requirements so that these questions are not overlooked.

Timed practice can help develop an effective test-day pace. Four hours for 100 questions means candidates must balance careful code research with efficient decision-making. Spending too much time searching for one difficult answer can reduce the time available for easier questions later.

When reviewing missed questions, identify whether the problem came from technical knowledge, code selection, navigation, table interpretation, or calculation. If a gas-piping question was missed because the wrong sizing table was selected, study table selection rather than merely memorizing the final answer.

The strongest preparation combines repeated practice with regular use of both references. The goal is to recognize the subject quickly, select the correct code, locate the appropriate requirement, and apply it accurately.

Plan reading deserves particular attention. Candidates may need to interpret pipe sizes, fixture locations, system layouts, connections, or other information shown on construction documents and then apply the appropriate code requirements.

Next, devote significant preparation time to Gas Piping. At 21%, this is one of the most important sections of the G27 exam. Study gas service and distribution piping, allowable materials, system installation, sizing, pressure considerations, valves, connections, and appliance requirements.

Review combustion air carefully. Fuel-burning appliances require adequate combustion, ventilation, and dilution air under applicable conditions. Candidates should understand the methods used to determine required air and how openings or ducts are arranged.

Also study fuel-burning appliances, vents, and connectors. Questions may involve vent materials, connectors, equipment installation, combustion products, clearances, or other requirements affecting safe appliance operation.

For Plumbing Fixtures, review water heaters, water closets, urinals, specialty fixtures, tubs, showers, sinks, and lavatories. Understand how fixture requirements interact with water supply, drainage, and general installation provisions.

For Water Supply and Distribution, review service piping, distribution systems, pressure, pipe sizing, valves, and protection against contamination. Potable-water protection should be studied carefully because plumbing systems must prevent cross-connections and other conditions that could compromise water quality.

For Sanitary Drainage, review drainage fixture units, horizontal and vertical drainage piping, building drains, cleanouts, slope, sumps, ejectors, indirect waste, and special waste systems.

For Plumbing Vent Requirements, study vent connections, grades, terminals, acceptable configurations, and the relationship between venting and trap-seal protection.

For Traps, Interceptors, and Separators, review trap installation, trap seals, prohibited arrangements, grease interceptors, oil separators, and other devices used for particular waste conditions.
