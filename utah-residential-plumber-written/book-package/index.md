## State Requirements

Utah recognizes the **Residential Journeyman Plumber** as a distinct plumbing license classification. Candidates can qualify through approved plumbing education combined with residential plumbing experience or through the applicable experience-only pathway.

For examination eligibility, the standard education pathway requires completion of **six semesters or the competency-exam equivalent** together with **6,000 hours of residential plumbing experience as a licensed Apprentice Plumber**.

An alternative examination-eligibility pathway requires **12,000 hours of residential plumbing experience as a licensed Apprentice Plumber**.

Utah's Residential Journeyman Plumber licensing requirements include approved apprenticeship education and documented work experience. The standard licensure pathway requires at least **432 hours of approved apprenticeship education** and **6,000 hours of qualifying apprentice plumbing experience**.

Utah also provides an experience-based pathway involving **12,000 hours of qualifying full-time experience** under the requirements established by the state's plumbing licensing statutes and rules.

The written examination is one portion of the licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable practical examination, education, experience, and application requirements before the Residential Journeyman Plumber license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Utah Residential Plumber study plan should begin with **Drain, Waste, and Vent systems**. With 20 questions, DWV represents more than one-fifth of the examination. Review drainage piping, fittings, cleanouts, traps, building drains, venting principles, sizing, slopes, and fixture connections.

**Water Supply** contains 15 questions and should also receive substantial study time. Review residential water service, distribution piping, pressure, sizing, valves, materials, fixture demand, and related code requirements.

**Water Heaters** account for 7 questions. Candidates should understand installation requirements, safety devices, temperature and pressure protection, connections, clearances, combustion requirements where applicable, and practical water-heater principles.

**Fuel Gas** contains 6 questions. Study gas piping, shutoff valves, equipment connections, combustion air, venting, materials, and installation requirements associated with residential gas systems.

**Plumbing Fixtures** also accounts for 6 questions. Review fixture installation, fixture fittings, connections, accessibility to components, clearances, and code requirements for common residential fixtures.

**Plumbing Math** contains 6 questions. Use Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters together with IPT’s Pipe Trades Handbook to practice measurements, grade, offsets, area, volume, conversions, and practical pipe calculations.

**Plumbing Safety** contains another 6 questions. OSHA preparation should include excavation safety and other construction hazards associated with residential plumbing operations.

**Indirect and Special Waste** contains 5 questions. Review indirect waste piping, receptors, air gaps, air breaks, special waste connections, interceptors, separators, and other specialized drainage requirements.

**Pipe and Fittings** also contains 5 questions. Candidates should understand common piping materials, joining procedures, fittings, valves, supports, and practical installation methods.

**Hydronics** contributes 4 questions. Review hydronic heating concepts, piping arrangements, components, valves, circulation, and the interaction between plumbing and mechanical systems.

**Plumbing Drawing** and **Structural Penetration and Insulation** each contain 3 questions. Candidates should practice reading plans, recognizing plumbing symbols, following piping routes, and understanding how plumbing installations interact with structural framing and insulation.

## Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

Timed reference practice should become part of the study routine. Start with a practice question, determine its category, choose the most appropriate book, and locate the supporting material. Recording how long each lookup takes can help identify books or topics that require more practice.

When a question is missed, determine whether the problem came from trade knowledge, mathematics, selecting the wrong reference, misunderstanding the code language, or overlooking an exception. Identifying the cause helps make future preparation more focused.

Candidates should also connect technical study to practical residential plumbing work. Understanding why a vent is required, how water demand affects pipe sizing, why a trap seal matters, or how a water heater is protected makes code provisions easier to interpret and remember.
