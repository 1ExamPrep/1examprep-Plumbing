Open Book Test

The Idaho Plumbing Specialty Water Pump Journeyman exam is an open book exam. Open book doesn’t mean you can look up everything slowly—it means you must be able to use your code book efficiently under a time limit. For specialty journeyman testing, your preparation should focus on the exact exam behavior: recognize what the question is testing, locate the right section quickly, confirm the requirement, and move on.

Idaho’s plumbing exam bulletin states you may:

  • Highlight your book
  • Tab different sections
  • Leave notes in your reference material

The bulletin also states that loose paper in your reference material will not be allowed. That means your study system needs to be built into the book itself through clean highlighting, a simple tab strategy, and repeated practice. It also means you should avoid study habits that rely on loose worksheets, removable note pages, or extra inserts you cannot use in the testing room.

How open-book exams are won:

  • Topic recognition: quickly identify whether the question is about an installation requirement, a limitation, a definition, a prohibited condition, or an exception
  • Navigation: find the correct chapter/section efficiently without guessing
  • Confirmation: verify the exact wording or requirement before selecting an answer
  • Pacing: protect your time by avoiding long searches that break your rhythm

Test Information and Study Materials

Your best results on a code-based open-book exam come from practicing how to use the code, not just reading it. The UPC is your tool—so your preparation should focus on building comfort with the code’s structure and training efficient “find-and-confirm” habits.

  • Learn the code’s structure first. Before you chase details, understand how the UPC is organized: chapters, section numbering, and where general requirements and definitions are located. Navigation speed improves dramatically when you know the “map.”
  • Study in “lookup mode.” Use practice questions as navigation drills: read the question, identify the topic, locate the section, confirm the requirement, then answer. This builds real exam skill faster than passive reading.
  • Tab by major topics. Tabs are helpful when they act like signposts. Keep tabs focused on major sections you repeatedly use. Too many tabs can slow you down and create clutter that makes the book harder to use.
  • Highlight for speed. Highlight keywords, thresholds, and exceptions—items that help your eyes land on what matters. Avoid highlighting entire paragraphs; heavy highlighting reduces scan speed.
  • Train to spot exceptions. Code questions often hinge on a small exception or condition. In practice, make it a habit to look for “except,” “shall not,” “unless,” and similar language so you don’t miss the detail that changes the correct answer.
  • Practice with a timer that matches your exam. Since the Specialty Journeyman exam is 2.5 hours, practice shorter timed sets. Your goal is to learn how long a “reasonable lookup” takes before you choose to move on.
  • Review missed questions by learning the location. Don’t only learn the correct answer—learn where it lives in the code. Open-book speed improves when your brain remembers “where to find it.”
  • Stay exam-compliant while studying. Since loose paper isn’t allowed in reference material, build your organization directly into the code book through allowed tabs, highlighting, and notes.

A practical study rhythm for specialty candidates:

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

  • Session 1: Topic review + navigation drill (learn where key sections live)
  • Session 2: Timed practice set (fast lookups and pace control)
  • Session 3: Review missed questions and confirm the exact supporting language
  • Session 4: Mixed timed set to build confidence and consistency

This approach keeps your prep practical and exam-aligned. The goal is to make the UPC feel familiar and usable—so exam day is more about confirming and answering, not searching and guessing.
