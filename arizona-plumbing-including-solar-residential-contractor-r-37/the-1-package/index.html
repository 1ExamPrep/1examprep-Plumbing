Exam Details

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors administers contractor licensing in Arizona, while PSI administers the trade examination program. Applicants pursuing the R-37 Plumbing, Including Solar Residential classification must complete the examination and licensing requirements that apply to the qualifying party and business entity.

The qualifying party is the individual whose trade experience and examination qualifications support the contractor license. The qualifying party must satisfy the applicable Arizona requirements and remain associated with the licensed business in the capacity recognized by the state.

The R-37 scope includes the installation and repair of residential water and gas piping systems, fire-protection systems, sewage-treatment systems, fixtures, vents, industry-related devices, and solar applications. The classification includes the applicable R-37R residential plumbing subclassifications.

The R-37 trade examination contains 100 questions. Candidates are allowed 240 minutes and must earn a minimum score of 70 percent. The exam may include questions involving code requirements, practical plumbing knowledge, calculations, safety, installation methods, system components, and solar applications.

Exam subjects may involve water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, vents, fixtures, fuel-gas piping, mechanical systems, hydronic heating, boilers, fire sprinklers, wastewater treatment, excavation safety, utility-location practices, solar water heating, and solar pool heating.

Arizona contractor applicants are also subject to the state’s Statutes and Rules requirement unless an approved waiver applies. This portion is separate from the R-37 trade examination and addresses laws and rules affecting licensed contractors.

Passing the examination does not automatically issue a contractor license. The Arizona Registrar of Contractors must review the complete application, qualifying-party information, background-check documentation, business records, bond, financial-protection requirements, and other required materials.

Open Book Test

The Arizona R-37 Plumbing, Including Solar Residential trade examination is an open-book test using approved reference materials. Candidates may consult permitted books during the examination, but the number and variety of references make organized preparation essential.

An open-book exam does not mean every answer can be found through a quick search. Candidates may need to interpret code language, apply a formula, compare installation requirements, select a pipe size, recognize a safety hazard, or determine which standard controls the situation described.

Sprinkler questions may involve NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, or NFPA 13R, depending on the building and system described.

State Requirements

The R-37 license is a residential specialty classification. It covers residential plumbing work within the scope established by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors, including applicable solar plumbing systems. Commercial plumbing work may require a commercial or dual classification.

The proposed qualifying party must demonstrate the qualifications required for the classification. Experience documentation may need to describe plumbing responsibilities, project types, systems installed or repaired, supervisory duties, and the length of relevant trade experience.

The legal entity applying for the license must be properly formed and identified. Arizona may issue contractor licenses to eligible sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, and corporations. The business name, ownership records, EIN documentation, bond, and application should remain consistent.

Arizona requires a contractor license bond. The required amount depends on the license category and anticipated annual gross volume. Residential contractors must also satisfy the state’s residential financial-protection requirements through the applicable Recovery Fund process or authorized alternative.

Applicants must submit required examination results and supporting documentation within the applicable acceptance period. Background checks are also part of the licensing process for individuals named on the contractor application.

After issuance, the contractor must work within the authorized R-37 scope, maintain the required bond, renew the license, and keep licensing records current. Changes involving the business entity, ownership, qualifying party, address, or bond may require additional filings.

Test Information and Study Materials

Effective R-37 preparation should combine code navigation, calculations, trade knowledge, safety review, and timed practice. Begin by learning the purpose of each reference so you can quickly select the most likely source for a question.

Plumbing-code study can include fixtures, potable-water systems, pipe sizing, drainage, waste and vent systems, traps, cleanouts, backflow protection, indirect waste, and storm drainage. Fuel-gas review may include piping materials, sizing, pressure, shutoff valves, appliance connections, combustion air, and venting.

Sprinkler preparation should include familiarity with the differences among NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. Candidates should recognize which standard applies to the building or occupancy described before searching for a requirement.

Trade mathematics should include fractions, decimals, conversions, area, volume, pressure, flow, pipe offsets, slopes, and system-sizing concepts. Hydronic and boiler review may involve heat transfer, piping layouts, pumps, controls, expansion, air removal, and system safety.

Solar study should address collectors, storage tanks, circulation methods, controls, freeze protection, system sizing, pool heating, water heating, installation practices, and maintenance. OSHA review should focus on the construction hazards commonly encountered during plumbing and solar work.

Timed practice helps candidates manage the 240-minute examination. A practical strategy is to answer direct questions first, mark questions requiring longer research, and return to them after completing faster items.
