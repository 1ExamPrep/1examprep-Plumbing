Exam Details

The Arizona R-37 Plumbing, Including Solar residential trade examination contains 100 questions. Candidates are allowed 240 minutes to complete the examination, and a minimum score of 70 percent is required to pass.

The classification covers the installation and repair of water and gas piping systems, fire-protection systems, sewage treatment systems, fixtures, vents, devices common to the plumbing industry, and solar applications. The scope also includes the work covered by the R-37R residential plumbing subclassifications.

Exam preparation may include drainage, waste and vent systems, indirect waste, water supply, backflow prevention, roof drains, storm drainage, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, accessibility, general regulations, interceptors, traps, isometric analysis, fuel gas, septic and sewer systems, residential fire sprinklers, excavation, hydronic heating, boilers, and solar thermal systems.

Questions may require candidates to locate a code provision, interpret a definition, use a sizing table, identify a permitted material, evaluate a piping arrangement, perform a calculation, recognize a safety requirement, or determine which technical standard applies to a particular situation.

Solar questions may involve solar energy concepts, collectors, storage, piping, circulation, controls, system configurations, heat transfer, solar water heating, solar pool heating, installation practices, operation, and maintenance.

Candidates should practice recognizing the subject of each question before opening a book. Selecting the correct reference quickly can reduce unnecessary searching and leave more time for interpreting requirements, completing calculations, and reviewing answers.

Open Book Test

The Arizona Plumbing, Including Solar Residential Contractor R-37 examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use approved reference materials during the examination under the testing provider’s current rules.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need for preparation. The reference library contains numerous detailed publications, and candidates may not have enough time to search every book for every answer. Effective preparation combines trade knowledge with familiarity with the books.

Begin by learning the purpose of each reference. The International Plumbing Code supports many questions involving water distribution, sanitary drainage, waste and vent systems, plumbing fixtures, traps, storm drainage, indirect waste, water heaters, materials, inspections, and testing.

The International Fuel Gas Code supports fuel gas piping, demand, sizing, appliance connections, shutoff valves, regulators, combustion air, venting, and testing. The International Mechanical Code addresses mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, hydronic systems, and related installations.

NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R apply to different sprinkler-system conditions. Candidates should understand the scope of each standard so they can select the appropriate reference based on the occupancy and system described.

The OSHA construction regulations support safety questions, while the Arizona Administrative Code material addresses onsite wastewater provisions. The remaining trade references provide information about calculations, piping, boilers, hydronic heating, underground utilities, and solar thermal systems.

Candidates should review the current examination rules before preparing their books. Requirements may address binding, tabs, highlighting, handwritten notes, attachments, inserts, and loose materials.

Licensing Steps

Begin by confirming that the R-37 Plumbing, Including Solar residential classification matches the contracting work the applicant or business intends to perform. Arizona offers separate residential, commercial, and dual classifications, so applicants should select the classification that corresponds with their planned scope of work.

Identify the qualifying party for the license. The qualifying party is responsible for meeting the applicable experience, knowledge, and examination requirements associated with the requested classification.

Complete the R-37 trade examination unless an authorized waiver applies. The qualifying party may also need to complete the applicable Arizona Statutes and Rules requirement as part of the contractor licensing process.

Complete the required background checks and gather the documents associated with the applicant’s business structure. Arizona contractor licenses may be issued to eligible business entities, including sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, and corporations.

Obtain the required contractor bond and prepare the application documents. Supporting materials may include identification, business formation records, qualifying-party information, examination results, experience documentation, background-check records, bond paperwork, disclosures, and signed forms.

Because the R-37 classification includes solar work, the applicant must submit the applicable solar warranty documentation with the license application.

Submit the completed application to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. The agency reviews the application and supporting documents before determining whether the license can be issued. Passing the examination is one part of the licensing process and does not automatically provide a contractor license.

State Requirements

Arizona contractor licensing is administered by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants must satisfy the requirements associated with the requested classification, qualifying party, relevant experience, required examinations or authorized waivers, background checks, contractor bond, business documentation, and license application.

The R-37 classification applies to residential plumbing, including solar work, within its established scope. It should not automatically be treated as authorization for commercial plumbing or contracting work outside the residential classification.

The qualifying party should have experience relevant to the plumbing and solar work covered by the license. Applicants should maintain accurate records describing employment, projects, duties, supervision, installation work, repairs, and hands-on trade responsibilities.

Business names, ownership information, identification records, bond documents, background-check materials, and qualifying-party details should remain consistent throughout the application. Missing or conflicting information may delay the state review process.

Applicants for a license classification that includes solar work must submit a copy of the applicable solar warranty. Current forms and instructions should be used when completing the licensing process.
