Exam Details

The Arizona Plumbing, Including Solar Residential Contractor R-37 examination evaluates knowledge associated with residential plumbing systems, related mechanical work, fire protection, sewage treatment, construction safety, and solar applications. The classification includes the installation and repair of water and gas piping systems, fire-protection systems, sewage treatment systems, fixtures, vents, devices common to the plumbing industry, and applicable solar work.

The R-37 trade examination contains 100 questions. Candidates are allowed 240 minutes to complete the examination, and a minimum score of 70 percent is required to pass.

Exam preparation may involve drainage, waste and vent systems, water distribution, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, backflow prevention, roof drains, storm drainage, indirect waste, specialty plumbing, interceptors, traps, isometric analysis, fuel gas systems, septic and sewer systems, fire sprinklers, excavation, boilers, hydronic heating, and solar thermal applications.

Questions may require candidates to interpret definitions, identify installation requirements, use code tables, select materials, evaluate piping arrangements, recognize safe work practices, perform trade calculations, or determine which reference applies to a specific residential condition.

Solar-related subjects may involve solar water-heating systems, solar pool heating, collectors, storage, system piping, circulation, pumps, controls, heat transfer, installation practices, operation, and maintenance. Candidates should understand how solar thermal work connects with plumbing, mechanical, hydronic, and safety requirements.

Strong preparation includes learning the purpose of every reference. Candidates should practice recognizing the subject of a question before beginning a search. Selecting the correct book quickly can reduce unnecessary navigation and provide more time for interpreting requirements and completing calculations.

Open Book Test

The Arizona R-37 trade examination is an open-book test. Approved reference materials may be used during the examination under the current testing rules.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need for preparation. The R-37 reference library contains multiple codes, standards, technical manuals, and trade publications. Candidates who are unfamiliar with the books may spend too much time searching for individual answers.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books help create a more organized navigation system. Tabs provide visual markers for major chapters and subjects, while highlighting helps selected information stand out during review. Students should use the books regularly throughout their 6 months of course access so the location and organization of important material become familiar.

Effective navigation begins by identifying the subject of the question. Water supply, drainage, venting, fixtures, traps, and water-heater questions may lead to the International Plumbing Code. Gas piping, appliance connections, regulators, combustion air, and venting may require the International Fuel Gas Code. Mechanical-system questions may require the International Mechanical Code.

Fire sprinkler questions may involve NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, or NFPA 13R. Candidates should understand the scope of each standard so they can select the appropriate reference based on the occupancy and sprinkler system described.

Students should practice using tables of contents, indexes, definitions, chapter headings, technical tables, formulas, diagrams, exceptions, footnotes, appendices, and cross-references. Reading the surrounding language is important because exceptions or qualifications may affect how a provision applies.

Reference materials and book preparation must comply with the testing provider’s current rules. Candidates should review the current examination bulletin before reporting for the test.

Licensing Steps

Begin by reviewing the Arizona contractor classifications and confirming that the R-37 Plumbing, Including Solar residential classification matches the work the applicant or business intends to perform. Arizona separates residential, commercial, and dual contractor classifications, so applicants should select the classification that fits their intended scope.

Identify the qualifying party for the license. The qualifying party is responsible for satisfying the experience, knowledge, and examination requirements associated with the requested classification and maintaining the required relationship with the applicant business.

Complete the required R-37 trade examination unless an authorized waiver applies. The qualifying party may also need to complete the applicable Arizona Statutes and Rules requirement as part of the contractor licensing process.

Complete the required background checks and gather the documentation associated with the applicant’s business structure. Arizona contractor licenses may be issued to eligible sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, corporations, and other approved entities.

Obtain the required contractor bond and prepare the supporting application materials. These may include identification, entity records, qualifying-party information, examination results, experience documentation, background-check records, bond paperwork, disclosures, and signed application forms.

Because the R-37 classification includes solar work, applicants must submit the applicable solar warranty documentation with the contractor license application.

Submit the completed application to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. The agency reviews the information and supporting documents before determining whether the license can be issued. Passing the trade examination is one part of the licensing process and does not automatically grant the contractor license.

State Requirements

Arizona contractor licenses are administered by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants must satisfy the requirements connected with the requested classification, qualifying party, relevant experience, required examinations or approved waivers, background checks, contractor bond, business records, and application documentation.

The R-37 classification applies to residential plumbing, including solar work, within the scope assigned to the classification. It should not automatically be treated as authorization for commercial plumbing or work outside the permitted residential scope.

The qualifying party should have experience relevant to the plumbing and solar work covered by the classification. Applicants should maintain clear records describing employment, projects, supervisory duties, installation work, repair responsibilities, and hands-on trade experience.

Business names, ownership information, identification records, bond documents, background-check materials, and qualifying-party details should remain consistent throughout the application. Missing or conflicting information may delay the review process.

Applicants pursuing a classification that includes solar work must provide the required solar warranty documentation. Current forms and application instructions should be used when preparing the license submission.
