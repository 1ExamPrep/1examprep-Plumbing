Exam Details

The Arizona Plumbing, Including Solar Residential Contractor R-37 examination evaluates knowledge associated with residential plumbing work and applicable solar systems. Candidates should be prepared to work with plumbing codes, fuel gas requirements, mechanical provisions, sprinkler standards, construction-safety regulations, and Arizona-specific wastewater rules.

Major plumbing subjects may include water supply, sanitary drainage, waste and vent systems, plumbing fixtures, traps, water heaters, indirect waste, storm drainage, materials, testing, and installation requirements. Questions may also involve fuel gas piping, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, mechanical equipment, residential fire sprinklers, excavation safety, and onsite wastewater treatment.

The R-37 classification includes residential plumbing and solar-related work within its authorized scope. Although the exam-room book package listed here focuses on the approved references included with this product, candidates should also prepare for the trade knowledge and solar concepts associated with the classification.

Questions may require candidates to locate a code section, interpret a definition, apply a technical table, identify a permitted material, recognize a safety requirement, evaluate a piping arrangement, or determine which standard applies to the condition described.

The reference library contains publications with different purposes. The International Plumbing Code supports core plumbing subjects. The International Fuel Gas Code addresses gas piping and related equipment. The International Mechanical Code covers mechanical installations, while the NFPA standards address sprinkler systems for different applications.

The OSHA construction regulations support questions involving excavation and workplace safety. The Arizona Administrative Code material provides state-specific provisions related to onsite wastewater treatment facilities. Understanding the function of each publication is essential for efficient open-book exam preparation.

Open Book Test

The Arizona R-37 trade examination is an open-book test. Approved references may be used during the examination under the testing provider’s current rules.

Open-book testing does not remove the need for study. Candidates must work with several detailed codes and standards, and searching every publication without a clear plan can consume valuable testing time. Preparation should include learning which reference applies to each subject and how that book is organized.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books in this package support a more efficient navigation process. Tabs provide visual markers for major chapters, while highlighting helps selected information stand out during review. Candidates should practice with the books regularly so the structure of each reference becomes familiar.

A practical open-book strategy begins by identifying the subject of the question. Water supply, drainage, venting, plumbing fixtures, traps, and water-heater questions may lead to the International Plumbing Code. Fuel gas piping, appliance connections, regulators, combustion air, and venting may require the International Fuel Gas Code.

Mechanical-system questions may require the International Mechanical Code. Fire sprinkler questions may involve NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, or NFPA 13R depending on the building, occupancy, and system described. Excavation and construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

Candidates should learn how to use the table of contents, index, definitions, chapter headings, diagrams, technical tables, exceptions, footnotes, appendices, and cross-references. Reading the surrounding language is important because a nearby qualification may affect the correct answer.

Before reporting for the examination, candidates should review the current testing rules concerning approved references, binding, tabs, highlighting, handwritten notes, loose materials, and other book modifications.

Licensing Steps

Begin by reviewing the Arizona contractor classifications and confirming that the R-37 Plumbing, Including Solar residential classification matches the work the applicant or business intends to perform. The classification should correspond with the planned residential plumbing and solar contracting services.

Identify the qualifying party for the license. The qualifying party is responsible for meeting the applicable knowledge, experience, and examination requirements connected with the requested classification.

Complete the R-37 trade examination unless an authorized waiver applies. The qualifying party may also need to complete the applicable Arizona statutes and rules requirement as part of the contractor licensing process.

Complete the required background-check process and gather the documents associated with the applicant’s business structure. Arizona contractor licenses may be issued to eligible sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, corporations, and other approved entities.

Obtain the required contractor bond and prepare the supporting application records. These may include identification, business documents, qualifying-party information, examination results, experience records, background-check information, bond paperwork, disclosures, and signed forms.

Because the R-37 classification includes solar work, applicants must also address the solar documentation required for the license application.

Submit the completed application to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Passing the trade examination is one part of the licensing process and does not automatically result in license issuance. The applicant must satisfy the applicable state requirements and receive approval.

State Requirements

Arizona contractor licensing is administered by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants must meet the requirements connected with the requested classification, qualifying party, relevant experience, examinations or approved waivers, background checks, contractor bond, business documentation, and license application.

The R-37 classification applies to residential plumbing, including solar work, within the scope assigned to the classification. It should not automatically be treated as authorization for commercial plumbing or work outside the permitted residential scope.

The qualifying party should have experience relevant to the plumbing and solar work covered by the license. Applicants should maintain organized records describing employment, projects, responsibilities, supervision, installations, repairs, and hands-on trade experience.

Business names, ownership information, identification records, qualifying-party details, background-check materials, and bond documents should remain consistent throughout the application. Missing or conflicting information may delay the licensing review.

Applicants should use the current state forms and instructions for their licensing cycle. State requirements, application procedures, and examination rules may be updated.
