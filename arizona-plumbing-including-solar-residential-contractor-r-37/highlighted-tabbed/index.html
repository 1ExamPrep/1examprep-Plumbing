Exam Details

The Arizona Plumbing, Including Solar Residential Contractor (R-37) examination evaluates knowledge associated with residential plumbing work and solar applications. The classification scope includes the installation and repair of water and gas piping systems, fire-protection systems, sewage treatment systems, fixtures, vents, devices common to the plumbing industry, and applicable solar work.

The R-37 trade examination contains 100 questions. Candidates are allowed 240 minutes to complete the examination, and a minimum score of 70 percent is required to pass.

Exam preparation may involve drainage, waste and vent systems, water distribution, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, fuel gas piping, mechanical installations, residential sprinkler systems, onsite wastewater treatment, excavation, boilers, hydronic heating, pipe-trade mathematics, and solar thermal systems.

Questions may require candidates to identify the correct code requirement, apply a sizing table, interpret a technical definition, select a material, evaluate a piping arrangement, perform a calculation, recognize a safety requirement, or determine which reference applies to the condition described.

Solar-related preparation may include solar energy principles, solar water-heating applications, solar pool heating, system components, collectors, storage, piping arrangements, controls, heat transfer, installation practices, system operation, and safety considerations.

Candidates should develop the ability to recognize the subject of a question before opening a reference. A drainage question may lead to the International Plumbing Code, while a fuel gas question may require the International Fuel Gas Code. A boiler or hydronic system question may involve the specialized heating references, and a solar thermal question may require the Solar Water and Pool Heating Design and Installation Manual.

Licensing Steps

Begin by reviewing the Arizona contractor license classifications and confirming that the R-37 Plumbing, Including Solar residential classification matches the work the business intends to perform. The classification includes residential plumbing and applicable solar work within its established scope.

Identify the qualifying party for the license. The qualifying party is responsible for satisfying the knowledge, experience, and examination requirements connected with the requested classification and maintaining the required relationship with the applicant business.

Complete the required trade examination and any additional examination requirements associated with the classification unless an authorized waiver applies. Applicants should also complete the applicable Arizona statutes and rules requirement as directed during the licensing process.

Complete the required background checks and gather the documentation associated with the applicant’s business structure. Arizona licenses may be issued to eligible sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, corporations, and other approved business entities.

Obtain the required contractor bond and prepare the supporting application materials. Documents may include identification, business records, qualifying-party information, examination results, experience documentation, background-check records, bond paperwork, disclosures, and signed application forms.

Applicants pursuing a classification that includes solar work must also address the applicable solar licensing documentation, including the required solar warranty materials.

Submit the completed application to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. The agency reviews the application and supporting records before determining whether the requested license can be issued. Passing an examination alone does not automatically provide a contractor license.

State Requirements

Arizona contractor licensing is administered by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants must satisfy the requirements associated with the selected classification, qualifying party, relevant experience, required examinations or authorized waivers, background checks, contractor bond, business records, and application documentation.

The R-37 classification applies to residential plumbing, including solar, within the scope established for the classification. It should not automatically be treated as authorization for commercial plumbing work or contracting activities outside the permitted residential scope.

The qualifying party should have experience relevant to plumbing and the other work covered by the classification. Applicants should maintain accurate records describing employment, project duties, supervision, system installation, repair work, and hands-on trade responsibilities.

Applicants should ensure that business names, ownership information, identification records, bond documents, background-check materials, and qualifying-party details remain consistent throughout the application.

Because the classification includes solar work, applicants must submit the required solar warranty documentation as part of the licensing process. The warranty should comply with the requirements applicable to the license application.

Applicants should use the current forms, examination information, and application instructions for their licensing cycle. Incomplete or inconsistent documentation may delay the state review process.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation by dividing the examination library into major subject groups. Use the International Plumbing Code for drainage, vents, water supply, fixtures, traps, water heaters, indirect waste, storm drainage, materials, and testing.

Use the International Fuel Gas Code for gas piping, demand, system sizing, appliance connections, regulators, shutoff valves, combustion air, venting, and testing. Review the International Mechanical Code for mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, and hydronic systems.

Compare NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R carefully. Each standard addresses different sprinkler-system applications and occupancy conditions. Candidates should identify the system or building described before selecting a sprinkler reference.

Review OSHA requirements for excavation, protective systems, access and egress, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, fall protection, electrical hazards, and safe construction practices. Use the Arizona 811 material to reinforce utility-location and safe-digging procedures.

Practice calculations involving measurements, areas, volumes, offsets, slopes, pressure, capacity, pipe sizing, flow, and system performance. Hydronic and boiler review should include equipment, piping arrangements, circulation, controls, heat transfer, and safe operation.

Solar study should address solar thermal principles, solar water-heating systems, pool-heating applications, collectors, storage, circulation, controls, system configurations, installation methods, and maintenance considerations.

Complete timed practice with the highlighted and tabbed books. Record which references take the longest to navigate and which topics produce repeated errors. Use those results to guide additional study.
