Exam Details

Preparation for the Arizona Plumbing Commercial Contractor (C-37) examination should focus on the technical knowledge involved in commercial plumbing and related systems. Candidates should build familiarity with the major plumbing codes while also reviewing the mechanical, fuel-gas, sprinkler, wastewater, mathematics, boiler, hydronic-heating, safety, and utility topics represented in the reference set.

The International Plumbing Code, 2018 should be one of the core study references. Candidates should practice locating information involving water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, plumbing fixtures, traps, indirect waste, storm drainage, and other plumbing-system requirements. Understanding the organization of the code can make it easier to identify the correct chapter or section during practice.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2018 supports preparation involving fuel-gas piping, system materials, sizing, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, testing, and related installation concepts. Commercial plumbing contractors can encounter fuel-gas systems as part of broader building-service work, making this reference an important part of preparation.

The International Mechanical Code, 2018 adds mechanical-system subjects that can overlap with plumbing and fuel-gas work. Candidates should become familiar with equipment, ventilation, piping-related concepts, and other mechanical requirements represented in the code.

Fire-sprinkler preparation is supported through NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. These standards cover different sprinkler-system applications, so candidates should understand the purpose of each reference and practice selecting the correct standard based on the system or occupancy involved.

Mathematics is another important part of plumbing and pipefitting preparation. Candidates should be comfortable working with measurements, dimensions, areas, volumes, slopes, offsets, and other calculations. Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition provides a trade-focused reference for developing these skills.

IPT’s Pipe Trades Handbook adds practical information involving pipe materials, dimensions, fittings, formulas, and field-reference data. Boiler Operator’s Guide and Modern Hydronic Heating support preparation involving boiler systems, hydronic circulation, controls, piping arrangements, and related mechanical concepts.

Open Book Test

The Arizona Plumbing Commercial Contractor (C-37) exam should be prepared for as an open book test. Open-book preparation requires both technical knowledge and efficient reference navigation.

Having access to books does not eliminate the need to study. Plumbing codes, NFPA standards, wastewater provisions, mechanical references, and trade manuals contain large amounts of information. Candidates who are not familiar with the books may spend too much time searching for answers during testing.

A stronger approach is to learn the purpose of each reference before focusing on detailed questions. If a question involves drainage, venting, fixtures, or water distribution, the International Plumbing Code may be the best starting point. Fuel-gas topics may lead to the International Fuel Gas Code, while mechanical-system topics may require the International Mechanical Code.

Sprinkler questions require careful attention to the correct standard. NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R should not be treated as interchangeable. Candidates should understand the basic scope of each book and practice choosing the correct reference before searching for the answer.

Safety questions may lead to OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Underground utility topics can be reviewed through the Arizona 811 - Arizona Blue Stake Brochure. Wastewater questions may point toward the Arizona Administrative Code reference, while boiler and hydronic-heating topics can be studied through their dedicated books.

During practice sessions, identify the subject first. Then choose the reference most likely to contain the answer. Use the table of contents, index, definitions, chapter headings, formulas, technical tables, and section organization to locate the supporting material efficiently.

Preparing for the C-37 trade examination is one part of pursuing the applicable Arizona contractor license. Candidates should also keep the administrative and qualification requirements associated with licensing organized.

  1. Determine the license applicant. Decide whether the contractor license will be pursued by an individual or through an eligible business entity.
  2. Select the appropriate classification. Identify the commercial plumbing classification associated with the work the business intends to perform.
  3. Complete the contractor license application. Provide the legal applicant information, business details, contact information, and requested classification.
  4. Identify the qualifying individual. Make sure the person responsible for satisfying the trade qualification is properly associated with the applicant.
  5. Complete applicable examination requirements. The qualifying individual completes the required trade and business-related testing associated with the licensing process.
  6. Provide required business documentation. Organize the entity records and other documentation requested through the licensing process.
  7. Complete applicable financial or bonding requirements. Prepare the required documentation associated with the contractor license application.
  8. Resolve remaining application requirements. Submit missing information and address any outstanding application items before license issuance.

Candidates should keep exam preparation and licensing responsibilities aligned. Organizing study activities while working through the contractor application can help create a more coordinated path toward completing the licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-37 study plan should combine online lessons, code review, practice-oriented questions, and regular reference-navigation exercises. Begin by learning the role of each book before trying to memorize detailed information.

Use the International Plumbing Code as a foundation for plumbing-system preparation. Practice locating water supply, drainage, venting, fixtures, traps, sanitary systems, and storm-drainage subjects through the index and chapter structure.

Next, review the International Fuel Gas Code and International Mechanical Code. Pay attention to where plumbing, fuel-gas, and mechanical systems overlap and practice deciding which book is most likely to contain the answer to a particular question.

Study NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R together so you understand the difference between the sprinkler-system applications covered by each standard. Practice choosing the correct NFPA reference before searching for specific details.

Use Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters to strengthen calculation skills. Work through formulas and trade problems rather than relying only on recognition. IPT’s Pipe Trades Handbook can support practical pipe dimensions, field data, and additional trade information.

Review Boiler Operator’s Guide and Modern Hydronic Heating for boiler, circulation, piping, equipment, and heating-system concepts. These references help broaden preparation beyond basic plumbing-code subjects.

Use the Arizona Administrative Code material for wastewater-related subjects and Arizona 811 for underground utility awareness. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 should remain part of your regular study rotation for construction safety.

When working through practice questions, identify the subject before opening a reference. Decide whether the question involves plumbing, fuel gas, mechanical systems, sprinklers, wastewater, mathematics, pipe trades, boilers, hydronic heating, safety, or utility locating. Then choose the reference most likely to contain the information.

If a practice question is answered incorrectly, return to the supporting reference and review the surrounding section. This helps build understanding of the technical concept rather than encouraging memorization of an isolated answer.
