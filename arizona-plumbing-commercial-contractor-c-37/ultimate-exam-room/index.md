## Test Information and Study Materials

A multi-reference contractor exam requires an organized preparation strategy. Instead of attempting to memorize every page, focus on understanding the major trade concepts while learning how to retrieve detailed information from the appropriate source.

Begin with the International Plumbing Code and become comfortable with its structure. Learn where major plumbing subjects are organized and practice using the index, chapters, tables, definitions, and technical sections. The goal is to recognize where you should search before you begin turning pages.

Apply the same approach to the International Fuel Gas Code and International Mechanical Code. Fuel gas and mechanical topics should become recognizable enough that you can move directly toward the appropriate reference when a practice question presents one of those subjects.

Construction safety preparation should include familiarity with 29 CFR Part 1926. Instead of treating safety as an isolated subject, incorporate the OSHA reference into regular practice so you become comfortable distinguishing safety questions from plumbing-code questions.

The three NFPA sprinkler references should also be studied individually. Learn the purpose and organization of NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. Developing familiarity with each publication can help you make faster reference decisions when working through sprinkler-related questions.

Arizona-specific wastewater provisions should receive their own dedicated review. State material may be organized differently from the international codes, so repeated practice with the Arizona Administrative Code reference can help you become more comfortable navigating it.

Use your highlighted and tabbed books from the beginning of the course. The more often you locate supporting information during preparation, the more natural reference navigation can become. When you miss a practice question, find the applicable provision in the book and review why the correct answer applies.

As your preparation progresses, introduce timed practice sessions. Work through groups of questions while using the references and pay attention to how long you spend locating information. If certain subjects consistently slow you down, return to those books and review their organization.
