Exam Details

Preparation for the Arizona Plumbing Commercial Contractor (C-37) examination should focus on the technical knowledge needed to understand commercial plumbing systems and related construction work. Candidates should build familiarity with plumbing codes, fuel-gas systems, mechanical requirements, fire-sprinkler standards, wastewater provisions, mathematics, pipe trades, boilers, hydronic heating, excavation safety, and utility-location responsibilities.

The International Plumbing Code, 2018 is a central reference for plumbing-system preparation. Candidates should become comfortable locating requirements involving water distribution, drainage, venting, fixtures, traps, indirect waste, sanitary drainage, storm drainage, and related plumbing-system components.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2018 supports preparation involving fuel-gas piping, sizing, materials, installation, appliances, combustion air, venting, testing, and other fuel-gas concepts. Commercial plumbing contractors may encounter fuel-gas systems as part of broader mechanical or plumbing work, making this an important reference to study.

The International Mechanical Code, 2018 adds material involving mechanical systems and their relationship to plumbing and building services. Candidates should practice navigating the code and understanding where mechanical requirements may overlap with piping, fuel-gas, ventilation, or equipment-related work.

Fire-sprinkler systems are represented through NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R. These standards address different types of sprinkler installations and occupancies, so candidates should understand which reference applies to a given situation before trying to locate a detailed requirement.

Mathematics also deserves dedicated review. Plumbing and pipefitting work can involve measurements, pipe dimensions, areas, volumes, offsets, slopes, pressure concepts, and other calculations. Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters provides trade-focused preparation for these types of problems.

IPT’s Pipe Trades Handbook can support practical study involving pipe trades, materials, fittings, dimensions, formulas, and field-reference information. Boiler Operator’s Guide and Modern Hydronic Heating add preparation involving boilers, hydronic heating, circulation, system components, and related mechanical concepts.

Licensing Steps

Preparing for the trade examination is one part of pursuing the applicable Arizona contractor license. Candidates should also organize the administrative, qualifying, business, and documentation requirements associated with the licensing process.

  1. Determine the license applicant. Decide whether the license will be pursued by an individual or through an eligible business entity.
  2. Select the appropriate classification. Identify the commercial plumbing classification associated with the work the business plans to perform.
  3. Complete the contractor license application. Provide the legal applicant information, business details, contact information, and requested classification.
  4. Identify the qualifying individual. Make sure the person responsible for satisfying the trade qualification is properly associated with the applicant.
  5. Complete applicable examination requirements. The qualifying individual completes the required trade and business-related testing associated with the license process.
  6. Provide business documentation. Business entities should organize the records required for their legal structure.
  7. Complete financial or bonding requirements when applicable. Prepare the documentation required as part of the licensing process.
  8. Resolve outstanding application items. Submit missing documentation or address application deficiencies before license issuance.

Candidates should keep licensing responsibilities and exam preparation organized at the same time. A clear study schedule can help prevent the trade examination from becoming disconnected from the administrative side of the license process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Arizona Plumbing Commercial Contractor study plan should combine direct reference review, practice-oriented questions, code navigation, and trade calculations. Begin by learning the purpose of each book before focusing on detailed memorization.

Start with the International Plumbing Code because it provides a broad foundation for plumbing-system study. Practice locating water-supply, drainage, venting, fixture, trap, storm-drainage, and sanitary-system provisions through the index and chapter structure.

Next, study the International Fuel Gas Code and International Mechanical Code. Look for the areas where fuel-gas and mechanical systems interact with commercial plumbing work. Building familiarity with the organization of these books can help you choose the correct reference more quickly.

Review NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R together so you understand the different sprinkler applications represented by each standard. Candidates should avoid treating the three books as interchangeable because each has a specific scope.

Use Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters to strengthen calculation skills. Practice working through formulas and trade-related math rather than relying only on recognition. Use IPT’s Pipe Trades Handbook as a practical reference for pipe-related information, dimensions, and field data.

Study Boiler Operator’s Guide and Modern Hydronic Heating for boiler and hydronic-system concepts. These references can help candidates review equipment, circulation, system layouts, heating components, and related mechanical topics.

Use the Arizona Administrative Code wastewater material for on-site wastewater treatment provisions, and review the Arizona 811 brochure for utility-location and excavation-awareness topics.

Keep OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 in your regular study rotation. Safety questions can involve excavation, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, tools, equipment, and other construction hazards.

When completing practice questions, identify the subject first. Ask whether the question is primarily about plumbing, fuel gas, mechanical systems, sprinklers, wastewater, mathematics, pipe trades, boilers, hydronic heating, safety, or utility locating. Then choose the most appropriate reference.

If a practice question is answered incorrectly, return to the supporting reference and review the surrounding section. This helps build an understanding of the technical concept rather than encouraging memorization of isolated answers.
