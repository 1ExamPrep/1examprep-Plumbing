## Exam Details

The North Carolina Plumbing Class I Contractor licensing process includes a technical Plumbing Contractor examination and a Business and Law examination. The technical plumbing examination allows four hours. The Business and Law examination allows one and one-half hours.

The Business and Law examination may not be required for an applicant who already holds a currently active contractor license issued by the same Board and passed the Board’s Business and Law examination after August 2003. Candidates seeking an exemption must meet the Board’s applicable requirements.

The technical examination may address plumbing-code administration, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, fixtures, fixture fittings, water heaters, indirect waste, special waste, storm drainage, traps, interceptors, separators, testing, inspections, residential plumbing, and fuel gas systems.

Water-supply preparation should include materials, sizing, pressure, distribution piping, valves, backflow protection, cross-connection control, service piping, fixture supply, and protection against contamination. Candidates should understand how code requirements change according to the type of system and installation condition.

Drainage preparation may involve drainage fixture units, pipe sizing, slope, cleanouts, changes in direction, building drains, building sewers, branch piping, stacks, horizontal drainage, sumps, ejectors, and connections to approved disposal systems.

Venting subjects may include individual vents, common vents, wet venting, circuit venting, stack venting, vent stacks, relief vents, air admittance valves, vent sizing, developed length, termination, and protection of the trap seal.

Fixture questions may involve minimum fixture requirements, installation, accessibility, spacing, supports, connections, water closets, lavatories, sinks, showers, bathtubs, drinking fountains, service sinks, food-service fixtures, and specialized plumbing equipment.

Fuel gas preparation may include pipe sizing, approved materials, pressure limitations, shutoff valves, appliance connections, regulators, testing, combustion air, venting systems, chimneys, clearances, equipment installation, and protection of gas piping.

The Class I examination also requires familiarity with administrative and licensing responsibilities. Candidates should understand the roles of contractors, permit holders, code officials, inspectors, property owners, and the licensing Board.

## Open Book Test

The North Carolina Plumbing Class I Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates are required to bring the approved reference books assigned to their examination classification. Only the publications authorized for the examination may be taken into the testing room.

The six books included in this package correspond to the references identified for the Plumbing Class I Contractor examination. Candidates should use the same books during preparation so they become familiar with chapter organization, indexes, definitions, tables, appendices, and cross-references.

The Board permits original hard-bound, spiral-bound, or ring-bound reference materials. Commercially available, permanently attached index tabs may be used. Highlighting is permitted, but written notes, formulas, underlining, calculations, added text, homemade tabs, unauthorized divider pages, loose paper, and printed PDF copies are not allowed.

Open-book testing rewards preparation and familiarity. Candidates should not expect to read entire chapters during the examination. A better approach is to recognize the subject, select the proper book, locate the governing section, and confirm the answer by reading the surrounding language.

For example, a question about drainage pipe sizing belongs in the Plumbing Code, while a question about gas-pipe sizing belongs in the Fuel Gas Code. A permit or inspection question may require the Administrative Code and Policies. A licensing or professional-conduct question should direct the candidate to the Board’s Laws & Rules.

Questions about contracts, estimating, project supervision, financial controls, or business organization may require the business, law, and project management reference. Residential plumbing questions may require the Residential Code rather than the general Plumbing Code.

Before test day, candidates should inspect every reference and confirm that it complies with the current examination rules. A book that contains prohibited markings, loose material, unauthorized tabs, or copied pages may not be accepted in the testing room.

## State Requirements

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors administers Plumbing Class I contractor licensing. The Board reviews applications, verifies experience, conducts examinations through its testing provider, issues licenses, and enforces the laws and rules governing licensed contractors.

Applicants for Plumbing Class I or Class II contractor licensing must document 4,000 hours of qualifying hands-on experience in the installation, maintenance, service, or repair of plumbing systems. The experience must be supported by the documentation required in the application process.

Approved education may account for up to 50 percent of the experience requirement. Qualifying education must be directly related to the license sought and completed through a college, university, community college, or technical institute as part of a certificate, diploma, or degree program.

Class I is the broader plumbing classification. It applies to commercial, residential, industrial, and institutional buildings. Class II is limited to plumbing work involving single-family detached residential dwellings. Candidates should apply for the classification that matches their experience and intended scope of work.

The application must be reviewed and approved before an applicant may sit for the examination. Current application requirements include experience verification, a nationwide criminal background report, required signatures, supporting documentation, and the applicable examination fee.

Applicants must possess the necessary current North Carolina codebooks, the Board-issued Laws & Rules book, and the approved business and project management reference when applying. Submitting the application indicates that the candidate has the required books and is prepared to test.

North Carolina does not offer general license reciprocity for this classification. A limited technical-examination waiver may be available when an applicant holds an eligible South Carolina commercial mechanical plumbing license, has passed the applicable South Carolina technical examination, meets North Carolina’s experience requirements, and completes the full North Carolina application process.

A technical-examination waiver does not remove the North Carolina business-examination requirement. The applicant must still satisfy the applicable business portion unless another Board-approved exemption applies.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Build a study schedule that separates technical plumbing preparation from business and law preparation. For the technical examination, rotate among water distribution, drainage, venting, fixtures, fuel gas, residential requirements, and administration.

Practice calculations and table lookups using the Plumbing Code and Fuel Gas Code. Work on identifying fixture units, pipe sizes, developed lengths, slopes, vent requirements, gas demand, and other values that require accurate use of code tables.

Create timed lookup exercises. Choose a topic, locate the index entry, identify the correct chapter, read the governing section, and confirm whether related exceptions or definitions apply. This develops the navigation habits needed for an open-book examination.

Study definitions carefully. Plumbing, fuel gas, administrative, residential, and licensing terms may have specific meanings that differ from ordinary jobsite language. Understanding the defined term can quickly narrow the answer choices.

For the business examination, review contracts, bidding, estimating, financial statements, project scheduling, employment responsibilities, insurance, safety programs, recordkeeping, and risk management. Learn how legal and business responsibilities affect daily contractor operations.
