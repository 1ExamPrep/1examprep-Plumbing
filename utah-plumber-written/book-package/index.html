Exam Details

The Utah Plumber Written Exam is administered through Prov as part of Utah's plumbing examination program.

The current written examination contains 100 questions. Candidates are allowed 4 hours to complete the exam and must achieve a minimum passing score of 70%.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Basic Valves – 2 questions
  • Drain, Waste, and Vent (DWV) – 20 questions
  • Fuel Gas – 6 questions
  • Hydronics – 5 questions
  • Indirect & Special Waste – 10 questions
  • Pipe and Fittings – 7 questions
  • Plumbing Drawing – 3 questions
  • Plumbing Fixtures – 6 questions
  • Plumbing Math – 9 questions
  • Plumbing Safety – 9 questions
  • Structural Penetration and Insulation – 3 questions
  • Water Heaters – 5 questions
  • Water Supply – 15 questions

Drain, Waste, and Vent is the largest individual category with 20 questions. Water Supply follows with 15 questions, while Indirect and Special Waste contains 10 questions. Plumbing Math and Plumbing Safety each contribute 9 questions.

The exam requires candidates to move between code requirements, calculations, system design concepts, installation practices, and safety principles. A balanced study plan should therefore include both code navigation and practical plumbing knowledge.

Open Book Test

The Utah Plumber Written Exam is an open book test. Candidates may use permitted references under the examination provider's rules.

Open-book testing rewards candidates who already know how their references are organized. Simply bringing books into the testing room is not enough. Candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question and immediately selecting the reference most likely to contain the answer.

Questions about drainage, water distribution, fixtures, piping systems, or plumbing installations may direct candidates toward the plumbing code. Fuel-gas questions may require the fuel-gas code, while hydronic and mechanical-system questions may point toward mechanical requirements.

Safety questions may require familiarity with 29 CFR Part 1926. Residential sprinkler questions may involve NFPA 13D, while mathematics and practical pipe-trade calculations can be reinforced through Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters and IPT’s Pipe Trades Handbook.

With four hours for 100 questions, candidates average approximately 2.4 minutes per question. Efficient reference navigation can preserve valuable time for difficult calculations and detailed code questions.

Licensing Steps

Utah plumbing licensing is administered through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing. The written examination is one component of the process for applicants pursuing qualifying plumber licensure.

  1. Enter the plumbing trade through the applicable Utah pathway. Candidates typically build qualifying experience through licensed apprentice plumbing work and approved training.
  2. Complete required plumbing education or training. Follow the education pathway applicable to the license level being pursued.
  3. Accumulate qualifying plumbing experience. Document work experience performed under the supervision required by Utah law and rule.
  4. Meet examination eligibility requirements. Complete the required experience or education milestones before testing.
  5. Prepare for the Utah Plumber Written Exam. Study plumbing codes, drainage, water supply, fuel gas, hydronics, fixtures, safety, calculations, and practical trade subjects.
  6. Practice open-book reference navigation. Become familiar with the organization of code books, tables, definitions, indexes, and calculations before testing.
  7. Register for the examination through Prov. Follow the current Utah plumbing examination registration process.
  8. Complete the written examination. Candidates have four hours to complete 100 questions.
  9. Meet the required passing score. The current Utah Plumber Written Exam requires a minimum score of 70%.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Passing the written examination is part of the licensing process and does not by itself issue a Utah plumbing license.

State Requirements

Utah regulates plumbing through the Division of Professional Licensing. Apprentice, residential journeyman, journeyman, residential master, and master-level plumbing licenses are subject to the applicable Utah statutes and administrative rules.

Plumbing candidates working toward licensure must complete the education and experience pathway applicable to the license they are pursuing. Apprentice plumbing work must be performed within Utah's supervision and licensing requirements.

The Utah Plumber Written Exam is a technical examination and is separate from any additional practical or law-and-rule examination requirements that may apply to a particular plumbing license level.

Candidates should also understand that professional plumbing work involves more than code knowledge. Proper system installation requires familiarity with water supply, drainage, venting, fixtures, fuel gas, water heaters, piping, hydronics, safety, calculations, plans, and coordination with structural conditions.

Passing the written examination demonstrates required examination competency, but applicants must still satisfy all applicable licensing requirements before performing plumbing work at the license level they are pursuing.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong study plan should begin with Drain, Waste, and Vent systems, which account for 20 questions. Review drainage piping, fittings, sizing, cleanouts, traps, venting principles, fixture connections, building drains, building sewers, and installation requirements.

Water Supply contains 15 questions. Candidates should review water service, distribution piping, valves, pressure, sizing, materials, cross-connection considerations, and the relationship between fixture demand and system design.

Indirect and Special Waste accounts for 10 questions. Study indirect waste receptors, air gaps, air breaks, special waste connections, separators, interceptors, and other requirements affecting specialized drainage.

Plumbing Math contains 9 questions. Use Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters and IPT's Pipe Trades Handbook to reinforce measurements, grade, offsets, areas, volumes, conversions, piping calculations, and other trade mathematics.

Plumbing Safety also contains 9 questions. Study OSHA requirements associated with excavations, ladders, scaffolds, fall hazards, tools, protective equipment, and general construction safety. Plumbing work often occurs in unfinished buildings and excavations where safety requirements are particularly important.

Pipe and Fittings contains 7 questions. Review common piping materials, fittings, joining methods, valves, supports, and installation principles.

Fuel Gas accounts for 6 questions. Review piping, appliances, combustion air, venting, sizing principles, shutoffs, and related fuel-gas installation requirements.

Plumbing Fixtures also contains 6 questions. Study fixture requirements, installation, connections, fixture fittings, clearances, and related plumbing-code provisions.

Hydronics and Water Heaters each contain 5 questions. Candidates should understand hot-water systems, hydronic piping, equipment, temperature and pressure protection, installation, and basic operating principles.

Plan-reading and structural-penetration questions require candidates to interpret drawings and understand how plumbing systems interact with building construction. Review common drawing symbols, piping routes, dimensions, penetrations, insulation, and coordination requirements.

Practice under timed conditions as the examination approaches. For each practice problem, identify whether the answer comes from code knowledge, trade knowledge, mathematics, or safety. Then use the most appropriate reference and track how long it takes to locate the answer.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

When a question is missed, determine the cause. A candidate may understand drainage but select the wrong fitting, know the correct formula but use incorrect units, or choose the correct code chapter but overlook an exception. Identifying the cause makes future study more focused.
