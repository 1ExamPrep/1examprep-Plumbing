## Open Book Test

The Utah Plumber Written Exam is an **open book test**. Candidates may use permitted references under the examination provider's rules.

Open-book testing rewards candidates who already know how their references are organized. Simply bringing books into the testing room is not enough. Candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question and immediately selecting the reference most likely to contain the answer.

Questions about drainage, water distribution, fixtures, piping systems, or plumbing installations may direct candidates toward the plumbing code. Fuel-gas questions may require the fuel-gas code, while hydronic and mechanical-system questions may point toward mechanical requirements.

Safety questions may require familiarity with **29 CFR Part 1926**. Residential sprinkler questions may involve **NFPA 13D**, while mathematics and practical pipe-trade calculations can be reinforced through **Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters** and **IPT’s Pipe Trades Handbook**.

With four hours for 100 questions, candidates average approximately 2.4 minutes per question. Efficient reference navigation can preserve valuable time for difficult calculations and detailed code questions.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong study plan should begin with **Drain, Waste, and Vent systems**, which account for 20 questions. Review drainage piping, fittings, sizing, cleanouts, traps, venting principles, fixture connections, building drains, building sewers, and installation requirements.

**Water Supply** contains 15 questions. Candidates should review water service, distribution piping, valves, pressure, sizing, materials, cross-connection considerations, and the relationship between fixture demand and system design.

**Indirect and Special Waste** accounts for 10 questions. Study indirect waste receptors, air gaps, air breaks, special waste connections, separators, interceptors, and other requirements affecting specialized drainage.

**Plumbing Math** contains 9 questions. Use Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters and IPT's Pipe Trades Handbook to reinforce measurements, grade, offsets, areas, volumes, conversions, piping calculations, and other trade mathematics.

**Plumbing Safety** also contains 9 questions. Study OSHA requirements associated with excavations, ladders, scaffolds, fall hazards, tools, protective equipment, and general construction safety. Plumbing work often occurs in unfinished buildings and excavations where safety requirements are particularly important.

**Pipe and Fittings** contains 7 questions. Review common piping materials, fittings, joining methods, valves, supports, and installation principles.

**Fuel Gas** accounts for 6 questions. Review piping, appliances, combustion air, venting, sizing principles, shutoffs, and related fuel-gas installation requirements.

**Plumbing Fixtures** also contains 6 questions. Study fixture requirements, installation, connections, fixture fittings, clearances, and related plumbing-code provisions.

**Hydronics** and **Water Heaters** each contain 5 questions. Candidates should understand hot-water systems, hydronic piping, equipment, temperature and pressure protection, installation, and basic operating principles.

Plan-reading and structural-penetration questions require candidates to interpret drawings and understand how plumbing systems interact with building construction. Review common drawing symbols, piping routes, dimensions, penetrations, insulation, and coordination requirements.

Practice under timed conditions as the examination approaches. For each practice problem, identify whether the answer comes from code knowledge, trade knowledge, mathematics, or safety. Then use the most appropriate reference and track how long it takes to locate the answer.
