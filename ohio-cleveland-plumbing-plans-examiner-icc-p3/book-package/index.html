Exam Details

The ICC Plumbing Plans Examiner (P3) exam is a certification exam for candidates who review plumbing construction documents for code compliance. The exam measures the ability to evaluate proposed plumbing systems, apply code provisions, identify required plan information, and determine whether a design meets applicable plumbing code requirements. Candidates should be prepared to work from plan-review-style questions that require both code navigation and practical interpretation.

The exam commonly includes 60 multiple-choice questions with a 2.5-hour time limit. The exam is open book and requires candidates to locate code provisions quickly and accurately. Candidates preparing for the 2021 exam version should study from the International Plumbing Code, 2021 and the International Fuel Gas Code, 2021. ICC A117.1-2017 is included in this package to support accessibility-related plumbing plan review preparation.

Major study areas include plumbing plan review procedures, administration, construction documents, plumbing fixtures, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, storm drainage, and special systems. Candidates should understand how information is shown on plans and how a plans examiner determines whether the submitted documents are complete enough for approval.

Administration and plan review questions may involve code scope, permit requirements, construction document requirements, approval authority, alternative materials, inspection coordination, plan submission information, and code applicability. A plans examiner may need to identify missing information, determine whether additional details are needed, or evaluate whether the submitted design demonstrates compliance.

Fixture and fitting questions may involve minimum plumbing fixture counts, fixture type, toilet room layout, drinking fountains, service sinks, employee facilities, public facilities, accessible plumbing fixtures, and occupancy-related requirements. Candidates should be ready to use both the International Plumbing Code and ICC A117.1-2017 when a question involves accessibility, clearances, usability, or accessible fixture arrangement.

Water supply and distribution questions may involve water service, water distribution piping, fixture units, pipe sizing concepts, pressure requirements, water heaters, hot water supply, valves, backflow protection, potable water protection, and system design information shown on plans. Candidates should know where to locate water supply provisions and how to use tables when a question requires sizing or system evaluation.

Sanitary drainage questions may involve drainage fixture units, horizontal and vertical drainage piping, pipe sizing, slope, cleanouts, offsets, indirect waste, special waste, traps, interceptors, separators, and system layout. Plans examiner candidates should become comfortable reading code-based scenarios that describe drainage conditions and then matching those facts to the correct code section.

Venting questions may involve individual vents, common vents, wet vents, circuit vents, vent stacks, stack vents, branch vents, vent termination, vent sizing, air admittance valves where permitted by the code, and required vent connections. Venting can be one of the more challenging study areas because small differences in the layout can change the correct answer.

Storm drainage questions may involve roof drains, secondary drainage, overflow scuppers, leaders, conductors, storm pipe sizing, rainfall rates, drainage area, and roof drainage design information. Candidates should study how the International Plumbing Code organizes storm drainage provisions and practice locating the tables used for sizing and review.

Special systems and fuel gas coordination may involve medical gas coordination, special waste, indirect waste, interceptors, fuel gas piping, gas appliances, appliance venting, combustion air, and coordinated building systems shown on construction drawings. The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021 is an important reference when plan review questions involve gas piping systems or fuel-burning appliances.

Open Book Test

The ICC Plumbing Plans Examiner (P3) exam is an open-book exam. Open book testing allows candidates to use approved references during the exam, but it still requires strong preparation. Candidates must know where information is located, how the books are organized, and how to apply code provisions under time pressure.

For the 2021 exam version, candidates should prepare with the International Plumbing Code, 2021 and the International Fuel Gas Code, 2021. ICC A117.1-2017 is included in this package to support accessibility-related plumbing plan review study. Candidates should make sure their books are organized according to ICC testing rules and should become familiar with allowed tabs, highlighting, and notes before exam day.

A good open-book strategy starts with the table of contents. Candidates should know where major plumbing topics are located before practicing questions. After that, the index becomes a useful tool for finding specific terms, such as fixture unit, cleanout, vent, storm drainage, backflow, interceptor, drinking fountain, accessible route, lavatory, water closet, or combustion air.

Open-book success depends on speed and accuracy. During the exam, a candidate may have only a few minutes to read the question, identify the topic, find the correct section, and select the best answer. Practice should focus on moving from question facts to book location quickly. Candidates should avoid slow page-by-page searching and instead build a repeatable method for finding answers.

Because the P3 exam involves more than one reference, candidates should practice switching between books. A fixture count question may begin in the International Plumbing Code, while an accessibility question may require ICC A117.1-2017. A coordinated plan review question involving gas appliances may require the International Fuel Gas Code. Becoming comfortable with all three books helps reduce stress during the test.

Test Information and Study Materials

The ICC P3 exam should be studied as a plan review exam. Candidates should practice evaluating the kind of information that appears on construction documents, including fixture schedules, riser diagrams, water supply layouts, sanitary drainage layouts, venting diagrams, roof drainage details, fuel gas information, and accessibility details. A plans examiner must determine whether the submitted documents show compliance clearly enough for approval.

Start with the International Plumbing Code, 2021. Review the table of contents, definitions, and chapter organization. Learn where plumbing fixtures are addressed, where water supply provisions are located, where drainage and venting requirements appear, and which tables are commonly used. Many exam questions can be answered more quickly when the candidate already knows which chapter controls the topic.

Fixture count preparation should include minimum plumbing fixtures, occupancy-related fixture requirements, public and employee facilities, required service sinks, drinking fountains, and accessible fixtures. Candidates should understand how plan review questions may combine fixture count requirements with accessibility requirements.

Accessibility preparation should include ICC A117.1-2017. Candidates should study toilet rooms, bathing facilities, drinking fountains, clear floor spaces, turning spaces, reach ranges, approaches, lavatories, water closets, grab bar relationships, and usability requirements. Accessibility-related plumbing plan review often requires attention to both fixture presence and fixture arrangement.

Water supply preparation should include water service, distribution piping, fixture units, pipe sizing, required pressure, hot water systems, water heaters, backflow protection, valves, and potable water safety. Plans examiner candidates should also pay attention to what information should appear on construction documents, such as pipe sizes, equipment information, fixture data, and system layout.

Sanitary drainage preparation should include drainage fixture units, pipe sizing, horizontal and vertical drainage, slope, cleanouts, traps, indirect waste, special waste, interceptors, separators, and drainage system arrangement. Candidates should practice using tables and code sections to evaluate whether a proposed design complies.

Venting preparation should include individual vents, common vents, wet vents, circuit vents, branch vents, vent stacks, stack vents, vent sizing, vent termination, and special venting methods addressed by the code. Storm drainage preparation should include roof drainage, secondary drainage, overflow requirements, conductors, leaders, scuppers, storm pipe sizing, rainfall rates, and drainage area. Fuel gas preparation should include gas piping layouts, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, chimneys, gas pipe sizing, equipment location, and coordination with plumbing or mechanical drawings. Timed code lookup practice is essential. During study, begin without a timer and focus on accuracy. The goal is to find answers efficiently without sacrificing careful reading.

A strong weekly study routine might include one session on administration and construction documents, one on fixtures and accessibility, one on water supply, one on sanitary drainage, one on venting, one on storm drainage, and one on fuel gas coordination. End each week with mixed practice questions so you become comfortable moving between topics and references.

Plan review preparation should also include learning how to read question details carefully. A question may ask what information is missing from a plan, whether a proposed fixture arrangement complies, whether a pipe size is acceptable, whether a venting method is allowed, or whether a storm drainage design includes required secondary drainage. The correct answer depends on matching the facts to the correct code provision.
