A timed exam won’t reward slow searching or rereading long sections. It rewards candidates who can recognize the topic quickly, start in the correct reference, confirm the deciding detail (especially conditions and exceptions), and keep moving with steady momentum.

A practical open-book routine to train during prep:

  1. Label the topic first. Identify whether the scenario is plumbing-system driven (IPC) or fuel-gas driven (IFGC).

Test Information and Study Materials

The most effective prep for a two-reference, open-book contractor exam is performance-based practice. Reading helps you get familiar with layout and terminology, but readiness improves fastest when you repeatedly train the same actions you’ll use under time pressure: identify the topic, choose the right book first, confirm the controlling language, and maintain steady pacing.

1) Master the first-book decision (IPC vs. IFGC)
With two code books, time is often won or lost at the start of a question. Build the habit of choosing your starting reference before you open anything. During practice, make it a rule: label the issue, choose IPC or IFGC, locate the best section, and confirm the deciding detail. If you start in the wrong book, identify what clue should have guided you to the other reference first—this is how speed improves.

2) Confirm the deciding detail instead of over-reading
Most questions are decided by a small piece of language. When you find the right area, scan for:

  • Conditions that define when a requirement applies (“when,” “where,” “if”)
  • Exceptions that modify the general rule
  • Definitions that change what the scenario means
  • Requirement language that signals what must be done

3) Train pacing on purpose
Open book doesn’t mean unlimited time. During timed practice, confirm what you can efficiently, choose the best supported answer, and keep moving. If time allows, return later. This protects momentum and reduces stress.

4) Use your full year of course access to stay consistent
With 1 year of course access, you can build steady progress instead of cramming. Consistency is how navigation becomes automatic and confidence becomes reliable.
