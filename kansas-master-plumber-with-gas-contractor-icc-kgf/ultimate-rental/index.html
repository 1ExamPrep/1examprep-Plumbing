Test Information and Study Materials

The most effective open-book prep is performance-based. Reading helps you get familiar with layout and terminology, but readiness improves fastest when you repeatedly train the actions you’ll use under time pressure: identify the topic, choose IPC vs. IFGC, confirm the controlling language, and maintain steady pacing.

Train the first-book decision (IPC vs. IFGC)
When two code books are in play, the first move matters. Build the habit of choosing your starting reference before you open anything. During practice, enforce this routine:

  • Label the issue in plain language
  • Choose IPC or IFGC as the best starting point
  • Locate the relevant section
  • Confirm the deciding detail

If you start in the wrong reference, don’t just correct it and move on—identify what clue in the question should have sent you to the other book first. That’s how your accuracy and speed improve together.

Confirm what decides the answer
One of the most common open-book traps is over-reading. Most exam questions are decided by a small piece of language: a condition, an exception, a definition, or a requirement statement. When you find the right area of the code, train your eyes to scan for:

  • Conditions that define when a rule applies (“when,” “where,” “if”)
  • Exceptions that modify or remove a general rule
  • Definitions that change what the scenario actually means
  • Requirement language that signals what must be done

This habit reduces “almost correct” answers and improves confidence because your final choice is supported by the controlling language.

Build memory of location with repeated lookups
Open-book exams reward familiarity. The fastest way to build familiarity is repetition. Each time you locate a similar type of requirement, you strengthen your memory of where that topic lives and how the code organizes related rules. Over time, you’ll notice your lookups get faster and your stress drops because you’re confirming in familiar territory.
