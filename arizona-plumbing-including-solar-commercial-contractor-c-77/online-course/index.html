Open Book Test

The Arizona C-77 Plumbing, Including Solar commercial contractor exam is an open-book trade exam using approved references. Candidates are responsible for bringing their allowed references to the examination center. Because the reference list is large, book familiarity is one of the most important parts of exam preparation.

Open-book testing does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates must still understand the material, know which reference applies to a question, locate the correct section, and read the surrounding language carefully. A student who has not practiced with the books may lose valuable time searching through unfamiliar indexes, tables, definitions, and chapters.

Students should prepare by using the same references they plan to use for exam practice. A strong study routine includes reviewing one subject area at a time, answering practice questions, locating supporting information in the books, and repeating weak topics until the reference locations become easier to find. The goal is to make the books feel familiar before exam day.

Exam references should be prepared according to PSI rules. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the exam session. Candidates should not write in the references during the exam. Additional papers, whether loose or attached, are not allowed with approved references. Permanent tabs may be used, while temporary removable tabs are not allowed. Downloaded references must be bound before being brought into the testing center.

Test Information and Study Materials

Effective preparation for the Arizona C-77 Plumbing, Including Solar exam should combine code study, trade math, safety review, solar system review, and repeated reference practice. The approved reference list is long, so students should avoid random study. A better approach is to study by topic, connect each topic to the correct reference, and practice locating answers under timed conditions.

For plumbing code preparation, students should focus heavily on the 2018 International Plumbing Code. Important study areas include water supply systems, sanitary drainage, storm drainage, venting, fixtures, traps, interceptors, cleanouts, materials, installation requirements, and inspection provisions. The IPC includes many tables, definitions, and exceptions, so candidates should practice reading questions carefully and locating the exact section that applies.

For fuel gas preparation, the 2018 International Fuel Gas Code should be studied closely. Students should review gas piping, sizing, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, shutoff valves, pressure testing, and installation requirements. Fuel gas questions often depend on system type, appliance conditions, pressure, location, or table usage, so careful reference navigation is important.

For mechanical and hydronic topics, students should use the 2018 International Mechanical Code, Modern Hydronic Heating, Boiler Operator’s Guide, and related pipe trade references. Preparation should include hydronic piping, boilers, pumps, controls, combustion air, venting, system operation, heating-system components, and equipment installation requirements.

For solar preparation, students should review solar water heating and solar pool heating concepts. Solar-related plumbing work can involve collectors, storage tanks, heat exchangers, piping, pumps, controls, circulation, heat transfer, freeze protection, mounting considerations, and installation practices. Candidates should connect solar system knowledge with plumbing, fuel gas, and mechanical code awareness when studying.

For fire sprinkler preparation, students should review the NFPA 13, NFPA 13D, and NFPA 13R references. These standards support study for sprinkler system concepts, occupancy differences, system components, installation rules, water supply considerations, and fire protection requirements. Students should learn which sprinkler reference applies to which type of building or occupancy.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

For wastewater and excavation-related study, candidates should review the Arizona wastewater provisions and the Arizona 811 Blue Stake Brochure. Plumbing work often involves underground systems, excavation, utility awareness, and site coordination. Understanding safe digging practices and utility-location responsibilities is important for both exam preparation and professional work.

For OSHA safety, students should study 29 CFR Part 1926 for common construction hazards. Plumbing contractors may encounter excavation hazards, confined work areas, ladders, scaffolds, material handling, personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, and general jobsite safety responsibilities. OSHA questions often describe a work condition and require the candidate to identify the correct safety practice or rule.

Trade math should be part of the study routine. Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters and IPT’s Pipe Trades Handbook can support preparation for measurements, conversions, offsets, slopes, pipe data, volume, area, pressure-related concepts, and jobsite calculations. Students should practice math problems step by step and become comfortable recognizing which formula or table applies.

A productive study routine includes reviewing course material, answering practice questions, locating answers in the books, reviewing missed questions, and repeating weak areas. Students should also practice under timed conditions. The C-77 exam allows 240 minutes for 100 questions, so candidates should build a steady pace and learn when to search a reference, when to rely on prepared knowledge, and when to move forward and return to a difficult question later.
