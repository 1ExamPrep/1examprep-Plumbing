Exam Details

The New Hampshire journeyman plumber licensing examination is administered through Prov for the New Hampshire Office of Professional Licensure and Certification. Candidates seeking plumbing licensure in New Hampshire must complete the required examination for the license category they are pursuing. Written plumbing exams may be taken remotely or at one of Prov’s approved testing centers.

The New Hampshire Journeyman Plumber exam includes 85 questions with 3 hours allowed for testing. Candidates must achieve a passing score of 70% on the written exam. Exam results are provided after testing according to the exam administrator’s reporting process.

The journeyman plumber exam includes the following subject areas:

  • Calculations, journey level
  • Drainage requirements, journey level
  • Fixtures and faucets, journey level
  • Plumbing and fundamentals, journey level
  • Traps and interceptors, journey level
  • Venting regulations, journey level
  • Water distribution, journey level

The exam is designed to test the plumbing knowledge a candidate should be able to use at the journeyman level.

Open Book Test

The New Hampshire journeyman plumber exam is an open book test using authorized references. Candidates may use approved references while taking the exam, and those references are checked before the candidate is permitted to use them in the testing room. The approved references for this package are the International Plumbing Code, 2018 and the Purpose and Scope of Administrative Rules of the Board.

Open book testing does not remove the need to study. The strongest candidates are familiar with the structure of the International Plumbing Code before exam day. They know how to move from a question topic to the correct chapter, section, table, or definition. They also understand how to use administrative materials without wasting time searching through unrelated pages.

Candidates should prepare their reference materials using only permitted methods. Highlighting is allowed, underlining in pen is allowed, and permanent tabs may be attached to important pages. Handwritten notes are not allowed in any portion of a reference book used for testing and must be erased before the materials are used. Movable tabs are not permitted. Post-it notes are not permitted. Photocopied documents cannot be added to a reference book, and photocopied versions of reference books are not permitted. Test preparation study guides and practice exams are not approved references for the testing room.

During the testing process, candidates may not share reference materials and may not highlight, underline, mark, or write information in their reference materials. Preparation should be completed before exam day. A practical approach is to tab the major chapters of the International Plumbing Code, highlight key section headings where useful, and practice locating common tables and definitions.

Test Information and Study Materials

The New Hampshire journeyman plumber exam is built around practical plumbing knowledge and the ability to use authorized references efficiently. Candidates should begin by reviewing the International Plumbing Code, 2018. The IPC is organized by subject, so learning the chapter layout is one of the most helpful study steps. Candidates should know where to find definitions, general regulations, fixtures, water heaters, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, indirect waste, vents, traps, interceptors, storm drainage, and referenced standards.

Calculations are part of the journeyman-level exam. Candidates should practice plumbing math and learn where relevant tables are located in the IPC. Calculation topics may involve sizing, drainage fixture units, water distribution, developed length, slope, volume, and other practical plumbing measurements. The goal is not only to memorize formulas, but to understand which code table or rule applies to a particular question.

Drainage requirements are another major area of preparation. Candidates should review sanitary drainage system rules, pipe sizing concepts, slope requirements, cleanout requirements, fixture unit values, and the code provisions that govern how drainage systems are designed and installed. Drainage questions often require careful reading because small details in the question can change the correct code answer.

Venting regulations are heavily represented in journeyman-level plumbing preparation. Candidates should be comfortable with vent types, vent sizing, vent connections, limitations, distances, and common code rules that apply to venting systems. Since venting topics can be detailed, candidates should tab the major venting chapter sections and practice locating requirements quickly.

Fixtures and faucets should also be reviewed in detail. The IPC includes requirements for plumbing fixtures, fixture installation, minimum facilities, faucets, connections, and related provisions. Candidates should understand how fixture requirements connect to occupancy, installation conditions, and system design.

Water distribution topics require familiarity with water supply rules, pipe sizing, pressure considerations, materials, valves, protection of potable water, and related code sections. Candidates should practice finding water distribution tables and applying them to question scenarios. Water distribution questions may combine code lookup with practical field knowledge.

Test Information and Study Materials (continued)

Traps and interceptors are also important. Candidates should review trap requirements, trap seals, prohibited traps, cleanouts, interceptors, separators, and related installation rules. These topics appear regularly in plumbing code study because they are essential to safe and sanitary plumbing system operation.

Plumbing and fundamentals questions may include general code concepts, definitions, materials, joints, supports, installation standards, and basic trade knowledge. Candidates should not skip foundational chapters because many exam questions begin with definitions or general rules before moving into more specific code applications.

The Purpose and Scope of Administrative Rules of the Board should be reviewed as a separate study subject. Candidates should become familiar with the language and organization of the administrative reference. Administrative questions can feel different from code questions because they focus on regulatory purpose and scope rather than physical installation. A candidate who studies only the IPC may miss opportunities to answer these questions quickly.

A strong study routine should include timed practice. Candidates can choose one topic area, open the IPC, locate the correct chapter, and answer practice questions while watching the clock. The goal is to build speed and accuracy together. Because the exam includes 85 questions in 3 hours, candidates need to avoid spending too much time on any single question.

Book preparation should be done before test day. Use permanent tabs to identify important chapters, definitions, drainage, venting, fixtures, water distribution, traps, calculations, and administrative material. Use highlighting and underlining in pen where permitted to make important sections easier to locate. Do not add handwritten notes, movable tabs, Post-it notes, loose sheets, or unauthorized materials.
